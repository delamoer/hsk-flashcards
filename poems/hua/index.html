导读About this poem

A riddle in four lines: mountains with colour but water without sound, flowers that outlast spring, birds that don’t fly when you come close. What is it? A painting — and one of the first poems Chinese children learn.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗其实是一个谜语，谜底就是题目“画”。远远看去，山的颜色很鲜明；走近了听，水却没有声音。春天过去了，花还开着；人走过来，鸟也不害怕、不飞走。每一句都和真实的自然相反——真山走远了才看不清颜色，真水有流动的声音，真花到春天结束就谢了，真鸟见人就飞。正因为这些“不合常理”，我们才猜出它是一幅画。全诗用了四组反义词：远—近、有—无、去—来、在—不（惊），对得整整齐齐，读起来很顺口，非常适合初学者认字、学反义词。

In English

This little poem is a riddle, and the answer is its title: a painting. From far away, the mountains show bright colour; up close, the water makes no sound. Spring has gone, yet the flowers are still here; people come near, yet the birds aren’t startled. Every line is the opposite of real nature. Real mountains fade into grey when you are far away; real water babbles; real flowers drop when spring ends; real birds fly off when you approach. Because each detail is “impossible”, we realise we must be looking at a picture. The poem is built on pairs of opposites — far and near (远/近), have and have not (有/无), go and come (去/来), and the flowers staying put against the birds not flying off — set neatly against each other line by line. That makes it easy to chant and remember, which is why Chinese children often learn it in their first year of school, both to recognise characters and to learn opposites.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The poem is widely printed under Wang Wei’s name, but it is not found in his collected works, and its origin is uncertain; similar lines appear in a Song-dynasty Buddhist commentary. Wang Wei (701–761) was a famous painter as well as a poet, which may be why the attribution stuck.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

远看山有色，

yuǎn kàn shān yǒu sè

From far away, the hills are bright with colour;

近听水无声。

jìn tīng shuǐ wú shēng

up close, the stream makes not a sound.

春去花还在，

chūn qù huā hái zài

Spring is gone, but the flowers are still blooming;

人来鸟不惊。

rén lái niǎo bù jīng

I walk right up, and the birds don’t fly away.
