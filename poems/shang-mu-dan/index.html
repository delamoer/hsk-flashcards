导读About this poem

Why is the peony called China’s “national beauty”? This little poem compares it with two other flowers and gives the answer — the whole capital comes out to see it.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗赞美牡丹，用的是对比和衬托：要夸一样东西，先拿别的来比。第一句说庭院前的芍药虽然艳丽，却缺少风骨；第二句说池塘里的荷花虽然干净，却显得冷淡少情。这两种花本来都很美，诗人故意挑它们的不足，是为了衬托牡丹。第三句直接说：只有牡丹才是真正的“国色”——全国最美的颜色。第四句写效果：牡丹一开，整个京城都轰动了，人人去看。唐代长安人特别爱牡丹，春天赏牡丹是全城的大事。“国色天香”后来成了形容牡丹、也形容美女的成语，今天牡丹仍被很多人看作中国的代表花。

In English

This poem praises the peony through comparison: to show how good something is, first set it beside something else. Line 1 says the herbaceous peony (芍药) in the courtyard is pretty but lacks dignity; line 2 says the lotus in the pond is pure but rather cold and unfeeling. Both flowers are lovely in their own right — the poet picks on their weak points only to make the peony shine. Line 3 states it plainly: only the peony has true “national beauty” (国色), the loveliest colour in the land. Line 4 shows the effect: when the peonies bloom, the whole capital is stirred and everyone goes out to see them. In Tang-dynasty Chang’an, people were crazy about peonies, and spring peony-viewing was a city-wide event; the best plants sold for a fortune. The phrase 国色天香 (“national beauty, heavenly fragrance”) later became an idiom for peonies and for beautiful women, and many people still think of the peony as China’s flower.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Yuxi (772–842) was a Tang poet known for his lively quatrains and folk-song style. This poem reflects the peony craze of Chang’an in his day.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

庭前芍药妖无格，

tíng qián sháo yào yāo wú gé

The courtyard’s peonies are pretty, but have no grace;

池上芙蕖净少情。

chí shàng fú qú jìng shǎo qíng

the lotus on the pond is pure, but cold.

唯有牡丹真国色，

wéi yǒu mǔ dān zhēn guó sè

Only the peony is the true beauty of the land —

花开时节动京城。

huā kāi shí jié dòng jīng chéng

when it blooms, the whole capital comes out to see.
