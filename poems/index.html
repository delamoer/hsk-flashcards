Pre-Qin
约前11世纪–前221年

中国诗歌的源头。北方有《诗经》——民间和宫廷里唱的歌，大多四个字一句；南方有以屈原为代表的《楚辞》，句子长短自由，充满神话和想象。

Where Chinese poetry begins. In the north, the Book of Songs collects songs people actually sang, mostly four characters per line; in the south, Qu Yuan and the Songs of Chu write freer, longer lines full of gods and myths.

前206年–公元220年

朝廷设立“乐府”到民间采集歌谣，留下许多朴素生动的民歌；五个字一句的“五言诗”在这时成熟，《古诗十九首》是代表。

The court set up a Music Bureau (yuèfǔ) to collect folk songs, preserving many lively, plain-spoken poems. The five-character line matured in this period, best seen in the Nineteen Old Poems.

220–589年

战乱不断、朝代更替频繁的年代。曹操父子写出慷慨悲壮的诗，陶渊明离开官场写田园生活，北方草原上流传着《敕勒歌》《木兰诗》这样的民歌。

Centuries of war and short-lived dynasties. Cao Cao and his sons wrote bold, stirring verse; Tao Yuanming quit office to write about farm life; the northern grasslands gave us folk songs like the Ballad of Mulan.

Táng
Tang
618–907年（含隋 581–618年）

中国诗歌的黄金时代，留下近五万首诗。格律严格的绝句、律诗在这时定型。李白豪放，杜甫沉郁，王维清静——中国人从小背的诗，大多来自唐朝。

The golden age of Chinese poetry — nearly 50,000 poems survive. The strict four-line and eight-line forms took their final shape. Li Bai the free spirit, Du Fu the conscience, Wang Wei the quiet painter-poet: most poems Chinese children learn by heart are Tang.

960–1279年（含五代 907–960年）

“词”成为最有代表性的诗体：按照现成的曲调填写歌词，句子长短不一，题目前的“水调歌头”“如梦令”就是曲调名。苏轼、李清照、辛弃疾是最有名的词人。

The age of cí — lyrics written to existing tunes, with lines of uneven length. Names like “Shui Diao Ge Tou” are the tune, not the title. Su Shi, Li Qingzhao and Xin Qiji are the great names.

Yuán
Yuan
1271–1368年

流行“散曲”和杂剧，也是配曲演唱的，语言更接近口语，活泼直白。马致远的《天净沙·秋思》只用几个名词就画出一幅秋景。

Sǎnqǔ songs and stage drama flourished — also sung to tunes, but in livelier, more everyday language. Ma Zhiyuan’s “Autumn Thoughts” paints a whole scene with little more than a string of nouns.

诗词继续发展，也出现在小说里——《三国演义》开篇词、《红楼梦》里的诗都广为流传。清代的纳兰性德以深情的词闻名。

Poetry carried on and moved into the great novels — the opening lyric of Romance of the Three Kingdoms and the poems in Dream of the Red Chamber are known by everyone. Nalan Xingde of the Qing is loved for his tender cí.
