导读About this poem

A stone path winds up an autumn mountain; the poet stops his carriage just to look at the maples, and finds frosted leaves redder than spring flowers. A happy, colourful autumn poem.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人在秋天坐车上山时看到的美景。第一句写一条石头小路弯弯曲曲地伸向远处的山上；第二句写在白云升起的地方，隐约有几户人家——画面一下子有了远近和生活的气息。第三句是全诗的转折：诗人忽然让车停下来，只是因为太喜欢傍晚的枫林了。“坐”在这里不是“坐下”，而是“因为”的意思，这是古今词义不同的好例子。最后一句是千古名句：经过霜打的枫叶，比二月的春花还要红。一般诗人写秋天，常常写悲凉；杜牧却觉得秋天比春天还美，写得明亮、热烈，充满生命力。用“红于二月花”作比较，就是拿秋叶和春花比，突出枫叶的鲜艳。

In English

Du Mu is travelling up a mountain by carriage in late autumn. The first line follows a stone path winding far up the cold, bare mountain. The second adds a detail that brings the scene to life: up where the white clouds are born, there are a few houses — people live up there. Now the picture has depth: near and far, wild and human. Then comes the turn. The poet suddenly has the carriage stop, simply because he loves the maple woods in the evening light. Be careful with 坐 here: it does not mean “to sit” but “because” — a good example of a word whose old meaning has disappeared from modern Chinese. The last line is the one everyone quotes: leaves touched by frost are redder than the flowers of the second month, that is, redder than spring blossoms. Most Chinese poets wrote autumn as a sad season of decline. Du Mu turns that around: for him autumn is even more beautiful than spring, glowing and full of life. Comparing autumn leaves with spring flowers, and letting the leaves win, is what makes the poem so fresh. Today people still quote 霜叶红于二月花 every autumn when the maples turn.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–852) was a late-Tang poet and official, famous for elegant quatrains; he is often called “Little Du” to distinguish him from Du Fu. When and where this poem was written is not known.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“石径斜”的“斜”：统编教材注 xié；古音读 xiá（与“家”“花”押韵），旧时教材和不少老师读 xiá。网站拼音按统编教材采用 xié。“生处”的“处”读 chù。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

远上寒山石径斜，

yuǎn shàng hán shān shí jìng xié

A stone path winds far up the cold mountain;

白云生处有人家。

bái yún shēng chù yǒu rén jiā

where the white clouds rise, a few homes are tucked away.

停车坐爱枫林晚，

tíng chē zuò ài fēng lín wǎn

I stop my carriage — just to love the maples in the evening light:

霜叶红于二月花。

shuāng yè hóng yú èr yuè huā

these frost-touched leaves are redder than spring flowers.
