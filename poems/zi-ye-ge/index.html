导读About this poem

A young woman, hair loose over her shoulders, stretches out on her lover’s lap and asks: is there any part of me you don’t find lovely? Twenty characters of playful, confident tenderness from 1,600 years ago.

中文解析

《子夜歌》是南朝江南一带流行的情歌，传说是晋代一位叫“子夜”的女子创作的，所以叫这个名字。这首是女子自己在说话：昨夜到现在都没有梳头，乌黑的长发披在两肩上；她柔柔地伸展身体，靠在情郎的膝上，问他：我哪里不可爱呢？“可怜”在古代是“可爱、惹人爱”的意思，和今天“值得同情”完全不同，这是学习古诗时要特别注意的古今词义变化。这首诗没有比喻，也没有景物描写，只用一个画面、一句问话，就写出了恋爱中女子的娇憨和自信。南朝民歌常常这样大胆直接，语言像说话一样自然，和北方民歌的豪迈形成鲜明对比——有“南柔北刚”的说法。

In English

The “Ziye songs” were love songs popular around the lower Yangtze in the 4th–6th centuries; legend says they were first sung by a Jin-dynasty woman named Ziye. In this one the woman speaks for herself. She hasn’t combed her hair since last night, and it falls loose over both shoulders. She stretches out, soft and easy, across her lover’s knees and asks him: “Is there anywhere at all that isn’t lovely?” Watch the word 可怜 kělián: in classical Chinese it means “lovable, adorable”, while in modern Chinese it means “pitiful”. It is one of the best examples of how a word can turn almost upside down over the centuries. There is no metaphor here and no landscape — just one picture and one question — yet we hear her teasing confidence perfectly. Southern folk songs are often this bold and conversational, and people contrast them with the tough, plain-spoken songs of the north: “soft south, hard north”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Ziye songs are anonymous folk lyrics of the Eastern Jin and Southern Dynasties (4th–6th century), traditionally said to have been created by a woman called Ziye. Forty-two of them survive in the Song-dynasty Collection of Yuefu Poems.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

宿昔不梳头，

sù xī bù shū tóu

I haven’t combed my hair since last night;

丝发被两肩。

sī fà pī liǎng jiān

it spills like silk over both my shoulders.

婉伸郎膝上，

wǎn shēn láng xī shàng

I stretch out, soft, across your knees —

何处不可怜。

hé chù bù kě lián

tell me, is any part of me not lovely?
