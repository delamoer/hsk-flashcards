导读About this poem

Two lines sung in 227 BC by the assassin Jing Ke as he set out to kill the King of Qin, knowing he would not come back: “The wind sighs, the Yi River is cold; the brave man goes, and will not return.”

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

公元前227年，燕国太子丹派荆轲去刺杀秦王（就是后来的秦始皇）。出发那天，太子和朋友们穿着白衣服（丧服）在易水边送他，好朋友高渐离击筑（一种乐器），荆轲合着音乐唱了这首歌。第一句写景：秋风萧萧地吹，易水冰冷——风声和寒水，既是真实的天气，也是送别时悲凉的心情。第二句写人：壮士这一去，就不会再回来了。荆轲知道刺杀几乎不可能成功，也知道自己一定会死，却还是去了。只有两句，没有一个字写害怕或犹豫，却让人感到一种悲壮——又悲伤又壮烈。据说当时在场的人都怒目圆睁，头发竖起来顶住了帽子。荆轲后来刺杀失败，被杀。“一去不复返”今天还是常用语。

In English

In 227 BC, Crown Prince Dan of Yan sent Jing Ke to assassinate the King of Qin — the man who would become the First Emperor. On the day he left, the prince and his friends, dressed in white, the colour of mourning, saw him off on the bank of the Yi River. His friend Gao Jianli played the zhù, a struck string instrument, and Jing Ke sang this song. The first line is the scene: the wind sighs, the river is cold — real weather, but also the grief of the parting. The second line is the man: the brave one goes, and will not come back. Jing Ke knew the mission was almost hopeless and that he would die either way; he went anyway. There is not one word of fear or doubt in the song, which is why it feels both tragic and heroic, a mood Chinese calls bēizhuàng (悲壮). The historian Sima Qian writes that the listeners glared, and their hair stood on end beneath their caps. The assassination failed and Jing Ke was killed. The phrase 一去不复返, “gone and never to return”, is still in daily use.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Jing Ke (died 227 BC) was a swordsman from the state of Wei who became the guest of Prince Dan of Yan. The song and the story of the farewell are recorded in Sima Qian’s Records of the Grand Historian (c. 90 BC).
