Bamboo Branch Song

导读About this poem

A girl by the river hears the boy she likes singing on the water. Does he care for her or not? Like the weather — sun in the east, rain in the west — she can’t tell. The poem hangs on a pun between “sunny” and “feeling”.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“竹枝词”本来是四川、重庆一带的民歌，刘禹锡在那里做官时，模仿民歌写了一组。这首用一个少女的口气来写。第一句写景：江边杨柳青青，江水涨得和岸一样平，是美好的春天。第二句，她忽然听见心上人在江上一边踏着节拍一边唱歌。他是不是在唱给我听？后两句用天气作比：东边出太阳，西边却下着雨，说是没有晴天吧，又有晴天。这里最妙的是“晴”和“情”同音，表面说天气“晴”，其实说的是感情的“情”——他对我到底有没有情？这种利用同音字一语双关的手法叫“谐音双关”，是民歌常用的。少女又期待、又不确定的心情，被写得含蓄又活泼。

In English

“Bamboo branch songs” were folk songs from the Sichuan–Chongqing area. When Liu Yuxi served as an official there, he wrote a set in the same style. This one is in the voice of a young girl. The first line sets the scene: green willows on the bank, and the river risen level with the shore — a lovely spring day. In the second line she suddenly hears the young man she likes singing out on the river, stamping the beat as he sings. Is he singing for her? The last two lines answer with the weather: the sun is out in the east, but it is raining in the west — you could say it isn’t sunny, and yet it is. The key is a pun: 晴 qíng (“sunny”) sounds exactly like 情 qíng (“feeling, love”). On the surface she is talking about the weather; underneath she is asking, “Does he have feelings for me or not?” Puns based on identical sounds are a favourite trick of Chinese folk songs. Her hope and uncertainty come across lightly and playfully, without a single direct word about love.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Yuxi (772–842), a Tang poet and official, wrote his “Bamboo Branch Songs” around 822 while serving as prefect of Kuizhou (today’s Fengjie, Chongqing), basing them on local folk songs he heard there.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“道是”的“道”是“说”的意思，读 dào。“晴”“情”都读 qíng，是谐音双关。“踏歌”读 tà gē。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材版本。刘禹锡《竹枝词二首》之一。重要异文：“闻郎江上踏歌声”一作“闻郎江上唱歌声”；“道是无晴却有晴”一作“还是无晴却有晴”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

杨柳青青江水平，

yáng liǔ qīng qīng jiāng shuǐ píng

Green willows, and the river brimming to its banks —

闻郎江上踏歌声。

wén láng jiāng shàng tà gē shēng

I hear my boy out on the water, singing and keeping time.

东边日出西边雨，

dōng biān rì chū xī biān yǔ

Sun in the east and rain in the west:

道是无晴却有晴。

dào shì wú qíng què yǒu qíng

is it clear or isn’t it — does he care or not?
