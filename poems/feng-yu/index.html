风雨

Fēng Yǔ
Wind and Rain
佚名（《诗经·郑风》）Anonymous (Book of Songs, Airs of Zheng)先秦 Pre-Qin
体裁Form
四言诗 · 国风

导读About this poem

A cold, stormy, dark morning, roosters crowing nonstop — and then the one she has been waiting for walks in. In three short verses, gloom turns to joy. Later readers also took it as a picture of keeping faith in dark times.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

诗中的女子在一个风雨交加的早晨等待心上人。外面风雨凄冷，天色昏暗得像夜晚一样，公鸡一声接一声地叫，景色又冷又乱，正像她焦急不安的心情。就在这时，她等的人来了！“既见君子，云胡不夷？”——见到你了，我怎么能不平静、不病愈、不高兴呢？三章里风雨越来越大（凄凄—潇潇—如晦），心情却越来越好（夷—瘳—喜），用阴暗的环境反衬重逢的喜悦，这种手法叫“以哀景写乐”。后来人们又从“风雨如晦，鸡鸣不已”里读出新的意思：在黑暗混乱的时代，依然坚守、不改变自己。这两句今天常被用来赞美在困境中坚持的人。

In English

A woman is waiting for the man she loves on a stormy morning. The wind and rain are cold, the sky is as dark as night, and the roosters crow again and again — a chilly, unsettled scene that matches her anxious heart. And then he arrives. 既见君子，云胡不夷 — “Now that I’ve seen you, how could I not be at peace?” Across the three verses the storm gets worse (chill, then lashing, then dark as night) while her feelings get better (calm, then cured of her heartache, then glad). Setting joy against a gloomy background makes the joy shine brighter; Chinese critics call this using a sad scene to write happiness. Later readers found a second meaning in 风雨如晦，鸡鸣不已, “wind and rain dark as night, yet the cock never stops crowing”: in dark, chaotic times, a good person keeps faith and doesn’t change. That is why the line is still quoted today in praise of people who hold firm through hard times.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from about the 11th to the 6th century BC. This is from the state of Zheng (郑, central Henan), known for its lively love songs. The author is unknown; the Mao commentary read it as longing for a gentleman in a time of disorder.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

喈喈 jiē jiē；潇潇 xiāo xiāo；胶胶 jiāo jiāo；瘳 chōu（病好了）；晦 huì；夷 yí。“瘳”机器朗读易错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

风雨凄凄，鸡鸣喈喈。

fēng yǔ qī qī, jī míng jiē jiē

Wind and rain, cold and bleak; the roosters call and call.

既见君子，云胡不夷？

jì jiàn jūn zǐ, yún hú bù yí

But now you’re here, my love — how could my heart not settle?

风雨潇潇，鸡鸣胶胶。

fēng yǔ xiāo xiāo, jī míng jiāo jiāo

Wind and rain, beating down; the roosters cry and cry.

既见君子，云胡不瘳？

jì jiàn jūn zǐ, yún hú bù chōu

But now you’re here, my love — how could my heartache not heal?

风雨如晦，鸡鸣不已。

fēng yǔ rú huì, jī míng bù yǐ

Wind and rain, dark as night; the roosters never stop.

既见君子，云胡不喜？

jì jiàn jūn zǐ, yún hú bù xǐ

But now you’re here, my love — how could I not be glad?
