谒金门（风乍起）

Yè Jīn Mén
Visiting the Golden Gate: A Sudden Breeze

导读About this poem

A sudden breeze ripples a spring pond — and a young woman’s heart. She idles by the water, twists apricot blossoms in her fingers and waits all day for someone who doesn’t come, until a magpie’s call gives her a little hope.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写一位女子在春天里等待心上人。开头“风乍起，吹皱一池春水”是千古名句：风忽然吹来，平静的池水起了一层层波纹。这既是眼前的景色，也暗暗写出女子的心被什么触动了，再也平静不下来。接着写她无聊的样子：在花香弥漫的小路上逗引池里的鸳鸯，手里揉搓着红杏的花蕊。鸳鸯总是成双成对，她却是一个人，所以越看越寂寞。下阕写她独自靠在看斗鸭的栏杆上，头上的碧玉簪子歪斜下来也顾不上整理，可见她一直心不在焉。“终日望君君不至”，等了一整天，人还是没来；正在失望时，忽然听见喜鹊叫——民间认为喜鹊叫是有喜事、有客人来的预兆，她心里又升起一点希望。全词不直说“想念”，只写动作和细节，情感却很细腻。相传南唐中主李璟曾跟作者开玩笑说：“吹皱一池春水，干卿何事？”（风吹皱池水，关你什么事？）

In English

A young woman waits for her lover on a spring day. The opening, 风乍起，吹皱一池春水 — “a breeze springs up and wrinkles a whole pond of spring water” — is one of the most famous lines in Chinese lyric poetry. It is the scene in front of her, but it also hints that something has stirred her heart and it can’t settle again. Next we see her killing time: on a fragrant garden path she coaxes the mandarin ducks on the pond and rubs red apricot blossoms between her fingers. Mandarin ducks always swim in pairs, and she is alone, so watching them only deepens her loneliness. In the second half she leans by herself on the railing where people watched duck fights; her green jade hairpin has slipped sideways and she doesn’t bother to fix it — her mind is elsewhere. She waits all day; he doesn’t come. Then, just as she is giving up, she hears a magpie. In Chinese folk belief a magpie’s call means good news or a visitor is on the way, and a little hope rises again. The poem never says “I miss him”; it lets small gestures do the talking. A well-known story says the Southern Tang ruler Li Jing teased the poet: “A breeze wrinkles a pond — what business is that of yours?” — proof the line was famous even then.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Feng Yansi (903–960) served as chief minister of the Southern Tang under Li Jing and was one of the leading lyric poets of the Five Dynasties period, admired for his delicate, melancholy style. Like many lyrics of the time, this one speaks in the voice of a waiting woman.

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本（《阳春集》）。重要异文：“斗鸭阑干独倚”一作“斗鸭阑干遍倚”；“碧玉搔头斜坠”各本相同。此词作者一说为成幼文，通行选本归冯延巳。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

风乍起，吹皱一池春水。

fēng zhà qǐ, chuī zhòu yī chí chūn shuǐ

A breeze springs up and ripples the whole spring pond.

闲引鸳鸯香径里，手挼红杏蕊。

xián yǐn yuān yāng xiāng jìng lǐ, shǒu ruó hóng xìng ruǐ

Idle on the scented path, she teases the paired ducks and twists red apricot blossoms in her fingers.

斗鸭阑干独倚，碧玉搔头斜坠。

dòu yā lán gān dú yǐ, bì yù sāo tóu xié zhuì

She leans alone on the railing by the duck pond, her jade hairpin slipping sideways.

终日望君君不至，举头闻鹊喜。

zhōng rì wàng jūn jūn bù zhì, jǔ tóu wén què xǐ

All day she waits and you don’t come — then she looks up: a magpie’s call, a sign of good news.
