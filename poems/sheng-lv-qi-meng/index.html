导读About this poem

Cloud pairs with rain, snow with wind — for three hundred years Chinese children learned to write poetry by chanting pairs like these. The lines grow from one character to eleven, and every pair rhymes and balances like a see-saw.

这首诗被谱成了歌Sung as a song

A Qing-dynasty primer that taught children to pair words — “cloud pairs with rain, snow with wind.” Singer-songwriter Zhao Zhao and young singer Ma Yirui sing the opening on Classics Sung Anew (经典咏流传).

01
云对雨，雪对风，晚照对晴空。

直译LiteralCloud pairs with rain, snow pairs with wind, evening glow pairs with clear sky.

意译MeaningCloud and rain, snow and wind; the glow of sunset and a cloudless sky.

02
来鸿对去燕，宿鸟对鸣虫。

直译LiteralArriving wild goose pairs with departing swallow; roosting bird pairs with chirping insect.

意译MeaningGeese flying in and swallows flying off; birds asleep in the trees and insects singing.

03
三尺剑，六钧弓，岭北对江东。

直译LiteralA three-foot sword, a six-jun bow; north of the ridges pairs with east of the river.

意译MeaningA three-foot sword, a bow only the strongest can draw; the lands beyond the ridges and the lands along the Yangtze.

04
人间清暑殿，天上广寒宫。

直译LiteralIn the human world, the Hall of Cool Summer; in the sky, the Palace of Vast Cold.

意译MeaningOn earth, the palace hall where emperors escaped the heat; in the sky, the cold palace of the moon.

05
两岸晓烟杨柳绿，一园春雨杏花红。

直译LiteralDawn mist on both banks, the willows green; spring rain in a whole garden, the apricot blossoms red.

意译MeaningMorning mist along both banks, the willows green; spring rain over the garden, the apricot blossoms red.

06
两鬓风霜途次早行之客，一蓑烟雨溪边晚钓之翁。

直译LiteralWind and frost on both temples — a traveller setting off early on the road; one straw cape in misty rain — an old man fishing late by the stream.

意译MeaningFrost in his hair, a traveller sets out at first light; in a straw cape, in the misty rain, an old man fishes late by the stream.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《声律启蒙》是清代教小孩子学写诗、学对对子的课本。古诗讲究“对仗”（上下两句字数相同、词性相同、意思相配）和“声律”（平仄、押韵），孩子们从小就通过背这样的句子打基础。这一段是全书的开头，属于“一东”韵：风、空、虫、弓、东、宫、红、翁，每一处停顿的最后一个字都押 ong 韵，读起来特别响亮顺口。它的结构很巧妙：先是一个字对一个字（云对雨、雪对风），然后是两个字对两个字（晚照对晴空、来鸿对去燕），再到三个字、五个字、七个字，最后是十一个字的长对子，越来越长，像台阶一样一级一级往上走。内容也很丰富：有天气（云雨风雪），有鸟虫（大雁、燕子），有兵器（剑、弓），有地理（岭北、江东），有人间的宫殿和月亮上的广寒宫，最后两句更像两幅画——江边的柳树和园中的杏花，赶早路的旅人和雨中钓鱼的老人。读这一段，既能学到很多词语，也能感受到汉语一对一对、整整齐齐的美。

In English

A Primer of Sound and Rhyme is a Qing-dynasty textbook that taught children how to write poetry. Classical Chinese poems depend on two skills. One is duìzhàng, “parallel pairs”: two lines with the same number of characters, matching word types and balanced meanings. The other is shēnglǜ, “sound rules”: the pattern of level and falling tones, and rhyme. Children built both skills by chanting books like this one until the patterns became second nature. This passage is the very beginning of the book, in the rhyme group called “One: Dong” (一东). Listen to the last character at each pause: 风, 空, 虫, 弓, 东, 宫, 红, 翁 — they all end in -ong, which gives the passage its ringing, bouncy sound. The structure is clever. It starts with one character against one (cloud / rain, snow / wind), then two against two (evening glow / clear sky), then three, five and seven, and finishes with a pair of eleven characters. The pairs grow longer step by step, like climbing a staircase. The content is a little world in itself: weather, birds and insects, a sword and a bow, places north and south, an emperor’s palace on earth and the Moon Palace in the sky. The last two pairs are small paintings — green willows and red apricot blossoms, an early traveller with frost in his hair and an old fisherman in a straw cape. Reading it, you learn a lot of words and feel the neat, two-by-two balance that runs all through Chinese.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Che Wanyu (1632–1705) came from Shaoyang in Hunan. He passed the highest imperial examination in 1664 and served as a censor at the Kangxi court, known for speaking his mind. He wrote this primer for children; it is arranged by the thirty rhyme groups used in classical poetry, with three passages for each.

云对雨，雪对风，晚照对晴空。

yún duì yǔ, xuě duì fēng, wǎn zhào duì qíng kōng

Cloud and rain, snow and wind; the glow of sunset and a cloudless sky.

来鸿对去燕，宿鸟对鸣虫。

lái hóng duì qù yàn, sù niǎo duì míng chóng

Geese flying in and swallows flying off; birds asleep in the trees and insects singing.

三尺剑，六钧弓，岭北对江东。

sān chǐ jiàn, liù jūn gōng, lǐng běi duì jiāng dōng

A three-foot sword, a bow only the strongest can draw; the lands beyond the ridges and the lands along the Yangtze.

人间清暑殿，天上广寒宫。

rén jiān qīng shǔ diàn, tiān shàng guǎng hán gōng

On earth, the palace hall where emperors escaped the heat; in the sky, the cold palace of the moon.

两岸晓烟杨柳绿，一园春雨杏花红。

liǎng àn xiǎo yān yáng liǔ lǜ, yī yuán chūn yǔ xìng huā hóng

Morning mist along both banks, the willows green; spring rain over the garden, the apricot blossoms red.

两鬓风霜途次早行之客，一蓑烟雨溪边晚钓之翁。

liǎng bìn fēng shuāng tú cì zǎo xíng zhī kè, yī suō yān yǔ xī biān wǎn diào zhī wēng

Frost in his hair, a traveller sets out at first light; in a straw cape, in the misty rain, an old man fishes late by the stream.
