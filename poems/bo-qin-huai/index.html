导读About this poem

A night mooring by the pleasure quarter of Nanjing: mist, moonlight, and a singing girl across the river performing a song once linked with a fallen dynasty. Du Mu’s quiet alarm about his own declining Tang.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

秦淮河流经南京，两岸自古是酒楼、歌楼集中的地方。诗人夜里把船停在这里。第一句写景：烟雾笼着寒冷的河水，月光笼着岸边的沙，两个“笼”字让画面朦胧又冷清。第二句交代地点：船停在酒家附近。后两句是全诗的重点：对岸的歌女在唱《后庭花》。这首曲子是南朝陈后主写的，他只顾享乐，最后亡了国，所以后人把它叫作“亡国之音”。诗人说歌女“不知亡国恨”，其实真正“不知”的是那些点歌、听歌的达官贵人。这种写法叫“借古讽今”：借历史的事批评当下。晚唐国力衰退，诗人借这个夜晚表达了深深的忧虑。

In English

The Qinhuai River runs through Nanjing, and its banks were famous for wine houses and singing girls. Du Mu moors his boat there one night. The first line is pure atmosphere: mist veils the cold water, moonlight veils the sand. The same verb 笼 (“to cover like a cage or a basket”) appears twice, making the scene hazy and chilly. The second line just tells us where he is — near the wine houses. The last two lines carry the weight of the poem. Across the river, a girl is singing “Flowers in the Back Court”, a song written by the last emperor of the Chen dynasty, who partied while his kingdom fell in 589. Ever since, the tune has been called “the music of a lost country”. Du Mu says the singer “doesn’t know the grief of a fallen nation” — but the real target is the officials and rich men who order the song and enjoy it. Using history to criticise the present like this is called jiè gǔ fěng jīn (“borrowing the past to satirise today”). The late Tang was in decline, and this calm, beautiful night hides a sharp warning.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–c. 852) served in several southern posts during the late Tang, when the court was weakened by eunuchs, factions and rebellious generals. The poem is traditionally dated to a visit to Jinling (Nanjing), once the capital of the Southern Dynasties.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

笼：这里是“笼罩”义，现代词典读 lǒng；按诗律此处应为平声，许多诵读本读 lóng，网站暂采用 lóng。泊 bó（不读 pō）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

烟笼寒水月笼沙，

yān lóng hán shuǐ yuè lóng shā

Mist lies over the cold river, moonlight over the sand;

夜泊秦淮近酒家。

yè bó qín huái jìn jiǔ jiā

I tie up my boat on the Qinhuai for the night, close to the wine houses.

商女不知亡国恨，

shāng nǚ bù zhī wáng guó hèn

The singing girl knows nothing of a kingdom’s fall —

隔江犹唱后庭花。

gé jiāng yóu chàng hòu tíng huā

across the water she is still singing “Flowers in the Back Court”.
