导读About this poem

Alone on a boat at night, the ageing poet sees stars hanging over a vast plain and the moon surging in the river — and compares himself to a single gull drifting between heaven and earth.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

杜甫晚年离开成都，坐船沿长江东下，这首诗写他在船上过夜时的心情。第一、二句写近景：岸边的细草在微风中摇动，高高的桅杆下，只有他一个人的小船停在夜里。第三、四句写远景，气势很大：星星低低地垂在天边，显得原野特别开阔；月光随着江水翻涌，大江滚滚向前流。景越大，人越显得渺小孤单。第五、六句是反话：我的名声难道是靠文章得来的吗？做官嘛，因为又老又病，也只好不做了。其实他真正想要的是做官为国家出力，却没有机会，只留下诗名，话里有很深的无奈。最后两句自问自答：我这样漂泊不定像什么呢？就像天地之间的一只沙鸥。用一只小鸟和整个天地对比，把孤独写到了极点。

In English

Late in life Du Fu left Chengdu and travelled down the Yangtze by boat. This poem records one night on that journey. The first couplet is close-up: fine grass on the bank stirring in a light breeze, a tall mast, and his boat alone in the dark. The second couplet suddenly opens out into one of the grandest views in Tang poetry: stars hang low over a plain that seems endless, and moonlight heaves on the current of the great river. The bigger the world, the smaller and lonelier the man in it. The third couplet sounds like bitter irony. “Is my name to be made by writing? As for office — old and sick, I should give it up.” What Du Fu really wanted was to serve the state, not to be famous for poems, and he never got the chance. The last couplet asks and answers its own question: drifting like this, what am I like? A single gull between heaven and earth. Setting one small bird against the whole universe, he makes his loneliness almost unbearably clear.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) is generally thought to have written this in 765, after his patron Yan Wu died and he left Chengdu, sailing east down the Yangtze with his family. He was in his fifties, in poor health, and had only a few years left to live.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

细草微风岸，

xì cǎo wēi fēng àn

Thin grass on the bank in a faint breeze,

危樯独夜舟。

wēi qiáng dú yè zhōu

a tall mast, one boat alone in the night.

星垂平野阔，

xīng chuí píng yě kuò

Stars hang low over the endless plain;

月涌大江流。

yuè yǒng dà jiāng liú

the moon heaves on the flowing river.

名岂文章著，

míng qǐ wén zhāng zhù

Should a man be known for his writing?

官应老病休。

guān yīng lǎo bìng xiū

And office — old and sick, I am done with it.

飘飘何所似，

piāo piāo hé suǒ sì

Drifting on and on, what am I?

天地一沙鸥。

tiān dì yī shā ōu

One gull between heaven and earth.
