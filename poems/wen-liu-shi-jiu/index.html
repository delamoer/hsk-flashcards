问刘十九

Wèn Liú Shíjiǔ
A Question for Liu the Nineteenth

导读About this poem

The shortest, warmest invitation in Chinese poetry: new wine is ready, the little stove is lit, snow is on its way — so, will you come over for a drink?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首写给朋友的小诗，其实就是一张请帖。第一句写酒：新酿的米酒还没有过滤，酒面上浮着一层细细的绿色泡沫，像小蚂蚁一样，所以叫“绿蚁”。第二句写炉子：红泥做的小火炉，火正旺。第三句写天气：天黑下来，看样子要下雪了。最后一句才问：“能饮一杯无？”——能来喝一杯吗？前三句准备好了一切：酒、火、雪夜，最后用一句很口语的问话收尾，好像朋友就站在面前。绿和红颜色温暖，冷冷的雪夜又和屋里的炉火形成对比，让这份邀请显得更暖。全诗二十个字，没有一个难字，却把朋友之间简单真诚的感情写得很亲切。

In English

This little poem is really an invitation card for a friend. Line one is the wine: newly brewed rice wine, not yet strained, with a film of tiny green bubbles floating on top — they look like little ants, so the Chinese call them “green ants” (绿蚁). Line two is the stove: a small one of red clay, the fire going well. Line three is the weather: evening is coming on, and it looks like snow. Only in the last line does he ask: “Could you manage a cup?” The first three lines set out everything — wine, fire, a snowy night — and then a casual, spoken question finishes it, as if the friend were right there in the doorway. The warm green and red, set against the cold snowy night outside, make the invitation feel even cosier. Twenty characters, not one difficult word, and the simple, honest warmth between two friends comes through completely.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) is said to have written this around 817, during his years in Jiangzhou (today’s Jiujiang). Liu the Nineteenth was a friend there — “nineteenth” is his rank among cousins of the same generation; his exact identity is uncertain.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

醅 pēi（未过滤的酒），生僻字；雪 xuě 这里作动词“下雪”，读音不变；无 wú（句末表疑问，相当于“吗”）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

绿蚁新醅酒，

lǜ yǐ xīn pēi jiǔ

New wine, still frothing green,

红泥小火炉。

hóng ní xiǎo huǒ lú

a little red-clay stove aglow.

晚来天欲雪，

wǎn lái tiān yù xuě

Dusk is falling, snow is on the way —

能饮一杯无？

néng yǐn yī bēi wú

won’t you come and share a cup?
