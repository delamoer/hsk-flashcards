导读About this poem

Sand white as snow, moonlight like frost, and somewhere in the dark a reed pipe — and every soldier on the frontier spends the night looking toward home.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人夜里登上受降城，听到笛声后的感受。受降城在今天的内蒙古和宁夏一带，是唐代边防重地。前两句写景：回乐烽前的沙地在月光下白得像雪，受降城外的月光冷得像霜。“沙似雪”“月如霜”，一片银白，冷清而荒凉，也让人想起李白“疑是地上霜”的思乡之情。第三句写声音：不知道从哪里传来了芦管的吹奏声。第四句写结果：这一夜，所有守边的战士都在遥望家乡。诗人没有写笛声多么哀怨，也没有写士兵怎样流泪，只用一个“尽望乡”，就把所有人的乡愁写了出来——不是一个人想家，而是“征人尽”想家。景、声、情一层层推进，含蓄而动人。这首诗在唐代就被谱成歌曲传唱。

In English

Li Yi climbed the walls of Shouxiang (“Accepting Surrender”) City one night and heard a reed pipe playing. The city lay in what is now Inner Mongolia and Ningxia, a key Tang frontier stronghold. The first two lines paint the scene: in front of the Huile beacon tower the sand is as white as snow under the moon, and outside the city the moonlight lies like frost. Snow, frost, silver light everywhere — cold and desolate, and for Chinese readers frost-like moonlight instantly recalls Li Bai’s homesick “Thoughts on a Quiet Night”. Line three adds sound: from somewhere unknown comes the music of a reed pipe. Line four gives the result: all night long, every soldier on the frontier looks toward home. The poet doesn’t say how sad the music is or describe anyone crying. One phrase, “all look toward home”, carries everyone’s homesickness — not one man missing his family, but the whole garrison. Scene, then sound, then feeling: the poem builds step by step, quietly and movingly. It was set to music and sung even in the Tang dynasty.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Yi (746–829) was a mid-Tang poet who spent many years on the staff of frontier commanders and was best known for his frontier quatrains. Musicians of his day are said to have competed to set his poems to music.

回乐烽前沙似雪，

huí lè fēng qián shā sì xuě
