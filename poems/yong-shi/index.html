导读About this poem

A tall pine stuck at the bottom of a gully is shaded by a tiny sapling on the hilltop — not because the sapling is better, but because of where it grows. A sharp, still-relevant poem about privilege and wasted talent.

第一段Part 1
01
郁郁涧底松，离离山上苗。

直译LiteralLush and thick, the pine at the gully’s bottom; drooping and thin, the sapling on the hill.

意译MeaningA thick green pine grows deep in the gully; a spindly sapling sways on the hilltop.

02
以彼径寸茎，荫此百尺条。

直译LiteralWith that stem an inch across, it shades these hundred-foot branches.

意译MeaningWith a stem no thicker than a thumb, it casts its shade on a hundred-foot tree.

第二段Part 2
03
世胄蹑高位，英俊沉下僚。

直译LiteralSons of noble houses step into high positions; men of talent sink in lowly posts.

意译MeaningSons of great families stroll into high office; men of real talent sink in minor posts.

04
地势使之然，由来非一朝。

直译LiteralPosition of ground makes it so; it did not begin in one morning.

意译MeaningIt is where they stand that makes it so — and it has been this way for ages.

第三段Part 3
05
金张藉旧业，七叶珥汉貂。

直译LiteralThe Jins and Zhangs relied on their ancestors’ achievements; for seven generations they wore Han sable tails on their caps.

意译MeaningThe Jin and Zhang clans lived off their forebears: seven generations wore the sable plumes of high office.

06
冯公岂不伟，白首不见招。

直译LiteralWas Lord Feng not great? Yet with white hair he was still not summoned.

意译MeaningWas old Feng Tang not a great man? His hair was white, and still no one sent for him.

中文解析

这首诗批评的是一个不公平的社会：出身决定一切。开头用了一个比喻：山涧底下长着茂盛的松树，山顶上长着细弱的小树苗。小树苗的茎只有一寸粗，却能遮住百尺高的松树——不是因为它更强，而是因为它长的地方高。接着点明比喻的意思：贵族子弟靠家世坐上高位，真正有才能的人却埋没在低级职位上。这是“地势”造成的，而且由来已久，不是一天两天了。最后举了两个汉代的例子：金家、张家靠祖上的基业，七代人都做高官；而冯唐那么有才干，到头发白了也没被重用。这种先打比方、再讲道理、最后用历史事例证明的写法，层次非常清楚。题目叫“咏史”，其实是借古讽今——借讲历史，批评诗人自己所处的西晋门阀社会。“门阀”就是几个大家族世代垄断官位。

In English

This poem attacks an unfair society in which birth decides everything. It opens with an image: a thick, tall pine grows at the bottom of a mountain gully, while a thin little sapling grows on the hilltop. The sapling’s stem is barely an inch across, yet it casts its shade over the hundred-foot pine — not because it is stronger, but because it stands higher. Then the poet spells out what he means: the sons of great families walk into high office, while truly gifted men are buried in junior posts. It is all a matter of position, and it has been this way for a very long time. Finally, two examples from the Han dynasty: the Jin and Zhang families held top posts for seven generations on the strength of their ancestors, while the able Feng Tang was still waiting for a proper job when his hair turned white. The argument moves neatly from image, to principle, to historical proof. The title says “On History”, but the real target is the poet’s own time: in the Western Jin, a handful of aristocratic clans controlled nearly every important office, and men from ordinary families like Zuo Si had little chance.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zuo Si (c. 250–c. 305) came from a humble family in the Western Jin dynasty. He is famous for his “Rhapsody on the Three Capitals”, which was so popular that copying it is said to have made paper expensive in Luoyang. His eight “On History” poems use figures from the past to protest against a system that shut out talented men of low birth.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

郁郁涧底松，离离山上苗。

yù yù jiàn dǐ sōng, lí lí shān shàng miáo

A thick green pine grows deep in the gully; a spindly sapling sways on the hilltop.

以彼径寸茎，荫此百尺条。

yǐ bǐ jìng cùn jīng, yìn cǐ bǎi chǐ tiáo

With a stem no thicker than a thumb, it casts its shade on a hundred-foot tree.

世胄蹑高位，英俊沉下僚。

shì zhòu niè gāo wèi, yīng jùn chén xià liáo

Sons of great families stroll into high office; men of real talent sink in minor posts.

地势使之然，由来非一朝。

dì shì shǐ zhī rán, yóu lái fēi yī zhāo

It is where they stand that makes it so — and it has been this way for ages.

金张藉旧业，七叶珥汉貂。

jīn zhāng jiè jiù yè, qī yè ěr hàn diāo

The Jin and Zhang clans lived off their forebears: seven generations wore the sable plumes of high office.

冯公岂不伟，白首不见招。

féng gōng qǐ bù wěi, bái shǒu bù jiàn zhāo

Was old Feng Tang not a great man? His hair was white, and still no one sent for him.
