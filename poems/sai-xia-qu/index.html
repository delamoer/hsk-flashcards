导读About this poem

A moonless night, geese startled into the sky, the enemy chief slipping away — and Tang cavalry about to give chase, their bows and swords heavy with snow.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写唐军雪夜追击敌人的场面。第一句写环境：没有月亮的黑夜，大雁被惊动，高高飞起——为什么大雁会惊飞？因为有人在黑暗中偷偷行动。第二句点明：原来是敌军首领单于趁着夜色逃跑了。后两句写唐军：将领正要带领轻骑兵去追，一转眼，大雪已经落满了弓和刀。诗在这里戛然而止，没有写追击的经过和结果，只停在出发前的一瞬间，却让人感受到将士们不怕严寒、勇往直前的气势。“大雪满弓刀”五个字，既写出了天气的严寒，又写出了战士整装待发的英武形象，画面感极强。短短二十个字，写出一场紧张的边塞夜战。

In English

This poem shows Tang troops about to chase the enemy on a snowy night. Line one sets the scene: a moonless night, and wild geese flying up high — why would geese take off in the dark? Because something is moving secretly below. Line two explains: the chanyu, the enemy chief, is fleeing under cover of night. The second half turns to the Tang side: the commander is about to lead light cavalry after him, and in that moment heavy snow has already covered their bows and swords. The poem stops right there. It doesn’t tell us about the chase or how it ended — it freezes the instant before they ride out — yet we feel the soldiers’ courage and their disregard for the cold. The last line, 大雪满弓刀, does two jobs at once: it shows how bitter the weather is, and it gives us a striking picture of warriors ready to charge. Twenty characters, and a whole tense night battle on the frontier.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu Lun (c. 739–c. 799) was one of the “Ten Talents of the Dali Era” of the mid-Tang and spent time on the staff of frontier commanders. “Frontier Song” is a set of six poems written in reply to a general; this is the third.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

单于 chán yú（古代匈奴首领的称号，不读 dān；机器朗读可能读错，已在“朗读用字”换成同音字“蝉”）；遁逃 dùn；将 jiāng（带领）。轻骑：统编教材注 qí（2016 年审音后“骑”统读 qí），旧读 jì。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

月黑雁飞高，

yuè hēi yàn fēi gāo

No moon tonight — the geese fly high;

单于夜遁逃。

chán yú yè dùn táo

the enemy chief is slipping away in the dark.

欲将轻骑逐，

yù jiāng qīng qí zhú

Just as we mount to ride him down,

大雪满弓刀。

dà xuě mǎn gōng dāo

deep snow lies heavy on our bows and blades.
