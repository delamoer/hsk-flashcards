导读About this poem

A poem written on a neighbour’s wall, praising his tidy cottage and garden — and the river and hills around it, which seem to protect the fields and push open the door to bring in the green.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

湖阴先生是王安石晚年在南京钟山的邻居，名叫杨德逢。这首诗题写在他家的墙上。前两句写院子：茅草屋檐下经常打扫，干净得连青苔都没有；花木一畦一畦，整整齐齐，都是主人亲手种的。从干净整齐里，可以看出主人勤劳、爱生活。后两句写院外的山水，是全诗最有名的一联：一条小河绕着田地流过，像在保护绿油油的庄稼；两座山推开门，把满眼的青翠送进来。“护”“绕”“排闼”“送”都是人的动作，这叫“拟人”——把山水写得有感情，好像也很喜欢这位主人。这两句对仗工整（“一水”对“两山”，“护田”对“排闼”），读起来又自然。

In English

Master Huyin was Yang Defeng, Wang Anshi’s neighbour when Wang lived in retirement below Zhong Mountain near Nanjing, and this poem was written on the wall of Yang’s house. The first two lines describe the courtyard: under the thatched eaves it is swept so often that not even moss can grow, and the flowers and trees stand in neat beds that the owner planted himself. The tidiness tells you about the man — hard-working and fond of a simple life. The last two lines, looking beyond the gate, are the famous ones. A stream curves round the fields as if guarding the green crops, and two hills push open the door to deliver their green to the house. “Guard”, “wrap round”, “push open the door” and “send” are all things people do. This is personification, and it makes the landscape seem to share the poet’s affection for his neighbour. The couplet is also a perfect parallel pair — “one stream” against “two hills”, “guarding the fields” against “opening the door” — yet it reads completely naturally.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Anshi (1021–1086) was a chancellor of the Northern Song famous for his sweeping political reforms. After leaving office in 1076 he spent his last decade living quietly near Nanjing, where he wrote many short landscape poems like this one.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“长扫”的“长”读 cháng（常常）。畦 qí。“排闼”的“闼”读 tà。“将绿绕”的“将”读 jiāng（把）。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材文本（统编小学语文六年级上册）。“茅檐长扫净无苔”有的本子作“茅檐常扫净无苔”，意思相同。原诗共二首，这是第一首。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

茅檐长扫净无苔，

máo yán cháng sǎo jìng wú tái

Under the thatched eaves, swept so often not a speck of moss is left,

花木成畦手自栽。

huā mù chéng qí shǒu zì zāi

flowers and trees grow in tidy beds, all planted by his own hand.

一水护田将绿绕，

yī shuǐ hù tián jiāng lǜ rào

A stream curls round the fields, guarding all their green;

两山排闼送青来。

liǎng shān pái tà sòng qīng lái

two hills push the door wide open and hand their green inside.
