导读About this poem

A fisherman comes home too sleepy to tie up his boat — and doesn’t care. Four lines about the freedom of a life with nothing to lose.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“即事”就是“眼前的事”。诗写一个渔人：钓完鱼回来，连船都懒得拴，月亮已经落下，正好倒头就睡。第三、四句是他心里的想法：就算夜里起风把船吹走了，又能漂到哪儿去呢？最多停在长着芦花的浅水边罢了。一般人怕船被吹走，他却一点不担心，这份满不在乎写出了江村生活的安宁，也写出了一种不计较得失、随遇而安的心态。诗里用了“纵然……只在……”的转折，先假设最坏的情况，再轻轻一笑说“没关系”，读起来很轻松。

In English

即事 means “what is in front of me right now”. The poem follows a fisherman home after a day on the river. He is so ready for bed that he doesn’t even tie up his boat; the moon has already set, and it’s the perfect time to sleep. The last two lines show what goes through his mind: even if the wind blows all night and carries the boat off, where could it go? At most it will drift into the shallows by the reed flowers. Most people would worry about losing their boat; he simply shrugs. That carelessness tells us how peaceful the village is — and also shows an attitude Chinese poets admired: not clinging to gain and loss, being content wherever you end up. Notice the structure 纵然……只在…… (“even if… still only…”): the poet imagines the worst that could happen, then smiles and says it hardly matters. That light touch is what makes this little poem so easy to love.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Sikong Shu (c. 720–c. 790) was one of the “Ten Talents of the Dali Era”, a group of mid-Tang poets. He held minor posts and lived through hard times after the An Lushan Rebellion; his poems are known for plain, quiet scenes.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

钓罢归来不系船，

diào bà guī lái bù xì chuán

Home from fishing, I don’t bother tying up the boat —

江村月落正堪眠。

jiāng cūn yuè luò zhèng kān mián

the moon is down, the village asleep, and so am I.

纵然一夜风吹去，

zòng rán yī yè fēng chuī qù

And if the wind should carry it off in the night,

只在芦花浅水边。

zhǐ zài lú huā qiǎn shuǐ biān

it will only drift to the reeds in the shallows.
