导读About this poem

Bees fly everywhere, from plains to mountaintops, gathering nectar from a hundred flowers. But once the honey is made — who did all that work for, and who gets the sweetness?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首“咏物诗”，就是借写一种东西来表达想法。前两句写蜜蜂到处飞：不管是平地还是山顶，凡是有花、风景好的地方，都被蜜蜂占满了，好像它们很自由、很风光。后两句突然一转：它们采了成百上千朵花，终于酿成了蜜，可是这么辛苦，到底是为谁？甜蜜又是给谁的？蜜蜂自己吃不到多少，蜜都被人拿走了。这个问题没有回答，答案却很清楚。诗人借蜜蜂，表达了对辛苦劳动却享受不到成果的人的同情，也讽刺了不劳动却坐享其成的人。今天人们常用“为谁辛苦为谁甜”来赞美默默奉献的人，意思比原诗更温和。语言简单，结尾一问让人回味。

In English

This is a poem “about a thing” (yǒngwù shī): the poet describes one object in order to say something bigger. The first two lines show bees everywhere — on flat land and on mountain peaks, every beautiful spot full of flowers is theirs. They seem free and glorious. Then the poem turns. The bees gather nectar from a hundred flowers and finally make honey — and the poet asks: who was all that hard work for? Who gets the sweetness? Of course, the bees don’t; people take the honey. The poem never answers its own question, but the answer is obvious. Through the bees, Luo Yin sympathises with ordinary working people who labour all their lives while others enjoy the results, and quietly mocks those who take without working. Today Chinese people often quote the last line in a gentler way, to praise anyone who works hard for others without asking for thanks. The vocabulary is easy, and the closing question stays with you.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Luo Yin (833–909) took the civil service exam about ten times and failed every time, partly, it is said, because his writing was too sharp about those in power. He later served a regional ruler in the Wuyue area. Many of his short poems use everyday things to mock injustice.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

不论平地与山尖，

bù lùn píng dì yǔ shān jiān

On the plains, on the mountaintops,

无限风光尽被占。

wú xiàn fēng guāng jìn bèi zhàn

every lovely place belongs to the bees.

采得百花成蜜后，

cǎi dé bǎi huā chéng mì hòu

But when a hundred flowers have become honey —

为谁辛苦为谁甜？

wèi shuí xīn kǔ wèi shuí tián

whose was the labour, and whose is the sweetness?
