观沧海

Guān Cāng Hǎi
Gazing at the Sea
曹操Cáo Cāo · Cao Cao魏晋南北朝 Wei, Jin & Southern and Northern Dynasties

导读About this poem

A general climbs a mountain by the sea after a victory and looks out at the waves — the sea seems big enough to hold the sun, the moon and all the stars. One of the earliest Chinese poems about a landscape, and a portrait of a man with huge ambitions.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

公元207年，曹操北征乌桓（Wūhuán）胜利回师，登上海边的碣石山，写下这首诗。前半首写眼前看到的景物：海水起伏荡漾，山岛高高耸立，树木茂密，秋风吹过，大浪涌起。秋风本来容易让人伤感，可在这里却显得雄壮有力。后面四句是想象：太阳和月亮的运行，好像是从大海里出来的；灿烂的银河，也好像出自大海之中。用大海“装下”日月星辰，写的是海，也是诗人自己的胸怀和统一天下的雄心——这叫“借景抒情”，借写景来表达感情。最后两句“幸甚至哉，歌以咏志”是乐府诗配乐时加的固定结尾，意思是“真幸运啊，用诗歌来表达志向”。这是中国文学史上第一首完整的山水诗。

In English

In 207 AD, Cao Cao was marching home after defeating the Wuhuan tribes in the north. He climbed Jieshi Mountain on the coast and wrote this poem. The first half is what he actually sees: the water rolling and heaving, rocky islands standing tall, thick trees and grass, then an autumn wind and huge waves rising. In Chinese poetry autumn wind usually brings sadness, but here it feels powerful, almost exciting. The next four lines leap into imagination: the sun and moon seem to rise out of the sea, and so does the shining Milky Way. A sea big enough to hold the whole sky is really a picture of the poet’s own mind — a man who wanted to unite all of China. Showing feelings through scenery like this is called jiè jǐng shū qíng (“using the scene to express feeling”). The last two lines, roughly “How lucky I am! Let me sing out my ambition”, are a set ending added when the poem was performed to music. It is often called the first complete landscape poem in Chinese literature.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Cao Cao (155–220) was a warlord, statesman and poet at the end of the Han dynasty; his son founded the state of Wei. He wrote this in 207 on the way back from a northern campaign, as the first part of a longer song suite, “Walking out of Xiamen Gate” (步出夏门行).

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

生僻字：碣石 jié shí、澹澹 dàn dàn（不读 tán）、竦峙 sǒng zhì。

版本说明 About the text

采用统编语文教材七年级上册文本。原是乐府诗《步出夏门行》的第一章（“观沧海”是后人加的小标题），全诗十四句，此处完整收录，按两个四字句一行排版。“树木丛生”一作“树木聚生”。

东临碣石，以观沧海。

dōng lín jié shí, yǐ guān cāng hǎi

Eastward I climb Jieshi Mountain and look out over the sea.

水何澹澹，山岛竦峙。

shuǐ hé dàn dàn, shān dǎo sǒng zhì

How the water heaves and sways, and the rocky islands rise up high!

树木丛生，百草丰茂。

shù mù cóng shēng, bǎi cǎo fēng mào

Trees crowd together, and every kind of grass grows thick.

秋风萧瑟，洪波涌起。

qiū fēng xiāo sè, hóng bō yǒng qǐ

The autumn wind whistles past, and great waves surge.

日月之行，若出其中；

rì yuè zhī xíng, ruò chū qí zhōng

The sun and the moon on their journey seem to rise from inside it;

星汉灿烂，若出其里。

xīng hàn càn làn, ruò chū qí lǐ

the bright river of stars seems to pour out of its depths.

幸甚至哉，歌以咏志。

xìng shèn zhì zāi, gē yǐ yǒng zhì

What luck is mine! Let this song tell what my heart is set on.
