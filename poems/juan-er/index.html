卷耳

Picking Mouse-ear

导读About this poem

A woman picking greens can’t fill her basket because she keeps thinking of her husband far away — then the poem jumps to him, climbing mountains on a worn-out horse, drinking to forget. One of the first poems to show two people missing each other at once.

第一章Stanza 1
01
采采卷耳，不盈顷筐。

直译LiteralI pick and pick the mouse-ear, yet do not fill my shallow basket.

意译MeaningI pick and pick the mouse-ear greens, but my little basket won’t fill.

02
嗟我怀人，寘彼周行。

直译LiteralAh, I miss my man — I set it down by the great road.

意译MeaningOh, I’m thinking of him — I set the basket down beside the highway.

第二章Stanza 2
03
陟彼崔嵬，我马虺隤。

直译LiteralI climb that rocky height; my horse is worn out.

意译MeaningI climb the rocky heights; my horse is worn and weak.

04
我姑酌彼金罍，维以不永怀。

直译LiteralFor now I pour from that bronze jar, so as not to long forever.

意译MeaningFor now I pour wine from the bronze jar, so the longing won’t last all night.

第三章Stanza 3
05
陟彼高冈，我马玄黄。

直译LiteralI climb that high ridge; my horse is sick and faded.

意译MeaningI climb the high ridge; my horse is sick, its coat gone dull.

06
我姑酌彼兕觥，维以不永伤。

直译LiteralFor now I pour from that horn cup, so as not to grieve forever.

意译MeaningFor now I pour wine from the horn cup, so the sorrow won’t go on and on.

第四章Stanza 4
07
陟彼砠矣，我马瘏矣，

直译LiteralI climb that stony hill; my horse is exhausted;

意译MeaningI climb the stony hill — my horse is spent,

直译Literalmy servant is sick — how sad I am!

意译Meaningmy servant is sick — how heavy my heart!

中文解析

第一章写一个女子在路边采卷耳（一种野菜），采了又采，连一个浅浅的斜口筐都装不满，因为她心不在焉：她在想念远方的丈夫，干脆把筐放在大路边，望着远方发呆。后面三章画面一下子转到远方：一个男子正在翻山越岭，山越来越高，马越来越累，生了病，仆人也累倒了；他只好倒一杯酒，希望能暂时不那么想家。一个家里，一个路上，两个人同时在思念对方。这种“从对面写起”的写法——不写自己多想他，而写想象中他多想我——后来被很多诗人学习，比如杜甫的《月夜》。关于后三章是谁在说话，一说是女子想象丈夫的处境，一说是男子自己的口吻，两种读法都很动人。

In English

In verse 1 a woman is picking juǎn’ěr, a wild green, by the roadside. She picks and picks but can’t even fill a shallow basket, because her mind is elsewhere — on her husband far away. In the end she just sets the basket down by the big road and stares into the distance. Then, in verses 2 to 4, the scene jumps to a man on a journey: the hills get steeper, his horse gets weaker and falls sick, his servant collapses, and he pours himself wine so he won’t ache with longing quite so much. One person at home, one on the road, each missing the other at the same moment. Writing about your longing by imagining the other person longing for you became a favourite technique of later poets — Du Fu’s “Moonlit Night” is a famous example. Who speaks the last three verses is debated: some say it is the wife picturing her husband’s hardships, others that the husband speaks for himself. Either way, the switch between the two scenes is what makes this old song so moving.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from about the 11th to the 6th century BC. This is from the Airs of Zhounan (周南), songs from the lands south of the Zhou capital — the same section that opens with “The Ospreys Call”. The author is unknown.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

采采卷耳，不盈顷筐。

cǎi cǎi juǎn ěr, bù yíng qīng kuāng

I pick and pick the mouse-ear greens, but my little basket won’t fill.

嗟我怀人，寘彼周行。

jiē wǒ huái rén, zhì bǐ zhōu háng

Oh, I’m thinking of him — I set the basket down beside the highway.

陟彼崔嵬，我马虺隤。

zhì bǐ cuī wéi, wǒ mǎ huī tuí

I climb the rocky heights; my horse is worn and weak.

我姑酌彼金罍，维以不永怀。

wǒ gū zhuó bǐ jīn léi, wéi yǐ bù yǒng huái

For now I pour wine from the bronze jar, so the longing won’t last all night.

陟彼高冈，我马玄黄。

zhì bǐ gāo gāng, wǒ mǎ xuán huáng

I climb the high ridge; my horse is sick, its coat gone dull.

我姑酌彼兕觥，维以不永伤。

wǒ gū zhuó bǐ sì gōng, wéi yǐ bù yǒng shāng

For now I pour wine from the horn cup, so the sorrow won’t go on and on.

陟彼砠矣，我马瘏矣，

zhì bǐ jū yǐ, wǒ mǎ tú yǐ

I climb the stony hill — my horse is spent,

我仆痡矣，云何吁矣！

wǒ pú pū yǐ, yún hé xū yǐ

my servant is sick — how heavy my heart!
