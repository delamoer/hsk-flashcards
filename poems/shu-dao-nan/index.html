蜀道难

Shǔ Dào Nán
The Road to Shu Is Hard
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang

导读About this poem

Li Bai describes the mountain road into Sichuan (ancient Shu) as harder to climb than the sky itself — cliffs, gorges, roaring torrents and a single pass one man could hold — and three times cries out the same refrain.

第一段Part 1
01
噫吁嚱，危乎高哉！

直译LiteralOh! Ah! How perilous, how high!

意译MeaningOh — how steep, how high!

02
蜀道之难，难于上青天！

直译LiteralThe difficulty of the Shu road is harder than climbing the blue sky!

意译MeaningThe road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven!

03
蚕丛及鱼凫，开国何茫然！

直译LiteralCancong and Yufu — how hazy is the founding of their kingdom!

意译MeaningCancong and Yufu, its first kings — how dim and far that founding is!

04
尔来四万八千岁，不与秦塞通人烟。

直译LiteralSince then forty-eight thousand years, it did not connect human smoke with the Qin frontier.

意译MeaningFor forty-eight thousand years since, no chimney smoke crossed over to the land of Qin.

05
西当太白有鸟道，可以横绝峨眉巅。

直译LiteralTo the west, facing Mount Taibai, there is a bird path that can cut across to the peak of Emei.

意译MeaningWest at Mount Taibai runs a path only birds can take, cutting straight across to the summit of Emei.

06
地崩山摧壮士死，然后天梯石栈相钩连。

直译LiteralThe earth collapsed, the mountain broke, the strong men died; only then were sky-ladders and stone walkways hooked together.

意译MeaningThe earth split, the mountain fell, the strong men died — and only then were sky-ladders and plank roads on stone linked up.

第二段Part 2
07
上有六龙回日之高标，下有冲波逆折之回川。

直译LiteralAbove is the high peak where the six dragons turn the sun back; below is the winding river whose surging waves break backward.

意译MeaningAbove, peaks so tall the sun’s six-dragon chariot must turn back; below, a river that crashes and twists back on itself.

08
黄鹤之飞尚不得过，猿猱欲度愁攀援。

直译LiteralEven the flight of the yellow crane cannot pass; the apes and monkeys want to cross but worry about climbing.

意译MeaningEven the yellow crane cannot fly over; even the apes, wanting to cross, despair of the climb.

09
青泥何盘盘，百步九折萦岩峦。

直译LiteralHow Qingni Ridge winds and winds — nine turns in a hundred steps, coiling around the crags.

意译MeaningQingni Ridge winds round and round — nine bends in every hundred steps, coiling about the rocks.

10
扪参历井仰胁息，以手抚膺坐长叹。

直译LiteralTouching the star Shen, passing the star Jing, looking up and holding one’s breath; with a hand pressing the chest, sitting down with a long sigh.

意译MeaningYou could touch the stars as you pass, looking up, not daring to breathe; you press your chest, sit down and sigh.

11
问君西游何时还？畏途巉岩不可攀。

直译LiteralI ask you, travelling west, when will you return? The fearful road and steep crags cannot be climbed.

意译MeaningWhen will you come back from your journey west? That dreadful road, those jagged cliffs, no one can climb.

12
但见悲鸟号古木，雄飞雌从绕林间。

直译LiteralOne only sees sad birds crying in ancient trees, the male flying and the female following, circling among the woods.

意译MeaningAll you see are mournful birds crying in old trees, the male in flight, the female behind, circling the woods.

13
又闻子规啼夜月，愁空山。

直译LiteralAnd one hears the cuckoo crying to the night moon, grieving in the empty mountains.

意译MeaningAnd you hear the cuckoo calling to the moon at night, filling the empty hills with sorrow.

第三段Part 3
14
蜀道之难，难于上青天，使人听此凋朱颜！

直译LiteralThe difficulty of the Shu road is harder than climbing the blue sky; hearing this makes people’s red cheeks wither!

意译MeaningThe road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven — just hearing of it turns young faces pale!

15
连峰去天不盈尺，枯松倒挂倚绝壁。

直译LiteralThe linked peaks are less than a foot from the sky; withered pines hang upside down, leaning on sheer cliffs.

意译MeaningRidge after ridge stands not a foot below the sky; dead pines hang head-down from the sheer rock.

16
飞湍瀑流争喧豗，砯崖转石万壑雷。

直译LiteralFlying rapids and waterfalls compete in roaring; dashing against cliffs and rolling stones, ten thousand gorges thunder.

意译MeaningRapids and falls roar against each other, beating the cliffs, rolling boulders — ten thousand gorges thunder.

17
其险也如此，嗟尔远道之人胡为乎来哉！

直译LiteralIts danger is like this — alas, you people from far away, why have you come?

意译MeaningSo dangerous is it — ah, traveller from far away, why ever have you come?

第四段Part 4
18
剑阁峥嵘而崔嵬，一夫当关，万夫莫开。

直译LiteralJiange Pass is steep and towering; one man guarding the pass, ten thousand men cannot open it.

意译MeaningSword Gate Pass rises jagged and towering: one man can hold it against ten thousand.

19
所守或匪亲，化为狼与豺。

直译LiteralIf those guarding it are not loyal kin, they turn into wolves and jackals.

意译MeaningBut if its guards are not loyal, they turn into wolves and jackals.

20
朝避猛虎，夕避长蛇，磨牙吮血，杀人如麻。

直译LiteralIn the morning avoid fierce tigers, in the evening avoid long snakes, grinding teeth and sucking blood, killing people like hemp.

意译MeaningBy morning dodge the tigers, by evening dodge the snakes, that grind their teeth and suck blood and kill men past counting.

21
锦城虽云乐，不如早还家。

直译LiteralAlthough the Brocade City is said to be pleasant, it is better to return home early.

意译MeaningChengdu may be a happy place, but better to go home soon.

22
蜀道之难，难于上青天，侧身西望长咨嗟！

直译LiteralThe difficulty of the Shu road is harder than climbing the blue sky; turning sideways to gaze west, I sigh long.

意译MeaningThe road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven — I turn and look west, and sigh, and sigh.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写的是从陕西进入四川（古代叫“蜀”）的山路有多难走。开头一声“噫吁嚱”，像是人一抬头就被大山吓得叫出声来，接着喊出全诗的主句：“蜀道之难，难于上青天”。第一部分讲历史：蜀国开国的事已经说不清，几万年和外面不通往来，直到“五丁开山”的传说里，壮士用生命换来了栈道。第二部分写山高路险：连太阳的车都要绕回去，黄鹤飞不过，猿猴也发愁；山路一百步拐九个弯，人仿佛伸手就能摸到星星。再加上悲鸟、子规的叫声，山里又空又冷。第三部分写水声和绝壁，瀑布、激流、滚石，像万道山谷一起打雷。最后写剑阁：“一夫当关，万夫莫开”，地势险要，如果守关的人不可靠，就会变成害人的豺狼，所以劝远行的人早点回家。“蜀道之难，难于上青天”在诗中出现三次，像歌曲的副歌。句子有长有短，三言、四言、七言、九言都有，读起来起伏很大，正好表现山路的高低曲折。这是李白最有名的长诗之一，想象非常大胆。

In English

The poem is about the mountain road from Shaanxi into Sichuan, the old kingdom of Shu. It opens with a gasp — “Yi xu xi!” — as if the poet looks up and is startled by the mountains, then shouts the line everyone remembers: the road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven. The first part is history: Shu’s founding is lost in legend, and for tens of thousands of years it had no contact with the outside, until (in another legend) five strong men died opening the mountain and the plank roads could be built. The second part is height: peaks that force the sun’s chariot to turn back, a crane that cannot fly over, apes that despair, a ridge with nine bends in every hundred steps, stars close enough to touch, and the cries of lonely birds and the cuckoo. The third part is water and cliffs — torrents, waterfalls and tumbling rocks thundering through ten thousand gorges. Finally comes Sword Gate Pass, where “one man can hold it against ten thousand”; if its guards are disloyal they become wild beasts, so the traveller had better go home. The refrain appears three times, like the chorus of a song. Notice how the lines change length — three, four, seven, even nine characters — rising and falling like the road itself. It is one of Li Bai’s most daring and famous long poems.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) grew up in Shu and wrote this old yuefu title around 742–744 in Chang’an. Legend says that when the senior poet He Zhizhang read it, he called Li Bai “an immortal banished from heaven”.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

噫吁嚱，危乎高哉！

yī xū xī, wēi hū gāo zāi

Oh — how steep, how high!

蜀道之难，难于上青天！

shǔ dào zhī nán, nán yú shàng qīng tiān

The road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven!

蚕丛及鱼凫，开国何茫然！

cán cóng jí yú fú, kāi guó hé máng rán

Cancong and Yufu, its first kings — how dim and far that founding is!

尔来四万八千岁，不与秦塞通人烟。

ěr lái sì wàn bā qiān suì, bù yǔ qín sài tōng rén yān

For forty-eight thousand years since, no chimney smoke crossed over to the land of Qin.

西当太白有鸟道，可以横绝峨眉巅。

xī dāng tài bái yǒu niǎo dào, kě yǐ héng jué é méi diān

West at Mount Taibai runs a path only birds can take, cutting straight across to the summit of Emei.

地崩山摧壮士死，然后天梯石栈相钩连。

dì bēng shān cuī zhuàng shì sǐ, rán hòu tiān tī shí zhàn xiāng gōu lián

The earth split, the mountain fell, the strong men died — and only then were sky-ladders and plank roads on stone linked up.

上有六龙回日之高标，下有冲波逆折之回川。

shàng yǒu liù lóng huí rì zhī gāo biāo, xià yǒu chōng bō nì zhé zhī huí chuān

Above, peaks so tall the sun’s six-dragon chariot must turn back; below, a river that crashes and twists back on itself.

黄鹤之飞尚不得过，猿猱欲度愁攀援。

huáng hè zhī fēi shàng bù dé guò, yuán náo yù dù chóu pān yuán

Even the yellow crane cannot fly over; even the apes, wanting to cross, despair of the climb.

青泥何盘盘，百步九折萦岩峦。

qīng ní hé pán pán, bǎi bù jiǔ zhé yíng yán luán

Qingni Ridge winds round and round — nine bends in every hundred steps, coiling about the rocks.

扪参历井仰胁息，以手抚膺坐长叹。

mén shēn lì jǐng yǎng xié xī, yǐ shǒu fǔ yīng zuò cháng tàn

You could touch the stars as you pass, looking up, not daring to breathe; you press your chest, sit down and sigh.

问君西游何时还？畏途巉岩不可攀。

wèn jūn xī yóu hé shí huán, wèi tú chán yán bù kě pān

When will you come back from your journey west? That dreadful road, those jagged cliffs, no one can climb.

但见悲鸟号古木，雄飞雌从绕林间。

dàn jiàn bēi niǎo háo gǔ mù, xióng fēi cí cóng rào lín jiān

All you see are mournful birds crying in old trees, the male in flight, the female behind, circling the woods.

又闻子规啼夜月，愁空山。

yòu wén zǐ guī tí yè yuè, chóu kōng shān

And you hear the cuckoo calling to the moon at night, filling the empty hills with sorrow.

蜀道之难，难于上青天，使人听此凋朱颜！

shǔ dào zhī nán, nán yú shàng qīng tiān, shǐ rén tīng cǐ diāo zhū yán

The road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven — just hearing of it turns young faces pale!

连峰去天不盈尺，枯松倒挂倚绝壁。

lián fēng qù tiān bù yíng chǐ, kū sōng dào guà yǐ jué bì

Ridge after ridge stands not a foot below the sky; dead pines hang head-down from the sheer rock.

飞湍瀑流争喧豗，砯崖转石万壑雷。

fēi tuān pù liú zhēng xuān huī, pīng yá zhuǎn shí wàn hè léi

Rapids and falls roar against each other, beating the cliffs, rolling boulders — ten thousand gorges thunder.

其险也如此，嗟尔远道之人胡为乎来哉！

qí xiǎn yě rú cǐ, jiē ěr yuǎn dào zhī rén hú wèi hū lái zāi

So dangerous is it — ah, traveller from far away, why ever have you come?

剑阁峥嵘而崔嵬，一夫当关，万夫莫开。

jiàn gé zhēng róng ér cuī wéi, yī fū dāng guān, wàn fū mò kāi

Sword Gate Pass rises jagged and towering: one man can hold it against ten thousand.

所守或匪亲，化为狼与豺。

suǒ shǒu huò fěi qīn, huà wéi láng yǔ chái

But if its guards are not loyal, they turn into wolves and jackals.

朝避猛虎，夕避长蛇，磨牙吮血，杀人如麻。

zhāo bì měng hǔ, xī bì cháng shé, mó yá shǔn xuè, shā rén rú má

By morning dodge the tigers, by evening dodge the snakes, that grind their teeth and suck blood and kill men past counting.

锦城虽云乐，不如早还家。

jǐn chéng suī yún lè, bù rú zǎo huán jiā

Chengdu may be a happy place, but better to go home soon.

蜀道之难，难于上青天，侧身西望长咨嗟！

shǔ dào zhī nán, nán yú shàng qīng tiān, cè shēn xī wàng cháng zī jiē

The road to Shu is harder than climbing to heaven — I turn and look west, and sigh, and sigh.
