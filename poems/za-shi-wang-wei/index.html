导读About this poem

You meet someone from home — what do you ask first? Not about family or friends, but whether the plum tree by your old window has blossomed yet. A tiny question that holds all of homesickness.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗像一段对话。在外地遇到从家乡来的人，诗人说：您从我的家乡来，一定知道家乡的事吧！人们以为他接下来会问家人好不好、朋友怎么样，可他只问了一件小事：您来的那天，我家雕花窗前的那棵梅树，开花了没有？为什么只问梅花？因为梅花是他记忆里家的样子，越是想家，越会想起这些小小的细节。千言万语都藏在这一个问题里，这就是“以小见大”。诗的语言像平常说话，问句结尾，留给读者很多想象。

In English

The poem reads like a short conversation. Far from home, the poet meets someone who has just come from his hometown. “You’ve come from my old home,” he says, “so you must know what’s happening there!” We expect him to ask about his family or his friends. Instead he asks one small thing: “The day you left, by my carved-lattice window — had the winter plum begun to flower?” Why only the plum tree? Because that is the picture of home he carries in his mind, and when you miss a place deeply, it is often the small details that come back to you. All his longing is packed into one question; critics call this “seeing the large through the small” (以小见大). The language is as plain as everyday speech, and the poem ends on a question we never hear answered, leaving the reader to imagine the rest — the blossoms, the window, the people he cannot ask about.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761) left his home in Shanxi young to seek his fortune in the capital. This is the second of three short poems titled “Miscellaneous Poems”; its date is not known.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

君自故乡来，

jūn zì gù xiāng lái
