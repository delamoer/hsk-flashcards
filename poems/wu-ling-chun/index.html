导读About this poem

A widow in exile at the end of spring: she cannot even bring herself to comb her hair. Friends say the river is lovely and she thinks of a boat trip — but fears the little boat could never carry so much sorrow.

上阕First stanza
01
风住尘香花已尽，日晚倦梳头。

直译LiteralThe wind stops, the dust is fragrant, the flowers are all gone; the day is late, too weary to comb my hair.

意译MeaningThe wind has died; the dust still smells of fallen petals. Though the sun is high, I am too tired to comb my hair.

02
物是人非事事休，欲语泪先流。

直译LiteralThings are the same, the person is not, everything is over; about to speak, tears flow first.

意译MeaningEverything is where it was — but he is gone, and all is finished. I try to speak; the tears come first.

下阕Second stanza
03
闻说双溪春尚好，也拟泛轻舟。

直译LiteralI hear that at Twin Brooks spring is still fine; I too plan to float a light boat.

意译MeaningThey say spring is still lovely on Twin Brooks; I think I might take a little boat out.

04
只恐双溪舴艋舟，载不动许多愁。

直译LiteralI only fear the grasshopper boats of Twin Brooks cannot carry so much sorrow.

意译MeaningBut I fear those tiny boats on Twin Brooks could never hold up this much grief.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词是李清照晚年的作品。丈夫已经去世，国家被金兵占了一半，她一个人逃难到浙江金华。上阕写暮春的早上：风停了，花全落了，泥土里还留着花香；太阳很高了，她却懒得梳头。“物是人非事事休”——东西还是原来的东西，人却不在了，一切都完了；想说话，眼泪先流下来。下阕本来有一点希望：听说双溪的春天还很好，也想去划船散心。可马上又担心：那么小的舴艋舟，载不动我这么多的愁。愁本来看不见、摸不着，作者却说它重得船都载不动，这种把抽象的感情写成有重量的东西的手法，非常新鲜，成了千古名句。

In English

Li Qingzhao wrote this late in life. Her husband was dead, northern China had fallen to the Jurchen Jin, and she had fled alone to Jinhua in Zhejiang. The first half is a late-spring morning: the wind has dropped, every blossom has fallen, and only a trace of their scent is left in the dust. The sun is high, yet she has no heart to comb her hair. Then the famous line 物是人非事事休: things are as they were, the person is gone, and nothing matters any more. She wants to speak, but the tears come first. The second half lifts for a moment. People say spring is still lovely at Twin Brooks, the local river, and she thinks she might take a boat out. But at once she doubts it: that little grasshopper boat could never carry so much grief. Sorrow has no weight, of course — giving it one, so heavy it could sink a boat, is what makes the ending unforgettable. Notice how quietly the poem moves: small daily details (hair, flowers, a boat trip) carry a loss too large to name directly.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Qingzhao (1084–c. 1155) is China’s most celebrated woman poet. After the Jin invasion of 1127 and her husband Zhao Mingcheng’s death in 1129 she lived as a refugee; this lyric is usually dated to 1135, when she was sheltering in Jinhua.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

舴艋 zé měng（小船）；载不动 zài；拟 nǐ；只恐 zhǐ。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

风住尘香花已尽，日晚倦梳头。

fēng zhù chén xiāng huā yǐ jìn, rì wǎn juàn shū tóu

The wind has died; the dust still smells of fallen petals. Though the sun is high, I am too tired to comb my hair.

物是人非事事休，欲语泪先流。

wù shì rén fēi shì shì xiū, yù yǔ lèi xiān liú

Everything is where it was — but he is gone, and all is finished. I try to speak; the tears come first.

闻说双溪春尚好，也拟泛轻舟。

wén shuō shuāng xī chūn shàng hǎo, yě nǐ fàn qīng zhōu

They say spring is still lovely on Twin Brooks; I think I might take a little boat out.

只恐双溪舴艋舟，载不动许多愁。

zhǐ kǒng shuāng xī zé měng zhōu, zài bù dòng xǔ duō chóu

But I fear those tiny boats on Twin Brooks could never hold up this much grief.
