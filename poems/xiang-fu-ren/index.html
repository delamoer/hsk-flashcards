导读About this poem

A river god waits on the shore of Lake Dongting for the goddess he loves. The autumn wind blows, leaves fall on the waves — and she does not come. The line 袅袅兮秋风，洞庭波兮木叶下 gave Chinese poetry its classic picture of autumn longing.

01
帝子降兮北渚，目眇眇兮愁予。

直译LiteralThe emperor’s daughter descends to the northern islet; my eyes strain far, and I am grieved.

意译MeaningThe goddess is coming down to the northern shore — I strain my eyes to find her, and my heart aches.

02
袅袅兮秋风，洞庭波兮木叶下。

直译LiteralGently, gently, the autumn wind; Dongting ripples, and the leaves of the trees come down.

意译MeaningA soft autumn wind keeps blowing; Lake Dongting stirs into waves, and the leaves come drifting down.

03
登白薠兮骋望，与佳期兮夕张。

直译LiteralClimbing among the white sedge, I gaze far; for the meeting with the fair one, I make ready in the evening.

意译MeaningI climb among the white sedge and gaze as far as I can; I have set everything out for our meeting tonight.

04
鸟何萃兮蘋中，罾何为兮木上？

直译LiteralWhy do birds gather among the water plants? What is a fishing net doing up in the trees?

意译MeaningBirds flocking in the water weeds, a fishing net caught up in a tree — nothing is where it should be.

05
沅有芷兮澧有兰，思公子兮未敢言。

直译LiteralThe Yuan has angelica, the Li has orchids; I long for the lady but dare not say it.

意译MeaningAngelica grows by the Yuan, orchids by the Li — I long for you, and dare not say so.

06
荒忽兮远望，观流水兮潺湲。

直译LiteralDazed, I gaze into the distance, and watch the flowing water murmuring on.

意译MeaningLost in a daze, I look far out, and watch the river slipping quietly by.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《湘夫人》是屈原《九歌》中的一篇。《九歌》本是楚国祭神时唱的歌，屈原把它改写得更加优美。湘夫人是湘水的女神，传说是尧的女儿，所以叫“帝子”。诗用湘水之神湘君的口吻，写他和湘夫人约好相会，却怎么也等不到。开头说女神好像要降临北边的小洲，他望得眼睛都花了，心里发愁。接着是千古名句“袅袅兮秋风，洞庭波兮木叶下”：秋风轻轻地吹，洞庭湖起了波浪，树叶纷纷落下——景色和等待的惆怅融在一起，后来“洞庭”“木叶”成了诗人写秋天和思念的常用语。鸟聚在水草里、渔网挂在树上，都是反常的事，比喻他的期待落空。“沅有芷兮澧有兰”，用香草起兴，引出“思公子兮未敢言”的深情。句中的“兮”是楚辞特有的语气词，相当于“啊”，读起来有一种悠长的歌唱感。

In English

“The Lady of the Xiang” comes from the Nine Songs, hymns sung at religious ceremonies in the ancient state of Chu, which Qu Yuan is said to have reworked into poetry. The Lady is the goddess of the Xiang River, traditionally a daughter of the sage-emperor Yao — hence 帝子, “the emperor’s daughter”. The voice is the Lord of the Xiang, the river god, who has arranged to meet her and waits in vain. At first he thinks she is about to descend on the northern islet; he strains his eyes until they blur, and sorrow fills him. Then comes one of the most famous couplets in Chinese: 袅袅兮秋风，洞庭波兮木叶下 — a soft autumn wind keeps blowing, Lake Dongting ripples, the leaves come down. Scenery and longing melt into each other, and “Dongting” and “falling leaves” became standard words for autumn sadness ever after. Birds gathering in water weeds and a fishing net hanging in a tree are things out of place — images of a hope that has gone wrong. The fragrant plants by the Yuan and Li rivers lead into his confession: “I long for you and dare not say it.” The little word 兮 (xī), a sigh-like particle typical of Chu poetry, gives every line a slow, singing rhythm.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC) was a nobleman and minister of Chu, traditionally regarded as China’s first great named poet. Exiled after court intrigues, he is said to have drowned himself in the Miluo River, an event remembered at the Dragon Boat Festival. The Nine Songs are traditionally attributed to him, reworking Chu ritual songs.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

降 jiàng；愁予 chóu yú（予：我）；眇眇 miǎo miǎo；白薠 bái fán；蘋 pín（水草，不是“苹果”的苹）；罾 zēng；何为 hé wéi；澧 lǐ；芷 zhǐ；潺湲 chán yuán；兮 xī。机器朗读的几个生僻字已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行本（与统编高中语文教材所收文本一致）。《湘夫人》全诗四十句，这里选开头十二句（到“观流水兮潺湲”）。洪兴祖《楚辞补注》记“白薠”前一本无“登”字；“荒忽”一作“慌忽”；“沅有芷”的“芷”，《楚辞补注》作“茝”（同一种香草），教材作“芷”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

帝子降兮北渚，目眇眇兮愁予。

dì zǐ jiàng xī běi zhǔ, mù miǎo miǎo xī chóu yú

The goddess is coming down to the northern shore — I strain my eyes to find her, and my heart aches.

袅袅兮秋风，洞庭波兮木叶下。

niǎo niǎo xī qiū fēng, dòng tíng bō xī mù yè xià

A soft autumn wind keeps blowing; Lake Dongting stirs into waves, and the leaves come drifting down.

登白薠兮骋望，与佳期兮夕张。

dēng bái fán xī chěng wàng, yǔ jiā qī xī xī zhāng

I climb among the white sedge and gaze as far as I can; I have set everything out for our meeting tonight.

鸟何萃兮蘋中，罾何为兮木上？

niǎo hé cuì xī pín zhōng, zēng hé wéi xī mù shàng

Birds flocking in the water weeds, a fishing net caught up in a tree — nothing is where it should be.

沅有芷兮澧有兰，思公子兮未敢言。

yuán yǒu zhǐ xī lǐ yǒu lán, sī gōng zǐ xī wèi gǎn yán

Angelica grows by the Yuan, orchids by the Li — I long for you, and dare not say so.

荒忽兮远望，观流水兮潺湲。

huāng hū xī yuǎn wàng, guān liú shuǐ xī chán yuán

Lost in a daze, I look far out, and watch the river slipping quietly by.
