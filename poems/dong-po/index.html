导读About this poem

After the rain, when the townspeople have gone home, an exiled poet walks his rough hillside path alone by moonlight — and loves the clack of his walking stick on the stones.

中文解析

这首诗写苏轼在黄州东坡夜里散步。东坡是他被贬到黄州后亲手开荒种地的一块山坡，他也因此自号“东坡居士”。第一句写雨后的东坡，月光格外清亮。第二句说：城里的人都走光了，只剩下我这个“山野之人”还在走。第三、四句是全诗的重点：别嫌坡上的路坑坑洼洼、高低不平，我偏偏喜欢手杖敲在石头上发出的“铿铿”声。表面写走路，其实写人生——路不好走（就像他被贬的处境），他却能从里面找到乐趣。城里的喧闹散去，只剩下手杖的声音，诗也就格外安静。这种乐观、豁达的态度，正是苏轼最让人喜欢的地方。

In English

Su Shi wrote this about a night walk on the East Slope at Huangzhou — the hillside plot he cleared and farmed with his own hands after being exiled there. He liked it so much that he took “Dongpo” (East Slope) as his pen name. The first line shows the slope just washed by rain, the moonlight unusually clear. In the second, the townspeople have all gone home and only he, a “man of the wilds”, is still out walking. The last two lines carry the meaning: don’t complain that the path is rough and stony — he actually enjoys the sharp clack of his stick against the rocks. On the surface it is a walk; underneath it is about life. The road is hard, like his exile, yet he finds pleasure in it. Notice, too, how much the poem is about sound: after the noise of the town fades, the only thing left is the ring of the stick. That cheerful, open-hearted attitude is exactly why Su Shi is so loved.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Su Shi (1037–1101), also called Su Dongpo, was the great all-round talent of the Song — poet, essayist, painter and calligrapher. Exiled to Huangzhou (1080–1084) after a political trial over his poems, he farmed the East Slope and wrote this short poem there.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

荦确 luò què（山石高低不平的样子）；铿然 kēng rán；曳杖 yè zhàng。行尽 xíng jìn、野人行 xíng。机器朗读“荦确、曳”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列换成同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

雨洗东坡月色清，

yǔ xǐ dōng pō yuè sè qīng

Rain has rinsed the East Slope; the moonlight is clean and clear.

市人行尽野人行。

shì rén xíng jìn yě rén xíng

The townsfolk have all gone home; now the man of the hills walks out.

莫嫌荦确坡头路，

mò xián luò què pō tóu lù

Don’t grumble that the hillside path is rough and stony —

自爱铿然曳杖声。

zì ài kēng rán yè zhàng shēng

I love the clack of my stick on the stones.
