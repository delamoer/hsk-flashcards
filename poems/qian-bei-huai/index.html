导读About this poem

A man mourns the wife who shared his years of poverty. Through her clothes, her sewing box and her old maid, he finds her everywhere — and ends with one of the most quoted lines in Chinese: for a poor couple, everything brings sorrow.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是元稹怀念亡妻的三首诗中的第二首，这类怀念死去妻子的诗叫“悼亡诗”。开头说：从前两人开玩笑，说起“谁先死了以后怎么办”，没想到今天全都成了眼前的事。中间四句全是生活小事：妻子的衣服已经送人，快送完了；她用过的针线盒还留着，却不忍心打开；因为念着她的旧情，对她身边的婢女仆人也格外怜惜；梦见她，醒来还给她烧纸钱。这些细节很普通，却让人看到他每天都在想她。最后两句说：我当然知道生离死别人人都会遇到，可我们是一起熬过贫贱日子的夫妻，回想起来，每一件事都让人心酸。“贫贱夫妻百事哀”原本是说这份特别的伤痛，今天常被借来说“穷夫妻事事难”，意思有了变化。

In English

This is the second of three poems Yuan Zhen wrote for his dead wife — a kind of Chinese poem called 悼亡, “mourning the departed”. It begins with a memory: once, half-joking, the two of them talked about what would happen “after one of us dies”. Now all those jokes have come true in front of him. The middle four lines are made entirely of everyday details. He has been giving her clothes away, and they are nearly gone. Her needle-and-thread box is still there, but he cannot bear to open it. Because she was kind to her maids and servants, he treats them gently too. He dreams of her, and when he wakes he burns paper money for her, as people did for the dead. Nothing here is dramatic, yet every small act shows that she is on his mind all day. The last couplet says: of course I know everyone suffers losses like this — but we were a couple who lived through poverty together, so every memory hurts. That final line, 贫贱夫妻百事哀, is still quoted today, though people now often use it to mean “a poor couple fights over everything”, which is not quite what Yuan Zhen meant.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yuan Zhen (779–831) was a Tang official and poet, and Bai Juyi’s closest friend. Around 802 he married Wei Cong, the daughter of a high official, who shared his early years of poverty; she died in 809 at the age of 27, just as his career was beginning to rise. He wrote three “Easing My Grief” poems in her memory.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

昔日戏言身后意，

xī rì xì yán shēn hòu yì

Once we joked about “when one of us is gone” —

今朝都到眼前来。

jīn zhāo dōu dào yǎn qián lái

now every word of it stands in front of me.

衣裳已施行看尽，

yī cháng yǐ shī xíng kàn jìn

I have given your clothes away; soon there will be none,

针线犹存未忍开。

zhēn xiàn yóu cún wèi rěn kāi

but your sewing box is still here — I cannot bring myself to open it.

尚想旧情怜婢仆，

shàng xiǎng jiù qíng lián bì pú

For your sake I am gentle with the servants you were fond of;

也曾因梦送钱财。

yě céng yīn mèng sòng qián cái

and once, after dreaming of you, I burned money for you to spend.

诚知此恨人人有，

chéng zhī cǐ hèn rén rén yǒu

I know that everyone, sooner or later, loses someone —

贫贱夫妻百事哀。

pín jiàn fū qī bǎi shì āi

but when a couple were poor together, every memory is grief.
