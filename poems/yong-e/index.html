导读About this poem

Said to be written by a seven-year-old: three calls of “goose!”, then a picture in bright colours — white feathers, green water, red feet. Many Chinese children learn it as their very first poem.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

相传这首诗是骆宾王七岁时写的。开头连叫三声“鹅，鹅，鹅”，像一个孩子在池塘边看见白鹅，高兴得喊出来，也像鹅自己“嘎嘎”的叫声。第二句写鹅弯着长脖子，朝着天空唱歌，样子很骄傲。后两句像一幅彩色的画：白色的羽毛浮在绿色的水上，红色的脚掌拨动清清的水波。“白”对“红”，“毛”对“掌”，“浮”对“拨”，“绿水”对“清波”，两句一一相对，这叫“对仗”。而且有动有静：“浮”是静静地漂，“拨”是用力地划。短短十八个字，有声音、有颜色、有动作，难怪成了很多孩子学的第一首诗。

In English

Tradition says Luo Binwang wrote this when he was seven. It opens with three cries of “goose, goose, goose!” — like a child spotting the birds by a pond and shouting with excitement, and also like the honking of the geese themselves. The second line shows a goose bending its long neck and singing up at the sky, rather proud of itself. The last two lines are a picture in colour: white feathers floating on green water, red feet paddling the clear ripples. The two lines match word for word — white/red, feathers/feet, float/paddle, green water/clear waves. This matching is called duìzhàng (“parallel couplets”), a key feature of Chinese poetry. There is also stillness and movement: the body floats calmly on top while the feet work hard underneath. In just eighteen characters you get sound, colour and motion, which is why so many children start with this poem.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Luo Binwang (c. 640–684?) was one of the “Four Talents of the Early Tang”. He later joined a rebellion against Empress Wu Zetian and disappeared after it failed. The story that he wrote this poem at seven is traditional and cannot be proved.

版本说明 About the text

采用通行的教材版本。首句“鹅，鹅，鹅”三字一顿，全诗十八字，不是标准的五言绝句，所以归为五言古诗。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

鹅，鹅，鹅，

é, é, é

Geese! Geese! Geese!

曲项向天歌。

qū xiàng xiàng tiān gē

Necks curved high, they sing to the sky.

白毛浮绿水，

bái máo fú lǜ shuǐ

White feathers drift on green water,

红掌拨清波。

hóng zhǎng bō qīng bō

red feet paddle the clear ripples.
