导读About this poem

A farewell poem that refuses to be sad: the rivers connect us, the same clouds and rain fall on us both, and the same bright moon shines on us — so how can we be in two different places?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

一般的送别诗都写离别的伤心，这首却反过来安慰朋友。王昌龄当时被贬到龙标（今湖南黔阳一带），朋友柴侍御要去武冈。第一句说：沅水的波浪一直通到武冈，两地有河相连。所以第二句说：送你走，我并不觉得有离别的悲伤。后两句是名句：两地之间的青山，共同承受着一样的云和雨；同一轮明月照着我们，怎么能说我们在两个地方呢？用“同”和“何曾是两乡”，把两地的距离一下子拉近了。其实正因为舍不得，才要这样劝慰对方——表面洒脱，底下是很深的情谊。后来日本长屋王赠给中国僧人的袈裟上绣着“山川异域，风月同天”，意思和这首诗很接近。

In English

Most farewell poems dwell on the sadness of parting; this one turns it around and comforts the friend. Wang Changling had been demoted to Longbiao (in today’s western Hunan), and his friend Censor Chai was leaving for Wugang. The first line says the waves of the Yuan River flow all the way to Wugang — the two places are joined by water. So, line two: seeing you off, I don’t feel the pain of parting. The last two lines are the famous ones: the green hills between us share the same clouds and rain, and one bright moon shines on us both — how could we ever be in two separate places? With the words 同 (“the same”) and 何曾是两乡 (“when were we ever in two places?”) the distance shrinks to nothing. Of course, it is because he will miss his friend so much that he talks this way: light on the surface, deep friendship underneath. The line has a modern echo: in 2020, aid boxes sent from Japan to China carried the old phrase “mountains and rivers in different lands, wind and moon under the same sky”, which says much the same thing.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Changling (c. 698–757) was a celebrated master of the quatrain. He wrote this while serving in exile as a minor official in Longbiao, in the remote south-west of today’s Hunan; nothing else is known of Censor Chai (侍御 is an official title).

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

沅水通波接武冈，

yuán shuǐ tōng bō jiē wǔ gāng

The Yuan River runs its waves all the way to Wugang;

送君不觉有离伤。

sòng jūn bù jué yǒu lí shāng

seeing you go, I don’t feel we are parting at all.

青山一道同云雨，

qīng shān yī dào tóng yún yǔ

One line of green hills, the same cloud and rain;

明月何曾是两乡。

míng yuè hé céng shì liǎng xiāng

one bright moon — how are we in two places?
