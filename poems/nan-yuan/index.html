导读About this poem

A frustrated young scholar asks why a real man doesn’t pick up a sword and win back the lost provinces — after all, which bookworm ever had his portrait hung in the Hall of Heroes? Short, punchy and full of youthful fire.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

李贺生活的年代，唐朝北方很多州郡被地方军阀（藩镇）控制，朝廷管不了。这首诗用两个问句表达他的不平和抱负。第一句问：男子汉为什么不佩上吴钩宝刀，去收回关山内外那五十个州？这是激励自己，也是激励别人。后两句换了个角度：请你登上凌烟阁看一看——那里挂着唐朝开国功臣的画像——有哪一个是书生出身、被封为“万户侯”的？意思是读书考试不能建功立业，不如去当兵报国。表面上是轻视读书人，其实是李贺自己的苦闷：他很有才华，却因为父亲名字的避讳问题不能参加进士考试。用反问句收尾，语气更有力，这种手法叫“反问”。

In English

In Li He’s time, many provinces in northern China were run by local warlords and no longer obeyed the Tang court. This short poem is built on two questions that burst with frustration and ambition. The first asks: why doesn’t a real man strap on a Wu hook — a curved sword — and go win back those fifty lost provinces beyond the passes and mountains? It is a call to himself as much as to anyone else. The second half changes angle. “Go up to the Lingyan Pavilion for a moment,” he says. That was where the emperor had hung portraits of the heroes who founded the dynasty. Which of them was a scholar who earned a fief of ten thousand households? None — they were all soldiers. So why waste your life over books? On the surface the poem mocks scholars, but the bitterness is personal. Li He was brilliant, yet he was barred from the state examinations on a technicality about his father’s name. Ending on a rhetorical question — one that expects no answer — makes the poem hit harder than a plain statement could.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li He (790–816), nicknamed the “Ghost of Poetry” for his dark, strange imagination, was a distant member of the Tang royal family. He was kept from the jinshi examination because his father’s name, Jinsu, sounded like the jin of jinshi, and he died at about 26. South Garden was his family’s garden at Changgu, in today’s Henan.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

男儿何不带吴钩，

nán ér hé bù dài wú gōu

Why not strap on a curved blade like a man

收取关山五十州。

shōu qǔ guān shān wǔ shí zhōu

and win back the fifty lost provinces beyond the passes?

请君暂上凌烟阁，

qǐng jūn zàn shàng líng yān gé

Climb the Hall of Heroes for a moment and look —

若个书生万户侯？

ruò gè shū shēng wàn hù hóu

which bookish man up there was ever made a lord?
