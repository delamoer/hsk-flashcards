导读About this poem

Ten years spent grinding one sword, and it has never been used. In four short lines a swordsman — really the poet — asks: is there an injustice I can put right?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗借一个剑客的口气说话。前两句说：我花了十年磨一把剑，剑刃像霜一样白亮锋利，却还从来没有试过。后两句说：今天我把它拿出来给你看，谁遇到了不公平的事？全诗表面写剑，其实写人——“十年磨一剑”比喻多年苦学、积累本领，“霜刃未曾试”是说才能还没有机会施展，最后一问充满自信，想为天下打抱不平。这种借一个东西来表达自己志向的写法叫“托物言志”。贾岛的诗多写清冷孤寂，这首却很豪爽。“十年磨一剑”今天还常用，比如说一部作品、一项研究花了很多年才完成。

In English

The poem speaks in the voice of a swordsman. “For ten years I have ground this one sword; its frost-white blade has never been tried. Today I hold it out to show you — who has a wrong that needs righting?” On the surface it is about a sword, but it is really about a person. Ten years of grinding stand for years of study and hard work; the untried blade is talent that has never had its chance. The final question is bold and confident: the speaker is ready to stand up for anyone treated unfairly. Expressing your own ambitions through an object like this is a common technique in Chinese poetry, called 托物言志 (“using a thing to speak your mind”). Jia Dao usually wrote quiet, chilly, lonely poems, which makes this short burst of heroism stand out. The phrase 十年磨一剑 is still used today for any work that took years to perfect — a novel, a film, a research project.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Jia Dao (779–843) was a former monk who struggled for years to pass the imperial exams and win office. The poem is usually read as a statement of the talent he hoped someone would finally put to use.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

十年磨一剑，

shí nián mó yī jiàn

Ten years I have spent grinding one sword;

霜刃未曾试。

shuāng rèn wèi céng shì

its frost-bright edge has never been tested.

今日把示君，

jīn rì bǎ shì jūn

Today I hold it out for you to see —

谁有不平事？

shuí yǒu bù píng shì

tell me, who has been wronged?
