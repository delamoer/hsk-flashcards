导读About this poem

Boating on the Han River near Xiangyang, Wang Wei paints like a landscape artist: the river flows beyond heaven and earth, the hills appear and vanish in the haze, whole towns seem to float on the water.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是王维在襄阳一带泛舟汉江时写的山水诗，像一幅水墨画。开头两句写汉江的地理形势：它连着楚地的边塞和湖南的三湘，在荆门与长江的众多支流相通，一下子把视野拉得很宽。第二联最有名：江水好像流到了天地之外，远山在雾气中若有若无。这正是中国山水画的“留白”——远处画得淡淡的，看不清，反而显得更远、更大。第三联写船在波浪中起伏，看岸边的城镇好像漂浮在水上，远处的天空也在晃动，写得真实又有趣。最后说襄阳风景这么好，我愿意留下来，和“山翁”一起喝个痛快——山翁指晋代在襄阳爱喝酒的山简。

In English

Wang Wei wrote this while boating on the Han River near Xiangyang, and it reads like an ink-wash landscape. The opening couplet sets out the geography in broad strokes: the river touches the old frontiers of Chu and the three Xiang rivers of Hunan, and at Jingmen it joins the nine branches of the Yangtze — a map that instantly makes the view enormous. The second couplet is the famous one: the river flows on beyond heaven and earth; the colours of the hills hover between there and not there. This is the poetic version of a key idea in Chinese painting, “leaving blank space” (留白): far-off things are painted faint and hazy, and that vagueness makes them feel farther and vaster. In the third couplet the boat rises and falls on the waves, so the towns on the shore seem to float, and even the distant sky seems to move — a real experience anyone who has been in a small boat will recognise. Finally he says Xiangyang’s wind and sunshine are so fine that he would like to stay and get drunk with the “old man of the mountain”, Shan Jian, a Jin-dynasty governor of Xiangyang famous for his love of wine.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761) travelled south to Xiangyang around 740 on an official assignment. The poem is also titled “Gazing from the Han River” (汉江临眺) in some editions.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

楚塞三湘接，

chǔ sài sān xiāng jiē

Here the old borders of Chu meet the Xiang rivers,

荆门九派通。

jīng mén jiǔ pài tōng

and at Jingmen nine streams run into one.

江流天地外，

jiāng liú tiān dì wài

The river flows on past the edge of the world;

山色有无中。

shān sè yǒu wú zhōng

the hills fade in and out of the haze.

郡邑浮前浦，

jùn yì fú qián pǔ

Towns seem to float on the shore ahead;

波澜动远空。

bō lán dòng yuǎn kōng

the waves set the far sky rocking.

襄阳好风日，

xiāng yáng hǎo fēng rì

Xiangyang’s breeze and sunlight are so fine,

留醉与山翁。

liú zuì yǔ shān wēng

I’ll stay and drink with the old man of the hills.
