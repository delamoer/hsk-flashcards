Xiàng Shǔ
Look at the Rat

导读About this poem

Probably the angriest poem in the Book of Songs: even a rat has skin, teeth and a body — so a person with no decency is worse than a rat. Short, repetitive and easy to chant, it shows that ancient poetry could be fierce.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首骂人的诗，也是《诗经》里语气最激烈的一首。诗人拿老鼠来比：老鼠这么让人讨厌，也还有皮、有牙齿、有身体；而有些人连“仪”（体面）、“止”（节制）、“礼”（规矩）都没有，那不就连老鼠都不如吗？每章最后一句一次比一次狠：“不死还干什么？”“还等什么不死？”“为什么不快点死？”这种层层加重的写法，叫“层递”。全诗三章，结构完全一样，只换几个字，是民歌“重章叠句”的特点，念起来很有节奏。古人一般认为这是讽刺统治者不讲礼义、行为无耻。它告诉我们：中国古代诗歌不只有温柔的风花雪月，也有直接的愤怒；同时也能看出“礼”在古代中国人心中有多重要。

In English

This is a poem of pure scorn — the fiercest in the Book of Songs. The poet compares someone to a rat. A rat is a lowly, hated creature, yet it still has skin, teeth and a body. Some people, though, have no 仪 (dignity), no 止 (self-control), no 礼 (sense of proper conduct). Doesn’t that make them lower than a rat? The last line of each stanza grows harsher: “What is he still alive for?” “What is he waiting for?” “Why doesn’t he hurry up and die?” Building up force step by step like this is called 层递 (“climax”). The three stanzas share one pattern with only a few words changed, the typical repetition of folk songs, so the poem is easy to chant and remember. Traditional readers saw it as an attack on rulers who behaved shamelessly and ignored the rules of conduct. It is a reminder that ancient Chinese poetry wasn’t only about moonlight and flowers — it could be blunt and furious. It also shows how central 礼, the idea of proper behaviour, was to how people judged each other.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one is from the “Airs of Yong” (鄘风), a small state in present-day Henan; traditionally it is read as a satire on corrupt nobles.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

相鼠有皮，人而无仪。

xiàng shǔ yǒu pí, rén ér wú yí

Look — even a rat has its skin, but some men have no decency at all.

人而无仪，不死何为？

rén ér wú yí, bù sǐ hé wéi

A man with no decency — what is he still alive for?

相鼠有齿，人而无止。

xiàng shǔ yǒu chǐ, rén ér wú zhǐ

Look — even a rat has its teeth, but some men know no limits.

人而无止，不死何俟？

rén ér wú zhǐ, bù sǐ hé sì

A man who knows no limits — what is he waiting for?

相鼠有体，人而无礼。

xiàng shǔ yǒu tǐ, rén ér wú lǐ

Look — even a rat has a body, limbs and all, but some men have no sense of right.

人而无礼，胡不遄死？

rén ér wú lǐ, hú bù chuán sǐ

A man with no sense of right — why doesn’t he hurry up and die?
