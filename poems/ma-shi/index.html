导读About this poem

Desert sand white as snow, a hook of moon over the mountains: a fine horse dreams of a golden bridle and a gallop across the clear autumn plain — and so does the poet.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

李贺写过二十三首《马诗》，这是第五首，统编教材选入。前两句写边塞夜景：大沙漠里的沙子在月光下白得像雪，燕山上的月亮弯弯的像一把钩子。“钩”是古代一种弯刀，所以这句也暗含打仗的味道。后两句是马的心愿：什么时候能给我戴上金子装饰的笼头，让我在清爽的秋天原野上奔驰？表面写马，其实写人：好马盼望遇到懂它的主人，诗人盼望得到重用、为国家建功立业。这种借物写自己的手法叫“托物言志”。“快走踏清秋”一句很有速度感，读起来像听见了马蹄声。

In English

Li He wrote a set of 23 horse poems; this is No. 5, the one taught in Chinese schools. The first two lines show a frontier night: under the moon the desert sand is white as snow, and over the Yan Mountains hangs a moon curved like a hook. A 钩 was also a curved sabre, so the line carries a hint of battle. The last two lines are the horse’s wish: when will someone put a gold-trimmed bridle on me, so I can gallop across the clear autumn plain? It is about a horse, but really about a man. A fine horse longs for a master who knows its worth; the poet longs for a ruler who will put his talents to use. Speaking about yourself through an object like this is called 托物言志. Listen to the last line — 快走踏清秋 — and you can almost hear hooves striking the ground.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li He (790–816) was a brilliant, sickly poet of royal descent who was barred from the imperial exams on a technicality and died at 26. His horse poems are often read as his frustration at having talent no one would use.

大漠沙如雪，

dà mò shā rú xuě

The desert sand lies white as snow;

燕山月似钩。

yān shān yuè sì gōu

over the Yan Mountains, a hook of moon.

何当金络脑，

hé dāng jīn luò nǎo

When will they put a gold bridle on me

快走踏清秋。

kuài zǒu tà qīng qiū

and let me race across the clear autumn plain?
