导读About this poem

An old, sick Du Fu climbs a height above the Yangtze on the autumn festival day. Endless falling leaves, an endless river, and a lifetime of wandering pour into eight lines often called the finest seven-character regulated poem in Chinese.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

重阳节有登高的习俗。这一年杜甫五十多岁，漂泊在长江边的夔州，又老又病，独自登上高处。前四句写景：秋风急，天很高，猿猴叫得悲哀；水中小洲清清的，沙滩白白的，鸟在空中盘旋。接着是最有名的一联：无边无际的落叶萧萧地飘下，望不到头的长江滚滚地流来。落叶让人想到生命一天天老去，长江让人想到时间永远不停，景色写得非常壮阔，又非常悲凉。后四句写情：离家万里，常年作客他乡，又遇上让人伤感的秋天；一辈子多病，今天只能一个人登台。艰难苦恨让鬓发全白，因为病又刚刚戒了酒，连借酒消愁都不行了。这首诗八句都对仗（上下句结构相同、意思相对），却读起来很自然，所以被称为“古今七言律第一”。

In English

On the Double Ninth Festival (the ninth day of the ninth month), people climb a height. In 767 Du Fu, in his mid-fifties, sick and far from home in Kuizhou on the Yangtze, climbs alone. The first half is landscape: a sharp wind, a high sky, the mournful cry of gibbons; a clear islet, white sand, birds circling back. Then the couplet everyone knows: “Boundless forests shed their leaves, rustling down; the endless Yangtze comes rolling on.” Falling leaves suggest a life running out; the river suggests time that never stops. The scene is grand and heartbreaking at once. The second half turns inward. “Ten thousand li from home, grieving at autumn, always a guest; a hundred years of illness, climbing the terrace alone” — every phrase adds another layer of sorrow. Hardship has turned his temples frost-white, and illness has just made him give up wine, so even drinking away his sorrow is denied him. All four couplets are in strict parallelism (matching structure line against line), yet the poem flows naturally, which is why critics called it the best seven-character regulated verse ever written.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) spent his last years drifting along the Yangtze. He wrote this in autumn 767 in Kuizhou (today’s Fengjie, Chongqing), three years before his death, when he was suffering from lung disease and other illnesses.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

风急天高猿啸哀，

fēng jí tiān gāo yuán xiào āi

Sharp wind, high sky, the gibbons’ cries are grief;

渚清沙白鸟飞回。

zhǔ qīng shā bái niǎo fēi huí

clear islet, white sand, birds wheeling back.

无边落木萧萧下，

wú biān luò mù xiāo xiāo xià

Leaves fall without end, rustling, rustling down;

不尽长江滚滚来。

bù jìn cháng jiāng gǔn gǔn lái

the endless Yangtze comes rolling, rolling on.

万里悲秋常作客，

wàn lǐ bēi qiū cháng zuò kè

Ten thousand miles from home, sad autumn, always a stranger;

百年多病独登台。

bǎi nián duō bìng dú dēng tái

a lifetime of sickness, I climb the terrace alone.

艰难苦恨繁霜鬓，

jiān nán kǔ hèn fán shuāng bìn

Hard years and bitter regrets have frosted my hair;

潦倒新停浊酒杯。

liáo dǎo xīn tíng zhuó jiǔ bēi

broken down, I have just had to give up my wine.
