导读About this poem

A spring morning in the hills, painted in four six-character lines: peach blossoms wet with last night’s rain, willows in morning mist, petals nobody has swept, and a man still asleep while orioles sing.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是王维《田园乐》七首中的第六首，写山中春天早晨的景象，像一幅小画。前两句颜色鲜明：桃花红红的，还含着昨夜的雨珠；柳树绿绿的，又笼着清晨的烟雾。“红”和“绿”、“宿雨”和“朝烟”字字相对，这叫“对仗”。后两句写人：花瓣落了一地，家里的小童还没有扫；黄莺在叫，山中的主人却还在睡觉。花落而不扫，鸟叫而不醒，说明这里没有人来打扰，日子过得从容自在。诗里没有一个字说“安静”“快乐”，读者却能感受到那份悠闲。这首诗是少见的六言诗，每句六个字，节奏是两字一顿，读起来平稳舒缓。苏轼说王维“诗中有画”，这首就是很好的例子。

In English

This is the sixth of Wang Wei’s seven poems on the “Joys of Country Life”, and it reads like a small painting of a spring morning in the hills. The first two lines are all colour: peach blossoms, red and still holding last night’s raindrops; willows, green and wrapped in the morning mist. Red faces green, overnight rain faces morning mist — word matched to word, the parallel pattern called duìzhàng. The last two lines bring in people, sort of. Petals have fallen everywhere, but the house boy hasn’t swept them yet; the orioles are singing, but the man of the hills is still asleep. Nobody hurries here. Flowers fall and no one sweeps, birds sing and no one wakes, because there is nothing to do and no one to disturb him. The poem never uses the words “quiet” or “happy”, yet you feel both. It is also a rare six-character poem: each line falls into three two-character beats, giving it a calm, even pace. The Song poet Su Shi said that in Wang Wei’s poems “there are paintings”, and this one shows exactly what he meant.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) was a high official, painter and devout Buddhist who spent much of his later life at his country estate at Wangchuan, south of Chang’an. The “Joys of Country Life” series is thought to describe that quiet life in the hills.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

桃红复含宿雨，

táo hóng fù hán sù yǔ

Red peach blossoms still hold last night’s rain;

柳绿更带朝烟。

liǔ lǜ gèng dài zhāo yān

green willows wear the morning mist.

花落家童未扫，

huā luò jiā tóng wèi sǎo

Petals fall and the boy hasn’t swept them;

莺啼山客犹眠。

yīng tí shān kè yóu mián

orioles sing, and the hermit sleeps on.
