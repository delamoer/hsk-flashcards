长干行·其一

Chánggān Xíng · Qí Yī
Song of Changgan
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang
体裁Form
五言古诗（乐府）
难度Level
挑战 Advanced
主题Themes
爱情 Love

导读About this poem

A young merchant’s wife tells her own story — from playing with the boy next door, to a shy wedding at fourteen, to waiting for him at sixteen. It gave Chinese two idioms for childhood sweethearts: 青梅竹马 and 两小无猜.

第一段Part 1
01
妾发初覆额，折花门前剧。

直译LiteralWhen my hair first covered my forehead, I picked flowers and played before the gate.

意译MeaningWhen my hair was just long enough to cover my brow, I played at the gate, picking flowers.

02
郎骑竹马来，绕床弄青梅。

直译LiteralYou came riding a bamboo horse, circling the well rail, playing with green plums.

意译MeaningYou came galloping on a bamboo-stick horse, and we ran round the well tossing green plums.

03
同居长干里，两小无嫌猜。

直译LiteralWe both lived in Changgan lane, two little ones with no suspicion.

意译MeaningWe grew up side by side in Changgan, two children with nothing to hide.

第二段Part 2
04
十四为君妇，羞颜未尝开。

直译LiteralAt fourteen I became your wife; my shy face never once opened.

意译MeaningAt fourteen I became your wife, too shy to show you a smile.

05
低头向暗壁，千唤不一回。

直译LiteralHead lowered, I faced the dark wall; called a thousand times, I did not once turn.

意译MeaningI hung my head and faced the dark wall; you called a thousand times — I never turned round.

06
十五始展眉，愿同尘与灰。

直译LiteralAt fifteen I first unknit my brows, wishing to be together as dust and ash.

意译MeaningAt fifteen my face began to brighten; I wanted us together till we turned to dust.

07
常存抱柱信，岂上望夫台。

直译LiteralI always kept the faith of the man who held the pillar; how would I climb the Watching-for-Husband Terrace?

意译MeaningYou were as faithful as the man who held the bridge post — why would I ever need to climb a tower to watch for you?

第三段Part 3
08
十六君远行，瞿塘滟滪堆。

直译LiteralAt sixteen you travelled far, to Qutang and the Yanyu Rock.

意译MeaningAt sixteen you went far away, past Qutang Gorge and the Yanyu Rock.

09
五月不可触，猿声天上哀。

直译LiteralIn the fifth month it must not be touched; the gibbons’ cries are mournful in the sky.

意译MeaningIn early summer floods no boat dares touch it, and gibbons wail high up in the cliffs.

10
门前迟行迹，一一生绿苔。

直译LiteralBefore the gate, the footprints where you lingered — one by one they grow green moss.

意译MeaningThe footprints you left at our gate are growing green moss, one by one.

11
苔深不能扫，落叶秋风早。

直译LiteralThe moss is too deep to sweep; leaves fall, the autumn wind comes early.

意译MeaningThe moss is too thick to sweep away; leaves are falling — autumn wind has come early.

12
八月蝴蝶黄，双飞西园草。

直译LiteralIn the eighth month, yellow butterflies fly in pairs over the west garden’s grass.

意译MeaningYellow butterflies of the eighth month flutter in pairs over the garden grass.

13
感此伤妾心，坐愁红颜老。

直译LiteralMoved by this, my heart is hurt; I sit and grieve that my rosy face grows old.

意译MeaningThe sight of them breaks my heart; I sit and worry, and my young face grows old.

第四段Part 4
14
早晚下三巴，预将书报家。

直译LiteralWhenever you come down from the Three Ba, send a letter home beforehand.

意译MeaningWhenever you sail down from Sichuan, send a letter home ahead of you,

15
相迎不道远，直至长风沙。

直译LiteralTo meet you I won’t say it is far — straight to Long Wind Sands.

意译Meaningand I’ll come to meet you, never mind how far — all the way to Long Wind Sands.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗用一位商人妻子的口气，讲她从小到大的爱情故事，像一段独白。她小时候在门前折花玩，邻家男孩骑着“竹马”跑来，两人绕着井栏扔青梅玩——成语“青梅竹马”“两小无猜”就出自这里。十四岁出嫁，害羞得不敢抬头，叫她一千遍也不回头；十五岁才放开心，愿意和他永远在一起。十六岁丈夫出远门，要经过长江最危险的瞿塘峡，她开始了漫长的等待：门前他走时的脚印长满了青苔，秋天的蝴蝶成双成对地飞，更让她伤心，觉得自己的青春在一点点变老。最后她说：你回来时提前写信，我一直迎到几百里外的长风沙去。诗按年龄一段段写，细节真实，感情从天真、害羞到深情、焦急，层次分明。

In English

Li Bai lets a young merchant’s wife tell her love story in her own voice. As a little girl she picks flowers by her gate; the boy next door gallops up on a bamboo-stick horse, and they chase each other round the well, playing with green plums. Two idioms for childhood sweethearts come straight from these lines: 青梅竹马 “green plums and a bamboo horse” and 两小无猜 “two little ones with no suspicion”. At fourteen she marries him and is so shy she faces the wall and will not turn round, though he calls her a thousand times. At fifteen she finally relaxes and wants to stay with him “till we are dust and ash”. At sixteen he leaves on business through Qutang Gorge, the most dangerous stretch of the Yangtze, and the waiting begins. Moss grows over the footprints he left at the gate; pairs of autumn butterflies make her feel her youth slipping away. She ends with a practical, loving request: write before you come down the river, and I’ll come all the way to Long Wind Sands to meet you. The poem moves through her life year by year, and her feelings grow with her — playful, shy, devoted, anxious.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) is thought to have written this while travelling along the Yangtze in his twenties. Changgan was a lane in Jinling (today’s Nanjing) where many river merchants lived; “Song of Changgan” was an old folk-song title.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

妾 qiè；剧 jù（游戏）；骑 qí；嫌猜 xián cāi；为君妇 wéi；瞿塘 qú táng；滟滪堆 yàn yù duī；三巴 sān bā；预将 jiāng；长风沙 cháng fēng shā。“迟行迹”的“迟”读 chí（等待；一作“旧”）。“千唤不一回”的“一”按本调标 yī。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本（《唐诗三百首》）。全诗三十句，网站按两句一行排为十五行。异文：“八月蝴蝶黄”《全唐诗》作“八月胡蝶来”（一作“黄”）；“门前迟行迹”一作“门前旧行迹”；“绕床”一作“遶床”（异体字）。题下原有二首，此为其一。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

妾发初覆额，折花门前剧。

qiè fà chū fù é, zhé huā mén qián jù

When my hair was just long enough to cover my brow, I played at the gate, picking flowers.

郎骑竹马来，绕床弄青梅。

láng qí zhú mǎ lái, rào chuáng nòng qīng méi

You came galloping on a bamboo-stick horse, and we ran round the well tossing green plums.

同居长干里，两小无嫌猜。

tóng jū cháng gān lǐ, liǎng xiǎo wú xián cāi

We grew up side by side in Changgan, two children with nothing to hide.

十四为君妇，羞颜未尝开。

shí sì wéi jūn fù, xiū yán wèi cháng kāi

At fourteen I became your wife, too shy to show you a smile.

低头向暗壁，千唤不一回。

dī tóu xiàng àn bì, qiān huàn bù yī huí

I hung my head and faced the dark wall; you called a thousand times — I never turned round.

十五始展眉，愿同尘与灰。

shí wǔ shǐ zhǎn méi, yuàn tóng chén yǔ huī

At fifteen my face began to brighten; I wanted us together till we turned to dust.

常存抱柱信，岂上望夫台。

cháng cún bào zhù xìn, qǐ shàng wàng fū tái

You were as faithful as the man who held the bridge post — why would I ever need to climb a tower to watch for you?

十六君远行，瞿塘滟滪堆。

shí liù jūn yuǎn xíng, qú táng yàn yù duī

At sixteen you went far away, past Qutang Gorge and the Yanyu Rock.

五月不可触，猿声天上哀。

wǔ yuè bù kě chù, yuán shēng tiān shàng āi

In early summer floods no boat dares touch it, and gibbons wail high up in the cliffs.

门前迟行迹，一一生绿苔。

mén qián chí xíng jì, yī yī shēng lǜ tái

The footprints you left at our gate are growing green moss, one by one.

苔深不能扫，落叶秋风早。

tái shēn bù néng sǎo, luò yè qiū fēng zǎo

The moss is too thick to sweep away; leaves are falling — autumn wind has come early.

八月蝴蝶黄，双飞西园草。

bā yuè hú dié huáng, shuāng fēi xī yuán cǎo

Yellow butterflies of the eighth month flutter in pairs over the garden grass.

感此伤妾心，坐愁红颜老。

gǎn cǐ shāng qiè xīn, zuò chóu hóng yán lǎo

The sight of them breaks my heart; I sit and worry, and my young face grows old.

早晚下三巴，预将书报家。

zǎo wǎn xià sān bā, yù jiāng shū bào jiā

Whenever you sail down from Sichuan, send a letter home ahead of you,

相迎不道远，直至长风沙。

xiāng yíng bù dào yuǎn, zhí zhì cháng fēng shā

and I’ll come to meet you, never mind how far — all the way to Long Wind Sands.
