导读About this poem

A merchant’s wife complains, half joking and half in tears: the tide at least comes back on time — she should have married a boy who rides the waves.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗用一个商人妻子的口吻说话。她嫁给了在长江瞿塘峡一带做生意的商人，丈夫总是说好哪天回来，却一天又一天地失约。后两句是一句“气话”：早知道潮水涨落那么守时，还不如嫁给在潮水里玩耍的弄潮儿！潮水“有信”（按时来），丈夫却“无信”，一个巧妙的对比，把等待的委屈、埋怨和思念都说出来了。这种话说得天真、直率，像民歌一样，所以特别动人。诗人是男性，却把女子的心思写得很真实，这类诗叫“闺怨诗”，是唐诗里常见的题材。

In English

The poem speaks in the voice of a merchant’s wife. Her husband trades along the Qutang Gorge on the Yangtze, and day after day he misses the date he promised to come home. The last two lines are what she blurts out in frustration: “If I had known the tide keeps its word, I’d have married a boy who plays in the tide!” The wordplay is the heart of the poem. 信 means both “on time, regular” and “keeping one’s word”: the tide comes back faithfully, her husband doesn’t. In one clever comparison she says everything — hurt, complaint and longing all at once. Of course she doesn’t really mean it; it is the kind of thing people say when they miss someone. The tone is naive and direct, like a folk song, and that is what makes it so touching. Many Tang poems were written by men in a waiting woman’s voice; this kind of poem is called a “boudoir lament” (闺怨诗).

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Yi (746–829) was a Tang poet famous above all for his frontier quatrains, which were set to music in his own lifetime. “Southern Song” was an old folk-song title, and he wrote this in the plain style of southern love songs.

嫁得瞿塘贾，

jià dé qú táng gǔ

I married a trader on the Qutang Gorge,

and every day he breaks the day he promised me.

jià yǔ nòng cháo ér
