导读About this poem

Fourteen years after a poem about peach trees got him exiled, Liu Yuxi comes back to the same temple: the peach trees are gone, the men who planted them are gone — and he is still here.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗背后有个故事。815年，刘禹锡被召回长安，游玄都观时写了一首看桃花的诗，说观里上千棵桃树“尽是刘郎去后栽”——暗讽朝中那些新得势的人都是他被贬后才爬上去的。当权者很生气，他又被贬到远方。十四年后他终于回来，再游玄都观，写下这首。前两句写眼前：宽大的庭院一半长满青苔，桃花一棵不剩，只有菜花在开，一片冷落。后两句问：当年种桃的道士去哪儿了？我这个“前度刘郎”今天又来了！“道士”和“桃花”暗指当年的权贵，他们已经倒台；而诗人还在，语气里带着骄傲和不服输。这就是成语“前度刘郎”的来历。

In English

There is a story behind this poem. In 815 Liu Yuxi was recalled to Chang’an after ten years of exile. Visiting Xuandu Temple, he wrote a poem about its thousand peach trees, saying they had “all been planted after Master Liu left” — a sly dig at the officials who had risen to power while he was away. They were not amused, and he was sent away again. Fourteen years later he finally returned, went back to the same temple, and wrote this. The first two lines show a temple gone to seed: half the great courtyard is moss, the peach blossoms have vanished, and only humble vegetable flowers bloom. Then come two questions with a grin: Where has the Daoist who planted the peaches gone? Master Liu, who was here before, has come back again! The Daoist and his peach trees stand for those powerful men, who have since lost their positions. The poet has outlasted them all, and he says so with pride and defiance. The phrase 前度刘郎 (“Master Liu from last time”) became an idiom for someone returning to a place after many years.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Yuxi (772–842) was a Tang poet and official who spent more than twenty years in exile after the failed reform of 805. He wrote this in 828, on returning to Chang’an; his original title adds a preface telling the whole story.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

百亩庭中半是苔，

bǎi mǔ tíng zhōng bàn shì tái

Half of the great courtyard has gone to moss;

桃花净尽菜花开。

táo huā jìng jìn cài huā kāi

the peach trees are gone — only cabbage flowers bloom.

种桃道士归何处？

zhòng táo dào shì guī hé chù

And the Daoist who planted the peaches — where is he now?

前度刘郎今又来。

qián dù liú láng jīn yòu lái

Master Liu, who was here before, is back again.
