浪淘沙（把酒祝东风）

Làng Táo Shā
Waves Washing Sand (I Raise My Cup to the East Wind)
欧阳修Ōuyáng Xiū · Ouyang Xiu宋 Song

导读About this poem

Walking with a friend among the flowers of Luoyang, the poet notices that this year’s blossoms are redder than last year’s — and wonders who he will be looking at next year’s with.

直译LiteralHolding wine, I toast the east wind: let us for now be unhurried together.

直译LiteralDrooping willows, the purple road, east of Luoyang city.

意译MeaningWillows hang over the broad road east of Luoyang.

直译LiteralAll are places where we held hands back then, roaming through every fragrant grove.

意译MeaningEvery step is where we once walked hand in hand, through grove after flowering grove.

直译LiteralMeeting and parting, painfully hurried; this regret has no end.

意译MeaningWe meet and part in such a hurry — and the ache of it never ends.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写和朋友在洛阳城东重游赏花。开头很有意思：词人举起酒杯向东风（春风）敬酒，请它慢一点，让大家从从容容地多玩一会儿。接着写地点：垂柳拂动的大道，正是去年两人手拉手游遍花丛的地方。下阕直接说出心里的遗憾：朋友相聚太难，分别太快，这种惆怅没有尽头。最后三句是全词最动人的地方：今年的花比去年开得更红，明年的花一定更好——可是明年还能和谁一起看呢？花一年比一年好，人却不一定还在一起，用“花”的越来越美来反衬“人”的聚散无常，这种写法叫“反衬”。语言平白，感情却很深。

In English

This lyric records a walk with a friend through the flowers east of Luoyang. It begins with a charming gesture: the poet raises his cup to the east wind — the spring wind — and asks it to slow down so they can enjoy the day without hurrying. Then he names the place: the willow-lined road where, a year ago, the two of them walked hand in hand through every flowering grove. The second stanza says plainly what hurts: friends meet so rarely and part so quickly, and the regret never ends. The last lines are the heart of the poem. This year the flowers are redder than last year; next year they will be lovelier still — but who will be there to see them with him? The flowers keep getting better while the people may scatter. Setting the beauty of the flowers against the uncertainty of human meetings makes the sadness sharper, though every word is simple.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Ouyang Xiu (1007–1072) was a leading statesman, historian and essayist of the Northern Song. This lyric is thought to date from around 1032, when he was a young official in Luoyang; the friend is traditionally said to be the poet Mei Yaochen.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

把酒祝东风，且共从容。

bǎ jiǔ zhù dōng fēng, qiě gòng cóng róng

I lift my cup to the east wind: stay a while, and let us take our time.

垂杨紫陌洛城东。

chuí yáng zǐ mò luò chéng dōng

Willows hang over the broad road east of Luoyang.

总是当时携手处，游遍芳丛。

zǒng shì dāng shí xié shǒu chù, yóu biàn fāng cóng

Every step is where we once walked hand in hand, through grove after flowering grove.

聚散苦匆匆，此恨无穷。

jù sàn kǔ cōng cōng, cǐ hèn wú qióng

We meet and part in such a hurry — and the ache of it never ends.

今年花胜去年红。

jīn nián huā shèng qù nián hóng

This year the flowers are redder than last year.

可惜明年花更好，知与谁同？

kě xī míng nián huā gèng hǎo, zhī yǔ shuí tóng

Next year they will be finer still — and who will I be seeing them with?
