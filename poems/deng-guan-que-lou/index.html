导读About this poem

From a tall tower the poet watches the sun sink behind the mountains and the Yellow River run to the sea — then says: to see a thousand miles, climb one more floor. That last line is now an everyday Chinese saying about self-improvement.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

鹳雀楼在今天山西永济，靠着黄河。诗人登上楼，前两句写看到的大景色：太阳靠着远山慢慢落下去，黄河一路奔流，流向大海。其实站在楼上看不到大海，“入海流”是诗人心里的想象，所以画面一下子变得特别宽广。后两句从写景变成说道理：想看到千里以外，就要再上一层楼。这里用了“对仗”：前两句“白日”对“黄河”，“依山尽”对“入海流”；后两句也是一一相对。“更上一层楼”既是真的再爬一层，也是比喻：想看得更远、做得更好，就要不断努力。今天这句话已经是常用语，比如“祝你的中文更上一层楼”。

In English

Stork Tower stood in today’s Yongji, Shanxi province, beside the Yellow River. The poet climbs it, and the first two lines give the huge view: the sun sinks slowly against the distant mountains, and the Yellow River rushes on toward the sea. In fact you could never see the sea from there — the poet imagines it, and that is what makes the picture feel so vast. Then the poem moves from scenery to an idea: if you want to see a thousand li (about 500 km), go up one more floor. Both halves use duìzhàng, parallel lines that match word for word: “white sun” against “Yellow River”, “leaning on the mountains, gone” against “entering the sea, flowing”. The last line works on two levels: literally, climb higher; figuratively, keep pushing yourself to see further and do better. Today 更上一层楼 is a common phrase — you might hear a teacher say 祝你的中文更上一层楼, “may your Chinese reach the next level”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Zhihuan (688–742) was famous in his day, but only six of his poems survive. The tower stood on the banks of the Yellow River in Puzhou (today’s Yongji, Shanxi); it was destroyed long ago and rebuilt in 2002.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

白日依山尽，

bái rì yī shān jìn

The pale sun sinks against the hills,

黄河入海流。

huáng hé rù hǎi liú

the Yellow River runs on to the sea.

欲穷千里目，

yù qióng qiān lǐ mù

If you would see a thousand miles,

更上一层楼。

gèng shàng yī céng lóu

climb one floor higher.
