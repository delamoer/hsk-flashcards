导读About this poem

At the river where the great warlord Xiang Yu took his own life rather than retreat, Du Mu argues with history: a real hero swallows his shame and tries again. The poem gave Chinese the idiom 卷土重来, “to come back with a whirl of dust”.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

公元前202年，楚霸王项羽被刘邦打败，逃到乌江边。乌江亭长劝他渡江回江东（今天的江浙一带）重新开始，他却说当年带八千江东子弟出来，如今没有一人回去，没脸再见江东父老，于是自杀。一千年后，杜牧经过乌江亭，写下这首诗，对这件事发表不同的看法：打仗胜败是兵家常事，谁也料不到；能忍受羞辱、不怕失败，才是真正的男子汉。江东人才那么多，如果他回去，说不定还能“卷土重来”——重新带着人马杀回来。古人写历史人物多是感叹或赞美，杜牧却提出“如果当时……”的假设，这种写法叫“翻案”，意思是推翻常见的看法。“卷土重来”后来成了常用成语，指失败后重新恢复力量。

In English

In 202 BC Xiang Yu, the mighty “Hegemon-King of Chu”, was crushed by Liu Bang, the future founder of the Han dynasty, and fled to the Wujiang River. The local official offered him a boat home to Jiangdong, the lands east of the Yangtze where he had first raised his army. Xiang Yu refused. He had led eight thousand young men of Jiangdong into war, and not one was coming back with him; how could he face their fathers? He took his own life on the riverbank. A thousand years later Du Mu passed the pavilion there and disagreed. Winning and losing, he says, are part of every soldier’s life — no one can predict them. Swallowing shame and enduring defeat is what makes a real man. Jiangdong had plenty of talented young men; had Xiang Yu gone home, who knows whether he might have come back again, raising a whirl of dust. Most poets wrote about historical heroes to praise or mourn them; Du Mu instead asks “what if?” and overturns the usual verdict. The phrase 卷土重来, “to roll up the dust and come again”, became a common idiom for making a comeback after failure.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–c. 852) was a late-Tang official and poet, famous for short, sharp poems about history. He is said to have written this when he passed the Wujiang pavilion (in today’s Anhui) while travelling as an official in the south.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

胜败兵家事不期，

shèng bài bīng jiā shì bù qī

No general can know in advance if he will win or lose;

包羞忍耻是男儿。

bāo xiū rěn chǐ shì nán ér

the one who can swallow his shame is the real man.

江东子弟多才俊，

jiāng dōng zǐ dì duō cái jùn

East of the river there were still plenty of brave young men —

卷土重来未可知。

juǎn tǔ chóng lái wèi kě zhī

who can say he would not have stormed back in a cloud of dust?
