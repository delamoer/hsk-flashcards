导读About this poem

A dazzling Lantern Festival night: fireworks, carriages, music, crowds. The poet searches the crowd a thousand times for one person — then turns his head, and there she is, where the lights are dimmest.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写南宋都城临安元宵夜的热闹。上阕全是热闹的画面：春风好像一夜之间吹开了千树花——其实是满城花灯；又吹落了满天“星雨”——是焰火；华丽的马车走过，一路飘香；箫声响起，月光流转，鱼灯、龙灯舞了一整夜。下阕写看灯的女子们，头上戴着漂亮的饰品，说说笑笑地走过去，只留下一阵香气。可是“我”要找的不是她们。在人群里找了千百遍，忽然一回头，那个人却站在灯光稀少、冷清的地方。前面写得越热闹，最后这个人就越显得与众不同，这叫“以闹衬静”的对比手法。很多人认为“那人”也是辛弃疾自己的影子：他想收复北方却不被重用，宁愿孤独也不随大流。王国维说，成大事业、大学问的人都要经过三种境界，第三种就是“众里寻他千百度，蓦然回首，那人却在，灯火阑珊处”。

In English

This lyric captures the Lantern Festival in Lin’an (today’s Hangzhou), capital of the Southern Song. The first half is pure spectacle. The east wind seems to blow a thousand trees into blossom overnight — really, the lanterns all over the city — and to shake down a shower of stars, which are fireworks. Carved carriages pass, leaving perfume on the road; flutes play, the moonlight turns, and fish and dragon lanterns dance all night. The second half turns to the crowd: young women in glittering hair ornaments go by laughing, leaving only a trace of fragrance. But none of them is the one he is looking for. He searches the crowd a hundred, a thousand times — then suddenly turns his head, and there she stands, where the lantern light is thin and fading. The louder and brighter the first half, the more striking this quiet figure becomes. Many readers see her as a self-portrait of Xin Qiji, a patriot who longed to win back the north but was kept on the sidelines, preferring to stand apart rather than join the crowd. The scholar Wang Guowei famously used these last lines to describe the final stage of any great search: after endless effort, the answer appears when you least expect it.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xin Qiji (1140–1207) grew up in the Jin-occupied north, led an uprising against the Jin as a young man and fled south to serve the Song, but was rarely allowed to fight again. This lyric is thought to date from his years in Lin’an, around the 1170s.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

词牌“青玉案”的“案”：通行读 àn；因典出张衡《四愁诗》“何以报之青玉案”，案指盛食物的托盘，也有人主张读 wǎn（同“碗”），此处按通行读法采用 àn。更吹落 gèng。凤箫 xiāo。蛾儿 é ér（指头饰，不是虫子）。黄金缕 lǚ。蓦然 mò rán（不读 mù）。阑珊 lán shān。众里寻“他”：统编教材作“他”（古代“他”不分男女），一作“她”的为现代改写。

版本说明 About the text

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

东风夜放花千树，更吹落、星如雨。

dōng fēng yè fàng huā qiān shù, gèng chuī luò, xīng rú yǔ

Overnight the spring wind sets a thousand trees in bloom, and shakes down stars like rain.

宝马雕车香满路。

bǎo mǎ diāo chē xiāng mǎn lù

Fine horses and carved carriages leave perfume all along the road.

凤箫声动，玉壶光转，一夜鱼龙舞。

fèng xiāo shēng dòng, yù hú guāng zhuǎn, yī yè yú lóng wǔ

Flutes sing out, the moon’s jade light wheels across the sky, and fish and dragon lanterns dance all night.

蛾儿雪柳黄金缕，笑语盈盈暗香去。

é ér xuě liǔ huáng jīn lǚ, xiào yǔ yíng yíng àn xiāng qù

Girls in silk moths, snowy willow sprays and golden threads drift past laughing, leaving a hint of perfume.

众里寻他千百度，

zhòng lǐ xún tā qiān bǎi dù

A thousand times I searched the crowd for her —

蓦然回首，那人却在，灯火阑珊处。

mò rán huí shǒu, nà rén què zài, dēng huǒ lán shān chù

then, turning by chance, I find her there, where the lantern light grows faint.
