君子于役

Jūn Zǐ Yú Yì
My Husband Is Away on Service
佚名（《诗经·王风》）Anonymous (Book of Songs, Airs of Wang)先秦 Pre-Qin

导读About this poem

At dusk the hens go back to their roost and the cattle come down the hill — everything comes home except her husband, sent far away on the king’s service. One of the earliest and gentlest poems of a wife missing her man.

第一章Stanza 1
01
君子于役，不知其期，曷至哉？

直译LiteralMy husband is away on service; I do not know for how long. When will he arrive?

意译MeaningMy man is off on the king’s service, and no one can tell me for how long. When will he be home?

02
鸡栖于埘，日之夕矣，羊牛下来。

直译LiteralThe chickens roost in their wall-nests; the day has reached dusk; the sheep and cattle come down.

意译MeaningThe hens settle into their nests in the wall, the day is fading, the sheep and cattle come down the hill.

03
君子于役，如之何勿思！

直译LiteralMy husband is away on service — how can I not think of him!

意译MeaningMy man is far away — how could I not miss him?

第二章Stanza 2
04
君子于役，不日不月，曷其有佸？

直译LiteralMy husband is away on service, not for days, not for months. When will we meet again?

意译MeaningMy man is off on service, too long to count in days or months. When will we be together again?

05
鸡栖于桀，日之夕矣，羊牛下括。

直译LiteralThe chickens roost on their perches; the day has reached dusk; the sheep and cattle come down and gather.

意译MeaningThe hens hop onto their perches, the day is fading, the sheep and cattle come down and crowd into the pen.

06
君子于役，苟无饥渴！

直译LiteralMy husband is away on service — may he at least not be hungry or thirsty!

意译MeaningMy man is far away — I only hope he is not hungry, not thirsty.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是一位农家妻子的歌。丈夫被派去服役（打仗或修工事），不知道什么时候能回来。她没有写自己怎么哭，只写了黄昏的一个画面：鸡回到墙洞里的窝，太阳落下去，牛羊从山坡上走下来。天黑了，连鸡和牛羊都回家了，只有丈夫还在远方——这种用眼前景物衬托心情的写法，叫“借景抒情”，后来“日暮怀人”成了中国诗歌里常见的主题。两章几乎一样，这是民歌“重章叠句”的特点，但结尾变了：第一章说“怎么能不想他”，第二章说“只求他别挨饿受渴”。从盼他回来，到只盼他平安，期望越来越小，感情反而越来越深，非常动人。

In English

This is a farm wife’s song. Her husband has been sent away on state service — fighting or building — and no one knows when he will return. She never tells us she is crying. Instead she paints one scene at dusk: the hens settle into their nests in the wall, the sun goes down, and the sheep and cattle come down the hillside. As night falls, even the animals are home; only he is still out there. Letting a scene carry the feeling like this is called “expressing feeling through scenery” (借景抒情), and “thinking of a loved one at sunset” became a lasting theme in Chinese poetry. The two stanzas are almost the same — repetition is typical of folk songs — but the endings change. First she says, “How can I not miss him?” Then, after time has passed beyond counting, she says only, “I just hope he isn’t hungry or thirsty.” Her hopes have shrunk from his return to his mere survival, and that small wish says more about her love than any cry of grief could.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one comes from the “Airs of Wang” (王风), songs from the region around the Eastern Zhou capital near present-day Luoyang, a time when ordinary men were often sent away on long military or labour service.

君子于役，不知其期，曷至哉？

jūn zǐ yú yì, bù zhī qí qī, hé zhì zāi

My man is off on the king’s service, and no one can tell me for how long. When will he be home?

鸡栖于埘，日之夕矣，羊牛下来。

jī qī yú shí, rì zhī xī yǐ, yáng niú xià lái

The hens settle into their nests in the wall, the day is fading, the sheep and cattle come down the hill.

君子于役，如之何勿思！

jūn zǐ yú yì, rú zhī hé wù sī

My man is far away — how could I not miss him?

君子于役，不日不月，曷其有佸？

jūn zǐ yú yì, bù rì bù yuè, hé qí yǒu huó

My man is off on service, too long to count in days or months. When will we be together again?

鸡栖于桀，日之夕矣，羊牛下括。

jī qī yú jié, rì zhī xī yǐ, yáng niú xià kuò

The hens hop onto their perches, the day is fading, the sheep and cattle come down and crowd into the pen.

君子于役，苟无饥渴！

jūn zǐ yú yì, gǒu wú jī kě

My man is far away — I only hope he is not hungry, not thirsty.
