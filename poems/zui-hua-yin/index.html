导读About this poem

The Double Ninth Festival, and her husband is far away. A young wife spends a long grey day alone, drinks among the chrysanthemums at dusk — and ends with one of the most famous lines in Chinese: “I am thinner than the yellow flowers.”

上阕First stanza
01
薄雾浓云愁永昼，瑞脑销金兽。

直译LiteralThin mist, thick clouds, grieving through the endless day; the camphor incense burns away in the golden beast.

意译MeaningThin mist and heavy cloud — a long, sad day. In the bronze beast the incense slowly burns away.

02
佳节又重阳，玉枕纱厨，半夜凉初透。

直译LiteralThe fine festival, Double Ninth again; jade pillow, gauze bed-curtain — at midnight the cold first seeps through.

意译MeaningDouble Ninth has come round again. On my jade pillow, behind gauze curtains, the midnight chill begins to creep in.

下阕Second stanza
03
东篱把酒黄昏后，有暗香盈袖。

直译LiteralBy the eastern hedge, holding wine after dusk; there is a faint fragrance filling my sleeves.

意译MeaningAt dusk I drink by the eastern hedge, and a faint scent of flowers fills my sleeves.

04
莫道不销魂，帘卷西风，人比黄花瘦。

直译LiteralDon’t say it does not melt the soul: the curtain rolls up in the west wind — the person is thinner than the yellow flowers.

意译MeaningDon’t tell me this doesn’t break a heart. When the west wind lifts the curtain, the woman inside is thinner than the chrysanthemums.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是李清照年轻时写的词，丈夫赵明诚出门在外，正赶上重阳节。上阕写白天和夜里：雾薄云浓，白天长得让人发愁，香炉里的香慢慢烧完；本该团聚的节日，她一个人躺在纱帐里，半夜凉意透进来。下阕写黄昏：她学陶渊明在“东篱”下赏菊饮酒，衣袖里沾满菊花的清香，却没人可以分享。最后三句是全词的高潮：别说这样不让人伤心——西风卷起帘子，帘里的人比外面的菊花还要瘦。“瘦”字一语双关：菊花在秋风里细瘦，人因为思念也消瘦了。用花比人，含蓄又生动。相传赵明诚读后不服，闭门写了五十首词，把这首混在里面请朋友看，朋友说只有这三句最好。

In English

Li Qingzhao wrote this as a young wife, when her husband Zhao Mingcheng was away from home on the Double Ninth Festival (重阳, the ninth day of the ninth lunar month), a day for being with family, climbing hills and enjoying chrysanthemums. The first half is the day and the night: thin mist, heavy clouds, a day that drags on; incense slowly burning down in a bronze burner shaped like a beast. Then night, alone on a jade pillow behind gauze curtains, and the first chill of autumn seeping in. The second half is dusk. She drinks by the “eastern hedge” — a nod to the poet Tao Yuanming, who picked chrysanthemums there — and the flowers’ scent fills her sleeves. But there is no one to share it with. The ending is the famous part: don’t say this doesn’t break the heart — when the west wind lifts the curtain, the woman inside is thinner than the yellow flowers. 瘦 (“thin”) works both ways: the chrysanthemums are slender in the autumn wind, and she has grown thin from missing him. A traditional story says her husband, a little jealous, wrote fifty lyrics of his own and slipped hers among them; a friend read them all and picked out just these last three lines.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Qingzhao (1084–c. 1155) is China’s most celebrated woman poet. This lyric is traditionally dated to the early years of her marriage, when her husband was serving away from home; she is said to have sent it to him.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

薄雾浓云愁永昼，瑞脑销金兽。

bó wù nóng yún chóu yǒng zhòu, ruì nǎo xiāo jīn shòu

Thin mist and heavy cloud — a long, sad day. In the bronze beast the incense slowly burns away.

佳节又重阳，玉枕纱厨，半夜凉初透。

jiā jié yòu chóng yáng, yù zhěn shā chú, bàn yè liáng chū tòu

Double Ninth has come round again. On my jade pillow, behind gauze curtains, the midnight chill begins to creep in.

东篱把酒黄昏后，有暗香盈袖。

dōng lí bǎ jiǔ huáng hūn hòu, yǒu àn xiāng yíng xiù

At dusk I drink by the eastern hedge, and a faint scent of flowers fills my sleeves.

莫道不销魂，帘卷西风，人比黄花瘦。

mò dào bù xiāo hún, lián juǎn xī fēng, rén bǐ huáng huā shòu

Don’t tell me this doesn’t break a heart. When the west wind lifts the curtain, the woman inside is thinner than the chrysanthemums.
