小池

导读About this poem

A tiny pond in early summer: a quiet spring, tree shade on the water, a lotus bud just poking out — and a dragonfly already standing on its tip. Like a small photo, full of fresh life.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写初夏一个小水池的风景，像一张小小的照片。第一句写泉眼没有声音，好像舍不得让细细的水流走；第二句写树的影子映在水里，好像喜欢这晴天柔和的光。诗人把泉眼和树都当成人来写，会“惜”，会“爱”，这叫“拟人”。后两句最有名：小荷叶刚刚露出一个尖尖的角，就已经有一只蜻蜓站在上面了。一个“才”，一个“早”，写出了大自然里刚刚开始的、新鲜的生命，也写出了诗人观察得多么细心。今天人们常用“小荷才露尖尖角”比喻刚刚崭露头角的年轻人。

In English

This short poem is a snapshot of a small pond in early summer. In the first line the spring that feeds the pond makes no sound, as if it were reluctant to let even a thin trickle go. In the second, trees cast their shade on the water, as if they loved the soft, clear weather. The poet treats the spring and the trees like people who can “cherish” and “love” — personification, nǐrén in Chinese. The last two lines are the famous ones: a little lotus leaf has only just shown its sharp, rolled-up tip above the water, and a dragonfly is already standing on top of it. The words 才 (“only just”) and 早 (“already”) work together to catch a split second of new life. Nothing big happens; the poem’s charm is in how closely Yang Wanli looks. Today people say 小荷才露尖尖角 about a young talent who is just beginning to show promise.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yang Wanli (1127–1206) was a Southern Song official and one of the major poets of his time, famous for a lively, natural style drawn from everyday scenes — later called the “Chengzhai style” after his pen name.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

才露 lù（不读 lòu）。尖尖角 jiǎo。树阴 yīn（教材作“树阴”，今也写“树荫”）。无争议读音。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

泉眼无声惜细流，

quán yǎn wú shēng xī xì liú

The spring runs silent, sparing its slender stream;

树阴照水爱晴柔。

shù yīn zhào shuǐ ài qíng róu

tree shade lies on the water, loving the gentle sunny day.

小荷才露尖尖角，

xiǎo hé cái lù jiān jiān jiǎo

A young lotus leaf has barely shown its pointed tip —

早有蜻蜓立上头。

zǎo yǒu qīng tíng lì shàng tóu

and a dragonfly already stands on top.
