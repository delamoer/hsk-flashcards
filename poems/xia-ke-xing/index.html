Xiákè Xíng
Song of the Knight-Errant

导读About this poem

A Tang poet’s version of a martial-arts hero: a swordsman who strikes like lightning, walks away without leaving his name, and keeps his word above everything. Li Bai clearly wished he were one.

第一段Part 1
01
赵客缦胡缨，吴钩霜雪明。

直译LiteralThe man of Zhao wears a plain barbarian tassel; his Wu hook-blade is bright as frost and snow.

意译MeaningA swordsman from Zhao, rough tassels on his cap, his curved Wu blade bright as frost on snow.

02
银鞍照白马，飒沓如流星。

直译LiteralA silver saddle shines on a white horse; swift and sweeping like a shooting star.

意译MeaningHis silver saddle gleams on a white horse; he streaks by like a falling star.

03
十步杀一人，千里不留行。

直译LiteralIn ten steps he kills one man; over a thousand li nothing halts his going.

意译MeaningTen paces — and a man lies dead; a thousand miles, and no one can hold him back.

04
事了拂衣去，深藏身与名。

直译LiteralThe deed done, he brushes his robe and leaves, deeply hiding his person and name.

意译MeaningHis work done, he shakes out his sleeves and goes, leaving neither face nor name behind.

第二段Part 2
05
闲过信陵饮，脱剑膝前横。

直译LiteralAt leisure he visits Xinling to drink, takes off his sword and lays it across his knees.

意译MeaningIdle, he drops by Lord Xinling’s for a drink, unbuckles his sword and lays it across his knees.

06
将炙啖朱亥，持觞劝侯嬴。

直译LiteralTaking roast meat he feeds Zhu Hai; holding a cup he toasts Hou Ying.

意译MeaningThe lord hands roast meat to Zhu Hai, and raises his cup to Hou Ying.

07
三杯吐然诺，五岳倒为轻。

直译LiteralAfter three cups they speak their promise; the Five Peaks become light by comparison.

意译MeaningThree cups, and they give their word — a promise that makes the Five Mountains feel light.

08
眼花耳热后，意气素霓生。

直译LiteralAfter eyes blur and ears grow hot, their spirit rises like a white rainbow.

意译MeaningEyes dazzled, ears burning with wine, their courage soars like a white rainbow across the sky.

09
救赵挥金槌，邯郸先震惊。

直译LiteralTo save Zhao he swung the metal mace; Handan was the first to be shaken.

意译MeaningTo save Zhao, Zhu Hai swung his iron mace, and Handan was the first to tremble.

10
千秋二壮士，烜赫大梁城。

直译LiteralFor a thousand autumns, two brave men, glorious in the city of Daliang.

意译MeaningA thousand years on, those two brave men still blaze in the city of Daliang.

第三段Part 3
11
纵死侠骨香，不惭世上英。

直译LiteralEven if they die, a hero’s bones are fragrant; no shame before the world’s finest.

意译MeaningThough they die, a hero’s bones stay fragrant; they need not bow to the great men of the world.

12
谁能书阁下，白首太玄经。

直译LiteralWho could, below the book tower, grow white-haired over the Classic of Supreme Mystery?

意译MeaningWho would sit in a library till his hair turns white, writing the Classic of Supreme Mystery?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“侠客”是古代行侠仗义、重义轻生的武士。这首诗前八句写侠客的形象：赵地的侠客帽缨粗犷，吴钩宝刀像霜雪一样亮，银鞍白马飞驰如流星；十步之内就能杀一人，千里之行没人拦得住；事情办完拂拂衣服就走，不留名字——这几句非常有名，金庸的武侠小说《侠客行》就用了这首诗。中间十二句讲战国时的故事：信陵君门下的侯嬴和朱亥，喝酒时许下诺言，把诺言看得比五岳还重；后来朱亥用铁锤杀了魏将晋鄙，帮信陵君救了赵国。最后四句是诗人的议论：侠客即使死了，侠骨也留香，比那些在书阁里写书到白头的人强多了。诗写得快、猛、有气势，表达了李白对建功立业、重诺守信的向往。

In English

A xiákè is a wandering swordsman who rights wrongs and values loyalty above his own life — China’s knight-errant. The first eight lines draw his portrait: a man from Zhao in a rough-tasselled cap, his curved Wu blade gleaming like frost and snow, silver saddle on a white horse, flashing past like a shooting star. Within ten paces he kills a man; across a thousand miles no one can stop him. When the deed is done he brushes off his sleeves and leaves, hiding his name and himself. These lines are so famous that Jin Yong borrowed the poem for his martial-arts novel “Ode to Gallantry”. The middle section retells a Warring States story: Lord Xinling of Wei wins over two humble men, the gatekeeper Hou Ying and the butcher Zhu Hai, and over wine they pledge themselves to him — a promise that makes the Five Great Mountains seem light. Later Zhu Hai kills a general with an iron mace so Wei can rescue the state of Zhao. The poem ends with Li Bai’s own verdict: even if a hero dies, his bones stay fragrant; who would rather grow white-haired copying books in a library? The pace is fast and fierce, and behind it is Li Bai’s longing to do something great and to be a man whose word is worth a mountain.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) practised swordsmanship as a young man and admired the old knights-errant. This yuefu poem is usually dated to around 744, after he left the court; the closing jab at Yang Xiong, who wrote the “Classic of Supreme Mystery” in a palace library, mocks the scholar’s life.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

缦 màn；缨 yīng；飒沓 sà tà；事了 liǎo（完结，不读 le）；炙 zhì；啖 dàn；朱亥 hài；觞 shāng；侯嬴 yíng；五岳倒为轻 dào wéi（倒：反而；也有理解为“倾倒”读 dǎo 的，）；烜赫 xuǎn hè；邯郸 hán dān；谁 shuí。机器朗读“事了”易读成 le、“烜”易读错，请人工听验；“烜”已在朗读用字中替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本（《李太白全集》）。全诗二十四句，网站按两句一行排为十二行。异文：“谁能书阁下”《全唐诗》作“书閤下”（閤同阁）；“救赵挥金槌”一作“挥金锤”。“劒”“慙”等异体字统一写作“剑”“惭”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

赵客缦胡缨，吴钩霜雪明。

zhào kè màn hú yīng, wú gōu shuāng xuě míng

A swordsman from Zhao, rough tassels on his cap, his curved Wu blade bright as frost on snow.

银鞍照白马，飒沓如流星。

yín ān zhào bái mǎ, sà tà rú liú xīng

His silver saddle gleams on a white horse; he streaks by like a falling star.

十步杀一人，千里不留行。

shí bù shā yī rén, qiān lǐ bù liú xíng

Ten paces — and a man lies dead; a thousand miles, and no one can hold him back.

事了拂衣去，深藏身与名。

shì liǎo fú yī qù, shēn cáng shēn yǔ míng

His work done, he shakes out his sleeves and goes, leaving neither face nor name behind.

闲过信陵饮，脱剑膝前横。

xián guò xìn líng yǐn, tuō jiàn xī qián héng

Idle, he drops by Lord Xinling’s for a drink, unbuckles his sword and lays it across his knees.

将炙啖朱亥，持觞劝侯嬴。

jiāng zhì dàn zhū hài, chí shāng quàn hóu yíng

The lord hands roast meat to Zhu Hai, and raises his cup to Hou Ying.

三杯吐然诺，五岳倒为轻。

sān bēi tǔ rán nuò, wǔ yuè dào wéi qīng

Three cups, and they give their word — a promise that makes the Five Mountains feel light.

眼花耳热后，意气素霓生。

yǎn huā ěr rè hòu, yì qì sù ní shēng

Eyes dazzled, ears burning with wine, their courage soars like a white rainbow across the sky.

救赵挥金槌，邯郸先震惊。

jiù zhào huī jīn chuí, hán dān xiān zhèn jīng

To save Zhao, Zhu Hai swung his iron mace, and Handan was the first to tremble.

千秋二壮士，烜赫大梁城。

qiān qiū èr zhuàng shì, xuǎn hè dà liáng chéng

A thousand years on, those two brave men still blaze in the city of Daliang.

纵死侠骨香，不惭世上英。

zòng sǐ xiá gǔ xiāng, bù cán shì shàng yīng

Though they die, a hero’s bones stay fragrant; they need not bow to the great men of the world.

谁能书阁下，白首太玄经。

shuí néng shū gé xià, bái shǒu tài xuán jīng

Who would sit in a library till his hair turns white, writing the Classic of Supreme Mystery?
