Mù Jiāng Yín

导读About this poem

Sunset on a river, then nightfall: half the water turns jade-green, half glows red, and later dew shines like pearls under a moon thin as a bow. Four lines, two pictures, all colour.

中文解析

这首诗写一个秋天的傍晚到夜晚，在江边看到的景色。前两句写黄昏：最后一道夕阳斜斜地照在江面上，用“铺”字，好像把阳光平平地铺在水上。照到阳光的一半江水是红的，没照到的一半是碧绿色（“瑟瑟”本是一种绿色宝石，这里指碧绿色）。“半江瑟瑟半江红”一句里有两种颜色，画面非常鲜明。后两句写入夜：诗人说九月初三的夜晚真可爱（“可怜”在古代是“可爱”的意思），草上的露水像一颗颗珍珠，弯弯的新月像一张弓。两个比喻都很简单、很准确。全诗从黄昏写到夜里，时间在流动，诗人一直舍不得离开，他的轻松愉快就藏在这些景色里。

In English

This poem follows one autumn evening on a riverbank, from sunset into night. The first two lines are dusk: the last band of sunlight lies across the river, and the verb 铺 (“to spread out”, as you would spread a cloth) makes the light look laid flat on the water. Where the sun touches, the river is red; where it doesn’t, the water is a deep blue-green — 瑟瑟 was the name of a green gemstone. “Half the river jade, half the river red” puts two colours side by side in one line. The last two lines are nightfall. The poet calls the night of the third day of the ninth month 可怜 — in classical Chinese this means “lovely”, not “pitiful” as it does today. Dew on the grass looks like real pearls, and the new moon is curved like a bow. Both comparisons are simple and exact. Time moves through the poem from sunset to night, and the poet clearly can’t bring himself to leave; his quiet happiness is hidden in the scenery.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) was among the most widely read Tang poets. This poem is traditionally dated to about 822, when he left the troubled politics of the capital to become governor of Hangzhou — which may explain its light, relaxed mood.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

铺 pū（铺开），不读 pù；瑟瑟 sè sè；可怜 kě lián（古义“可爱”）。

版本说明 About the text

采用统编语文教材（四年级上册）文本。“真珠”即珍珠，教材作“真珠”，有的版本写作“珍珠”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

一道残阳铺水中，

yī dào cán yáng pū shuǐ zhōng

A last ribbon of sunset lies spread across the water —

半江瑟瑟半江红。

bàn jiāng sè sè bàn jiāng hóng

half the river jade-green, half the river aflame.

可怜九月初三夜，

kě lián jiǔ yuè chū sān yè

How lovely, this third night of the ninth moon:

露似真珠月似弓。

lù sì zhēn zhū yuè sì gōng

dewdrops like pearls, and a moon bent like a bow.
