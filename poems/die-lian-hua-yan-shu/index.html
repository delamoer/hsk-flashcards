导读About this poem

After a sleepless night of missing someone, a lonely figure climbs a tall tower and looks down the road to the edge of the world. The image of “last night the west wind stripped the green trees” became one of the most quoted lines in Chinese literature.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写深秋时节对远方之人的思念。开头就把感情放进景物里：栏杆边的菊花笼着烟雾，好像在发愁；兰花上挂着露水，好像在哭泣。这叫“移情于物”，把人的心情移到花草身上。丝帘里透着一点寒意，燕子成双成对地飞走了，只留下孤单的人。明月不懂离别的痛苦，偏偏把斜斜的月光一直照进屋里，照到天亮——其实不是月亮无情，是人整夜没睡。下阕写第二天：昨夜西风吹落了树叶，他独自登上高楼，一直望到天边的路尽头，也看不到要等的人。想寄一封信去，可是山那么长、水那么宽，不知道对方在哪里。“昨夜西风凋碧树，独上高楼，望尽天涯路”境界开阔，后来王国维在《人间词话》里说，成就大事业、大学问的人要经过三种境界，第一种就是这三句——先要登高望远，看清目标。

In English

This lyric is about missing someone in late autumn. It opens by putting feelings into the plants: chrysanthemums by the railing are wrapped in mist as if they were worrying; orchids carry dew as if they were crying. This is 移情于物, “moving one’s emotions onto things.” A slight chill comes through the silk curtains, and the swallows fly away in pairs, leaving the speaker alone. The bright moon doesn’t understand the pain of parting, and keeps shining its slanting light through the red door all night long — which really means the speaker never slept. The second half moves to the next day. Last night the west wind stripped the green trees bare. The speaker climbs the tall tower alone and gazes all the way down the road to the edge of the sky, but the one they are waiting for is nowhere in sight. They want to send a letter, but with mountains so long and rivers so wide, where would it go? The lines 昨夜西风凋碧树，独上高楼，望尽天涯路 are vast and lonely. Centuries later the critic Wang Guowei borrowed them as the first of his “three stages” of any great achievement: first you must climb high, alone, and see the whole road ahead.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yan Shu (991–1055) was a child prodigy who became chief minister of the Northern Song, and a leading lyric poet of his time, known for elegant, restrained melancholy. His son Yan Jidao was also a famous poet.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

槛菊愁烟兰泣露，

jiàn jú chóu yān lán qì lù

Chrysanthemums by the rail grieve in the mist, orchids weep with dew;

罗幕轻寒，燕子双飞去。

luó mù qīng hán, yàn zi shuāng fēi qù

a light chill seeps through the silk curtains, and the swallows leave two by two.

明月不谙离恨苦，斜光到晓穿朱户。

míng yuè bù ān lí hèn kǔ, xié guāng dào xiǎo chuān zhū hù

The moon knows nothing of the ache of parting, and slants through the red door until dawn.

昨夜西风凋碧树，

zuó yè xī fēng diāo bì shù

Last night the west wind stripped the green trees bare;

独上高楼，望尽天涯路。

dú shàng gāo lóu, wàng jìn tiān yá lù

alone I climb the tower and follow the road with my eyes to the end of the world.

欲寄彩笺兼尺素，山长水阔知何处！

yù jì cǎi jiān jiān chǐ sù, shān cháng shuǐ kuò zhī hé chù

I would send you a letter, a poem — but over such long mountains and wide rivers, where are you?
