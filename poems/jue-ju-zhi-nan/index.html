导读About this poem

An old monk ties up his boat and crosses a bridge with his walking stick, while spring rain dampens his robe and willow wind brushes his face. The last two lines are some of the most quoted words about spring in Chinese.

中文解析

这首诗的作者是一位南宋和尚，名叫志南。前两句写他出游：在古树的浓荫下把小船拴好，拄着藜杖过桥往东走。“杖藜扶我”说得很有趣——不说“我拄着拐杖”，而说拐杖“扶着我”，好像拐杖是一个老朋友。后两句是千古名句：杏花开时下的细雨，好像要把衣服打湿，却又没有真湿；吹在脸上的杨柳风，一点也不冷。“杏花雨”和“杨柳风”都是早春特有的东西，“欲湿”和“不寒”写出了皮肤的感觉，让人仿佛亲身感受到春雨的细、春风的柔。诗里没有写“喜欢春天”，可是那种悠闲、舒服的心情，读者一下就能体会到。今天人们常用“吹面不寒杨柳风”形容春天温和的风。

In English

The poet was a Southern Song monk called Zhinan. In the first two lines he goes out walking: he ties his little covered boat in the deep shade of old trees, then crosses a bridge heading east, leaning on his walking stick. Notice the phrasing 杖藜扶我 — not “I lean on my stick” but “my stick helps me along”, as if the stick were an old friend. The last two lines are famous all over China. The rain that falls when the apricot trees blossom is so fine it seems about to soak his robe, yet never quite does; the wind through the willows blows on his face and is not cold at all. Apricot-blossom rain and willow wind both belong to early spring, and the words 欲湿 (“almost wet”) and 不寒 (“not cold”) are about how things feel on the skin — we feel the fine rain and soft wind ourselves. The poem never says “I love spring”, but its easy, contented mood is impossible to miss. Even today people describe a gentle spring breeze with the line 吹面不寒杨柳风.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhinan was a Buddhist monk of the Southern Song whose dates are unknown. Only a few of his poems survive; this one is said to have been praised by the great scholar Zhu Xi, which helped make it famous.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

系 jì：“系船”的“系”读 jì（拴、绑），不读 xì。杖藜 zhàng lí；篷 péng。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

古木阴中系短篷，

gǔ mù yīn zhōng jì duǎn péng

In the shade of old trees I tie up my little boat,

杖藜扶我过桥东。

zhàng lí fú wǒ guò qiáo dōng

and my walking stick helps me east across the bridge.

沾衣欲湿杏花雨，

zhān yī yù shī xìng huā yǔ

Apricot-blossom rain clings to my robe, almost wet;

吹面不寒杨柳风。

chuī miàn bù hán yáng liǔ fēng

willow wind on my face, and none of it cold.
