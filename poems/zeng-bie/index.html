导读About this poem

Du Mu says goodbye to a young woman in Yangzhou. They are too sad even to show it, so the candle on the table weeps for them all night. One of the most tender farewell poems in Chinese.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

杜牧离开扬州时，写了两首诗送给一位相好的歌女，这是第二首。第一句很耐人寻味：感情太深的人，到了分别的时候反而像没有感情一样，说不出话，脸上也没有表情。第二句接着说：在送别的酒杯前，只觉得想笑一笑都笑不出来。后两句把目光转向桌上的蜡烛：蜡烛也有“心”，也舍不得分别，替我们流泪一直到天亮。“心”是双关——蜡烛的“芯”和人的“心”同音。把蜡烛当作有感情的人来写，叫“拟人”；借蜡烛流泪写人的伤心，叫“借物抒情”。最后一句也告诉我们，两个人一夜没睡。全诗不写一个“愁”字，却把离别的痛写得深而含蓄。

In English

When Du Mu left Yangzhou, he wrote two farewell poems for a young singer he loved; this is the second. The opening line is famous for its paradox: someone full of feeling can look as though they feel nothing at all at the moment of parting — the emotion is too big for words or expressions. Line two goes on: sitting before the farewell wine, he finds he cannot even manage a smile. Then the poem turns to the candle on the table. The candle “has a heart” and grieves at the parting too, shedding tears for the two of them until the sky grows light. There is a pun here: 心 xīn “heart” sounds the same as 芯 xīn, a candle’s wick. Treating the candle as if it had feelings is personification, and letting an object express what people feel is a favourite move in Chinese poetry, called jiè wù shū qíng (“expressing feeling through things”). The last line also quietly tells us the two stayed up all night. Without ever naming sorrow, the poem makes it unmistakable.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–c. 852) spent several years on the staff of the military governor in Yangzhou, then the liveliest city of the empire. These two poems are traditionally dated to 835, when he left Yangzhou for a post in the capital.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

多情却似总无情，

duō qíng què sì zǒng wú qíng

Too much in love, we look as though we feel nothing;

唯觉樽前笑不成。

wéi jué zūn qián xiào bù chéng

over the farewell wine, not even a smile will come.

蜡烛有心还惜别，

là zhú yǒu xīn hái xī bié

The candle has a heart, and it too hates to see us part —

替人垂泪到天明。

tì rén chuí lèi dào tiān míng

it weeps for us until the sky turns light.
