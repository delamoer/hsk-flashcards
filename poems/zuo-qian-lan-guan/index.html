导读About this poem

Sent into exile for criticising the emperor, a fifty-one-year-old official stops in the snow at a mountain pass and speaks to the young relative who has come after him: I did what was right, and if I die down south, collect my bones.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

公元819年，唐宪宗要把一节“佛骨”迎进皇宫供奉，韩愈上书反对，皇帝大怒，差点杀了他，最后把他贬到八千里外的潮州。这首诗是他在路上写给赶来相送的侄孙韩湘的。开头两句写事情的经过：早上递上一封奏章，晚上就被贬，节奏极快，写出处罚的严厉。三四句是全诗的骨头：我本想替皇上除掉有害的事，哪里会因为年老体衰就爱惜自己剩下的日子！这是在说自己不后悔。五六句写眼前的景：云横在秦岭上，回头看不见家；大雪堵住蓝关，连马都走不动。景色既是真实的路途艰难，也是心里的迷茫和悲凉。最后两句转向侄孙：我知道你远道而来是有心的，等我死在南方瘴气弥漫的江边，你好替我收拾尸骨。语气平静，却极沉痛。全诗把刚直和悲伤写在一起，是唐代七律名篇。

In English

In 819 the emperor planned to bring a relic — a finger bone of the Buddha — into the palace to be worshipped. Han Yu sent in a memorial attacking the idea. The emperor was so angry he nearly had him executed, then banished him to Chaozhou, some 8,000 li to the south. On the road, his grand-nephew Han Xiang caught up with him, and Han Yu wrote this for him. The opening couplet is breathless: one memorial in the morning, exiled by evening. The second couplet is the backbone of the poem: I only wanted to rid the throne of a harmful practice — would I, old and worn out, cling to my remaining years? He regrets nothing. The third couplet is the scene in front of him: clouds lie across the Qinling Mountains, so he can no longer see home; snow blocks Lan Pass and even the horse will not go on. The landscape is real hardship, but it is also his own bleak, lost feeling. The final couplet turns to the young man: I know you came this far for a reason — so you can gather my bones by the fever-ridden rivers of the south. Said calmly, it is devastating. Uprightness and grief sit side by side, which is why this is one of the great Tang regulated verses.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Han Yu (768–824), a leading Tang writer and Confucian scholar, wrote this in 819 on his way into exile in Chaozhou (Guangdong), after protesting against the emperor’s worship of a Buddhist relic. He survived and was recalled the next year. Han Xiang was later turned by legend into Han Xiangzi, one of the Eight Immortals.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“朝奏”的“朝”读 zhāo（早晨，与下句“夕贬”相对），“潮州”的“潮”读 cháo；“九重天”的“重”读 chóng；“欲为”的“为”读 wèi（替）；“衰朽”的“衰”读 shuāi；“应有意”的“应”读 yīng（应该）；“瘴”读 zhàng。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材版本。重要异文：“夕贬潮州路八千”一作“夕贬潮阳路八千”（潮阳是潮州的古郡名，许多古本作“潮阳”）；“欲为圣明除弊事”一作“本为圣朝除弊事”；“肯将衰朽惜残年”一作“岂将衰朽惜残年”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

一封朝奏九重天，

yī fēng zhāo zòu jiǔ chóng tiān

One letter to the throne at dawn,

夕贬潮州路八千。

xī biǎn cháo zhōu lù bā qiān

and by dusk I’m banished, eight thousand li to Chaozhou.

欲为圣明除弊事，

yù wèi shèng míng chú bì shì

I only meant to rid my sovereign of a harmful thing —

肯将衰朽惜残年！

kěn jiāng shuāi xiǔ xī cán nián

would a worn-out old man cling to the years he has left?

云横秦岭家何在？

yún héng qín lǐng jiā hé zài

Clouds lie across the Qinling hills; where is home now?

雪拥蓝关马不前。

xuě yōng lán guān mǎ bù qián

Snow chokes Lan Pass, and my horse will not go on.

知汝远来应有意，

zhī rǔ yuǎn lái yīng yǒu yì

I know you didn’t come all this way for nothing:

好收吾骨瘴江边。

hǎo shōu wú gǔ zhàng jiāng biān

you’ll be there to gather my bones by the fever rivers of the south.
