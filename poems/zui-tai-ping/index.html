导读About this poem

A comic, biting song about greedy people who will scrape profit from anywhere — mud from a swallow’s beak, gold leaf from a Buddha’s face, fat from inside a mosquito. Wild exaggeration, very funny.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首讽刺小令，用夸张得近乎荒唐的比喻，嘲笑那些贪得无厌、连一点点小便宜都不放过的人。全曲一口气列出七件“不可能的事”：从燕子嘴里抢泥（燕子筑巢用的一点泥），从针头上削铁，从佛像脸上刮金箔，在什么都没有的地方硬找出东西来；在鹌鹑的嗉子里找豌豆，在鹭鸶细细的腿上割瘦肉，在蚊子肚子里挖油——最后一句冷冷地说：“亏老先生下手！”真难为您老人家下得了手。这种把同一类比喻一个接一个排列的写法叫“排比”，越排越夸张，讽刺的力量越来越强。元曲语言通俗、泼辣，敢说敢骂，这首就是典型。今天“鹭鸶腿上劈精肉”“蚊子腹内刳脂油”仍被用来形容极端的吝啬和贪婪。

In English

This is a satirical song that mocks people so greedy they won’t pass up even the tiniest gain, using comparisons so exaggerated they become absurd. In one breath it lists seven impossible feats: snatching mud from a swallow’s beak (the little bit it carries to build its nest), shaving iron off the tip of a needle, scraping the gold leaf off a Buddha statue’s face — finding something where there is nothing. Then: hunting for a pea in a quail’s crop, carving lean meat from an egret’s skinny leg, digging fat out of a mosquito’s belly. And the last line, dry and cutting: “How can you bring yourself to do it, sir!” Piling up comparisons of the same kind, each more extreme than the last, is a favourite device in Chinese writing, and here every new image sharpens the mockery. Yuan songs are known for plain, earthy, outspoken language — they are not afraid to ridicule — and this one is a perfect example. Some of its lines are still used today to describe extreme stinginess and greed.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The author is unknown. The song survives in Yuan-dynasty anthologies of sanqu, popular song lyrics written to fixed tunes, which often poked fun at social vices.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

夺泥燕口，削铁针头，

duó ní yàn kǒu, xuē tiě zhēn tóu

He’d snatch the mud from a swallow’s beak, shave iron off a needle’s point,

刮金佛面细搜求，

guā jīn fó miàn xì sōu qiú

scrape the gilt from a Buddha’s face, searching every inch —

无中觅有。

wú zhōng mì yǒu

he’ll find something where there’s nothing.

鹌鹑嗉里寻豌豆，

ān chún sù lǐ xún wān dòu

He’d hunt for a pea in a quail’s crop,

鹭鸶腿上劈精肉，

lù sī tuǐ shàng pī jīng ròu

carve lean meat from an egret’s leg,

蚊子腹内刳脂油，

wén zi fù nèi kū zhī yóu

dig the fat out of a mosquito’s belly —

亏老先生下手！

kuī lǎo xiān shēng xià shǒu

Only you, good sir, could bring yourself to do it!
