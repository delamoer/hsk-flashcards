导读About this poem

Said to be sung by an old farmer in the days of the legendary sage-king Yao: I work at sunrise, rest at sunset, dig my own well, plough my own field — what has the emperor’s power got to do with me? Simple words, still used today.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

传说在上古尧帝的时候，天下太平，有一位老人一边玩“击壤”的游戏（一种扔木块的游戏），一边唱了这首歌。前四句写农民最简单的生活：太阳出来就去干活，太阳落山就回家休息；自己挖井喝水，自己种田吃饭。最后一句很有意思：“帝王的力量对我有什么用呢？”这不是不尊敬帝王，而是说老百姓生活得自由自在，根本感觉不到有人在管着他们——古人认为，这正是最好的政治。“日出而作，日入而息”今天仍然常用，表示规律、简朴的生活，是汉语里最早的劳动歌之一。句子都是四个字，节奏整齐，读起来很顺口。

In English

Legend says that in the time of the sage-king Yao, when the whole land was at peace, an old man was playing jīrǎng — a game of throwing wooden blocks — and sang this song. The first four lines are the simplest farming life: when the sun comes up I go to work; when it goes down I rest; I dig a well to drink and plough a field to eat. Then comes the surprising last line: “What has the emperor’s power to do with me?” This is not disrespect. It means ordinary people are living so freely that they don’t even feel anyone governing them — which, the ancient Chinese believed, is exactly what the best government looks like. The phrase 日出而作，日入而息 is still everyday Chinese for a regular, simple, hard-working life. It is one of the oldest work songs in the language, and with its even four-character lines it is easy to read aloud.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The song is first recorded in texts of the 3rd century AD, such as Huangfu Mi’s Records of Emperors and Kings (帝王世纪), which say it was sung in the time of the legendary Emperor Yao. Its real date is unknown; it is traditionally counted among the oldest Chinese songs.

版本说明 About the text

采用皇甫谧《帝王世纪》所载、最通行的五句本。异文：末句或作“帝何力于我哉”“帝力何有于我哉”，有的本子前面还有“吾”字。网站每两句一行，末句单独一行（共三行）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

日出而作，日入而息。

rì chū ér zuò, rì rù ér xī

Up with the sun to work, down with the sun to rest.

凿井而饮，耕田而食。

záo jǐng ér yǐn, gēng tián ér shí

I dig my well for water and plough my field for bread.

帝力于我何有哉！

dì lì yú wǒ hé yǒu zāi

What has the emperor’s power to do with me?
