导读About this poem

A general shoots at a “tiger” in the dark; at dawn he finds his arrow buried deep in solid rock. Twenty characters retelling a famous Han legend.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗化用了汉代名将李广的故事：李广夜里在山中看见草丛里有块石头，以为是老虎，一箭射去，第二天一看，箭头整个射进了石头里。第一句写夜晚的树林黑沉沉的，风一吹草突然乱动——好像有猛兽，气氛一下子紧张起来。第二句只写将军“引弓”，不写射，却让人感到他的镇定和力量。后两句时间跳到第二天天亮：去找那支白羽箭，竟然深深扎在石棱里！诗人不直接说将军多勇猛，而是用结果让读者自己吃惊，这就是“以小见大”。短短二十个字，有声音、有动作、有悬念，像一个微型故事。

In English

This poem retells a legend about Li Guang, a famous Han-dynasty general. Out hunting at night, he saw a rock in the grass, took it for a crouching tiger and shot it. The next morning he found his arrow buried in the stone. The first line sets the mood: the woods are dark, and suddenly the grass thrashes in the wind, as if some beast is moving there. The second line shows only the general drawing his bow — we never see the shot, but we feel his calm and his strength. Then the poem jumps to dawn: they go looking for the white-feathered arrow and find it sunk deep into the edge of a rock. The poet never says “the general was brave and powerful”; he lets the result surprise us and draw the conclusion ourselves. In just twenty characters there is sound, movement, suspense and a twist — a miniature story. Chinese readers know it from childhood, and the phrase 没石饮羽 (“an arrow sunk into stone”) still describes amazing strength.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu Lun (c. 739–c. 799) was one of the “Ten Talents of the Dali Era” and served for a time on the staff of a frontier commander. His six “Frontier Songs” were written in reply to a general’s poems; this is the second.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

林暗草惊风，

lín àn cǎo jīng fēng

Dark woods — the grass leaps in a sudden wind;

将军夜引弓。

jiāng jūn yè yǐn gōng

in the night the general draws his bow.

平明寻白羽，

píng míng xún bái yǔ

At first light they look for the white-feathered arrow —

没在石棱中。

mò zài shí léng zhōng

it is buried deep in the rock.
