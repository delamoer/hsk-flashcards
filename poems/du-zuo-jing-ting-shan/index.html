Sitting Alone Facing Jingting Mountain

导读About this poem

The birds fly off, the last cloud drifts away, and Li Bai is left alone with a mountain. They look at each other and never tire of it — the mountain has become his only friend.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是李白晚年在宣城写的。前两句写身边的东西一个个离开：鸟群高高飞走，越飞越远，看不见了；天上只有一片云，也悠闲地飘走了。连鸟和云都不陪他，诗人的孤独一点点加深。后两句一转：只有敬亭山，我看它，它看我，谁也看不厌。山本来不会看人，诗人却把它写得像人一样，这叫“拟人”。山越有情，越说明人间没有可以说话的人——这是用“热闹”写孤单。但这份孤单又很平静：诗人和山相对，好像找到了真正懂自己的朋友。“相看两不厌”今天也常用来形容两个人（或人和地方）怎么看都不腻。

In English

Li Bai wrote this late in life, in Xuancheng (in today’s Anhui). In the first two lines, everything around him leaves one by one: a flock of birds flies higher and further until it disappears; a single cloud drifts lazily away. Not even the birds and clouds will stay with him, and his loneliness grows. Then the poem turns: only Jingting Mountain remains. I look at it, it looks at me, and neither of us ever gets tired. A mountain can’t really look at anyone — the poet treats it like a person, which is called nǐrén, personification. The warmer the mountain seems, the clearer it is that he has no human company: loneliness is shown through a kind of friendship. Yet the mood is calm, not bitter. Facing the mountain, he seems to have found a friend who truly understands him. The phrase 相看两不厌 is still used today for two people — or a person and a place — who never tire of each other.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) visited Xuancheng several times in his later years, after leaving the court in Chang’an disappointed. Jingting Mountain lies just north of the city; the poem is usually dated to around 753.
