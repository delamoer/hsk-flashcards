卖炭翁

Mài Tàn Wēng
The Old Charcoal Seller
白居易Bái Jūyì · Bai Juyi唐 Tang
体裁Form

导读About this poem

An old man burns charcoal all year, hoping for a cold day so he can sell it — and then palace envoys take the whole cartload for a scrap of silk. A short, angry story-poem about power and the poor, told without a single word of comment.

第一段Part 1
01
卖炭翁，伐薪烧炭南山中。

直译LiteralAn old charcoal seller, cutting firewood and burning charcoal in the Southern Mountains.

意译MeaningAn old man who sells charcoal cuts wood and burns it down to charcoal deep in the Southern Hills.

02
满面尘灰烟火色，两鬓苍苍十指黑。

直译LiteralA whole face of dust and ash, the colour of smoke and fire; both temples grey-white, ten fingers black.

意译MeaningHis face is grimy with ash and smoke, his temples grey, all ten fingers black.

03
卖炭得钱何所营？身上衣裳口中食。

直译LiteralSelling charcoal, getting money — what is it for? Clothes on his body, food in his mouth.

意译MeaningWhat does he want from the money he earns? Only clothes for his back and food for his mouth.

04
可怜身上衣正单，心忧炭贱愿天寒。

直译LiteralPitiful — the clothes on his body are thin; his heart worries charcoal will be cheap, he wishes the weather cold.

意译MeaningPoor man — his coat is thin, yet he prays for colder weather, afraid his charcoal will sell too cheap.

第二段Part 2
05
夜来城外一尺雪，晓驾炭车辗冰辙。

直译LiteralOvernight outside the city, a foot of snow; at dawn he drives the charcoal cart, crushing icy ruts.

意译MeaningIn the night a foot of snow falls outside the city; at dawn he drives his cart over the frozen ruts.

06
牛困人饥日已高，市南门外泥中歇。

直译LiteralThe ox tired, the man hungry, the sun already high; outside the market’s south gate, resting in the mud.

意译MeaningBy the time the sun is high, the ox is worn out and the man is starving; they rest in the mud outside the market’s south gate.

第三段Part 3
07
翩翩两骑来是谁？黄衣使者白衫儿。

直译LiteralLightly riding, two horsemen come — who are they? An envoy in yellow and a lad in a white shirt.

意译MeaningTwo riders come trotting up — who are they? A palace envoy in yellow, and his errand boy in white.

08
手把文书口称敕，回车叱牛牵向北。

直译LiteralHands holding a document, mouths declaring an imperial order; turning the cart, shouting at the ox, leading it north.

意译MeaningWaving a paper and crying “By order of the emperor!”, they turn the cart around, shout at the ox, and drive it north to the palace.

09
一车炭，千余斤，宫使驱将惜不得。

直译LiteralOne cart of charcoal, over a thousand jin; the palace envoys drive it off — regret it as he may, he cannot keep it.

意译MeaningA whole cartload of charcoal, more than a thousand pounds — the envoys take it, and all his grief can’t hold it back.

10
半匹红纱一丈绫，系向牛头充炭直。

直译LiteralHalf a bolt of red gauze, ten feet of silk damask; tied onto the ox’s head, counted as the charcoal’s price.

意译MeaningHalf a bolt of red gauze and a strip of silk, tied to the ox’s head — that is the price of his charcoal.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗讲一个卖炭老人的遭遇。前半写他的辛苦：在终南山里砍柴烧炭，满脸灰尘、两鬓花白、十个手指都黑了。最让人难过的是“可怜身上衣正单，心忧炭贱愿天寒”——自己穿得很少，却盼着天更冷，因为天冷炭才能卖上好价钱。这种矛盾的心理写得非常真实。好不容易下了一场大雪，他天没亮就拉炭进城。后半突然转折：宫里派来的“使者”拿着文书，说是皇帝的命令，把一千多斤炭全部拉走，只给了半匹红纱、一丈绫。唐代宫里派人到市场上“买”东西，实际上几乎是抢，叫“宫市”。诗人只讲故事，结尾不加一句评论，读者的愤怒却自然产生了。

In English

The poem follows one old man through one bad day. The first half shows his labour: he cuts wood and burns charcoal in the Zhongnan Mountains south of Chang’an, his face grey with ash and smoke, his hair white at the temples, all ten fingers black. The most painful lines come next: 可怜身上衣正单，心忧炭贱愿天寒 — his clothes are thin, yet he hopes the weather turns colder, because only cold weather keeps the price of charcoal up. He needs the very thing that hurts him. At last a heavy snow falls, and before dawn he drives his ox cart into the city, where he rests in the mud outside the market. Then the story turns. Two riders arrive — a eunuch in yellow and his assistant in white. They wave a document, announce an imperial order, and drive the whole cart, over a thousand jin of charcoal, off towards the palace. In return they tie half a bolt of red gauze and a length of silk to the ox’s head as “payment”. This was the “palace market” (宫市) of Bai Juyi’s time: palace agents “buying” goods at whatever price they liked, which in practice meant taking them. Bai Juyi ends the poem right there, with no comment and no moral. He trusts the reader to feel the anger for themselves — and that is what makes it so strong.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) believed poetry should speak about real problems and be understood by ordinary people. Around 809, as a young court official, he wrote a series of fifty “New Yuefu” poems criticising social wrongs; this is one of them, aimed at the “palace market” abuses of his day.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

卖炭翁，伐薪烧炭南山中。

mài tàn wēng, fá xīn shāo tàn nán shān zhōng

An old man who sells charcoal cuts wood and burns it down to charcoal deep in the Southern Hills.

满面尘灰烟火色，两鬓苍苍十指黑。

mǎn miàn chén huī yān huǒ sè, liǎng bìn cāng cāng shí zhǐ hēi

His face is grimy with ash and smoke, his temples grey, all ten fingers black.

卖炭得钱何所营？身上衣裳口中食。

mài tàn dé qián hé suǒ yíng, shēn shàng yī cháng kǒu zhōng shí

What does he want from the money he earns? Only clothes for his back and food for his mouth.

可怜身上衣正单，心忧炭贱愿天寒。

kě lián shēn shàng yī zhèng dān, xīn yōu tàn jiàn yuàn tiān hán

Poor man — his coat is thin, yet he prays for colder weather, afraid his charcoal will sell too cheap.

夜来城外一尺雪，晓驾炭车辗冰辙。

yè lái chéng wài yī chǐ xuě, xiǎo jià tàn chē niǎn bīng zhé

In the night a foot of snow falls outside the city; at dawn he drives his cart over the frozen ruts.

牛困人饥日已高，市南门外泥中歇。

niú kùn rén jī rì yǐ gāo, shì nán mén wài ní zhōng xiē

By the time the sun is high, the ox is worn out and the man is starving; they rest in the mud outside the market’s south gate.

翩翩两骑来是谁？黄衣使者白衫儿。

piān piān liǎng jì lái shì shuí, huáng yī shǐ zhě bái shān ér

Two riders come trotting up — who are they? A palace envoy in yellow, and his errand boy in white.

手把文书口称敕，回车叱牛牵向北。

shǒu bǎ wén shū kǒu chēng chì, huí chē chì niú qiān xiàng běi

Waving a paper and crying “By order of the emperor!”, they turn the cart around, shout at the ox, and drive it north to the palace.

一车炭，千余斤，宫使驱将惜不得。

yī chē tàn, qiān yú jīn, gōng shǐ qū jiāng xī bù dé

A whole cartload of charcoal, more than a thousand pounds — the envoys take it, and all his grief can’t hold it back.

半匹红纱一丈绫，系向牛头充炭直。

bàn pǐ hóng shā yī zhàng líng, jì xiàng niú tóu chōng tàn zhí

Half a bolt of red gauze and a strip of silk, tied to the ox’s head — that is the price of his charcoal.
