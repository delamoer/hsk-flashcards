西洲曲

Xī Zhōu Qǔ
Song of the West Isle
佚名（南朝乐府）Anonymous (Southern Dynasties yuefu folk song)魏晋南北朝 Wei, Jin & Southern and Northern Dynasties

导读About this poem

A young woman south of the Yangtze misses her lover across the river — from plum-blossom time through the lotus summer to the autumn geese. Gentle, musical and full of word-play, it is the loveliest love song of the Southern Dynasties.

第一段Part 1
01
忆梅下西洲，折梅寄江北。

直译LiteralRemembering the plum blossoms, I go down to the West Isle; I break off a plum branch to send north of the river.

意译MeaningThinking of the plum trees, I go down to the West Isle, and snap a sprig to send you across the river.

02
单衫杏子红，双鬓鸦雏色。

直译LiteralMy thin robe is apricot red; my two temple-locks are the colour of a young crow.

意译MeaningMy light robe is apricot red, my hair as glossy black as a fledgling crow.

第二段Part 2
03
西洲在何处？两桨桥头渡。

直译LiteralWhere is the West Isle? Two oars cross over from the bridgehead.

意译MeaningWhere is the West Isle? A few strokes of the oars from the bridge.

04
日暮伯劳飞，风吹乌臼树。

直译LiteralAt sunset the shrikes fly; the wind blows the tallow trees.

意译MeaningAt dusk the shrikes take wing, and wind stirs the tallow trees.

第三段Part 3
05
树下即门前，门中露翠钿。

直译LiteralUnder the tree is my gate; in the doorway my kingfisher hairpin shows.

意译MeaningThe tree stands right by my gate; in the doorway, a glint of my blue-green hairpin.

06
开门郎不至，出门采红莲。

直译LiteralI open the gate, my love does not come; I go out the gate to pick red lotus.

意译MeaningI open the gate — you don’t come. I go out to gather red lotus.

第四段Part 4
07
采莲南塘秋，莲花过人头。

直译LiteralPicking lotus at South Pond in autumn, the lotus flowers rise above my head.

意译MeaningGathering lotus on South Pond as autumn comes, the flowers stand taller than I am.

08
低头弄莲子，莲子清如水。

直译LiteralLowering my head I play with the lotus seeds; the lotus seeds are clear as water.

意译MeaningI bow my head and toy with the lotus seeds — seeds as pure as water, a love as clear.

第五段Part 5
09
置莲怀袖中，莲心彻底红。

直译LiteralI put the lotus in my sleeve; the lotus heart is red right through.

意译MeaningI tuck the lotus into my sleeve; its heart is red all the way through.

10
忆郎郎不至，仰首望飞鸿。

直译LiteralI think of my love, my love does not come; I raise my head to watch the flying geese.

意译MeaningI long for you, and you don’t come; I lift my eyes to the passing geese.

第六段Part 6
11
鸿飞满西洲，望郎上青楼。

直译LiteralThe geese fly, filling the West Isle; to look for my love I climb the blue tower.

意译MeaningGeese fill the sky over the West Isle; I climb the painted tower to look for you.

12
楼高望不见，尽日栏杆头。

直译LiteralThe tower is high, yet I cannot see you; all day I stay by the railing.

意译MeaningHigh as it is, I still can’t see you, and lean on the railing all day long.

第七段Part 7
13
栏杆十二曲，垂手明如玉。

直译LiteralThe railing has twelve bends; my hanging hands are bright as jade.

意译MeaningThe railing winds through twelve turns; my hands rest on it, pale as jade.

14
卷帘天自高，海水摇空绿。

直译LiteralRolling up the curtain, the sky is high by itself; the sea-wide water sways, empty and green.

意译MeaningI roll up the blind: the sky is high and far, and the wide river rocks, an empty green.

第八段Part 8
15
海水梦悠悠，君愁我亦愁。

直译LiteralLike the wide water, my dream is long and far; if you are sad, I am sad too.

意译MeaningMy dream drifts on and on like that water; if you are sad, then so am I.

16
南风知我意，吹梦到西洲。

直译LiteralIf the south wind knows my heart, it will blow my dream to the West Isle.

意译MeaningSouth wind, you know what I want — carry my dream to the West Isle.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《西洲曲》是南朝民歌里最长、最美的一首情歌。一位江南女子思念在江北的情郎，诗跟着季节走：春天想起和他在西洲相会，折梅寄给他；夏天在门前等他不来，出门采莲；秋天抬头望飞来的鸿雁，盼着有他的消息；最后登楼远望，整日倚着栏杆，只能把梦托给南风，吹到西洲去。诗里最有名的是“谐音双关”：“莲”和“怜”（爱）同音，“莲子”就是“怜子”（爱你），“莲心彻底红”就是一颗爱你的心红透了——不直说“我爱你”，却句句是爱。另一个特点是“顶针”（前一句的末尾就是后一句的开头）：“……飞鸿。鸿飞……”“……青楼。楼高……”“……栏杆头。栏杆……”，一句接一句，像歌声连绵不断，也像思念一样剪不断。全诗四句一换韵，读起来轻柔婉转。

In English

This is the longest and loveliest love song from the Southern Dynasties. A young woman in the south misses her lover on the north bank of the river, and the poem follows the seasons with her. In spring she remembers meeting him at the West Isle and sends him a sprig of plum. In summer she waits at her gate; he doesn’t come, so she goes out to pick lotus. In autumn she looks up at the wild geese, hoping for news. At last she climbs a tower and leans on the railing all day, and can only ask the south wind to carry her dream to the West Isle. The poem’s great trick is the pun: 莲 lián “lotus” sounds exactly like 怜 lián “to love”. So “lotus seeds” also means “loving you”, and “the lotus heart is red right through” means a heart completely in love — she never says “I love you”, yet every line does. The other trick is chain-linking: one line ends with a word and the next begins with it (“…flying geese. / Geese fly…”, “…the tower. / The tower is high…”), so the song never seems to pause — just like her longing. The rhyme changes every four lines, giving it a soft, swaying rhythm.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The song comes from the Southern Dynasties (5th–6th century), probably a folk song polished by literary hands. The Yuefu Shiji (Collection of Yuefu Poems) lists it as anonymous; some early anthologies attribute it to Jiang Yan or to Emperor Wu of Liang, but most scholars treat it as a folk song from the lake country south of the Yangtze.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

伯劳 读 bó láo（一种鸟）。乌臼 读 wū jiù。翠钿 读 cuì diàn。露翠钿的“露”读 lù。栏杆十二曲的“曲”读 qū（弯曲），不读 qǔ。卷帘的“卷”读 juǎn。“莲子”“杏子”的“子”按本调标 zǐ。栏杆现代口语“杆”读轻声，这里按本调标 gān。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行本（据《乐府诗集》卷七十二“杂曲歌辞”）。“乌臼”一作“乌桕”，“栏杆”一作“阑干”，意思相同。《玉台新咏》有的版本题作江淹作，不取。全诗三十二句，网站按一联一行排版，四句一段（共八段）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

忆梅下西洲，折梅寄江北。

yì méi xià xī zhōu, zhé méi jì jiāng běi

Thinking of the plum trees, I go down to the West Isle, and snap a sprig to send you across the river.

单衫杏子红，双鬓鸦雏色。

dān shān xìng zǐ hóng, shuāng bìn yā chú sè

My light robe is apricot red, my hair as glossy black as a fledgling crow.

西洲在何处？两桨桥头渡。

xī zhōu zài hé chù, liǎng jiǎng qiáo tóu dù

Where is the West Isle? A few strokes of the oars from the bridge.

日暮伯劳飞，风吹乌臼树。

rì mù bó láo fēi, fēng chuī wū jiù shù

At dusk the shrikes take wing, and wind stirs the tallow trees.

树下即门前，门中露翠钿。

shù xià jí mén qián, mén zhōng lù cuì diàn

The tree stands right by my gate; in the doorway, a glint of my blue-green hairpin.

开门郎不至，出门采红莲。

kāi mén láng bù zhì, chū mén cǎi hóng lián

I open the gate — you don’t come. I go out to gather red lotus.

采莲南塘秋，莲花过人头。

cǎi lián nán táng qiū, lián huā guò rén tóu

Gathering lotus on South Pond as autumn comes, the flowers stand taller than I am.

低头弄莲子，莲子清如水。

dī tóu nòng lián zǐ, lián zǐ qīng rú shuǐ

I bow my head and toy with the lotus seeds — seeds as pure as water, a love as clear.

置莲怀袖中，莲心彻底红。

zhì lián huái xiù zhōng, lián xīn chè dǐ hóng

I tuck the lotus into my sleeve; its heart is red all the way through.

忆郎郎不至，仰首望飞鸿。

yì láng láng bù zhì, yǎng shǒu wàng fēi hóng

I long for you, and you don’t come; I lift my eyes to the passing geese.

鸿飞满西洲，望郎上青楼。

hóng fēi mǎn xī zhōu, wàng láng shàng qīng lóu

Geese fill the sky over the West Isle; I climb the painted tower to look for you.

楼高望不见，尽日栏杆头。

lóu gāo wàng bù jiàn, jìn rì lán gān tóu

High as it is, I still can’t see you, and lean on the railing all day long.

栏杆十二曲，垂手明如玉。

lán gān shí èr qū, chuí shǒu míng rú yù

The railing winds through twelve turns; my hands rest on it, pale as jade.

卷帘天自高，海水摇空绿。

juǎn lián tiān zì gāo, hǎi shuǐ yáo kōng lǜ

I roll up the blind: the sky is high and far, and the wide river rocks, an empty green.

海水梦悠悠，君愁我亦愁。

hǎi shuǐ mèng yōu yōu, jūn chóu wǒ yì chóu

My dream drifts on and on like that water; if you are sad, then so am I.

南风知我意，吹梦到西洲。

nán fēng zhī wǒ yì, chuī mèng dào xī zhōu

South wind, you know what I want — carry my dream to the West Isle.
