导读About this poem

Peach blossoms fall in Luoyang every spring; a young girl sighs at them, and an old man remembers when he was young. Its line “year after year the flowers look alike, but the people are not the same” is still quoted everywhere.

第一段Part 1
01
洛阳城东桃李花，飞来飞去落谁家？

直译LiteralPeach and plum blossoms east of Luoyang city, flying here and there — whose house do they fall on?

意译MeaningPeach and plum blossoms east of Luoyang drift here and there — whose yard will they settle in?

02
洛阳女儿惜颜色，坐见落花长叹息。

直译LiteralA Luoyang girl cherishes her looks; seeing the falling flowers, she sighs long.

意译MeaningA Luoyang girl, careful of her beauty, watches the petals fall and lets out a long sigh.

03
今年花落颜色改，明年花开复谁在？

直译LiteralThis year flowers fall and faces change; next year when flowers open, who will still be here?

意译MeaningThis year the flowers fall and faces fade; next year, when they bloom again, who will be left?

04
已见松柏摧为薪，更闻桑田变成海。

直译LiteralI have seen pines and cypresses cut down for firewood; I have also heard of mulberry fields becoming sea.

意译MeaningI have seen pines and cypresses chopped into firewood, and heard of mulberry fields turned to sea.

05
古人无复洛城东，今人还对落花风。

直译LiteralThe ancients are no longer east of Luoyang; people today still face the wind of falling flowers.

意译MeaningThe people of old are gone from east Luoyang; the people of today still stand in the wind of falling petals.

06
年年岁岁花相似，岁岁年年人不同。

直译LiteralYear after year the flowers are alike; year after year the people are not the same.

意译MeaningEvery year the flowers look the same; every year the people are different.

第二段Part 2
07
寄言全盛红颜子，应怜半死白头翁。

直译LiteralA message to you rosy-cheeked youths in your prime: you should pity the half-dead white-haired old man.

意译MeaningA word to you young people in your bloom: spare some pity for this half-dead, white-haired old man.

08
此翁白头真可怜，伊昔红颜美少年。

直译LiteralThis old man with white hair is truly pitiful; in former days he was a rosy-cheeked handsome youth.

意译MeaningHis white head is truly sad to see — yet once he was a fine young man with a rosy face.

09
公子王孙芳树下，清歌妙舞落花前。

直译LiteralWith young lords and princes under fragrant trees, clear songs and fine dances before falling flowers.

意译MeaningHe sat with lords and princes under blossoming trees, among sweet songs and graceful dances as the petals fell.

10
光禄池台开锦绣，将军楼阁画神仙。

直译LiteralThe Guanglu official’s pools and terraces spread silk brocade; the general’s towers are painted with immortals.

意译MeaningHe knew gardens hung with silk and brocade, and generals’ halls painted with gods and immortals.

11
一朝卧病无相识，三春行乐在谁边？

直译LiteralOne morning he lay ill and no one knew him; the spring pleasures — where are they now?

意译MeaningThen one day he fell ill, and nobody knew him; where did those springtime pleasures go?

12
宛转蛾眉能几时？须臾鹤发乱如丝。

直译LiteralCurving moth-eyebrows — how long can they last? In a moment, crane-white hair, tangled like silk.

意译MeaningHow long can lovely curving eyebrows last? In no time, hair turns white and tangles like silk thread.

13
但看古来歌舞地，惟有黄昏鸟雀悲。

直译LiteralJust look at the places of song and dance since ancient times: only birds and sparrows grieve at dusk.

意译MeaningLook at the old places of song and dance: only the birds are left, crying sadly at dusk.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

题目的意思是“替一位白头老人悲叹”。前半首写洛阳城东的桃花李花随风飘落，不知落到谁家；一位洛阳姑娘爱惜自己的青春容貌，看到落花，就长长地叹气——今年花落，人的容颜也跟着变老，明年花再开，又有谁还在呢？松柏会被砍成柴，桑田会变成大海，古人早已不在，今人仍对着落花。于是出现了全诗最有名的两句：“年年岁岁花相似，岁岁年年人不同”——花每年都差不多，人却一年年老去、换了一批。这两句只是把“年年岁岁”颠倒了一下，却说出了自然永恒、人生短暂的道理。后半首写白头翁：他年轻时也是英俊少年，和公子王孙在花下歌舞；可是一旦生病，就没人认识他了。美人的青春能有多久？转眼间白发乱得像丝。最后只剩黄昏时鸟雀在古时的歌舞场上悲鸣。全诗用少女和老翁对照，提醒年轻人：今天的白头翁，就是明天的你。

In English

The title means “grieving on behalf of a white-haired old man”. The first half begins with peach and plum blossoms east of Luoyang, blown here and there — whose yard will they land in? A young Luoyang girl, proud of her looks, sees the petals fall and sighs: this year the flowers fall and faces fade; when they bloom next year, who will still be here? The poet widens the view — pines are cut down for firewood, mulberry fields turn into sea. The people of the past are gone from east Luoyang; the people of today still face the falling flowers in the wind. Then come the two most famous lines: 年年岁岁花相似，岁岁年年人不同 — “year after year the flowers look alike; year after year the people are not the same.” The poet simply flips the order of 年年 and 岁岁, and the mirror-like pair says it all: nature returns, human life does not. The second half turns to the old man. He too was once a handsome youth, singing and dancing with princes under flowering trees, at home in grand gardens and painted halls. But once he fell ill, nobody knew him any more. How long can a beauty’s youth last? In a moment, white hair tangles like silk thread. In the end, only birds cry at dusk over the old places of song and dance. By setting the girl and the old man side by side, the poem quietly warns the young: today’s old man is tomorrow’s you.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Xiyi (651–c. 680) passed the imperial examination young but never held high office, and died before 30. A famous but doubtful legend says his uncle Song Zhiwen wanted the “year after year” couplet for himself and, when Liu refused, had him killed.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

洛阳城东桃李花，飞来飞去落谁家？

luò yáng chéng dōng táo lǐ huā, fēi lái fēi qù luò shuí jiā

Peach and plum blossoms east of Luoyang drift here and there — whose yard will they settle in?

洛阳女儿惜颜色，坐见落花长叹息。

luò yáng nǚ ér xī yán sè, zuò jiàn luò huā cháng tàn xī

A Luoyang girl, careful of her beauty, watches the petals fall and lets out a long sigh.

今年花落颜色改，明年花开复谁在？

jīn nián huā luò yán sè gǎi, míng nián huā kāi fù shuí zài

This year the flowers fall and faces fade; next year, when they bloom again, who will be left?

已见松柏摧为薪，更闻桑田变成海。

yǐ jiàn sōng bǎi cuī wéi xīn, gèng wén sāng tián biàn chéng hǎi

I have seen pines and cypresses chopped into firewood, and heard of mulberry fields turned to sea.

古人无复洛城东，今人还对落花风。

gǔ rén wú fù luò chéng dōng, jīn rén hái duì luò huā fēng

The people of old are gone from east Luoyang; the people of today still stand in the wind of falling petals.

年年岁岁花相似，岁岁年年人不同。

nián nián suì suì huā xiāng sì, suì suì nián nián rén bù tóng

Every year the flowers look the same; every year the people are different.

寄言全盛红颜子，应怜半死白头翁。

jì yán quán shèng hóng yán zǐ, yīng lián bàn sǐ bái tóu wēng

A word to you young people in your bloom: spare some pity for this half-dead, white-haired old man.

此翁白头真可怜，伊昔红颜美少年。

cǐ wēng bái tóu zhēn kě lián, yī xī hóng yán měi shào nián

His white head is truly sad to see — yet once he was a fine young man with a rosy face.

公子王孙芳树下，清歌妙舞落花前。

gōng zǐ wáng sūn fāng shù xià, qīng gē miào wǔ luò huā qián

He sat with lords and princes under blossoming trees, among sweet songs and graceful dances as the petals fell.

光禄池台开锦绣，将军楼阁画神仙。

guāng lù chí tái kāi jǐn xiù, jiāng jūn lóu gé huà shén xiān

He knew gardens hung with silk and brocade, and generals’ halls painted with gods and immortals.

一朝卧病无相识，三春行乐在谁边？

yī zhāo wò bìng wú xiāng shí, sān chūn xíng lè zài shuí biān

Then one day he fell ill, and nobody knew him; where did those springtime pleasures go?

宛转蛾眉能几时？须臾鹤发乱如丝。

wǎn zhuǎn é méi néng jǐ shí, xū yú hè fà luàn rú sī

How long can lovely curving eyebrows last? In no time, hair turns white and tangles like silk thread.

但看古来歌舞地，惟有黄昏鸟雀悲。

dàn kàn gǔ lái gē wǔ dì, wéi yǒu huáng hūn niǎo què bēi

Look at the old places of song and dance: only the birds are left, crying sadly at dusk.
