导读About this poem

A banquet song from ancient China: deer call to each other as they graze, and a host welcomes his guests with music and gifts. Over the centuries its opening lines became the classic way to say “welcome, honoured friends”.

中文解析

《鹿鸣》是古代宴请宾客时唱的歌。开头写一群鹿在野外吃草，发现好吃的苹草，就“呦呦”地叫着呼唤同伴一起来吃。用鹿的友爱引出主人对客人的热情，这就是《诗经》常用的“起兴”。接着写宴会的热闹：弹起瑟，吹起笙，捧着装满礼物的竹筐送给客人。最后两句说出主人的心意：希望爱护我的朋友们，能给我指出正确的道路。可见这场宴会不只是吃喝，更是真诚地向朋友求教。这首诗影响很大：三国时曹操在《短歌行》里直接引用了“呦呦鹿鸣，食野之苹。我有嘉宾，鼓瑟吹笙”，表达他渴望人才；唐代以后，科举考中的人参加的宴会也叫“鹿鸣宴”。

In English

This was a song performed at banquets for guests. It opens with deer grazing in the wild: when they find good grass, they call “yoo, yoo” to bring their companions over to share it. The friendliness of the deer leads into the host’s warmth towards his guests — the Book of Songs’ classic opening move, xìng (“evoking”). Then comes the party itself: zithers are plucked, reed pipes are played, and baskets of gifts are offered to the guests. The last two lines reveal what the host really wants: he hopes these friends who care about him will show him the right path. So the feast is not just food and music, but a sincere request for good advice. The poem has had a long life. In the Three Kingdoms period, the warlord-poet Cao Cao quoted its first four phrases word for word in his “Short Song”, to show how eagerly he welcomed talented men. From the Tang dynasty on, the banquet held for successful candidates of the imperial exams was called the “Deer Call Banquet” (鹿鸣宴).

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is the first poem of the “Minor Odes” (小雅) in the Book of Songs, compiled roughly between the 11th and 6th centuries BC. Its author is unknown. It is traditionally said to have been sung when the Zhou king feasted his ministers and guests.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

多音字：人之好我的“好”读 hào（喜爱）；示我周行的“行”读 háng（道路）；承筐是将的“将”读 jiāng（奉献）。生僻字：呦呦 yōu yōu；笙 shēng；簧 huáng。食野之苹的“苹”读 píng，指一种艾蒿类野草，不是苹果。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行的《毛诗》文本。全诗三章，每章八句，共二十四句；这里节选首章八句（曹操《短歌行》引用的就是本章前四句），网站按两个分句一行排版，共四行。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

呦呦鹿鸣，食野之苹。

yōu yōu lù míng, shí yě zhī píng

“Yoo, yoo,” the deer call to each other, grazing on the wild grass.

我有嘉宾，鼓瑟吹笙。

wǒ yǒu jiā bīn, gǔ sè chuī shēng

I have honoured guests — let the zithers play and the reed pipes sing.

吹笙鼓簧，承筐是将。

chuī shēng gǔ huáng, chéng kuāng shì jiāng

The reed pipes hum, and baskets of gifts are held out to them.

人之好我，示我周行。

rén zhī hào wǒ, shì wǒ zhōu háng

Friends who care for me will show me the right road to take.
