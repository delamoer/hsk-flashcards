导读About this poem

Written for a wife who died young: once you have seen the ocean, no other water is worth the name; once you have seen the clouds of Mount Wu, nothing else is cloud. Its first two lines are still quoted for a love that cannot be replaced.

中文解析

这首诗是元稹悼念亡妻韦丛的组诗中的一首。前两句用了两个比喻：见过大海的人，别的水都很难再叫作“水”；看过巫山云的人，别处的云都算不上“云”。沧海和巫山云都是世上最壮美的景物，诗人用它们比喻妻子和他们的爱情——有过最好的，别的再也看不上了。第三句说：如今就算走过花丛（比喻遇到许多美丽的女子），也懒得回头看。第四句说原因：一半是因为在修道，一半是因为你。“半缘君”三个字最打动人，前面所有的话都落在这个“君”字上。前两句后来变成了成语一样的名句，常用来形容感情专一、无可替代。不过元稹本人的感情生活并不简单，读者可以把诗和诗人分开来看。

In English

This poem belongs to a set Yuan Zhen wrote in memory of his wife, Wei Cong. The first two lines are two comparisons: someone who has seen the ocean can hardly call any other water “water”; someone who has seen the clouds over Mount Wu won’t count any other cloud as “cloud”. The ocean and the legendary clouds of Mount Wu are the grandest sights in the world, and they stand for his wife and their love: having known the best, nothing else measures up. Line three: now, even passing through a crowd of flowers — a common image for beautiful women — he can’t be bothered to look back. Line four gives the reason: half because he is practising the Way (a spiritual discipline), half because of you. Those last words, 半缘君, carry the weight of everything before them. The opening couplet became almost a proverb for a love that cannot be replaced. That said, Yuan Zhen’s own love life was far from simple, and readers are free to separate the poem from the man.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yuan Zhen (779–831) was a Tang official and poet, a close friend of Bai Juyi. His wife Wei Cong died in 809 at the age of 27, and he wrote several sequences of poems mourning her; this is traditionally read as one of them.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

曾经沧海难为水，

céng jīng cāng hǎi nán wéi shuǐ

Once you have known the sea, no other water is water;

除却巫山不是云。

chú què wū shān bù shì yún

once you have seen Wu’s clouds, nothing else is cloud.

取次花丛懒回顾，

qǔ cì huā cóng lǎn huí gù

I wander past whole gardens of flowers and never turn my head —

半缘修道半缘君。

bàn yuán xiū dào bàn yuán jūn

half for the path I follow now, and half for you.
