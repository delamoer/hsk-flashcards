导读About this poem

A farmer’s calendar sung some 3,000 years ago: the Fire Star sinks in the sky, the cold winds come, and in spring the whole family goes out to the fields. This opening stanza is the start of the longest farming song in the Book of Songs.

七月流火，九月授衣。

直译LiteralIn the seventh month the Fire Star sinks west; in the ninth month clothes are handed out.

意译MeaningIn the seventh month the Fire Star slides down the sky; by the ninth, it is time to hand out warm clothes.

02
一之日觱发，二之日栗烈。

直译LiteralIn the days of the first month the wind blows cold; in the days of the second, the air is bitter.

意译MeaningThen comes the month of howling wind, then the month of biting cold.

03
无衣无褐，何以卒岁？

直译LiteralWithout clothes, without coarse wool — how can we reach the end of the year?

意译MeaningNo clothes, not even rough wool — how will we last out the year?

04
三之日于耜，四之日举趾。

直译LiteralIn the days of the third month we mend the ploughs; in the days of the fourth, we lift our feet into the fields.

意译MeaningIn the third month we fix our ploughs; in the fourth we step out into the fields.

05
同我妇子，馌彼南亩，田畯至喜。

直译LiteralWith my wife and children, we bring food to those southern fields; the field officer comes and is glad.

意译MeaningWives and children carry meals out to the southern fields, and the overseer comes by, smiling.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《七月》是《诗经》里最长的一首农事诗，共八章，一个月一个月地唱农家一年的活儿，像一本唱出来的农家日历。这里选的是第一章。开头“七月流火”说的是天上的大火星（心宿二）往西落下去，表示暑热过去、秋天快到了——不是说七月热得像火，今天很多人这样误用。接着从寒冬写到开春：冬天风冷刺骨，没有衣服怎么熬过年底？天一暖，就修好农具下地，妻子孩子把饭送到田头，管农事的官看了也高兴。诗里的“一之日、二之日”是周代的历法，大约相当于农历的十一月、十二月。全章没有一句直接抒情，只是一件件写农活和天气，却让人感到农民对季节的敏感、过冬的忧虑和春耕的忙碌。读它，像看到三千年前中国乡村的真实生活。

In English

“The Seventh Month” is the longest farming song in the Book of Songs: eight stanzas that walk month by month through a farming family’s year, like a calendar you can sing. This is the first stanza. The famous opening 七月流火 does not mean “July is as hot as fire”, a common modern misreading. 火 is the Fire Star (Antares), and 流 means it is sliding west and down the sky — a sign that summer is ending and autumn is near. So in the ninth month, warm clothes are handed out. The song then jumps into the hard months: biting winds, bitter cold, and the worry of having no proper clothes to last the year. Then spring: the ploughs are mended, people step into the fields, wives and children carry food out to the workers, and the official in charge of the farms is pleased. The odd phrases 一之日, 二之日 (“the days of the first, the second”) use the Zhou calendar, roughly the eleventh and twelfth months of the later lunar calendar. There is no line of open feeling here — only weather and work — yet you sense how closely these people watched the sky, how they feared the winter, and how busy spring was. It is one of the best windows we have onto daily life in ancient China.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC; most authors are unknown. This one belongs to the “Airs of Bin” (豳风), from the area of present-day Shaanxi where the Zhou people are said to have farmed before founding their dynasty.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

觱发 bì bō：“发”按旧注读 bō（形容寒风），也有注本读 fā，当前采用 bì bō。栗烈 lì liè；褐 hè；卒岁 zú suì；耜 sì；馌 yè；田畯 tián jùn；南亩 nán mǔ。“一之日”的“一”逐字注本调 yī。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行的《毛诗》文本。《七月》全诗八章、八十八句，这里只选第一章（十一句）。“栗烈”有的版本作“栗冽”。网站按两个分句一行排版，最后一行三个分句。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

七月流火，九月授衣。

qī yuè liú huǒ, jiǔ yuè shòu yī

In the seventh month the Fire Star slides down the sky; by the ninth, it is time to hand out warm clothes.

一之日觱发，二之日栗烈。

yī zhī rì bì bō, èr zhī rì lì liè

Then comes the month of howling wind, then the month of biting cold.

无衣无褐，何以卒岁？

wú yī wú hè, hé yǐ zú suì

No clothes, not even rough wool — how will we last out the year?

三之日于耜，四之日举趾。

sān zhī rì yú sì, sì zhī rì jǔ zhǐ

In the third month we fix our ploughs; in the fourth we step out into the fields.

同我妇子，馌彼南亩，田畯至喜。

tóng wǒ fù zǐ, yè bǐ nán mǔ, tián jùn zhì xǐ

Wives and children carry meals out to the southern fields, and the overseer comes by, smiling.
