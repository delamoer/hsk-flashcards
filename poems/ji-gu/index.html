导读About this poem

A soldier sent far south to war cannot go home, loses his horse in the woods, and remembers a promise: “to hold your hand and grow old with you.” That line is now one of the most loved expressions of lifelong love in Chinese.

第一章Stanza 1
01
击鼓其镗，踊跃用兵。

直译LiteralThe drums are beaten, boom, boom; eagerly we train with weapons.

意译MeaningThe war drums boom; men leap to their weapons.

02
土国城漕，我独南行。

直译LiteralSome build earthworks in the capital, some build walls at Cao; I alone march south.

意译MeaningOthers stay home to build walls at Cao — I alone am sent marching south.

03
从孙子仲，平陈与宋。

直译LiteralFollowing Sun Zizhong, we settle peace between Chen and Song.

意译MeaningUnder General Sun Zizhong we go to make peace between Chen and Song.

04
不我以归，忧心有忡。

直译LiteralHe does not let me go home; my worried heart is troubled.

意译MeaningThey will not let me go home, and my heart is heavy with worry.

第三章Stanza 3
05
爰居爰处？爰丧其马？

直译LiteralWhere do we stay, where do we stop? Where did we lose our horses?

意译MeaningWhere will we camp, where will we rest? Where has my horse wandered off to?

06

直译LiteralWhere shall we look for them? Down in the woods.

意译MeaningWhere should I look for it? Down among the trees.

第四章Stanza 4

直译LiteralIn death or life, together or apart — with you I made this vow.

意译Meaning“In life or in death, near or far,” I promised you,

直译LiteralTo hold your hand, and grow old together with you.

意译Meaning“I will hold your hand and grow old with you.”

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是一个士兵的自述。开头战鼓咚咚，别人在国内修城墙，只有他被派到南方打仗。仗打完了，却不让他回家，他心里非常忧愁。第三章写行军中的狼狈：住在哪里？马丢在哪里？最后在树林里才找到——这些小事写出了士兵的疲惫和迷茫。第四章笔锋一转，回忆从前和亲人立下的誓言：“死生契阔，与子成说。执子之手，与子偕老。”不论生死离合都不分开，要拉着你的手一起变老。最后一章是绝望的叹息：离得太远了，恐怕活着见不到你，誓言也无法实现。前面写战争的苦，后面写誓言的深，对比之下更让人心痛。“执子之手，与子偕老”今天常用在婚礼上，表达白头到老的爱情；也有学者认为原诗是战友之间的约定。

In English

The poem is told by a soldier. It opens with war drums booming. Other men stay home building city walls, but he alone is sent south to fight. Even when the campaign is over, he is not allowed to return, and he is sick with worry. The third stanza shows how lost and worn out he is: Where do we camp? Where is my horse? He finally finds it somewhere in the woods — small, real details of army life. Then the fourth stanza suddenly looks back to a vow he once made: “In life or death, together or apart, I promised you: I will hold your hand and grow old with you.” The last stanza is a cry of despair: we are so far apart that I may not live to see you, and I cannot keep my word. The hardship of war in the first half makes the tenderness of the vow hurt even more. Today 执子之手，与子偕老 is a favourite line at Chinese weddings, meaning “to grow old together”. Most readers hear it as words to a wife; some scholars think the vow was originally made between comrades in arms.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The poem is one of the “Airs of Bei” (邶风), songs of the region around the state of Wey (卫) in today’s Henan, collected in the Book of Songs. Its author is unknown. Traditional commentators link it to a campaign of around 719 BC, when Wey joined the states of Chen and Song in a war against Zheng.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

生僻字：镗 tāng、漕 cáo、忡 chōng、爰 yuán、偕 xié、洵 xún。多音字：爰丧其马的“丧”读 sàng；契阔 qì kuò（契不读 qiè）；与子成说的“说”读 shuō（誓约）。以下各注本不一，此处采用多数注本：于嗟 xū jiē（于通“吁”，也有注 yú jiē）；不我信兮的“信”读 shēn（通“申”，实践誓言；也有直接读 xìn）；不我活兮的“活”读 huó（《韩诗》作“佸”，读 huó 或 kuò，相会之意）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

击鼓其镗，踊跃用兵。

jī gǔ qí tāng, yǒng yuè yòng bīng

The war drums boom; men leap to their weapons.

土国城漕，我独南行。

tǔ guó chéng cáo, wǒ dú nán xíng

Others stay home to build walls at Cao — I alone am sent marching south.

从孙子仲，平陈与宋。

cóng sūn zǐ zhòng, píng chén yǔ sòng

Under General Sun Zizhong we go to make peace between Chen and Song.

不我以归，忧心有忡。

bù wǒ yǐ guī, yōu xīn yǒu chōng

They will not let me go home, and my heart is heavy with worry.

爰居爰处？爰丧其马？

yuán jū yuán chǔ, yuán sàng qí mǎ

Where will we camp, where will we rest? Where has my horse wandered off to?

于以求之？于林之下。

yú yǐ qiú zhī, yú lín zhī xià

Where should I look for it? Down among the trees.

死生契阔，与子成说。

sǐ shēng qì kuò, yǔ zǐ chéng shuō

“In life or in death, near or far,” I promised you,

执子之手，与子偕老。

zhí zǐ zhī shǒu, yǔ zǐ xié lǎo

“I will hold your hand and grow old with you.”

于嗟阔兮，不我活兮。

xū jiē kuò xī, bù wǒ huó xī

Oh, how far apart we are — I may not live to see you again.

于嗟洵兮，不我信兮。

xū jiē xún xī, bù wǒ shēn xī

Oh, how long the road — I cannot keep my word to you.
