导读About this poem

Twenty characters with a sharp edge. Li Qingzhao praises the defeated hero Xiang Yu, who chose death rather than flee across the river — a pointed rebuke to a Song court that kept running south from the invaders.

中文解析

这首诗一般认为写于宋朝南渡之后。金兵攻占北方，宋朝皇帝和大臣一路往南逃，不敢收复失地。李清照借古讽今：活着就要做人中的豪杰，死了也要做鬼中的英雄。接着她想到项羽——秦末的英雄，兵败退到乌江边，有人劝他渡江回江东重新开始，他觉得没脸见江东父老，不肯过江，在江边自尽。“至今思项羽”表面上是怀念古人，其实是在问：今天的人为什么只会逃跑？诗只有二十个字，没有一个字直接批评朝廷，但对比非常鲜明。一位女诗人写出这样刚烈的句子，所以特别有名，前两句今天还常被引用。

In English

This short poem is usually dated to the years after the Song court fled south. The Jurchen Jin had taken northern China, and the emperor and his ministers kept retreating rather than fight to win it back. Li Qingzhao answers with history. The first couplet is a statement of values: in life, be a hero among men; in death, be a hero among ghosts. Then she turns to Xiang Yu, the great warlord who lost to the founder of the Han dynasty around 202 BC. Trapped at the Wu River, he was offered a boat to escape east of the Yangtze, where he could raise a new army. He refused — he could not face the families of the soldiers he had lost — and died on the riverbank. “To this day I think of Xiang Yu, who would not cross to the east” looks like simple admiration for an ancient hero. But the contrast is obvious to any reader: Xiang Yu would not run, and the Song court did nothing but run. The poem never criticises anyone by name, which makes the point sharper. Strong, bare lines like these, from a poet best known for delicate love lyrics, are why the first two lines are still quoted today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Qingzhao (1084–c. 1155) fled south after the Jin conquest of the north in 1127. The poem is traditionally linked to 1129, around the time her husband abandoned his post in Jiankang (Nanjing) during a mutiny — some read it as aimed at him as well as at the court.

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本，题一作《乌江》。异文：“作”“为”二字一本互换（“生当为人杰，死亦作鬼雄”）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

生当作人杰，

shēng dāng zuò rén jié

Alive, be the bravest of the living;

死亦为鬼雄。

sǐ yì wéi guǐ xióng

dead, be the boldest of the dead.

至今思项羽，

zhì jīn sī xiàng yǔ

I still think of Xiang Yu

不肯过江东。

bù kěn guò jiāng dōng

who would not cross the river to save himself.
