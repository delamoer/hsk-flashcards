导读About this poem

A young poet stands in a grand old pavilion by the river: the prince who built it is long gone, the parties are over, and only the river keeps flowing. A short poem about how quickly glory passes.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

滕王阁在今天江西南昌的赣江边，是唐太宗的弟弟滕王李元婴建的。王勃路过这里，写了著名的《滕王阁序》，这首诗就附在序的后面。前两句写滕王阁高高地靠着江边，可当年佩玉叮当、车铃响亮的歌舞早已结束。三、四句写空荡荡的阁楼：早上南浦的云飞过彩画的屋梁，晚上西山的雨打在卷起的珠帘上，没有人来看。五、六句由景入情：云在飘，潭水里的影子天天一样，可是“物换星移”——万物在变，星星在移动，已经过了多少个秋天。最后一问一答：当年住在这里的王子现在在哪儿？只有栏杆外的长江白白地流着。“空”字最有力：江水不管人间的兴衰，照样流。这种借眼前景物感叹历史变化的写法叫“怀古”。“物换星移”今天还是常用成语。

In English

The Pavilion of Prince Teng stands by the Gan River in today’s Nanchang. It was built by Li Yuanying, Prince Teng, a younger brother of Emperor Taizong. When the young Wang Bo passed by, he wrote a famous prose preface for a banquet there, and this short poem comes at the end of it. The first two lines set the scene: the tall pavilion leans over the river, but the jingling jade and carriage bells of the prince’s parties are long over. Lines three and four show the empty building: morning clouds from the southern shore drift past the painted beams, and evening rain from the western hills blows against the rolled-up pearl curtains — with nobody there to see. Then the poem turns from scene to thought. Clouds float, the reflections in the pool look the same day after day, yet “things change and the stars move” (物换星移) — how many autumns have gone by? The ending asks and answers at once: where is the prince now? Beyond the railing, the Yangtze flows on “in vain” (空). The river does not care who rises or falls. Using a real place to reflect on the passing of history is called huáigǔ, “thinking of the past”. The phrase 物换星移 is still a common idiom for the passage of time.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Bo (c. 650–676) was one of the “Four Paragons of the Early Tang”. In 675, on his way south to visit his father, he attended a banquet at the pavilion and is said to have written the preface and this poem on the spot. He drowned crossing the sea the next year, aged about 26.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

槛 jiàn（栏杆，不读 kǎn）；渚 zhǔ；鸾 luán；画栋朝飞 的“朝”读 zhāo（早晨）；几度 jǐ。机器朗读“朝”“槛”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用《滕王阁序》篇末所附诗的通行文本。异文：“珠帘”一作“朱帘”，“几度秋”一作“度几秋”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

滕王高阁临江渚，

téng wáng gāo gé lín jiāng zhǔ

Prince Teng’s tall pavilion leans over the river bank,

佩玉鸣鸾罢歌舞。

pèi yù míng luán bà gē wǔ

but the jingle of jade and carriage bells, the songs and dances, are done.

画栋朝飞南浦云，

huà dòng zhāo fēi nán pǔ yún

Mornings, clouds from the south shore drift past the painted beams;

珠帘暮卷西山雨。

zhū lián mù juǎn xī shān yǔ

evenings, rain from the western hills beats on the rolled-up pearl blinds.

闲云潭影日悠悠，

xián yún tán yǐng rì yōu yōu

Idle clouds and pool reflections, the same day after day —

物换星移几度秋。

wù huàn xīng yí jǐ dù qiū

yet things change and stars wheel on: how many autumns now?

阁中帝子今何在？

gé zhōng dì zǐ jīn hé zài

And the prince who lived up here — where is he today?

槛外长江空自流。

jiàn wài cháng jiāng kōng zì liú

Beyond the railing the Yangtze just keeps flowing, for no one.
