Féng Xuě Sù Fúróng Shān Zhǔrén
Caught in the Snow, Staying the Night with the Master of Hibiscus Mountain

导读About this poem

Twenty characters, four quick pictures: blue hills at dusk, a poor thatched hut, a dog barking at the gate — and someone coming home through the wind and snow.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人在雪夜投宿山中人家的经历。第一句写傍晚赶路，远处青山显得越来越远，路还很长；第二句写天气寒冷，终于看到一间简陋的茅屋，白屋（没有装饰的草房）显得更加清贫。后两句写夜里：柴门外忽然传来狗叫声，原来是有人顶着风雪回家来了。这个“夜归人”是谁？可能是这家的主人，也可能是诗人自己（傍晚到达时的情景）——历来有不同理解，正好给读者留下想象的余地。全诗只有二十个字，却像四个电影镜头：远山、茅屋、狗叫、风雪中归来的人，有画面也有声音。冷清的山村夜晚里，一声犬吠、一个归人，带来了一点人间的温暖。

In English

This poem records a night when the poet, caught by snow, found shelter in a mountain cottage. In line one he is still on the road at dusk, and the blue-grey hills seem to grow farther away. In line two, in the bitter cold, he reaches a humble hut — a “white house”, meaning a bare thatched cottage with no decoration — which looks all the poorer in the snow. The second half moves to night: a dog suddenly barks at the brushwood gate, and someone comes home through the wind and snow. Who is this person? Perhaps the master of the house returning late, perhaps the poet himself arriving — readers have long disagreed, and the open question leaves room for imagination. With just twenty characters the poem works like four film shots — distant hills, a poor hut, a barking dog, a figure in the snowstorm — with both pictures and sound. In a cold, lonely mountain night, one bark and one homecoming bring a small warmth of human life.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Changqing (c. 726–c. 790) was a mid-Tang poet admired for his five-character poems, which he was called the “Great Wall” of. He was demoted and exiled twice; this poem is thought to date from his years of wandering.

日暮苍山远，

rì mù cāng shān yuǎn

fēng xuě yè guī rén
