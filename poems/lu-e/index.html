Tall Grows the Mugwort

导读About this poem

One of the most moving poems about parents ever written in Chinese. A son who could not care for his father and mother before they died lists everything they did for him — “gave birth to me, fed me, carried me” — and cries that he can never repay them.

蓼蓼者莪，匪莪伊蒿。

直译LiteralTall, tall is the mugwort — no, not mugwort, it is wormwood.

意译MeaningTall grows the mugwort — but look closer: it is only weeds.

直译LiteralAlas, alas, my father and mother — bearing me, they toiled hard.

意译MeaningOh, my poor father and mother, how hard you worked to raise me.

第二章Stanza 2
03
蓼蓼者莪，匪莪伊蔚。

直译LiteralTall, tall is the mugwort — no, not mugwort, it is artemisia weed.

意译MeaningTall grows the mugwort — but look closer: it is only weeds.

04
哀哀父母，生我劳瘁。

直译LiteralAlas, alas, my father and mother — bearing me, they wore themselves out.

意译MeaningOh, my poor father and mother, raising me wore you out.

第三章Stanza 3
05
瓶之罄矣，维罍之耻。

直译LiteralWhen the small jar is empty, it is the shame of the big jar.

意译MeaningWhen the little jar runs dry, the shame belongs to the big one.

06
鲜民之生，不如死之久矣。

直译LiteralThe life of an orphaned man — better to have died long ago.

意译MeaningAn orphan’s life? Better to have died long ago.

07
无父何怙？无母何恃？

直译LiteralWithout a father, on whom to rely? Without a mother, on whom to depend?

意译MeaningWith no father, who will I lean on? With no mother, who will I trust?

08
出则衔恤，入则靡至。

直译LiteralGoing out, I carry grief; coming in, there is no one to go to.

意译MeaningI go out carrying my grief; I come home, and there is no one there.

第四章Stanza 4
09
父兮生我，母兮鞠我。

直译LiteralFather, you begot me; mother, you nourished me.

意译MeaningFather, you gave me life; mother, you fed me.

10
拊我畜我，长我育我。

直译LiteralYou stroked me, fed me; you raised me, reared me.

意译MeaningYou stroked me, you fed me, you helped me grow, you brought me up.

11
顾我复我，出入腹我。

直译LiteralYou watched me, came back to me; going out and in, you held me to your belly.

意译MeaningYou watched over me, you kept coming back to me, you carried me in your arms wherever you went.

12
欲报之德，昊天罔极！

直译LiteralI wish to repay this kindness — vast heaven, without limit!

意译MeaningI want to repay all that love — but it is as boundless as the sky.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首儿子悼念父母的诗。开头用起兴：长得高高的“莪”（一种好草），走近一看却是没用的“蒿”——诗人说自己就像这蒿，没能成为父母期望的好孩子，也没能报答他们。第三章用瓶和罍作比：小瓶空了，是大罍的耻辱，暗指子女不能养活父母，是做儿女的羞耻。“无父何怙？无母何恃？”——没有了父母，我还能依靠谁？第四章最有名，一口气用了九个动词：生、鞠、拊、畜、长、育、顾、复、腹，把父母从生下孩子、喂养、抚摸、照看、抱在怀里的辛苦一样样写出来，读起来像在哭诉。最后一句“昊天罔极”：父母的恩情像天一样大，怎么也报答不完。成语“哀哀父母”“昊天罔极”都出自这里，古人常在父母的祭日读这首诗。

In English

A son mourns his parents. The poem opens with xìng (“evoking”): what looks like tall, fine mugwort (莪) turns out to be useless wormwood (蒿) — the son feels he has become worthless too, never the child his parents hoped for and never able to repay them. In the third verse a small jar has run dry, and that is a shame on the big jar: when children cannot provide for their parents, it is the children’s disgrace. Then comes the cry “Without a father, whom can I lean on? Without a mother, whom can I trust?” The fourth verse is the most famous. In four short lines it piles up nine verbs — bore me, nourished me, stroked me, fed me, raised me, reared me, watched over me, came back to me, held me — every small, tiring thing a parent does, and the rhythm sounds like someone sobbing. It ends: their kindness is as vast as the sky, and I can never repay it. People in imperial China were known to stop reading this poem in tears; phrases from it are still used in Chinese funeral writing today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one is from the “Lesser Odes” (小雅). Traditional commentators said it was written by a man kept away on forced labour or military service who could not look after his parents before they died.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

蓼 lù（“蓼蓼”是高大的样子，不读 liǎo）；莪 é；蒿 hāo；劬 qú；蔚 wèi；瘁 cuì；罄 qìng；罍 léi；鲜 xiǎn（少）；怙 hù；恃 shì；衔恤 xián xù；鞠 jū；拊 fǔ；畜 xù（养育，不读 chù）；长 zhǎng；复 fù；腹 fù；昊 hào。机器朗读易错处已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

蓼蓼者莪，匪莪伊蒿。

lù lù zhě é, fěi é yī hāo

Tall grows the mugwort — but look closer: it is only weeds.

哀哀父母，生我劬劳。

āi āi fù mǔ, shēng wǒ qú láo

Oh, my poor father and mother, how hard you worked to raise me.

蓼蓼者莪，匪莪伊蔚。

lù lù zhě é, fěi é yī wèi

Tall grows the mugwort — but look closer: it is only weeds.

哀哀父母，生我劳瘁。

āi āi fù mǔ, shēng wǒ láo cuì

Oh, my poor father and mother, raising me wore you out.

瓶之罄矣，维罍之耻。

píng zhī qìng yǐ, wéi léi zhī chǐ

When the little jar runs dry, the shame belongs to the big one.

鲜民之生，不如死之久矣。

xiǎn mín zhī shēng, bù rú sǐ zhī jiǔ yǐ

无父何怙？无母何恃？

wú fù hé hù, wú mǔ hé shì

chū zé xián xù, rù zé mí zhì

父兮生我，母兮鞠我。
