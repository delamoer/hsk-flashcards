导读About this poem

A willow in early spring looks like a lady dressed in green jade with ten thousand silk ribbons — and who cut its tiny leaves? The spring wind, sharp as scissors.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首写柳树的诗，专门描写一种东西的诗叫“咏物诗”。第一句把一棵高高的柳树比作用碧玉打扮起来的美人，“碧玉”写出嫩绿的颜色，也像一位年轻的姑娘。第二句写无数柳条垂下来，像一条条绿色的丝带。第三句诗人提出一个问题：这些细细的柳叶，是谁剪出来的呢？第四句自己回答：是二月的春风，它就像一把剪刀。春风本来看不见、摸不着，诗人却把它比作剪刀，说柳叶是它“剪”出来的，非常新奇。全诗连用比喻：柳树像美人、柳条像丝带、春风像剪刀，一句比一句有想象力，写出了早春的生机和诗人的喜悦。“二月春风似剪刀”今天仍是写春天最常引用的句子之一。

In English

This is a yǒngwù poem — a poem written to describe one object. The object is a willow tree in early spring. The first line compares the tall willow to a woman adorned in green jade: 碧玉 gives the tender, bright-green colour, and it was also a set phrase for a pretty young girl. In the second line, ten thousand branches hang down like ribbons of green silk. Then the poet asks a playful question: those tiny, slender leaves — who cut them out? And he answers himself: the spring wind of the second month, sharp as a pair of scissors. Wind is invisible; you cannot hold it. Turning it into scissors that snip out leaves is a surprising, delightful image. The whole poem is a chain of comparisons — tree as lady, branches as ribbons, wind as scissors — each one more imaginative than the last, and together they catch the freshness of early spring and the poet’s pleasure in it. The second month of the lunar calendar is around March, when willows are the first trees to turn green in northern China. 二月春风似剪刀 is still one of the most quoted lines about spring.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

He Zhizhang (659–744) was a senior Tang official, calligrapher and poet, admired for his easy-going character. When he wrote this poem is not known; it is one of his two most famous works, with “Coming Home”.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“丝绦”的“绦”读 tāo。“谁裁出”的“谁”按统编教材读 shuí（口语也读 shéi）。其余无特殊读音。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

碧玉妆成一树高，

bì yù zhuāng chéng yī shù gāo

Dressed in green jade, a tall willow stands,

万条垂下绿丝绦。

wàn tiáo chuí xià lǜ sī tāo

ten thousand branches hanging down like green silk ribbons.

不知细叶谁裁出，

bù zhī xì yè shuí cái chū

Who cut out all these tiny leaves?

二月春风似剪刀。

èr yuè chūn fēng sì jiǎn dāo

The spring wind of March, sharp as scissors.
