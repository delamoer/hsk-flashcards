导读About this poem

Don’t treasure a robe of gold thread — treasure your youth. Pick the flower while it’s blooming, before you’re left holding a bare branch. China’s own “seize the day” poem.

这首诗被谱成了歌Sung as a song

Ye Xuanqing sings "The Gold-Threaded Robe" (Du Qiuniang), the poem heard as a song in the TV drama Empresses in the Palace on CCTV's poetry-in-song show Everlasting Classics (season 1, 2018), setting the original text to music.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗只有一个意思：珍惜青春。前两句用了两个“劝君”，一个说“莫惜”，一个说“惜取”：不要太看重用金线绣成的华丽衣服，要珍惜少年时光。衣服再贵还能再做，青春过去了就回不来了。后两句用了一个比喻：花开了，能折的时候就赶快折，不要等到花谢了，只能折一根空树枝。这首诗读起来像唱歌一样，“劝君”“惜”“折”都重复出现，朗朗上口，非常好记。“花开堪折直须折”今天还常被引用，意思是机会来了要抓住，不要等到失去了才后悔。

In English

The whole poem says one thing: make the most of your youth. The first two lines both begin “I urge you” and play “don’t treasure” against “do treasure”: don’t set too much store by a splendid robe embroidered with gold thread; treasure the years when you are young. A costly robe can always be made again, but youth, once gone, never comes back. The second half turns this into a picture: when a flower is open and ready to be picked, pick it — don’t wait until the blossoms are gone and you are left snapping off an empty branch. Because the words 劝君 (“I urge you”), 惜 (“to treasure”) and 折 (“to pick, to snap off”) keep coming back, the poem sounds like a song and sticks in the memory, which is exactly what it was: a tune sung at banquets. The line about picking the flower while you can is still quoted today to mean “grab the chance while you have it, or you’ll regret it later”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The poem is traditionally attributed to Du Qiuniang (c. 791–?), a talented singer who later served in the Tang palace; the poet Du Mu wrote that she used to sing it. Many scholars think it was an anonymous popular song of the time, and some editions list it as anonymous.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

劝君莫惜金缕衣，

quàn jūn mò xī jīn lǚ yī

Don’t set your heart on a robe of golden thread;

劝君惜取少年时。

quàn jūn xī qǔ shào nián shí

set it on the years while you are young.

花开堪折直须折，

huā kāi kān zhé zhí xū zhé

When the flower is open and ready, pick it now —

莫待无花空折枝。

mò dài wú huā kōng zhé zhī

don’t wait until it’s gone and break off a bare branch.
