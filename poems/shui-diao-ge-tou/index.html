导读About this poem

The Mid-Autumn Festival poem: on a full-moon night, a little drunk and missing his brother, Su Shi talks to the moon — and ends with a wish every Chinese family knows, that loved ones far apart can share the same moonlight.

直译LiteralI don’t know, in the palaces in heaven, what year tonight is.

意译MeaningUp in the palaces of heaven, I wonder, what year is it tonight?

直译LiteralI want to ride the wind and go back there,

意译MeaningI would like to ride the wind and go home to the sky,

直译Literalbut fear the jade towers and jade halls — so high up, I couldn’t bear the cold.

意译Meaningbut I fear those towers of jade would be too cold, so high above the world.

05

直译LiteralI rise and dance, playing with my clear shadow — how could that compare to being in the human world?

意译MeaningSo I dance with my moonlit shadow — what heaven could beat being here among people?

06

直译LiteralIt turns round the red pavilion, lowers to the carved windows, shines on the sleepless.

意译MeaningThe moonlight circles the red pavilion, sinks to the latticed windows, and finds me still awake.

07

直译LiteralIt should have no grudge — why is it always full at times of parting?

意译MeaningThe moon can bear us no ill will — so why is it always full when people are apart?

08

直译LiteralPeople have sorrow and joy, parting and meeting; the moon has dark and bright, full and waning;

意译MeaningPeople have their sorrows and joys, their partings and reunions; the moon is dim or bright, full or thin;

09

直译Literalthis has been hard to make perfect since ancient times.

意译Meaningnothing has ever been perfect, since time began.

10

直译LiteralI only wish people last long, and a thousand li apart share the lovely moon.

意译MeaningI only wish that we may live long, and, a thousand miles apart, share this lovely moon.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写于中秋节，苏轼在山东密州做官，已经七年没见到弟弟苏辙（字子由）。那天晚上他喝酒喝到天亮，写下了这首词。上阕他拿着酒杯问天：月亮是什么时候有的？天上的宫殿今晚是哪一年？他想乘风飞到月宫，又怕那里太高太冷，不如在人间跳舞、和自己的影子玩。这里能看出他在“出世”（离开人间）和“入世”（留在人间）之间犹豫，最后还是选择了人间。下阕写月光转过楼阁，照着睡不着的人。他先埋怨月亮：为什么总在人们分别的时候圆？接着又想通了：人有悲欢离合，月有阴晴圆缺，自古就难两全。最后的愿望是“但愿人长久，千里共婵娟”——只要人都平安，虽然相隔千里，也能一起看这美丽的月亮。从埋怨到释然，这就是苏轼的豁达（想得开）。这首词被谱成歌《但愿人长久》，邓丽君、王菲都唱过。

In English

Su Shi wrote this on the Mid-Autumn Festival of 1076, when he was a local official in Mizhou (in today’s Shandong) and had not seen his younger brother Su Zhe for seven years. A short preface says he drank happily until dawn, got very drunk, and wrote this while thinking of his brother. In the first half he raises his cup and questions the sky: when did the moon first appear? What year is it tonight in the palaces of heaven? He would like to ride the wind up there, but fears it would be too cold so high up — better to stay down here and dance with his own shadow. You can feel him torn between leaving the human world and staying in it, and he chooses to stay. In the second half the moonlight moves round the red pavilion and shines on someone who cannot sleep. At first he complains: why is the moon always full when people are apart? Then he lets it go. People have joy and sorrow, partings and reunions; the moon waxes and wanes — nothing has ever been perfect. His final wish is simple: may we all live long, and though a thousand miles apart, share the same lovely moon. That turn from complaint to calm acceptance is classic Su Shi. The poem became a hugely popular song, 但愿人长久, sung by Teresa Teng and later Faye Wong.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Su Shi (1037–1101), also called Su Dongpo, was a statesman, painter, calligrapher and one of the greatest writers of the Song dynasty. His preface dates this lyric to the Mid-Autumn Festival of the bingchen year (1076), written “while thinking of Ziyou” — his brother Su Zhe.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

明月几时有？把酒问青天。

míng yuè jǐ shí yǒu, bǎ jiǔ wèn qīng tiān

Since when has there been a bright moon? Cup in hand, I ask the clear blue sky.

不知天上宫阙，今夕是何年。

bù zhī tiān shàng gōng què, jīn xī shì hé nián

Up in the palaces of heaven, I wonder, what year is it tonight?

我欲乘风归去，

wǒ yù chéng fēng guī qù

I would like to ride the wind and go home to the sky,

又恐琼楼玉宇，高处不胜寒。

yòu kǒng qióng lóu yù yǔ, gāo chù bù shèng hán

but I fear those towers of jade would be too cold, so high above the world.

起舞弄清影，何似在人间。

qǐ wǔ nòng qīng yǐng, hé sì zài rén jiān

So I dance with my moonlit shadow — what heaven could beat being here among people?

转朱阁，低绮户，照无眠。

zhuǎn zhū gé, dī qǐ hù, zhào wú mián

The moonlight circles the red pavilion, sinks to the latticed windows, and finds me still awake.

不应有恨，何事长向别时圆？

bù yīng yǒu hèn, hé shì cháng xiàng bié shí yuán

The moon can bear us no ill will — so why is it always full when people are apart?

人有悲欢离合，月有阴晴圆缺，

rén yǒu bēi huān lí hé, yuè yǒu yīn qíng yuán quē

People have their sorrows and joys, their partings and reunions; the moon is dim or bright, full or thin;

此事古难全。

cǐ shì gǔ nán quán

nothing has ever been perfect, since time began.

但愿人长久，千里共婵娟。

dàn yuàn rén cháng jiǔ, qiān lǐ gòng chán juān

I only wish that we may live long, and, a thousand miles apart, share this lovely moon.
