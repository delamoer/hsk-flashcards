A mother sews her son’s clothes the night before he leaves, stitch by tight stitch. Six short lines that every Chinese child learns — and the most loved poem about a mother’s love.

中文解析

这首诗写母亲为要出远门的儿子缝衣服。前两句很简单：慈母手里的线，变成了游子身上的衣服。一根线把母亲和儿子连在一起。三、四句写出一个细节：临走之前，母亲一针一针缝得又密又结实，心里担心儿子很久很久才能回来，衣服要穿得久。母亲什么话也没说，爱全在针线里。最后两句用比喻：小草那一点点心意，怎么能报答春天阳光的恩情呢？小草比儿女，春天的阳光比母爱。这两句告诉我们，母亲的爱太大了，儿女永远报答不完。诗写得像说家常话，没有难字，所以几乎每个中国人都会背，今天母亲节也常被引用。

In English

A son is about to leave home, and his mother sews his clothes. The first two lines are almost bare: the thread in a loving mother’s hand becomes the coat on her travelling son’s back — one thread ties the two of them together. Then comes the detail that makes the poem: before he leaves she stitches close and tight, afraid he will be gone a long, long time, so the clothes must last. She says nothing; all her love is in the needlework. The last two lines turn into a comparison. How could the little heart of an inch-long blade of grass ever repay the sunshine of the whole spring? The grass is the child, the spring sun is the mother’s love — a love so large it can never be paid back. There are no hard words here; it reads like everyday talk, which is why nearly every Chinese person can recite it and why it appears on Mother’s Day cards today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Meng Jiao (751–814) was a poet known for a hard life and spare, sad poems. He passed the imperial exam only at about 45, and is said to have written this around the age of 50, when he finally had a small post and could bring his mother to live with him.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

密密缝 féng（动词，缝衣服，不读 fèng）；谁言 一般读 shéi，旧读 shuí，网站按 shuí 注音，两读皆可；报得 dé。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

慈母手中线，

cí mǔ shǒu zhōng xiàn

The thread in a loving mother’s hand

游子身上衣。

yóu zǐ shēn shàng yī

becomes the coat her son will wear.

临行密密缝，

lín xíng mì mì féng

Before he goes, she sews it tight,

意恐迟迟归。

yì kǒng chí chí guī

afraid he will be long in coming home.

谁言寸草心，

shuí yán cùn cǎo xīn

Who says a tiny blade of grass

报得三春晖。

bào dé sān chūn huī

could ever repay the whole spring’s sun?
