导读About this poem

War has scattered Du Fu's family, and he has no idea whether his brothers are alive. One of its lines — “the moon is brighter over home” — is still how Chinese people talk about missing where they come from.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写于战乱年代。开头两句先写环境：守边的战鼓一响，路上就没有行人了；秋天的边地，只听见一只孤雁在叫。孤雁离开了雁群，正像诗人和弟弟们分散在各地。第三、四句最有名：“露从今夜白”，点出这天是白露节气；“月是故乡明”，月亮哪里都一样，诗人却偏说故乡的更亮——这是心里的感觉，不是事实，所以特别动人。后四句直接说出痛苦：弟弟们都分散了，家也没有了，连打听他们是死是活都没地方问；寄出的信常常收不到，何况战争还没停。全诗从景写到情，越写越沉重。

In English

Du Fu wrote this during the An Lushan Rebellion, when the war had split his family apart. The opening sets the scene: the drums of the garrison have sounded, so no one is out on the roads, and over the autumn frontier there is only the call of a single wild goose. A goose that has lost its flock is an obvious picture of the poet himself, cut off from his brothers. The next couplet is the famous one. “From tonight the dew turns white” tells us it is the White Dew solar term in early autumn; “the moon is brighter over home” is not a fact but a feeling — the same moon shines everywhere, yet the one over home seems clearer to him. The second half speaks plainly. His brothers are scattered, the family home is gone, and there is nowhere even to ask whether they are alive or dead. Letters rarely get through, and the fighting has not stopped. The poem moves from landscape to grief, and each couplet weighs more than the last.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770), often called the “Sage of Poetry”, lived through the An Lushan Rebellion and wrote more about war and ordinary suffering than any poet of his age. This poem is usually dated to autumn 759, when he was in Qinzhou (in today's Gansu) and his younger brothers were scattered in the war zones of the east.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

戍鼓断人行，

shù gǔ duàn rén xíng

The watch-drums sound, and the roads go empty;

边秋一雁声。

biān qiū yī yàn shēng

one wild goose calls across the frontier autumn.

露从今夜白，

lù cóng jīn yè bái

From tonight the dew turns white —

月是故乡明。

yuè shì gù xiāng míng

and the moon is brighter over home.

有弟皆分散，

yǒu dì jiē fēn sàn

My brothers are scattered far and wide,

无家问死生。

wú jiā wèn sǐ shēng

and with no home left, who can I ask if they live?

寄书长不达，

jì shū cháng bù dá

The letters I send seldom reach them,

况乃未休兵。

kuàng nǎi wèi xiū bīng

and still the soldiers are not sent home.
