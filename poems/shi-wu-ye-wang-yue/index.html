十五夜望月

Shíwǔ Yè Wàng Yuè
Gazing at the Moon on the Fifteenth Night

导读About this poem

A Mid-Autumn night: white moonlight in the courtyard, crows asleep, cold dew on the osmanthus. Everyone is looking at the same moon — and the poet wonders whose home its longing will fall on.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写中秋夜（农历八月十五）赏月。前两句写景：院子里月光照得地面一片白，乌鸦在树上栖息；夜深了，冷冷的露水悄悄打湿了桂花。这里没有直接写月，却处处是月光——地是被月光照白的，露水和桂花也是在月下才看得见。后两句由景转到人：今晚月亮这么亮，人人都在望月，不知道这秋天的思念会落到谁家？诗人不说自己想念谁，而是用一个问句，把自己的心情扩大成天下人的心情，含蓄又有余味。“落”字很妙，好像思念也像月光、露水一样能落下来。这是写中秋最有名的诗之一。

In English

This poem is set on the night of the Mid-Autumn Festival, the 15th day of the eighth lunar month. The first two lines paint the scene: moonlight turns the courtyard floor white, crows roost in the tree, and late in the night cold dew silently wets the osmanthus flowers. The moon itself is never named, yet it is everywhere — it is what makes the ground white and lets us see the dew on the blossoms. The second half turns from the scene to people. Tonight the moon is so bright that everyone is looking at it; whose home, the poet asks, will this autumn longing land on? He never says whom he misses. Instead, a gentle question turns his private feeling into something everyone under the same moon shares, and it leaves us thinking. The verb 落 (“to fall, to land”) is lovely: longing seems to drift down like moonlight or dew. It is one of the best-loved Mid-Autumn poems, often recited at the festival today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Jian (c. 767–c. 830) was a Tang poet and minor official, known for his yuefu ballads and his palace poems. The full title says the poem was sent to a friend, Secretary Du (杜郎中).

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

秋思：作名词“思绪”时旧读 sì，现多按统编教材读 sī，网站采用 sī。栖 qī（栖息）。湿 shī。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

中庭地白树栖鸦，

zhōng tíng dì bái shù qī yā

The courtyard floor is white; crows sleep in the tree;

冷露无声湿桂花。

lěng lù wú shēng shī guì huā

cold dew, without a sound, settles on the osmanthus.

今夜月明人尽望，

jīn yè yuè míng rén jìn wàng

The moon is so bright tonight that all eyes turn to it —

不知秋思落谁家。

bù zhī qiū sī luò shuí jiā

I wonder whose home this autumn longing will fall on.
