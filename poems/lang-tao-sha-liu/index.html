导读About this poem

The Yellow River winds ten thousand li, carrying sand from the end of the earth. In this playful poem it flows straight up into the Milky Way — all the way to the home of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《浪淘沙》是唐代的民歌曲调，刘禹锡用它写了九首诗，这是第一首，写黄河。第一句写黄河的样子：弯弯曲曲，带着万里泥沙。第二句写它的来历：被浪淘洗、被风簸荡，从遥远的天边一路奔来。“淘”“簸”两个动词，写出黄河一路的艰辛和力量。后两句展开想象：古人相信黄河和天上的银河相通，于是诗人说，如今就顺着黄河直上银河，一起到牛郎织女的家里去。这里用了“牛郎织女”的神话：他们被银河隔开，每年只能相见一次。诗的语言像民歌一样朴素，想象却非常大胆，把地上的大河和天上的银河连在一起，显出开阔豪迈的气势，也可以看出诗人向往远方、不甘平凡的心。

In English

Làng Táo Shā, “Waves Sifting Sand”, was a popular tune in the Tang dynasty. Liu Yuxi wrote nine poems to it; this first one is about the Yellow River. The opening line gives its shape: winding through “nine bends”, carrying sand for ten thousand li. The second line tells where it comes from: washed by waves and tossed by wind, it has rushed all the way from the edge of the sky. The two verbs 淘 (“to wash, sift”) and 簸 (“to toss, winnow”) give a sense of the river’s long, hard journey and its strength. Then the last two lines take off into fantasy. People in ancient China believed the Yellow River was connected to the Milky Way, the “Silver River” of the sky. So the poet says: now let’s ride the river straight up into the Milky Way, all the way to the home of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl — the lovers of the famous legend, separated by the Milky Way and allowed to meet only once a year. The language is as plain as a folk song, but the imagination is bold, linking the great river on earth with the river in the heavens. It shows a big, generous spirit and a longing to go far beyond the ordinary.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Yuxi (772–842), a Tang poet and official, wrote a set of nine “Waves Sifting Sand” poems, traditionally dated to his later years, adapting a popular folk tune of the time.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“九曲”的“曲”读 qū（弯曲）；“风簸”的“簸”读 bǒ（颠簸）；“牵牛”就是牛郎；“直上”读 zhí shàng。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

九曲黄河万里沙，

jiǔ qū huáng hé wàn lǐ shā

The Yellow River twists and turns, ten thousand li of sand,

浪淘风簸自天涯。

làng táo fēng bǒ zì tiān yá

washed by waves and tossed by winds from the end of the earth.

如今直上银河去，

rú jīn zhí shàng yín hé qù

Now let us follow it straight up into the Milky Way

同到牵牛织女家。

tóng dào qiān niú zhī nǚ jiā

and call on the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl at home.
