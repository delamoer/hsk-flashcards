导读About this poem

A poet visits a friend who lives far from the world, finds him out, and leaves a promise to come back. This is the poem behind the Chinese word 推敲 (“push or knock”), which still means weighing every word.

中文解析

贾岛去拜访隐居的朋友李凝，没有见到人，就在门上题了这首诗。开头写住处的冷清：很少有邻居，长满草的小路通向荒废的园子。第三、四句最有名：鸟已经在池边的树上睡了，一个和尚在月光下敲门。传说贾岛骑在驴上，想不好用“推”还是“敲”，一边想一边做手势，撞上了大官韩愈的车队。韩愈不但没生气，还替他想了想，说“敲”字好——夜深人静，敲门声更显出四周的安静。从此“推敲”成了一个词，意思是反复斟酌字句。后四句写回去路上的山野景色，最后说：我暂时走了，还会再来，不会忘记约定。

In English

Jia Dao went to visit his friend Li Ning, who lived as a recluse, but no one seemed to be home, so he wrote this poem — traditionally on the door itself. The first lines set the scene: hardly any neighbours, a grassy path leading into an overgrown garden. Then comes the famous couplet: birds asleep in the trees by the pond, and a monk knocking at a gate under the moon. The story goes that Jia Dao, riding a donkey, couldn’t decide between “push” (推) and “knock” (敲). Lost in thought and miming both gestures, he rode straight into the procession of the senior official Han Yu. Instead of punishing him, Han Yu thought it over and chose “knock”: on a silent night, a knock makes the stillness even deeper. Ever since, 推敲 has been an everyday Chinese word for carefully weighing your words. The second half follows the walk home — a bridge dividing the colours of the fields, stones that seem to move the roots of the clouds — and ends with a promise: I’m leaving for now, but I’ll be back, and I won’t break our appointment.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Jia Dao (779–843) was a monk before he returned to lay life and tried, mostly without success, to build an official career. He was famous for polishing single lines for years; this poem and its “push or knock” story are the best-known example.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

闲居少邻并，

xián jū shǎo lín bìng

He lives at ease, with hardly a neighbour near;

草径入荒园。

cǎo jìng rù huāng yuán

a grassy path runs into a wild garden.

鸟宿池边树，

niǎo sù chí biān shù

Birds have settled in the trees by the pond,

僧敲月下门。

sēng qiāo yuè xià mén

and a monk knocks at a gate in the moonlight.

过桥分野色，

guò qiáo fēn yě sè

Over the bridge, the fields split into two colours;

移石动云根。

yí shí dòng yún gēn

the stones I pass seem to shift the roots of clouds.

暂去还来此，

zàn qù huán lái cǐ

I go for now, but I will come again —

幽期不负言。

yōu qī bù fù yán

our quiet promise is one I’ll keep.
