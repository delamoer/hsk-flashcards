导读About this poem

Du Fu visits the shrine of Zhuge Liang, the brilliant strategist of the Three Kingdoms who died before he could finish his life's work. The last couplet is one of the most quoted lines in Chinese about heroes who fall short.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“蜀相”指三国时蜀汉的丞相诸葛亮，他是中国人心中智慧和忠诚的代表。杜甫到成都后，专门去拜访他的祠堂（纪念他的庙）。第一、二句一问一答：丞相的祠堂在哪里？就在锦官城（成都）外，柏树长得又高又密。第三、四句写祠堂里的景色：台阶边的青草自己绿着，树叶后的黄鹂白白地唱着好听的歌。“自”和“空”两个字很重要——春色再美，也没有人来欣赏，写出了祠堂的冷清和诗人的感伤。第五、六句概括诸葛亮的一生：刘备三次去请他，向他求教统一天下的大计；他辅佐了刘备、刘禅两代君主，一片忠心。最后两句是千古名句：出兵北伐还没有成功，他就先死了，这让后来的英雄们常常泪流满襟。杜甫自己也有报国的志向却无法实现，所以写得格外动情。

In English

The “Prime Minister of Shu” is Zhuge Liang, chief minister of the Shu Han kingdom in the Three Kingdoms period and, for Chinese readers, the model of wisdom and loyalty. Soon after arriving in Chengdu, Du Fu went looking for his shrine. The poem opens with a question and an answer: where is it? Just outside the city, among dense, towering cypresses. The second couplet describes the grounds: green grass lining the steps shows its spring colour “by itself”, and an oriole behind the leaves sings its lovely song “in vain”. Those two small words carry the mood — there is beauty here, but no one to enjoy it, and the shrine feels empty. The third couplet sums up Zhuge Liang's life in fourteen characters: Liu Bei visited him three times to ask for his plan to unite the empire, and he served two generations of rulers with total devotion. The final couplet is the famous one: he led his army out but died before victory, and ever since, heroes have wept until their robes were wet. Du Fu, who also longed to serve his country and never could, clearly includes himself among them.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this in spring 760, shortly after settling in Chengdu, on a visit to the shrine of Zhuge Liang (181–234). The shrine, the Wuhou Temple, still stands in Chengdu today.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

丞相祠堂何处寻，

chéng xiàng cí táng hé chù xún

Where shall I find the shrine of the great minister?

锦官城外柏森森。

jǐn guān chéng wài bǎi sēn sēn

Outside the City of Brocade, where the cypresses stand dark and tall.

映阶碧草自春色，

yìng jiē bì cǎo zì chūn sè

Green grass along the steps is spring-bright for no one,

隔叶黄鹂空好音。

gé yè huáng lí kōng hǎo yīn

and behind the leaves an oriole sings its sweet song to nobody.

三顾频烦天下计，

sān gù pín fán tiān xià jì

Three times his lord came, asking how to win the empire;

两朝开济老臣心。

liǎng cháo kāi jì lǎo chén xīn

through two reigns he built and held the kingdom, a loyal old servant.

出师未捷身先死，

chū shī wèi jié shēn xiān sǐ

He marched out to war, but died before the victory —

长使英雄泪满襟。

cháng shǐ yīng xióng lèi mǎn jīn

and heroes ever since have wept until their robes were wet.
