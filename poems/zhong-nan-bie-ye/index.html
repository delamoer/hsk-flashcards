导读About this poem

Walk until the stream runs out, then sit down and watch the clouds rise. Wang Wei’s portrait of his own carefree life in the mountains contains one of the most quoted lines on how to live when you reach a dead end.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人晚年隐居终南山时自在的生活。他说自己中年以后喜欢佛道，晚年就把家安在南山脚下。兴致来了常常一个人去山里走走，那些美好的事只有自己知道，也不需要别人知道。第三联最有名：“行到水穷处，坐看云起时。”沿着溪水走，走到了水的尽头，没有路了，那就坐下来，看山间的云慢慢升起。表面写的是散步，里面却有人生的道理：走到“绝路”时不必着急，换个角度，也许就是新的风景。这两句今天常被人引用来劝人放宽心。结尾偶然遇到山中老人，聊得开心，忘了回家的时间，写出诗人随遇而安、悠然自得的心境。

In English

Wang Wei describes the free and easy life of his later years at his home below Zhongnan Mountain. In middle age, he says, he came to love the Way (here meaning Buddhist teaching), and late in life he settled at the foot of these hills. When the mood takes him he wanders off alone; the beautiful things he finds there are his alone to know, and that is enough. The third couplet is the famous one: “I walk to where the water ends, then sit and watch the clouds rise.” On the surface it is just a walk. He follows a stream up the mountain until it disappears at its source and there is no path left — so he sits down and watches clouds forming in the valley. Underneath is a way of looking at life: when you reach a dead end, there is no need to panic; stop, look from another angle, and a new view may open up. Chinese people still quote these lines to comfort someone in difficulty. The poem ends with a chance meeting with an old man of the woods; they talk and laugh and forget all about going home. Everything is unplanned — and that is the point.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761) turned increasingly to Buddhism in his later years and spent much time at his estate in the Zhongnan Mountains south of Chang’an, while still holding office. The poem is also titled “Arriving in the Mountains” in some sources.
