导读About this poem

A plum tree blooms alone by a broken bridge, battered by wind and rain, ignored by all. Its blossoms will be crushed into dust — yet the fragrance stays. A portrait of the poet himself.

中文解析

这首词表面写梅花，实际写诗人自己，这种手法叫“托物言志”（借一个东西表达自己的志向）。上阕写梅花的处境：长在驿站外的断桥边，没人照顾，也没人欣赏，孤零零地开着；到了黄昏，已经够愁了，偏偏又遭受风吹雨打。下阕写梅花的品格：它在冬末早早开放，并不想和百花争夺春天，别的花嫉妒它也随便。最后两句最有名：花瓣落下，变成泥土，被车轮碾成灰尘，可是它的香气还和从前一样。陆游一生主张抗金，却屡次被排挤、罢官，这朵梅花就是他：可以被冷落、被打压，但气节不变。后来毛泽东读了这首词，“反其意而用之”，也写了一首《卜算子·咏梅》。

In English

On the surface this lyric is about a plum tree; underneath, it is about the poet. Chinese critics call this tuō wù yán zhì — “speaking your heart through an object”. The first half describes the tree’s situation. It grows beside a broken bridge outside a post station, where no one tends it or stops to look, and it blooms alone, with no owner. Dusk brings its own sadness, and on top of that comes wind and rain. The second half describes its character. Plum blossoms open in late winter, before other flowers, but this tree is not trying to compete for spring; if the other flowers are jealous, let them be. Then the famous close: its petals fall, turn into mud, are ground into dust under wheels — and still the fragrance is the same as before. Lu You spent his life arguing for a war to recover northern China and was repeatedly pushed aside and dismissed for it. The lonely plum is his self-portrait: you can ignore it, beat it down, even destroy it, but you cannot change what it is. In 1961 Mao Zedong wrote a reply to this lyric, a more upbeat “Ode to the Plum Blossom” to the same tune.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) was a Southern Song poet and official whose calls to fight the Jin made him unpopular at court. The date of this lyric is uncertain; it is generally read as an expression of his loneliness and integrity after setbacks in his career.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

驿外断桥边，寂寞开无主。

yì wài duàn qiáo biān, jì mò kāi wú zhǔ

Past the post station, by a broken bridge, it blooms alone, belonging to no one.

已是黄昏独自愁，更著风和雨。

yǐ shì huáng hūn dú zì chóu, gèng zhuó fēng hé yǔ

Dusk has come and it grieves by itself — and now the wind and rain beat down.

无意苦争春，一任群芳妒。

wú yì kǔ zhēng chūn, yī rèn qún fāng dù

It does not fight for a place in spring; let all the other flowers envy it.

零落成泥碾作尘，只有香如故。

líng luò chéng ní niǎn zuò chén, zhǐ yǒu xiāng rú gù

Its petals fall to mud and are ground to dust — but the fragrance stays as it always was.
