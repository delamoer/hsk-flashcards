于易水送人

Yú Yìshuǐ Sòng Rén
Seeing a Friend Off at the Yi River

导读About this poem

Saying goodbye at the very river where the hero Jing Ke once left on a mission he knew he would not survive. The heroes are gone — but the water is still cold.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

易水在今天河北省，是一个很有名的地方。战国末年，燕国太子丹派勇士荆轲去刺杀秦王，在易水边为他送行。荆轲唱道：“风萧萧兮易水寒，壮士一去兮不复还。”送行的人都气得头发竖起来，把帽子都顶起来了（“发冲冠”）。骆宾王在这里送朋友，想起了这个故事。前两句写过去：就在这个地方，荆轲告别了燕太子丹，壮士们怒发冲冠。后两句写现在：当年的人早已不在了，可今天的易水还是那样寒冷。“寒”字一语双关：既是水冷，也是那股悲壮的精神还在，也透出诗人自己心中的冷——他有抱负却不被重用。诗题是送人，全诗却没写朋友一句，只写古人，这样送别也带上了一股豪气。

In English

The Yi River is in today’s Hebei province, and it is famous for one farewell. Near the end of the Warring States period, Prince Dan of Yan sent the hero Jing Ke to assassinate the King of Qin (the future First Emperor). At the river, Jing Ke sang: “The wind is wailing, the Yi River is cold; once the hero leaves, he will not come back.” The people seeing him off were so stirred that their hair stood up and pushed against their caps. Luo Binwang is seeing a friend off at the same river, and the old story fills his mind. The first two lines are the past: right here, Jing Ke parted from Prince Dan, and the brave men’s hair bristled under their caps. The last two lines are the present: those people are long dead, but today the water is still cold. That word 寒 (“cold”) carries a lot. It is the real chill of the river; it is the heroic spirit that still lingers there; and it hints at the poet’s own coldness of heart — a man of ambition who could not find anyone to use his talent. Interestingly, a poem titled “Seeing Someone Off” never mentions the friend at all. By talking only about heroes, Luo turns an ordinary goodbye into something proud and stirring.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Luo Binwang (c. 619–c. 684) was one of the “Four Paragons of the Early Tang”, known for his talent and his frustrated career. This poem is thought to date from around 679–680, when he travelled in the north near the old state of Yan.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

燕丹 的“燕”读 yān（国名，不读 yàn）；人已没 的“没”读 mò（死去，不读 méi）；发冲冠 的“发”读 fà（头发）。机器朗读“燕”“没”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。
