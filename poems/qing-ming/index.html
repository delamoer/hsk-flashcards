导读About this poem

Drizzle on Qingming, the day for remembering the dead; a lonely traveller asks where to find a tavern, and a cowherd boy points to a village among apricot blossoms. Every Chinese child learns it.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

清明节在每年四月初，是中国人扫墓、纪念去世亲人的节日，这时候常常下着蒙蒙细雨。第一句“雨纷纷”写细雨不停地下，也带出一种迷蒙、伤感的气氛。第二句“欲断魂”写路上的行人心情低落，好像魂都要散了——清明本来就让人想起亲人，又一个人在外赶路，被雨淋着，更觉得难过。后两句像一个小故事：行人想找个酒店喝点酒暖暖身子、解解愁，就问路边的人；放牛的孩子不说话，只用手指向远处一片开着杏花的村子。诗到这里就停了，后面的事留给读者去想象。前两句低沉，后两句明亮，有人物、有动作、有对话，像一幅画。“杏花村”后来成了酒家的代称，很多地方都说自己是诗里的杏花村。

In English

Qingming (“Clear and Bright”), in early April, is the day Chinese families sweep their ancestors’ graves and remember the dead — and it often comes with a fine spring drizzle. The first line sets the mood: the rain keeps falling, thin and endless. In the second line, travellers on the road look as if their souls are about to leave them. Qingming already makes people think of family members they have lost; being on the road, alone and soaked, makes it worse. The second half is like a tiny scene from a play. The traveller wants a tavern — somewhere to warm up and drink away the gloom — so he asks the way. A boy herding cattle doesn’t answer in words; he just points far off to a village among apricot blossoms. And there the poem stops, leaving us to imagine the rest. Notice the shape: the first two lines are grey and heavy, the last two are bright, with a character, an action, a question and a gesture. That shift is why the poem feels like a painting. “Apricot Blossom Village” later became a common nickname for a wine shop, and several places in China claim to be the real one.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This poem is traditionally attributed to Du Mu (803–852), but it does not appear in his collected works; it first shows up in Song-dynasty anthologies and became famous through the children’s primer “Poems by a Thousand Poets” (千家诗). Scholars still debate who wrote it.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“行人”的“行”读 xíng。“何处”的“处”读 chù。其余无特殊读音。

lù shàng xíng rén yù duàn hún

mù tóng yáo zhǐ xìng huā cūn

A cowherd boy points far off, to a village in apricot bloom.
