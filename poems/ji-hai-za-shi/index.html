导读About this poem

Leaving the capital in sadness, a poet sees fallen petals on the ground and finds hope in them: they are not heartless — they turn into spring soil and feed the flowers to come.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

1839年（己亥年），龚自珍辞官离开北京，这首诗写在离京的路上。前两句写离别：太阳西斜，离别的愁绪像水一样浩浩荡荡；他扬起马鞭向东一指，从此就是远在天涯了。后两句是全诗最有名的地方：诗人看到地上的落花，想到自己——花虽然落了，却不是没有感情的东西，它化成春天的泥土，还要保护、滋养新开的花。“落红”就是落花，诗人用它比喻自己：离开了官场，也还要为国家、为下一代出力。前面伤感，后面却很积极，情绪有一个大转变。现在人们常用“落红不是无情物，化作春泥更护花”来赞美老师、长辈默默奉献、培养后人。

In English

In 1839, the year called jǐhài in the traditional calendar, Gong Zizhen resigned from his post and left Beijing. He wrote this on the road. The first two lines are about parting: the sun is slanting low in the west, and his sorrow at leaving spreads out wide like a flood of water. He raises his riding whip and points east — from here on, he is at the far edge of the world. The last two lines are the famous ones. He sees fallen petals (落红, literally “fallen red”) on the ground and thinks of himself. The flowers have dropped, but they are not things without feeling: they become spring soil and go on protecting the flowers that come after them. The fallen petals are a metaphor for the poet: he has left office, but he still means to serve his country and help the next generation. So the mood turns around completely, from sadness to quiet hope. Today the couplet “Fallen petals are not heartless things; they turn to spring earth to nourish the flowers” is often quoted to praise teachers, parents and elders who give their lives to bringing up young people.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Gong Zizhen (1792–1841) was a Qing thinker and poet who called for reform as the empire declined. In 1839 he resigned and travelled south from Beijing, writing 315 short poems on the journey and after, the Miscellaneous Poems of the Year Jihai; this is No. 5.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

浩荡离愁白日斜，

hào dàng lí chóu bái rì xié

My grief at parting spreads wide as the sun sinks west;

吟鞭东指即天涯。

yín biān dōng zhǐ jí tiān yá

I point my whip to the east, and the far end of the world begins.

落红不是无情物，

luò hóng bù shì wú qíng wù

Fallen petals are not heartless things —

化作春泥更护花。

huà zuò chūn ní gèng hù huā

they turn to spring earth and nourish the flowers to come.
