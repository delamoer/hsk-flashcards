导读About this poem

On a fine day by the Si River, everything feels new; one breath of the east wind and the world bursts into “ten thousand purples and a thousand reds”. A happy spring poem — with a philosopher’s hidden meaning.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗表面上写春游：在晴朗的好日子里，到泗水边去寻找春天的花草，眼前无边的风景一下子焕然一新。人们很容易就认出了春风的样子，因为万紫千红、到处都是花，全都是春天。“等闲识得东风面”说得很巧：风是看不见的，诗人却说认出了东风的“面孔”——看到满眼的花，就知道东风来了。“万紫千红”今天还是常用成语，形容百花盛开、色彩丰富。不过朱熹是有名的哲学家（理学家），很多人认为这首诗还有一层意思：泗水在山东，是孔子讲学的地方，当时在金国手里，朱熹并没有去过。所以“寻芳泗水”可能是比喻学习孔子的思想，“东风”比喻儒家的道理，懂得了这个道理，就觉得世界处处充满生机。两种读法都可以，初学者先读出春天的快乐就很好。

In English

On the surface this is a spring outing. On a fine, sunny day the poet goes looking for flowers along the banks of the Si River, and the endless scenery seems to turn new all at once. The east (spring) wind is easy to recognise, he says, because everywhere is “ten thousand purples and a thousand reds” — all of it spring. That third line is clever: wind is invisible, yet he claims to know the east wind’s “face”. You see it in the flowers it has opened. 万紫千红 is still an everyday idiom for a riot of colour or a thriving scene. But Zhu Xi was China’s most influential Confucian philosopher, and many readers find a second meaning. The Si River is in Shandong, the homeland of Confucius, where he taught his students — and in Zhu Xi’s time it lay in Jin territory, so Zhu Xi never went there. “Seeking spring by the Si” may therefore mean studying Confucius’s teaching, and the east wind may stand for that teaching: once you understand it, the whole world looks fresh and alive. You can read the poem either way; for a beginner, enjoying the burst of spring is already enough.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhu Xi (1130–1200) was the leading Neo-Confucian thinker of the Song; his commentaries on the Confucian classics were the basis of the imperial exams for centuries. He also wrote poetry, often with a philosophical edge, and this is his best-known poem.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

胜日寻芳泗水滨，

shèng rì xún fāng sì shuǐ bīn

On a fine day I go looking for spring by the Si River;

无边光景一时新。

wú biān guāng jǐng yī shí xīn

the whole wide world looks new at once.

等闲识得东风面，

děng xián shí dé dōng fēng miàn

Anyone can tell the spring wind’s face —

万紫千红总是春。

wàn zǐ qiān hóng zǒng shì chūn

ten thousand purples, a thousand reds: it is spring everywhere.
