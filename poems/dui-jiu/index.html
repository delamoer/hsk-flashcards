导读About this poem

A woman revolutionary spends a fortune on a sword and trades her fur coat for wine — and tells herself to treasure her blood, so it can one day turn into a jade-green wave. Four lines of pure courage from one of modern China’s heroines.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

秋瑾是清末的女革命家，这首诗写出了她的豪气。前两句说：不在乎花千金去买一把宝刀；把贵重的貂皮大衣拿去换酒喝，也算得上豪迈。买刀，是准备战斗；换酒，是不把钱财放在心上。这种豪放的气概过去多写在男子身上，出自一位女子之口，更加让人佩服。后两句说：一腔热血要好好珍惜，等到洒出去的时候，还能化成碧绿的波涛。这里用了一个典故（古代的故事）：周代忠臣苌弘被冤杀，传说他的血三年后变成了碧玉，所以“碧血”代表忠诚的鲜血。意思是：我的血不能白流，要为国家流得有价值。全诗语言简单，气势很足，几年后秋瑾真的为革命献出了生命，让这首诗更加感人。

In English

Qiu Jin was a woman revolutionary at the end of the Qing dynasty, and this poem is pure boldness. In the first two lines she doesn’t mind paying a thousand in gold for a fine sword, and trading her sable fur coat for wine is grand enough for her. Buying the sword means getting ready to fight; swapping a fur for wine means not caring about money. That kind of swagger was usually written by men; coming from a woman, it is all the more striking. The second couplet turns serious: she must treasure the hot blood in her chest, so that when it is finally spilled, it can still turn into jade-green waves. Behind this is an old legend: Chang Hong, a loyal minister of the Zhou dynasty, was wrongly killed, and three years later his blood is said to have turned into green jade. Ever since, “green blood” (碧血) has meant blood shed out of loyalty. Her meaning: my blood must not be wasted — when I give it, let it be for my country. The words are simple and the tone is fearless. A few years later Qiu Jin really did give her life for the revolution, which gives these four lines their weight.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Qiu Jin (1875–1907) left an unhappy marriage, studied in Japan and joined the movement to overthrow the Qing dynasty. She also campaigned for women’s rights. In 1907 she was arrested after a failed uprising and executed at 31. This poem is usually dated to her years in the revolutionary movement.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

不惜千金买宝刀，

bù xī qiān jīn mǎi bǎo dāo

I would gladly pay a fortune for a fine sword,

貂裘换酒也堪豪。

diāo qiú huàn jiǔ yě kān háo

and trading my sable coat for wine — that is a bold life too.

一腔热血勤珍重，

yī qiāng rè xuè qín zhēn zhòng

Guard well the hot blood in your chest:

洒去犹能化碧涛。

sǎ qù yóu néng huà bì tāo

when it is spilled, it will rise again as a jade-green tide.
