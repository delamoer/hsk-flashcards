导读About this poem

A scruffy little boy is fishing by the water. When a passer-by asks him the way, he just waves — he won’t say a word in case he scares the fish. A sweet, funny snapshot of a child at play.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一个乡下小孩学钓鱼，像一张生动的照片。前两句写外貌和动作：头发乱蓬蓬的小孩，侧着身子坐在长满青苔的岸边，野草遮住了他的身体。“蓬头”写出孩子的天真，不讲究打扮；“侧坐”说明他坐得很随便，心思全在鱼竿上。后两句写一个小故事：有路人过来问路，小孩远远地向他招手，却不出声回答——原来他怕说话会把鱼吓跑。一个“怕”字，把孩子认真又可爱的样子写活了。诗人没有直接说孩子多么专心，而是用招手不说话这个细节让读者自己感受，这就是古诗常说的“以动作写心理”。全诗语言浅显，充满生活情趣。

In English

This poem is like a lively snapshot of a country boy learning to fish. The first two lines give his look and posture: hair a tangled mess, he sits sideways on a mossy bank, half hidden by the long grass. “Tousled hair” shows a child who doesn’t care about looking neat; “sitting sideways” shows he has plonked himself down anyhow — all his attention is on the line. The last two lines tell a tiny story. A passer-by comes along and asks for directions. The boy waves from a distance but doesn’t answer, because he is afraid that speaking will frighten the fish away. That one word 怕 (“afraid”) brings his serious, adorable little face to life. The poet never says “the child was concentrating”; he lets one gesture — a wave instead of a reply — show us what is going on in the boy’s head. The language is simple and full of everyday charm, which is why Chinese children love this poem.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Hu Lingneng (c. 785–826) was a Tang poet of humble background who is said to have mended pots and pans for a living before turning to poetry, then lived as a recluse. Only a handful of his poems survive, and this is the best known.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“稚子”的“稚”读 zhì；“垂纶”的“纶”读 lún（钓鱼线），不读 guān；“莓苔”读 méi tái。“不应人”的“应”读 yìng（回应）。“怕得鱼惊”的“得”读 dé。“应”有的旧注读 yīng，网站按通行注音读 yìng。

版本说明 About the text

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

蓬头稚子学垂纶，

péng tóu zhì zǐ xué chuí lún

A boy with tangled hair is learning to fish,

侧坐莓苔草映身。

cè zuò méi tái cǎo yìng shēn

perched sideways on the moss, half hidden in the grass.

路人借问遥招手，

lù rén jiè wèn yáo zhāo shǒu

When a stranger asks the way, he only waves from afar —

怕得鱼惊不应人。

pà dé yú jīng bù yìng rén

he won’t say a word, in case he scares the fish.
