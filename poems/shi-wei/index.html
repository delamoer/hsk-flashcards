导读About this poem

“It’s getting dark — why can’t we go home?” Workers still out in the dew and mud at nightfall grumble about their master. Just two short verses, and its opening words became a common expression for “in decline”.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗只有两章，每章三句，非常短。天快黑了（“式微”），劳动的人还在外面，忍不住问：天都黑了，为什么还不回家？接着自己回答：要不是为了君主的事，我们怎么会站在露水里、泡在泥水里！语气里有抱怨，也有无奈。全诗用问句开头，又用问句作答，像劳动的人随口唱出来的牢骚，简单直接。第二章只把“故”换成“躬”、“中露”换成“泥中”，越唱越苦。“式微”后来变成一个常用词，意思是“衰落、走下坡路”，比如“家道式微”；“胡不归”也常被后人引用，表达想回家、想归隐的心情。

In English

The whole poem is just two verses of three lines each. Night is falling (shì wēi), and the workers are still out in the fields. One of them can’t help asking: it’s getting dark — why don’t we go home? Then he answers himself: if it weren’t for our lord’s business, why would we be standing in the dew? In the second verse, why would we be stuck in the mud? There is complaint in his voice, and also helplessness. Questions that answer questions give it the feel of a grumble sung on the spot, plain and direct. The second verse changes only two words — “for your sake” becomes “for your person”, “in the dew” becomes “in the mud” — and the hardship grows. The opening words 式微 went on to become an ordinary Chinese expression meaning “to decline, to go downhill” (a family or a business can be 式微), and 胡不归, “why not go home?”, has been quoted ever since by people longing to return home or to leave official life.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from about the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one belongs to the Airs of Bei (邶), in today’s northern Henan. The author is unknown; one traditional story says it was sung by followers of an exiled lord urging him to go home.
