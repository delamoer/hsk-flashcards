导读About this poem

A painter who lost his career to a scandal builds a hut among peach trees, drinks, and laughs at the rich. Lots of repetition and very little hard vocabulary — a cheerful, defiant poem about choosing a free life over a successful one.

第一段Part 1
01
桃花坞里桃花庵，桃花庵里桃花仙。

直译LiteralIn Peach Blossom Hollow is the Peach Blossom Hut; in the Peach Blossom Hut is the Peach Blossom Immortal.

意译MeaningIn Peach Blossom Hollow stands a peach blossom hut; in the peach blossom hut lives a peach blossom immortal.

02
桃花仙人种桃树，又摘桃花换酒钱。

直译LiteralThe Peach Blossom Immortal plants peach trees, and also picks peach blossoms to trade for wine money.

意译MeaningThe immortal plants peach trees, then picks their blossoms and sells them to pay for wine.

03
酒醒只在花前坐，酒醉还来花下眠。

直译LiteralSober, I just sit before the flowers; drunk, I come back to sleep under the flowers.

意译MeaningSober, I sit in front of the flowers; drunk, I lie down and sleep beneath them.

04
半醉半醒日复日，花落花开年复年。

直译LiteralHalf drunk, half sober, day after day; flowers fall, flowers open, year after year.

意译MeaningHalf drunk, half awake, day follows day; blossoms fall and open again, year follows year.

第二段Part 2
05
但愿老死花酒间，不愿鞠躬车马前。

直译LiteralI only wish to grow old and die among flowers and wine, not to bow before carriages and horses.

意译MeaningLet me grow old and die among flowers and wine, rather than bow beside rich men’s carriages.

06
车尘马足富者趣，酒盏花枝贫者缘。

直译LiteralCarriage dust and horses’ hooves are the pleasure of the rich; wine cups and flowering branches are the lot of the poor.

意译MeaningDust from carriages and the beat of hooves — that is what the rich enjoy; a wine cup and a branch of blossom — that is what a poor man gets.

07
若将富贵比贫贱，一在平地一在天。

直译LiteralIf you compare wealth and rank with poverty and low status, one is on flat ground, one is in the sky.

意译MeaningSet wealth beside poverty and they are as far apart as the ground and the sky.

08
若将贫贱比车马，他得驱驰我得闲。

直译LiteralIf you compare poverty with carriages and horses, he gets rushing about, I get leisure.

意译MeaningSet poverty beside carriages and horses: they get to rush about, and I get to rest.

第三段Part 3
09
别人笑我太疯癫，我笑他人看不穿。

直译LiteralOthers laugh at me for being too crazy; I laugh at others for not seeing through.

意译MeaningPeople laugh at me for being mad; I laugh at them for not seeing through it all.

10
不见五陵豪杰墓，无花无酒锄作田。

直译LiteralDon’t you see the tombs of the heroes at Wuling? No flowers, no wine — hoed into fields.

意译MeaningLook at the tombs of the great men of old: no flowers, no wine, only ground hoed into fields.

中文解析

唐寅在苏州桃花坞盖了一座“桃花庵”，自称“桃花仙人”，这首诗就是他的生活宣言。开头四句连用六个“桃花”，像绕口令一样，读起来轻快好玩。接着写他的日子：醒了在花前坐，醉了在花下睡，一天又一天，一年又一年。中间用对比（把两种东西放在一起比较）：富人追求的是“车尘马足”，在车马前鞠躬；穷人有的是“酒盏花枝”，自由自在。最后两句最有力量：别人笑我疯，我笑别人看不透——你看那些富贵豪杰的坟墓，如今没有花也没有酒，早被人锄平种了地。语言像说话一样直白，带着一点自嘲，也带着骄傲，所以“别人笑我太疯癫，我笑他人看不穿”到今天还常被人引用。

In English

Tang Yin built a cottage called the Peach Blossom Hut in Suzhou and called himself the “Peach Blossom Immortal”. This poem is his manifesto. The opening lines use the word 桃花 (“peach blossom”) six times, spinning round like a tongue-twister, so the poem starts off light and playful. Then he describes his days: sober, he sits in front of the flowers; drunk, he sleeps under them — day after day, year after year. The middle of the poem is built on contrast. The rich chase dust kicked up by carriages and horses and bow to powerful men; the poor man has only a wine cup and a flowering branch, but he is free. If you compare wealth with poverty, he says, one is on the ground and one is in the sky — and he makes it clear which one he thinks is the sky. The ending hits hardest: people laugh at me for being mad, I laugh at them for not seeing through it all. Look at the tombs of the great men of the past: no flowers, no wine — the land has been hoed into fields. The language is as plain as speech, half self-mocking and half proud, and the couplet 别人笑我太疯癫，我笑他人看不穿 is still quoted today by anyone who refuses to follow the crowd.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Tang Yin (1470–1524) was one of the most famous painters of the Ming dynasty. In 1499 he was caught up in an exam-cheating scandal and barred from an official career for life. He is said to have written this poem around 1505, after building his Peach Blossom Hut in Suzhou.

版本说明 About the text

采用明代刻本中最通行的文本（维基文库“古籍版本”）。“半醉半醒”今天也常被引作“半醒半醉”。“车尘马足富者趣，酒盏花枝贫者缘”一联，另有版本作“车尘马足显者事，酒盏花枝隐士缘”；“别人笑我太疯癫”也有作“别人笑我忒风颠”的。苏州唐寅园的碑拓本另有不少异文（如“又折花枝当酒钱”“世人笑我太疯癫”），网站不用。全诗二十句，网站按一联一行排版。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

桃花坞里桃花庵，桃花庵里桃花仙。

táo huā wù lǐ táo huā ān, táo huā ān lǐ táo huā xiān

In Peach Blossom Hollow stands a peach blossom hut; in the peach blossom hut lives a peach blossom immortal.

桃花仙人种桃树，又摘桃花换酒钱。

táo huā xiān rén zhòng táo shù, yòu zhāi táo huā huàn jiǔ qián

The immortal plants peach trees, then picks their blossoms and sells them to pay for wine.

酒醒只在花前坐，酒醉还来花下眠。

jiǔ xǐng zhǐ zài huā qián zuò, jiǔ zuì hái lái huā xià mián

Sober, I sit in front of the flowers; drunk, I lie down and sleep beneath them.

半醉半醒日复日，花落花开年复年。

bàn zuì bàn xǐng rì fù rì, huā luò huā kāi nián fù nián

Half drunk, half awake, day follows day; blossoms fall and open again, year follows year.

但愿老死花酒间，不愿鞠躬车马前。

dàn yuàn lǎo sǐ huā jiǔ jiān, bù yuàn jū gōng chē mǎ qián

Let me grow old and die among flowers and wine, rather than bow beside rich men’s carriages.

车尘马足富者趣，酒盏花枝贫者缘。

chē chén mǎ zú fù zhě qù, jiǔ zhǎn huā zhī pín zhě yuán

Dust from carriages and the beat of hooves — that is what the rich enjoy; a wine cup and a branch of blossom — that is what a poor man gets.

若将富贵比贫贱，一在平地一在天。

ruò jiāng fù guì bǐ pín jiàn, yī zài píng dì yī zài tiān

Set wealth beside poverty and they are as far apart as the ground and the sky.

若将贫贱比车马，他得驱驰我得闲。

ruò jiāng pín jiàn bǐ chē mǎ, tā dé qū chí wǒ dé xián

Set poverty beside carriages and horses: they get to rush about, and I get to rest.

别人笑我太疯癫，我笑他人看不穿。

bié rén xiào wǒ tài fēng diān, wǒ xiào tā rén kàn bù chuān

People laugh at me for being mad; I laugh at them for not seeing through it all.

不见五陵豪杰墓，无花无酒锄作田。

bù jiàn wǔ líng háo jié mù, wú huā wú jiǔ chú zuò tián

Look at the tombs of the great men of old: no flowers, no wine, only ground hoed into fields.
