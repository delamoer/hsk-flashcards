The Man from Afar

导读About this poem

A woman tells her own story: a sweet-talking young man, a hasty marriage, years of hard work — and then his cruelty. Nearly 3,000 years old, it is one of the first poems in which an abandoned wife speaks for herself.

第一章Stanza 1
01
氓之蚩蚩，抱布贸丝。

直译LiteralThe man from afar, grinning, carried cloth to trade for silk.

意译MeaningYou came along all smiles, a stranger with cloth to trade for silk —

02
匪来贸丝，来即我谋。

直译LiteralYou did not come to trade silk; you came to me to plan a marriage.

意译Meaningbut silk was not what you were after: you came to ask for me.

03
送子涉淇，至于顿丘。

直译LiteralI saw you off across the Qi, as far as Dunqiu.

意译MeaningI walked you across the Qi River, all the way to Dunqiu.

04
匪我愆期，子无良媒。

直译LiteralIt is not that I delayed the date; you had no good matchmaker.

意译Meaning“It isn’t me putting off the day — you haven’t sent a proper go-between.”

05
将子无怒，秋以为期。

直译LiteralI beg you, do not be angry; let autumn be our date.

意译Meaning“Please don’t be angry. Let’s marry in the autumn.”

第二章Stanza 2
06
乘彼垝垣，以望复关。

直译LiteralI climbed that crumbling wall to look toward Fuguan.

意译MeaningI climbed the broken wall to watch the road to Fuguan.

07
不见复关，泣涕涟涟。

直译LiteralNot seeing Fuguan, I wept, tears streaming.

意译MeaningWhen I couldn’t see you coming, my tears ran and ran;

08
既见复关，载笑载言。

直译LiteralOnce I saw Fuguan, I laughed and talked.

意译Meaningwhen I saw you at last, I laughed and chattered.

09
尔卜尔筮，体无咎言。

直译LiteralYou read the shell, you read the stalks: the signs spoke no ill.

意译MeaningYou asked the oracle bones and the yarrow sticks — no bad omens at all.

10
以尔车来，以我贿迁。

直译LiteralCome with your carriage, and I will move with my dowry.

意译Meaning“Bring your cart,” I said, “and I’ll come with everything I own.”

第三章Stanza 3
11
桑之未落，其叶沃若。

直译LiteralBefore the mulberry sheds, its leaves are glossy and rich.

意译MeaningBefore the mulberry drops its leaves, they shine green and full.

12
于嗟鸠兮，无食桑葚！

直译LiteralAh, you doves, do not eat the mulberries!

意译MeaningOh, little doves, don’t gorge yourselves on mulberries!

13
于嗟女兮，无与士耽！

直译LiteralAh, you girls, do not lose yourselves in love with men!

意译MeaningOh, young girls, don’t lose yourselves in a man’s love!

14
士之耽兮，犹可说也。

直译LiteralWhen a man is lost in love, he can still break free.

意译MeaningA man lost in love can still walk away;

15
女之耽兮，不可说也。

直译LiteralWhen a woman is lost in love, she cannot break free.

意译Meaninga woman lost in love cannot get free.

第四章Stanza 4
16
桑之落矣，其黄而陨。

直译LiteralWhen the mulberry sheds, its leaves turn yellow and fall.

意译MeaningNow the mulberry is shedding: the leaves go yellow and drop.

17
自我徂尔，三岁食贫。

直译LiteralSince I went to you, for three years I have eaten poverty.

意译MeaningSince I came to your house, three years of thin meals and hardship.

18
淇水汤汤，渐车帷裳。

直译LiteralThe Qi runs high and wide, soaking the curtains of my carriage.

意译MeaningGoing back, the swollen Qi splashes the curtains of my cart.

19
女也不爽，士贰其行。

直译LiteralThe woman did nothing wrong; the man was two-faced in his conduct.

意译MeaningI never failed you; it was you who changed.

20
士也罔极，二三其德。

直译LiteralThe man has no standard; his heart is now two, now three.

意译MeaningYou had no fixed heart — your love shifted this way and that.

第五章Stanza 5
21
三岁为妇，靡室劳矣；

直译LiteralThree years a wife, there was no housework I did not do;

意译MeaningThree years your wife, I did every task in the house,

22
夙兴夜寐，靡有朝矣。

直译LiteralUp early, to bed late, not a single morning off.

意译Meaningup before dawn, in bed late — not one morning to myself.

23
言既遂矣，至于暴矣。

直译LiteralOnce you had what you wanted, you turned to cruelty.

意译MeaningOnce you had what you wanted, you turned harsh and violent.

24
兄弟不知，咥其笑矣。

直译LiteralMy brothers did not understand; they just sneered and laughed.

意译MeaningMy brothers didn’t understand — they only laughed at me.

25
静言思之，躬自悼矣。

直译LiteralQuietly I think it over, and grieve for myself.

意译MeaningAlone and quiet, I think it through, and grieve for myself.

第六章Stanza 6
26
及尔偕老，老使我怨。

直译Literal“Grow old with you” — growing old has made me bitter.

意译Meaning“We’ll grow old together,” you said — now the thought of it only hurts.

27
淇则有岸，隰则有泮。

直译LiteralEven the Qi has banks; even the marsh has an edge.

意译MeaningEven the Qi River has its banks, even the marsh has an edge — but your cruelty has none.

28
总角之宴，言笑晏晏。

直译LiteralWhen we were young with our hair in tufts, we talked and laughed so happily.

意译MeaningWhen we were young, our hair still tied in tufts, how gently we laughed and talked.

29
信誓旦旦，不思其反。

直译LiteralYour vows were sincere and clear; I never thought you would turn against them.

意译MeaningYour promises were so earnest — I never dreamed you would break them.

30
反是不思，亦已焉哉！

直译LiteralYou broke them without a thought — then let it be over!

意译MeaningYou broke them without a second thought. Then let it end — that’s all!

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一个女子自己讲的婚姻故事。第一、二章回忆恋爱：一个小伙子笑嘻嘻地抱着布来换丝，其实是来求婚；她送他过淇水，约好秋天成亲，见不到他就哭，见到了又说又笑。第三、四章用桑树打比方：桑叶没落时又绿又亮，就像年轻时的爱情；桑叶黄了、掉了，就像自己老了、被嫌弃了。中间她劝其他姑娘：男人沉迷爱情还能脱身，女人一旦陷进去就难了。第五章写婚后三年起早贪黑，丈夫却变得凶暴，兄弟也不理解，只会笑她。最后一章她清醒过来：当年的誓言他早忘了，那就算了吧！诗里有回忆、有比喻、有议论，女主人公从痴情到觉醒，形象非常真实，这在三千年前的诗歌里很少见。

In English

Here a woman tells the story of her marriage in her own words. Verses 1 and 2 look back on the romance: a grinning young man came to trade cloth for silk — really to court her. She saw him off across the Qi River, set the wedding for autumn, cried when he didn’t come and laughed when he did. Verses 3 and 4 use the mulberry tree as a mirror. While its leaves are fresh and glossy, love is young; when they turn yellow and fall, she has grown older and her husband no longer wants her. In between she warns other girls: a man who falls in love can still get out, but a woman who does cannot. Verse 5 describes three years of rising early and working late, only for him to turn violent, while her own brothers simply laugh at her. In the last verse she sees clearly: he has forgotten every promise he made, so she lets it go — “that’s the end of it!” The poem moves between memory, image and comment, and its speaker grows from a girl in love into a woman who understands. A voice like hers is rare in poetry this old.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs collects 305 songs from about the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one is from the state of Wey (卫), on the Qi River in today’s northern Henan; its songs are known for frank love stories. The author is unknown, but the speaker is clearly a woman.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

氓 méng（指外来的男子，不读 máng）；蚩蚩 chī chī；匪 fěi（通“非”）；愆 qiān；将 qiāng（请）；垝垣 guǐ yuán；载笑载言 zài；筮 shì；咎 jiù；贿 huì；桑葚 shèn；耽 dān；说 tuō（通“脱”，解脱）；陨 yǔn；徂 cú；汤汤 shāng shāng（水大的样子）；渐 jiān（浸湿）；帷裳 wéi cháng；士贰其行 xíng；罔 wǎng；靡 mǐ；夙 sù；朝 zhāo；咥 xì；隰 xí；泮 pàn；总角 zǒng jiǎo。“于嗟”的“于”按统编教材读 xū（通“吁”），也有读 yú 的。机器朗读易错的字已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

氓之蚩蚩，抱布贸丝。

méng zhī chī chī, bào bù mào sī

You came along all smiles, a stranger with cloth to trade for silk —

匪来贸丝，来即我谋。

fěi lái mào sī, lái jí wǒ móu

but silk was not what you were after: you came to ask for me.

送子涉淇，至于顿丘。

sòng zǐ shè qí, zhì yú dùn qiū

I walked you across the Qi River, all the way to Dunqiu.

匪我愆期，子无良媒。

fěi wǒ qiān qī, zǐ wú liáng méi

“It isn’t me putting off the day — you haven’t sent a proper go-between.”

将子无怒，秋以为期。

qiāng zǐ wú nù, qiū yǐ wéi qī

“Please don’t be angry. Let’s marry in the autumn.”

乘彼垝垣，以望复关。

chéng bǐ guǐ yuán, yǐ wàng fù guān

I climbed the broken wall to watch the road to Fuguan.

不见复关，泣涕涟涟。

bù jiàn fù guān, qì tì lián lián

When I couldn’t see you coming, my tears ran and ran;

既见复关，载笑载言。

jì jiàn fù guān, zài xiào zài yán

when I saw you at last, I laughed and chattered.

尔卜尔筮，体无咎言。

ěr bǔ ěr shì, tǐ wú jiù yán

You asked the oracle bones and the yarrow sticks — no bad omens at all.

以尔车来，以我贿迁。

yǐ ěr chē lái, yǐ wǒ huì qiān

“Bring your cart,” I said, “and I’ll come with everything I own.”

桑之未落，其叶沃若。

sāng zhī wèi luò, qí yè wò ruò

Before the mulberry drops its leaves, they shine green and full.

于嗟鸠兮，无食桑葚！

xū jiē jiū xī, wú shí sāng shèn

Oh, little doves, don’t gorge yourselves on mulberries!

于嗟女兮，无与士耽！

xū jiē nǚ xī, wú yǔ shì dān

Oh, young girls, don’t lose yourselves in a man’s love!

士之耽兮，犹可说也。

shì zhī dān xī, yóu kě tuō yě

A man lost in love can still walk away;

女之耽兮，不可说也。

nǚ zhī dān xī, bù kě tuō yě

a woman lost in love cannot get free.

桑之落矣，其黄而陨。

sāng zhī luò yǐ, qí huáng ér yǔn

Now the mulberry is shedding: the leaves go yellow and drop.

自我徂尔，三岁食贫。

zì wǒ cú ěr, sān suì shí pín

Since I came to your house, three years of thin meals and hardship.

淇水汤汤，渐车帷裳。

qí shuǐ shāng shāng, jiān chē wéi cháng

Going back, the swollen Qi splashes the curtains of my cart.

女也不爽，士贰其行。

nǚ yě bù shuǎng, shì èr qí xíng

I never failed you; it was you who changed.

士也罔极，二三其德。

shì yě wǎng jí, èr sān qí dé

You had no fixed heart — your love shifted this way and that.

三岁为妇，靡室劳矣；

sān suì wéi fù, mǐ shì láo yǐ

Three years your wife, I did every task in the house,

夙兴夜寐，靡有朝矣。

sù xīng yè mèi, mǐ yǒu zhāo yǐ

up before dawn, in bed late — not one morning to myself.

言既遂矣，至于暴矣。

yán jì suì yǐ, zhì yú bào yǐ

Once you had what you wanted, you turned harsh and violent.

兄弟不知，咥其笑矣。

xiōng dì bù zhī, xì qí xiào yǐ

My brothers didn’t understand — they only laughed at me.

静言思之，躬自悼矣。

jìng yán sī zhī, gōng zì dào yǐ

Alone and quiet, I think it through, and grieve for myself.

及尔偕老，老使我怨。

jí ěr xié lǎo, lǎo shǐ wǒ yuàn

“We’ll grow old together,” you said — now the thought of it only hurts.

淇则有岸，隰则有泮。

qí zé yǒu àn, xí zé yǒu pàn

Even the Qi River has its banks, even the marsh has an edge — but your cruelty has none.

总角之宴，言笑晏晏。

zǒng jiǎo zhī yàn, yán xiào yàn yàn

When we were young, our hair still tied in tufts, how gently we laughed and talked.

信誓旦旦，不思其反。

xìn shì dàn dàn, bù sī qí fǎn

Your promises were so earnest — I never dreamed you would break them.

反是不思，亦已焉哉！

fǎn shì bù sī, yì yǐ yān zāi

You broke them without a second thought. Then let it end — that’s all!
