导读About this poem

Four lines, one long night: a palace lady lets down the curtain, watches fireflies drift, and sews a silk robe for a man who does not come. A tiny poem that later inspired Li Bai’s own “Jade Steps” lament.

中文解析

“玉阶怨”是乐府旧题，专写宫中女子被冷落的哀怨（这类诗叫“宫怨诗”）。第一句：天黑了，女子放下宫殿的珠帘——帘子一放，她和外面的世界就隔开了。第二句写帘外的萤火虫飞一会儿、停一会儿，一闪一灭，好像她忽起忽落的心情；能看清萤火，也说明殿里很暗、很静。第三句“长夜缝罗衣”，她睡不着，只好整夜缝衣服，这件衣服多半是为心上人做的。最后一句才把心事说出来：“思君此何极”——想你，这思念哪里有尽头？前三句只写动作和景物，最后一句点明感情，这叫“卒章显志”（在结尾点出主旨）。全诗只有二十个字，却写出了一整夜的孤单。李白后来也写过一首《玉阶怨》，就是受了它的影响。

In English

“Lament on the Jade Steps” was an old yuefu song title reserved for one subject: a palace woman neglected by the emperor or the man she loves. In the first line night falls and she lets down the beaded curtain — a small act that shuts her off from the world. Outside, fireflies drift, settle, then rise again; their on-and-off glow mirrors her restless mood, and the fact that she can see them tells us how dark and silent the hall is. In the third line she cannot sleep, so she sews a silk robe all night, most likely for him. Only in the last line does she say what she feels: “Missing you — where could this ever end?” Three lines of pure action and scenery, then one line that names the emotion: holding back the feeling until the very end is a classic Chinese technique. In twenty characters the poem covers a whole sleepless night. Li Bai later wrote his own “Jade Steps” poem under the same title, clearly with this one in mind.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xie Tiao (464–499) was a poet of the Southern Qi dynasty, famous for clear, fresh landscape lines; Li Bai admired him all his life. He died in prison at 35 after being caught up in court politics. This short piece uses an old yuefu title about lonely palace women.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

缝 读 féng（动词“缝衣服”），不读 fèng。下 读 xià，这里是动词“放下”。作者名“朓”读 tiǎo，不读 tiào 或 zhào。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行本，末句作“思君此何极”；维基文库所录《玉台新咏》本作“思君何此极”，意思相同。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

夕殿下珠帘，

xī diàn xià zhū lián

Dusk in the palace hall; she lets the bead curtain fall.

流萤飞复息。

liú yíng fēi fù xī

Fireflies drift outside, alight, then rise again.

长夜缝罗衣，

cháng yè féng luó yī

Through the long night she sews a silken robe —

思君此何极。

sī jūn cǐ hé jí

missing you, and the missing has no end.
