Grass on the Ancient Plain: A Farewell

导读About this poem

Grass on an old plain dies every year and grows back every spring — “wildfire can’t burn it all; the spring wind blows and it lives again.” A teenage Bai Juyi turns that grass into a farewell to a friend.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

题目里的“赋得”说明这是一首按指定题目写的诗，题目是“古原草”，内容是送别。前四句写草：古原上的草长得很茂盛，每年枯一次、绿一次。野火烧也烧不完，春风一吹，又长出来了。这两句写出了小草顽强的生命力，今天常用来比喻困难打不倒的精神。五、六句写草一直长到古道上、荒城边，远远的花草香气和阳光下的绿色连成一片。最后两句才说到送别：我又在这里送朋友远行，茂盛的春草好像也充满了离别的情意。草长得越远越多，送别的情也越长越深——用景物来表达感情，这叫“借景抒情”。传说白居易十几岁时带着这首诗去拜见名人顾况，顾况拿他的名字开玩笑说“长安米贵，居大不易”，读到“野火烧不尽”两句，马上改口称赞。

In English

“Fu de” in the title means the poem was written on an assigned topic — here, “grass on an ancient plain”, combined with saying goodbye. The first half is about the grass itself: thick on the old plain, it withers and turns green once every year. Then the lines everyone knows: “Wildfire cannot burn it all away; the spring wind blows and it grows again.” They praise the toughness of a small, ordinary plant, and today people quote them for any spirit that cannot be crushed. Next the grass spreads far — its distant fragrance creeps over the old road, its sunlit green reaches a ruined town. Only in the last couplet does the farewell arrive: once again I see a friend off, and the thick spring grass seems full of the feeling of parting. The further the grass spreads, the longer the sorrow stretches — expressing feeling through scenery like this is a classic Chinese technique. A well-known story says the young Bai Juyi showed this poem to the famous scholar Gu Kuang, who joked about his name (“Juyi” sounds like “living is easy”): “Rice is expensive in Chang’an; living here isn’t easy.” After reading the wildfire lines, Gu Kuang said that with talent like this, living anywhere would be easy.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) was one of the most popular Tang poets, famous for writing so plainly that ordinary people could understand him. He is said to have written this at about 16, as a practice piece for the imperial exam; the Gu Kuang story is traditional and may be legend.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

离离原上草，

lí lí yuán shàng cǎo

Lush grass covers the ancient plain;

一岁一枯荣。

yī suì yī kū róng

each year it dies and grows green again.

野火烧不尽，

yě huǒ shāo bù jìn

Wildfire can never burn it all —

春风吹又生。

chūn fēng chuī yòu shēng

spring wind blows, and back it comes.

远芳侵古道，

yuǎn fāng qīn gǔ dào

Its far-off scent spreads over the old road;

晴翠接荒城。

qíng cuì jiē huāng chéng

its sunlit green runs up to a ruined town.

又送王孙去，

yòu sòng wáng sūn qù

Once again I see a friend on his way,

萋萋满别情。

qī qī mǎn bié qíng

and all this thick grass is full of goodbye.
