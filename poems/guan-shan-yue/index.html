关山月

Guān Shān Yuè
The Moon over the Mountain Pass
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang

导读About this poem

A vast moonrise over the Tianshan mountains opens onto centuries of frontier wars — and ends with soldiers gazing homeward while their wives sigh in a tower far away, under the same moon.

直译LiteralThe bright moon comes out over Tianshan, amid a vast, misty sea of clouds.

03

04

直译LiteralFrom of old, in these places of war, none has been seen to return.

意译MeaningOn these battlefields, since time began, no one has ever come home.

第三段Part 3
05

直译LiteralThe garrison men gaze at the border town; longing to go home, many wear bitter faces.

意译MeaningThe soldiers stare out over the frontier town, faces worn with longing for home.

直译LiteralIn the high towers on this night, the sighing surely does not stop.

意译MeaningAnd tonight, in a tall house far away, the sighing will not cease.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《关山月》是乐府旧题，多写边塞士兵的离别之苦。前四句写景，气魄非常大：一轮明月从天山升起，在苍茫的云海中穿行；长风吹过几万里，一直吹过玉门关。月亮和风把广阔的西北边疆连在一起。中间四句写历史：汉朝军队曾在白登山被围，吐蕃一直窥视着青海湾；自古以来这些战场上，从没见过有人活着回来。最后四句写人：守边的士兵望着边城，想家想得满脸愁苦；而今夜家中高楼上的妻子，一定也在不停地叹息。从月亮写到战争，又从战争写到两地相思，场面由大到小，感情越来越深。全诗没有直接说反对战争，却让人感到战争带给普通人的痛苦。

In English

“The Moon over the Mountain Pass” was an old yuefu title for songs about soldiers far from home. Li Bai opens with an enormous landscape: the bright moon rises over the Tianshan mountains and sails through a boundless sea of cloud; a long wind blows tens of thousands of miles, all the way through Jade Gate Pass. Moon and wind tie the whole northwestern frontier together in two strokes. The middle four lines turn to history. Han armies were once trapped on the road to Baideng; the Tibetan armies still eye Qinghai Lake. On these ancient battlefields, no one has ever been seen coming home. The last four lines narrow to people: the garrison soldiers look out over the border town, homesick, their faces full of misery; and tonight, in a tall house far away, their wives must be sighing without rest. The camera moves from sky to history to two single hearts, from very large to very small, and the feeling deepens as it goes. The poem never says war is wrong. It simply shows what war costs ordinary people, and lets the moon shine on both sides of the separation.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) is traditionally said to have been born in Suyab, in Central Asia, so the western frontier was part of his family story. In his time the Tang fought long wars with the Tibetan Empire around Qinghai Lake.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

几万里 jǐ；度 dù（同“渡”）；窥 kuī；戍客 shù kè；有人还 huán；当此夜 dāng；未应闲 yīng。机器朗读“还”“应”易读错，已在朗读用字中替换同音字。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

明月出天山，苍茫云海间。

míng yuè chū tiān shān, cāng máng yún hǎi jiān

The bright moon climbs out of the Tianshan range into a boundless sea of cloud.

长风几万里，吹度玉门关。

cháng fēng jǐ wàn lǐ, chuī dù yù mén guān

A wind that has travelled ten thousand miles comes sweeping through Jade Gate Pass.

汉下白登道，胡窥青海湾。

hàn xià bái dēng dào, hú kuī qīng hǎi wān

Once Han armies marched down the road to Baideng; now raiders watch the shores of Qinghai Lake.

由来征战地，不见有人还。

yóu lái zhēng zhàn dì, bù jiàn yǒu rén huán

On these battlefields, since time began, no one has ever come home.

戍客望边邑，思归多苦颜。

shù kè wàng biān yì, sī guī duō kǔ yán

The soldiers stare out over the frontier town, faces worn with longing for home.

高楼当此夜，叹息未应闲。

gāo lóu dāng cǐ yè, tàn xī wèi yīng xián

And tonight, in a tall house far away, the sighing will not cease.
