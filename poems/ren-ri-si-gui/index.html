人日思归

Rén Rì Sī Guī
Longing for Home on Renri, the Seventh Day of Spring

导读About this poem

Only seven days into the new year, a traveller counts how long he has been away — and finds that the wild geese will fly home before he does. A tiny poem with a sharp sting at the end.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“人日”是农历正月初七，古人把这一天当作“人的生日”。诗人出门在外，过完年才七天，却已经离家两年了。第一、二句把“七日”和“二年”放在一起对比：日子刚开始，离家却已经很久，越短越显得长。第三句说，北飞的大雁会先回去，自己还要落在大雁后面；第四句又说，想家的念头早在春花开放以前就有了。“人归落雁后，思发在花前”两句对得很工整（这种上下句字数相同、意思相对的写法叫“对仗”），一句写回不去，一句写早就想回去，思乡之情一下子就出来了。传说薛道衡出使南方时写这首诗，南方文人听了前两句还笑他写得平常，听完后两句才佩服。

In English

Renri (人日), the seventh day of the lunar new year, was traditionally called “everyone’s birthday”. The poet is away from home, and the new year is only seven days old — yet he has already been gone for two years. The first two lines simply set the two numbers side by side: “seven days” of spring against “two years” of absence. The short span makes the long one feel even longer. The last two lines are a neat matched pair (this kind of parallel couplet is called duìzhàng). Line three: the wild geese will fly north before he can go home — he will arrive “behind the geese”. Line four: his longing began even before the spring flowers opened. One line says he cannot go back yet; the other says he has wanted to for a long time. A well-known story says that Xue wrote this while on a diplomatic mission to the southern court. When the southern poets heard the first two plain lines they laughed — “Is this the famous Xue?” — but after the last two lines they fell silent in admiration. It is a good lesson in how much a short poem can do with simple words.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xue Daoheng (540–609) served the Northern Qi, Northern Zhou and then the Sui dynasty, and was one of the best-known poets of his time. He is said to have written this poem around 584, when he was sent as an envoy to the Chen court in the south and spent the new year far from home.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

入春才七日，

rù chūn cái qī rì

Spring is just seven days old,

离家已二年。

lí jiā yǐ èr nián

yet I have been gone from home two years.

人归落雁后，

rén guī luò yàn hòu

The geese will fly home before I can,

思发在花前。

sī fā zài huā qián

though my longing woke before the flowers.
