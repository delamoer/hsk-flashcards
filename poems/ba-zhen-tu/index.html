导读About this poem

Twenty characters on Zhuge Liang: his genius split China into three, his stone battle-maze still stands in the Yangtze — and so does his one great regret.

中文解析

这首诗也是写诸葛亮的。八阵图是诸葛亮创造的一种军事阵法，传说他在长江边的鱼复浦用石头摆过八阵图的遗迹。第一、二句赞美他的功业：他的功劳超过了所有人，帮刘备建立了三国鼎立（像鼎的三只脚一样三方并立）的局面；他的名声也因为八阵图而更加响亮。第三句写眼前景：江水日夜冲刷，这些石头却一块也没有被冲走。石头不动，象征诸葛亮的忠心和功业永远不变。第四句写他的遗憾：刘备不听劝告，执意去攻打吴国，结果大败，破坏了联吴抗魏的大计，这成了诸葛亮一生的遗恨。短短二十个字，有赞美，有景物，也有惋惜。

In English

This is another poem about Zhuge Liang. The Eightfold Formation was a battle array he is said to have devised, and on a sandbank of the Yangtze near Kuizhou there were rows of stones traditionally believed to be his layout of it. The first couplet praises him: his achievements outshone everyone's in an age when the empire was split three ways, and the Eightfold Formation made his name. The third line turns to what Du Fu can actually see: the river rushes past day and night, yet the stones have not been rolled away. Their stubbornness becomes a picture of Zhuge Liang's unshakeable loyalty and lasting fame. The last line names the regret that endures like the stones. Zhuge Liang's grand strategy depended on alliance with the kingdom of Wu against the stronger Wei; but his ruler Liu Bei insisted on attacking Wu, was crushed, and the plan was ruined. In just twenty characters the poem moves from praise, to landscape, to sorrow.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this around 766, soon after arriving in Kuizhou (today's Fengjie, Chongqing), near the stone formation on the Yangtze traditionally linked to Zhuge Liang (181–234). The last line has been read in more than one way; the reading above is the one most textbooks follow.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材版本，无重要异文。末句“遗恨失吞吴”历来有几种理解，通行解释为“以刘备攻吴失策为遗恨”。

功盖三分国，

gōng gài sān fēn guó

His deeds outshone all in a land split three ways;

míng chéng bā zhèn tú

the Eightfold Formation made his name.

江流石不转，

jiāng liú shí bù zhuǎn

遗恨失吞吴。

yí hèn shī tūn wú

nor will his regret for the war on Wu.
