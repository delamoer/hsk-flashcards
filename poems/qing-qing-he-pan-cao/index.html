青青河畔草

Qīng Qīng Hé Pàn Cǎo
Green, Green the Grass by the River
佚名（《古诗十九首》）Anonymous (Nineteen Old Poems)汉 Han
体裁Form
五言古诗
难度Level
进阶 Intermediate
主题Themes

导读About this poem

Spring grass, garden willows, a lovely young woman at an upstairs window — and an empty bed, because her husband never comes home. The first six lines all begin with doubled words, a famous effect you can hear even as a beginner.

点 虚线字 看读音和意思 · Tap a dotted word for its sound and meaning

01
青青河畔草，郁郁园中柳。

直译LiteralGreen, green the grass by the river; lush, lush the willows in the garden.

意译MeaningGreen, green the grass along the river, thick and leafy the willows in the garden.

02
盈盈楼上女，皎皎当窗牖。

直译LiteralGraceful the woman upstairs; bright and fair, she faces the window.

意译MeaningUpstairs a graceful young woman, pale and radiant, stands at the window.

03
娥娥红粉妆，纤纤出素手。

直译LiteralLovely her rouge and powder; slender, slender, she puts out her white hands.

意译MeaningHer face is lovely with rouge and powder; she rests her slim white hands on the sill.

04
昔为倡家女，今为荡子妇。

直译LiteralOnce she was a girl of an entertainers’ house; now she is a wanderer’s wife.

意译MeaningOnce she sang and danced for a living; now she is married to a man who is always away.

05
荡子行不归，空床难独守。

直译LiteralThe wanderer goes and does not return; an empty bed is hard to keep alone.

意译MeaningHe travels on and never comes home — how hard it is to keep an empty bed alone.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一位独守空房的年轻女子。前四句像电影镜头，由远到近：河边青青的草，园中茂盛的柳，楼上体态美好的女子，她站在窗前，容貌明丽；再近一点，是她化着淡妆的脸和伸出窗外的纤细白手。春天这样美，她也这样美，可是后四句一转：她从前是歌舞艺人，如今嫁给了一个常年在外、不回家的“荡子”，空床难以独守。美景、美人和孤独形成强烈的对比，让人更觉得她可怜。这首诗最有名的是前六句开头连用六个叠字：青青、郁郁、盈盈、皎皎、娥娥、纤纤，读起来音韵和谐，画面也一层层展开，后人非常推崇。注意“倡家”是古代以歌舞为业的人家，“荡子”是长年漂泊在外的人，和今天的“浪荡子”意思不同。

In English

This poem is about a young woman left alone at home. The first four lines move like a film camera, from far to near: green grass along the river, thick willows in the garden, a graceful woman upstairs standing at the window, bright and fair. Closer still: her made-up face and the slender white hands she rests on the sill. Spring is beautiful, and so is she — but the last four lines turn. She was once a singing and dancing girl; now she is married to a 荡子, a man who is always travelling and never comes home, and an empty bed is hard to keep alone. The contrast between the lovely scene and her loneliness makes her situation all the more painful. The poem is best known for its first six lines, each opening with a doubled word: 青青, 郁郁, 盈盈, 皎皎, 娥娥, 纤纤. Even if you don’t know every character, you can hear the rhythm, and each pair adds a layer to the picture; later critics admired this deeply. Note that 倡家 meant a family of professional entertainers, and 荡子 a man who roams far from home — not the modern sense of a “playboy”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首) are anonymous five-character poems from the late Eastern Han (2nd century AD), collected in the Wen Xuan anthology. This is the second of the nineteen.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

青青河畔草，郁郁园中柳。

qīng qīng hé pàn cǎo, yù yù yuán zhōng liǔ

Green, green the grass along the river, thick and leafy the willows in the garden.

盈盈楼上女，皎皎当窗牖。

yíng yíng lóu shàng nǚ, jiǎo jiǎo dāng chuāng yǒu

Upstairs a graceful young woman, pale and radiant, stands at the window.

娥娥红粉妆，纤纤出素手。

é é hóng fěn zhuāng, xiān xiān chū sù shǒu

Her face is lovely with rouge and powder; she rests her slim white hands on the sill.

昔为倡家女，今为荡子妇。

xī wéi chāng jiā nǚ, jīn wéi dàng zǐ fù

Once she sang and danced for a living; now she is married to a man who is always away.

荡子行不归，空床难独守。

dàng zǐ xíng bù guī, kōng chuáng nán dú shǒu

He travels on and never comes home — how hard it is to keep an empty bed alone.
