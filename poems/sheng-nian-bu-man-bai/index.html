导读About this poem

“Life is under a hundred years, yet we worry for a thousand.” A frank, almost modern poem telling you to stop hoarding, light a candle and enjoy the night.

01
生年不满百，常怀千岁忧。

直译LiteralYears of life do not fill a hundred; yet we always carry a thousand years’ worry.

意译MeaningWe live less than a hundred years, yet carry a thousand years of worry.

02
昼短苦夜长，何不秉烛游！

直译LiteralDays are short, one suffers the long nights; why not hold a candle and roam!

意译MeaningIf the days are short and the nights drag on, why not take a candle and go out!

03
为乐当及时，何能待来兹？

直译LiteralMaking merry should be done in time; how can one wait for the coming year?

意译MeaningFind your joy while there is time — why wait for next year?

04
愚者爱惜费，但为后世嗤。

直译LiteralFools grudge the expense; they are only laughed at by later ages.

意译MeaningFools who cling to every coin will only give the future a laugh.

05
仙人王子乔，难可与等期。

直译LiteralThe immortal Wangzi Qiao — it is hard to expect to be his equal.

意译MeaningAs for Wangzi Qiao, who never died — few of us can hope to join him.

中文解析

这首诗讨论人生短暂。开头两句就很有名：人活不到一百岁，心里却常常装着一千年的忧愁——替很远很远的将来发愁，多么不值得。接着说：白天短，夜里长，为什么不拿着蜡烛出去玩呢？想要快乐就要趁现在，哪能等到明年？后面讽刺那些舍不得花钱的“愚者”：他们省吃俭用，最后只会被后人笑话。最后又说，传说中的仙人王子乔能长生不老，可普通人很难跟他一样。这首诗用的是对比和反问：“百年”对“千岁”，“昼短”对“夜长”，一问接一问，语气直率有力。它表面上劝人及时行乐，背后是东汉末年社会动荡、人们对生命无常的焦虑。“秉烛夜游”后来成了成语，指珍惜时光、尽情享受。读的时候不必把它当成教人享乐，而是一种看清人生短暂后的清醒。

In English

This poem is about how short life is. Its first two lines are famous: we live fewer than a hundred years, yet carry a thousand years of worry — fretting about a future far beyond our reach. Then the advice: the days are short and the nights are long, so why not take a candle and go out and enjoy them? If you want to be happy, do it now — why wait until next year? Next the poet mocks the “fools” who can’t bear to spend money: all their saving will only make later generations laugh at them. Finally he mentions Wangzi Qiao, a legendary immortal who never died — ordinary people can hardly hope to keep him company. The poem is built on contrasts (a hundred years against a thousand, short days against long nights) and blunt rhetorical questions, which give it a direct, almost conversational force. On the surface it says “seize the day”; underneath lies the anxiety of the late Eastern Han, a time of disorder when life felt fragile. 秉烛夜游 (“to hold a candle and roam at night”) became an idiom for treasuring every moment.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is the fifteenth of the Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首), by unknown poets of the late Eastern Han (around the 2nd century AD). It echoes a Han folk song with similar lines, and reflects the uncertain mood of a dynasty in decline.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

生年不满百，常怀千岁忧。

shēng nián bù mǎn bǎi, cháng huái qiān suì yōu

We live less than a hundred years, yet carry a thousand years of worry.

昼短苦夜长，何不秉烛游！

zhòu duǎn kǔ yè cháng, hé bù bǐng zhú yóu

If the days are short and the nights drag on, why not take a candle and go out!

为乐当及时，何能待来兹？

wéi lè dāng jí shí, hé néng dài lái zī

Find your joy while there is time — why wait for next year?

愚者爱惜费，但为后世嗤。

yú zhě ài xī fèi, dàn wéi hòu shì chī

Fools who cling to every coin will only give the future a laugh.

仙人王子乔，难可与等期。

xiān rén wáng zǐ qiáo, nán kě yǔ děng qī

As for Wangzi Qiao, who never died — few of us can hope to join him.
