望天门山

Wàng Tiān Mén Shān
Gazing at Heaven’s Gate Mountains
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang
体裁Form
七言绝句 7-character quatrain
难度Level
入门 Beginner
主题Themes
山水 Landscape

导读About this poem

The Yangtze River smashes through two mountains, and a single sail comes gliding out of the sunrise. A young Li Bai paints a whole landscape — in motion — in four lines.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

天门山是长江边上隔江相对的两座山，远远看去像一道天上的大门。第一句说，长江像一把巨斧，把天门山从中间劈开，江水冲了过去；第二句说，碧绿的江水向东奔流，到了这里却被山挡得打着旋儿转回来。一“开”一“回”，写出了江的力量和山的险要。第三句最妙：明明是船在走，诗人却说两岸青山“相对出”，好像山自己迎面走出来——这是坐在船上看到的真实感觉。最后，一片孤帆从太阳那边驶来，画面一下子开阔明亮。全诗二十八个字，有山有水，有动有静，颜色鲜明，像一幅会动的山水画。

In English

Heaven’s Gate is a pair of mountains facing each other across the Yangtze, like the two posts of a giant gate. In the first line the river seems to split the mountain in two and burst through. In the second, the clear green water racing east is forced by the cliffs to swirl and turn back. One verb shows the river’s power (开, “to open”), the other the mountains’ strength (回, “to turn back”). The third line is the cleverest. It is really the boat that moves, yet the poet says the green mountains on both banks “come out” to meet each other — exactly what you feel sitting on a moving boat. Finally a single sail glides in from where the sun is rising, and the whole scene opens up into light and space. In twenty-eight characters we get mountains and water, motion and stillness, green, blue and gold — like a landscape painting that moves. It is a favourite in Chinese primary schools.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) is traditionally said to have written this around 725, aged about 25, when he left Sichuan and sailed down the Yangtze for the first time. Heaven’s Gate Mountains stand on the river near today’s Ma’anshan in Anhui.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

天门中断楚江开，

tiān mén zhōng duàn chǔ jiāng kāi

Heaven’s Gate is split in two where the great river forces through;

碧水东流至此回。

bì shuǐ dōng liú zhì cǐ huí

the green water rushing east swirls back against the cliffs.

两岸青山相对出，

liǎng àn qīng shān xiāng duì chū

On either bank, green mountains step out face to face,

孤帆一片日边来。

gū fān yī piàn rì biān lái

and one lone sail comes gliding from the sun.
