导读About this poem

Du Fu defends four earlier poets against their critics with a famous put-down: you and your names will vanish, but their work will flow on like the great rivers forever.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首“论诗诗”：用诗来评论诗人。“王杨卢骆”是初唐四杰——王勃、杨炯、卢照邻、骆宾王，他们的诗在当时很流行。到杜甫的时代，有些人看不起他们，觉得他们的文章轻浮，不停地讥笑。杜甫回答得很干脆：你们这些人，身体和名字都会一起消失；而四杰的作品，就像长江黄河一样，万古长流，永远不会断。前两句写批评者的态度，后两句用江河作比喻，对比鲜明，气势很大。“不废江河万古流”今天仍常被引用，形容真正有价值的东西经得起时间考验，别人的嘲笑改变不了它。题目里的“戏”是“随便写写”的意思，其实杜甫说得很认真。

In English

This is a “poem about poetry” — literary criticism in verse. Wang, Yang, Lu and Luo are the Four Paragons of the Early Tang: Wang Bo, Yang Jiong, Lu Zhaolin and Luo Binwang, whose style was all the fashion in their day. By Du Fu’s time some critics looked down on them, calling their writing shallow and mocking them endlessly. Du Fu’s reply is blunt: you people — your bodies and your names will vanish together, while the Four Paragons’ work will run on like the Yangtze and the Yellow River, for ten thousand ages. The first two lines describe the critics, the last two answer with the image of the great rivers, and the contrast is sharp and powerful. The last line, 不废江河万古流, is still quoted today to say that work of real value survives time, no matter who laughs at it. The 戏 (“playful”) in the title means something like “just jotting this down” — but Du Fu means every word.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this set of six quatrains around 761 in Chengdu. Together they set out his views on poetry: respect the past, learn from every good writer, and don’t sneer at predecessors.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

王杨卢骆当时体，

wáng yáng lú luò dāng shí tǐ

Wang, Yang, Lu and Luo wrote in the style of their age,

轻薄为文哂未休。

qīng bó wéi wén shěn wèi xiū

and shallow critics write to mock them without end.

尔曹身与名俱灭，

ěr cáo shēn yǔ míng jù miè

But you — your bodies and your names will vanish,

不废江河万古流。

bù fèi jiāng hé wàn gǔ liú

while their rivers run on through every age.
