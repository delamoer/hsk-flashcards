导读About this poem

A young woman begs her sweetheart not to climb over the wall to see her — not because she doesn’t love him, but because her parents, her brothers and the neighbours will talk. A love song 2,500 years old that still sounds completely real.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首姑娘对情人说的话。她请求心上人仲子：别再翻进我们的村子、别爬我家的墙、别跳进我家的园子，别折断我家种的树。她马上解释：“岂敢爱之？”——我哪是舍不得那几棵树啊，我是怕父母、怕哥哥们、怕别人说闲话。最后每章都说“仲可怀也”：仲子，我心里是想你的；可是父母的话、哥哥们的话、众人的闲话，也真让人害怕。三章层层推进：仲子越来越近（村子→墙→园子），她害怕的人越来越多（父母→哥哥→所有人），左右为难的心情也越来越强。诗里的“爱”是古义“吝惜、舍不得”，不是今天的爱情。今天的成语“人言可畏”就出自这首诗。它写出了在家庭和礼教约束下恋爱的女子，又爱又怕的真实心理。

In English

The whole poem is a young woman talking to her lover, Zhongzi. She begs him: don’t climb into our village, don’t climb over our wall, don’t jump into our garden — and don’t break the trees we planted. Then she quickly explains herself: 岂敢爱之? — “It’s not that I care about the trees.” (爱 here has its old meaning, “to grudge, to hold dear”, not modern “love”.) What she fears is her parents, then her brothers, then everyone’s gossip. Each stanza ends the same way: “Oh Zhong, I do long for you — but what they would say frightens me too.” The three stanzas build carefully. Zhongzi gets closer each time (village, wall, garden), the circle of people she fears grows wider (parents, brothers, the whole town), and her dilemma grows sharper. The modern idiom 人言可畏, “gossip is a fearsome thing”, comes straight from this poem. Later Confucian scholars disapproved of the “songs of Zheng” as too romantic, but that is exactly why readers love this one: it catches a girl torn between love and family pressure, sounding as if she were speaking today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. The “Airs of Zheng” (郑风), from a state in present-day Henan, are known for their frank love songs — Confucius is said to have called Zheng music too sensual.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

将 qiāng（请求，不读 jiāng）；仲 zhòng；杞 qǐ；檀 tán；逾 yú。机器朗读容易把“将”读错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

将仲子兮，无逾我里，无折我树杞。

qiāng zhòng zǐ xī, wú yú wǒ lǐ, wú zhé wǒ shù qǐ

Please, Zhongzi, don’t climb over into our lane, don’t snap the willows we planted.

岂敢爱之？畏我父母。

qǐ gǎn ài zhī, wèi wǒ fù mǔ

It isn’t the trees I care about — I’m afraid of my parents.

仲可怀也，父母之言，亦可畏也。

zhòng kě huái yě, fù mǔ zhī yán, yì kě wèi yě

Oh Zhong, I do miss you — but what my parents would say, that frightens me too.

将仲子兮，无逾我墙，无折我树桑。

qiāng zhòng zǐ xī, wú yú wǒ qiáng, wú zhé wǒ shù sāng

Please, Zhongzi, don’t climb over our wall, don’t snap the mulberries we planted.

岂敢爱之？畏我诸兄。

qǐ gǎn ài zhī, wèi wǒ zhū xiōng

It isn’t the trees I care about — I’m afraid of my brothers.

仲可怀也，诸兄之言，亦可畏也。

zhòng kě huái yě, zhū xiōng zhī yán, yì kě wèi yě

Oh Zhong, I do miss you — but what my brothers would say, that frightens me too.
