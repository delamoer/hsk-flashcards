导读About this poem

“To catch the bandits, first catch their king” — this poem gave Chinese a proverb still used every day. But its real point comes at the end: the purpose of war is to stop aggression, not to kill as many as possible.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《前出塞》是杜甫写的一组九首诗，用一个出征士兵的口气来写。这是第六首。前四句像军中流行的口诀（顺口好记的话）：拉弓就要拉硬弓，用箭就要用长箭；射人先射他的马，抓贼先抓他们的头领。这四句讲的是打仗的方法，抓住关键，干脆有力，“擒贼先擒王”今天已经成了常用成语，意思是做事要先解决最关键的问题。后四句意思一转，讲打仗的道理：杀人也应该有个限度，每个国家都有自己的边界；只要能制止敌人的侵犯，何必杀伤那么多人呢？唐玄宗晚年常常为了扩张领土而发动战争，杜甫在这里委婉地表示反对。前面讲“怎么打”，后面讲“为什么打、打到什么程度”，层次清楚，见识很高。

In English

“Marching to the Frontier” is a set of nine poems in which Du Fu speaks in the voice of a soldier sent to the northwest. This is the sixth. The first four lines sound like a soldiers' rhyme, short and easy to remember: if you draw a bow, draw the strongest; if you use arrows, use the longest; to shoot a man, shoot his horse first; to catch the bandits, catch their king first. It is practical advice — go for what matters most — and the last line, 擒贼先擒王, is now a common idiom meaning “tackle the key problem first.” Then the poem changes direction. Killing, too, should have its limits; every country has its own borders. As long as you can stop the invader, why kill and wound so many? In Du Fu's time, Emperor Xuanzong was waging costly wars to push the borders outward, and this is a quiet but clear protest. The first half is about how to fight; the second asks why, and how far. That turn is what makes the poem more than a war song.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) is thought to have written the nine “Marching to the Frontier” poems around 751–752, when the Tang was fighting costly campaigns against Tibet in the northwest. (A later set of five is called “Later Marching to the Frontier”.)

挽弓当挽强，

wǎn gōng dāng wǎn qiáng

If you draw a bow, draw the strongest;

用箭当用长。

yòng jiàn dāng yòng cháng

if you use arrows, use the longest.

射人先射马，

shè rén xiān shè mǎ

To bring down the rider, first shoot his horse;

擒贼先擒王。

qín zéi xiān qín wáng

to catch the bandits, first catch their king.

杀人亦有限，

shā rén yì yǒu xiàn

But killing, too, should have its limits,

列国自有疆。

liè guó zì yǒu jiāng

and every nation has its borders.

苟能制侵陵，

gǒu néng zhì qīn líng

If we can stop the invader,

岂在多杀伤。

qǐ zài duō shā shāng

why kill and maim so many?
