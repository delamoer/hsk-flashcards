导读About this poem

A lazy spring morning: the poet oversleeps, wakes to birdsong, and suddenly remembers last night’s wind and rain — how many flowers fell? One of the first poems Chinese children learn.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写春天早上醒来的一刹那。第一句说春天的觉睡得太香，连天亮了都不知道；第二句写醒来听见到处都是鸟叫，春天热闹又有生气。第三句忽然一转，诗人想起昨夜听到的风声雨声；第四句问：花被打落了多少呢？前两句是喜欢春天，后两句是心疼春天，快乐里带着一点点可惜。全诗没有直接写看到的景色，而是用耳朵写春天——鸟声、风雨声，再让读者自己去想象满地落花，这就是“以声写景”。语言简单得像说话，却把春天的美好和短暂都写出来了，所以一千多年来人人会背。

In English

The poem captures the moment of waking on a spring morning. In the first line the poet has slept so soundly that he didn’t notice the dawn; in the second, birds are singing everywhere — spring is alive and noisy. Then the third line turns: he remembers hearing wind and rain during the night. The last line asks a simple question: how many flowers have fallen? So the first half is delight in spring, the second half a small worry for it — joy with a touch of regret. Notice that the poet never describes what he sees. Everything comes through the ears: birdsong, wind, rain. The fallen petals are left for us to picture. Chinese critics call this “painting a scene with sound”. With words as easy as everyday speech, the poem shows both how lovely spring is and how quickly it passes, which is why almost every Chinese person can recite it.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Meng Haoran (689–740) spent most of his life as a recluse near his hometown, Xiangyang (in today’s Hubei), and is known for poems about mountains and the countryside. This poem is thought to date from his years living in the hills there.
