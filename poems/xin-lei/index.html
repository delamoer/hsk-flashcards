导读About this poem

Nature has already arranged every flower of spring; all it needs is the first clap of thunder to set them blooming. A cheerful poem about waiting for a new beginning.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

“新雷”是春天的第一声雷。诗人说：大自然不会说话，却很有情意，每当寒冬过去，人们就能感觉到春天来了。后两句很有想象力：千万种红红紫紫的花，大自然早就安排好了，只等新雷响起第一声，它们就一齐开放。这里用了拟人的手法——把“造物”（大自然）写成一个有感情、会安排的人。诗人写的是春雷，心里盼的却不只是春天：他生活在清朝后期，社会沉闷，他盼望像春雷一样的新气象，让人才和新事物像百花一样开放。这首诗明快乐观，“千红万紫”“只待新雷第一声”读起来让人充满希望。

In English

“New thunder” is the first thunder of spring. The poet says that nature never speaks, yet it has feelings: every year, as the cold ends, you can sense spring arriving. The second half is a delightful piece of imagination. Nature has already arranged “a thousand reds and ten thousand purples” — every flower in its place — and is only waiting for the first roll of thunder to let them all bloom. This is personification: 造物, “the Creator” or nature, is treated like a person who cares and makes plans. The poem is about spring thunder, but many readers hear more in it. Zhang Weiping lived in the late Qing, a time that felt stagnant, and he seems to be longing for a fresh start that would let talent and new ideas burst out like spring flowers. Whatever the reading, the tone is bright and hopeful, and the last line — just waiting for the first sound of new thunder — is easy to remember. In the traditional calendar, the first spring thunder is linked with the solar term Jingzhe, “Awakening of Insects”, when everything starts to stir.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhang Weiping (1780–1859) was a poet and official from Panyu, near Guangzhou. He later lived through the Opium War and wrote poems about it; this short poem welcomes the first thunder of spring.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

却 què。觉 jué（感觉，不读 jiào）。著：安排著，古同“着”，一般注 zhuó（附着、安放），也有读 zhe 的；网站暂注 zhuó。机器朗读已替换。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

造物无言却有情，

zào wù wú yán què yǒu qíng

Nature never speaks, yet it cares;

每于寒尽觉春生。

měi yú hán jìn jué chūn shēng

each time the cold is done, you feel spring stir.

千红万紫安排著，

qiān hóng wàn zǐ ān pái zhuó

A thousand reds and purples stand ready in their places,

只待新雷第一声。

zhǐ dài xīn léi dì yī shēng

waiting only for the first new thunder.
