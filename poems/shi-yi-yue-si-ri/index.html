十一月四日风雨大作·其二

Shíyī Yuè Sì Rì Fēng Yǔ Dà Zuò
A Great Storm on the Fourth Day of the Eleventh Month (No. 2)

导读About this poem

An old man lies in bed in a lonely village, too frail to get up — yet still dreams of guarding the frontier. On a stormy night the wind and rain turn, in his dream, into armoured horses crossing frozen rivers.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写于陆游六十八岁时，他已经退居家乡山阴多年，生活清苦，身体也不好。一、二句是自述：我直挺挺地躺在孤零零的村子里，却不为自己悲哀，还想着为国家去守卫边疆。“轮台”是汉代西域的地名，这里泛指边关。“僵卧”和“尚思”形成强烈对比：身体老了、动不了，心却还年轻。三、四句写夜深了，躺着听外面的风雨声，听着听着睡着了，梦里披着铁甲的战马正跨过结冰的河流去作战。风雨声和战马奔腾的声音连在一起，现实与梦境自然衔接。“铁马冰河入梦来”写出了一个老人至死不变的报国之心，成了陆游最有名的诗句之一。

In English

Lu You wrote this at sixty-seven, after years of retirement in his home village near Shaoxing, poor and in poor health. The first two lines are a self-portrait: I lie stiff in bed in a lonely village, but I do not pity myself — I still dream of guarding the frontier for my country. Luntai was a Han-dynasty outpost in the far west, used here to mean any border post. Notice the contrast between 僵卧 (“lying stiff”) and 尚思 (“still thinking of”): the body is old and stuck, the heart is still young. In the last two lines the night is late; he lies listening to the wind driving the rain, and drifts off. In his dream, armoured horses are crossing frozen rivers into battle. The roar of the storm outside slides into the thunder of hooves, so reality and dream join without a seam. The final line, 铁马冰河入梦来, has become one of Lu You’s best-known: the picture of an old man whose wish to serve his country never fades, even in sleep. This is the second of two poems with the same title; the first describes the storm itself.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) spent his life urging the Southern Song court to win back the north from the Jin. He wrote this in the winter of 1192, living in retirement at Shanyin (today’s Shaoxing).

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

僵卧孤村不自哀，

jiāng wò gū cūn bù zì āi

Stiff in my bed in a lonely village, I don’t feel sorry for myself —

尚思为国戍轮台。

shàng sī wèi guó shù lún tái

I still long to stand guard at the frontier.

夜阑卧听风吹雨，

yè lán wò tīng fēng chuī yǔ

Deep in the night I lie and hear the wind lash the rain,

铁马冰河入梦来。

tiě mǎ bīng hé rù mèng lái

and armoured horses on frozen rivers gallop into my dreams.
