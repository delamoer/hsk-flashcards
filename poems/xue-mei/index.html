导读About this poem

Snow and plum blossom are arguing over which of them is the true sign of spring, and the poet can’t decide. His verdict: the snow is whiter, the plum smells sweeter — each has what the other lacks.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗把雪和梅花写成两个争强好胜的人：它们都说自己最能代表早春，谁也不肯认输。诗人（“骚人”）只好放下笔，很难给它们评出高低。后两句是有名的评判：论白，梅花要比雪差三分；论香，雪又比梅花少了一段。“逊”和“输”都是“比不上”的意思，两句一对，公平地说出了各自的长处和短处。这里用了“拟人”的手法，把雪和梅写得像会吵架的小孩，很有趣味。诗里也藏着一个道理：每样东西都有长处，也有不足，不要只看一面；人和人之间也是这样，应该取长补短。所以这首诗常被用来说明“尺有所短，寸有所长”。冬末春初，雪和梅常常同时出现，中国诗人喜欢把它们放在一起写。

In English

The poet turns snow and plum blossom into two competitive rivals. Both claim to be the true sign of early spring, and neither will give in. The poet — a 骚人, “man of letters” — puts down his brush: he finds it very hard to judge between them. The last two lines give his famous verdict. In whiteness, the plum must yield to the snow by three parts; in fragrance, the snow loses to the plum by a whole stretch. 逊 and 输 both mean “to fall short of”, and the two lines balance each other perfectly, fairly naming each side’s strength and weakness. Giving the snow and plum human feelings — personification — makes them sound like two squabbling children, which is part of the fun. There is a lesson hidden here too: everything has its strong points and its weak points, so don’t judge from one side only; people should learn from each other’s strengths. The poem is often quoted with the saying “a foot can be too short and an inch can be long enough” (尺有所短，寸有所长). In late winter and early spring, snow and plum blossom often appear together, and Chinese poets love to pair them.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The poem is traditionally attributed to Lu Meipo of the Southern Song, whose given name is recorded as Lu Yue; almost nothing is known about his life. He is remembered mainly for his two poems on snow and plum blossom.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

梅雪争春未肯降，

méi xuě zhēng chūn wèi kěn xiáng

Plum and snow both claim the spring, and neither will give in;

骚人阁笔费评章。

sāo rén gé bǐ fèi píng zhāng

the poet sets down his brush, unable to judge.

梅须逊雪三分白，

méi xū xùn xuě sān fēn bái

The plum is three shades less white than the snow,

雪却输梅一段香。

xuě què shū méi yī duàn xiāng

but the snow lacks the plum’s sweet scent.
