导读About this poem

A poem written for a painting — yet Su Shi makes the picture come alive: peach blossoms by the bamboo, ducks paddling in water that is just turning warm. Its second line is still an everyday Chinese saying about who notices change first.

中文解析

这是一首题画诗，就是写在画上、为一幅画写的诗。画是僧人惠崇画的春江晚景。第一句写画面：竹林外面，两三枝桃花开了，“三两枝”说明春天刚刚来。第二句最有名：“春江水暖鸭先知”——画上只有鸭子在游，诗人却写出了画不出来的东西：水的温度。鸭子整天在水里，所以最先感到江水变暖。第三句写地上长满蒌蒿，芦苇刚冒出短短的新芽；第四句由此联想到：这正是河豚从海里游上江来的时候。从眼睛看到的，写到身体感觉到的，再写到想象中的味道，一幅静止的画就变得有温度、有生命了。今天“春江水暖鸭先知”常用来比喻身在其中的人最先感到变化。

In English

This is a tíhuà shī, a poem written for a painting — here a spring river scene by the monk Huichong. The first line simply describes the picture: beyond the bamboo, two or three sprays of peach blossom. Only “two or three”, so spring has only just arrived. The second line is the famous one: “The spring river is warming — the ducks know it first.” The painting shows ducks swimming; Su Shi adds something no brush can paint, the temperature of the water. The ducks spend all day in it, so they are the first to feel it change. The third line looks at the bank, covered with wild artemisia, with short new reed shoots just poking up. That leads to the last line: this is exactly when the river pufferfish swim up from the sea — a seasonal delicacy cooked with those very plants. So the poem moves from what the eye sees, to what the body feels, to what the mouth imagines, and a still picture becomes warm and alive. Today 春江水暖鸭先知 is used to say that the people closest to something notice a change before anyone else.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Su Shi (1037–1101), also called Su Dongpo, was a Song-dynasty official, poet, essayist, painter and calligrapher. He wrote two poems for paintings by his friend the monk Huichong, probably around 1085, in Kaifeng; this is the first.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

竹外桃花三两枝，

zhú wài táo huā sān liǎng zhī

Past the bamboo, a few sprigs of peach in bloom —

春江水暖鸭先知。

chūn jiāng shuǐ nuǎn yā xiān zhī

the river is warming, and the ducks are the first to know.

蒌蒿满地芦芽短，

lóu hāo mǎn dì lú yá duǎn

Wild artemisia everywhere, the reed shoots still short:

正是河豚欲上时。

zhèng shì hé tún yù shàng shí

just the season when the pufferfish swim upriver.
