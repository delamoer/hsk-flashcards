导读About this poem

The poem that gave Chinese the phrase “seventy is rare”: a court official pawns his spring clothes to drink by the water, watching butterflies and dragonflies, and begs the spring to stay a little longer.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

曲江是唐代长安城东南的风景名胜。这时杜甫在朝廷做一个小官，但政治上很不得意。诗一开头就很特别：每天下朝回来，就把春天的衣服拿去当铺换钱，到江边喝得大醉才回家。为什么这样？第二联给了答案：酒债到处都欠着，反正“人生七十古来稀”——人能活到七十岁，自古就少见，不如及时享受。这句后来变成常用语，“古稀”成了七十岁的代称。第三联转写春景，是有名的工整对仗：蝴蝶在花丛深处时隐时现，蜻蜓点一下水面，慢慢地飞。“深深”“款款”两个叠词，把小动物写得又细又美。最后两句对春光说话（拟人）：请你和我一起流转，哪怕只是短暂地互相欣赏，也别违背我。表面是赏春行乐，底下藏着失意和对时光流逝的不安。

In English

Qujiang was a famous scenic lake in the south-east of Chang’an. Du Fu held a minor post at court at the time and felt frustrated and useless. The opening is startling: every day after court he pawns his spring clothes and comes home from the riverside drunk. Why? The second couplet answers: he owes wine debts everywhere, but “since ancient times, few people live to seventy” — so why not enjoy life now? This line became a proverb, and 古稀 (“rare since ancient times”) is still a polite word for the age of seventy. The third couplet turns to the spring scene with a perfectly matched pair of lines: butterflies weave deep among the flowers, appearing and disappearing, while dragonflies dip the water and fly on, unhurried. The doubled words 深深 and 款款 make the tiny creatures delicate and vivid. Finally he speaks to the spring itself, a kind of personification: let us drift along together; even if we can only enjoy each other for a short while, don’t turn away from me. On the surface it is a poem about drinking and enjoying spring; underneath lie disappointment and unease about time slipping away.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this in late spring 758 in Chang’an, where he served as a Reminder (左拾遗), a low-ranking adviser to the emperor. After speaking up for a dismissed minister he had fallen out of favour, and he was sent away from the capital later that year.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

朝回日日典春衣，

cháo huí rì rì diǎn chūn yī

Home from court, day after day, I pawn my spring robes

每日江头尽醉归。

měi rì jiāng tóu jìn zuì guī

and walk back from the water drunk each night.

酒债寻常行处有，

jiǔ zhài xún cháng xíng chù yǒu

I owe for wine wherever I go — so what?

人生七十古来稀。

rén shēng qī shí gǔ lái xī

Few have ever lived to seventy.

穿花蛱蝶深深见，

chuān huā jiá dié shēn shēn xiàn

Butterflies thread the flowers, glimpsed deep inside;

点水蜻蜓款款飞。

diǎn shuǐ qīng tíng kuǎn kuǎn fēi

dragonflies tap the water and drift away.

传语风光共流转，

chuán yǔ fēng guāng gòng liú zhuǎn

Spring, I have a message: travel on with me,

暂时相赏莫相违。

zàn shí xiāng shǎng mò xiāng wéi

and for this short while, don’t leave me behind.
