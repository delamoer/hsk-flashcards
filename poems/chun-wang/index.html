导读About this poem

Spring comes to a capital ruined by war. Du Fu, trapped in the occupied city and cut off from his family, sees flowers and hears birds — and they only make him weep.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写于安史之乱期间。叛军攻占了长安，杜甫被困在城里，和家人失去联系。第一句“国破山河在”非常沉重：国家破碎了，只有山河还在。春天来了，城里却草木丛生，说明已经没有人了。三、四句有两种理解：花和鸟本来让人高兴，可诗人伤心时代、怨恨离别，看到花反而流泪，听到鸟叫反而心惊——这叫“以乐景写哀情”，用美好的景物衬托悲伤。五、六句写战火连着烧了好几个月，一封家书比万两黄金还珍贵，“家书抵万金”今天还常被引用。最后两句写自己：白发越抓越少，连簪子都快插不住了。一个人的白头，写出了国家的苦难。全诗把爱国和想家两种感情紧紧连在一起。

In English

Du Fu wrote this in the spring of 757, during the An Lushan Rebellion. Rebel troops held the capital, Chang’an, and Du Fu was stuck inside, with no news of his wife and children. The first line is one of the heaviest in Chinese poetry: the country is broken, only the mountains and rivers remain. Spring has come, but the city is overgrown — a quiet way of saying the people are gone. The second couplet can be read two ways: the flowers “splash tears” and the birds “startle the heart”, or the poet weeps at flowers and is startled by birdsong. Either way, things that should be joyful only deepen the sorrow — a technique Chinese critics call using a happy scene to express grief. Then comes another famous line: with beacon fires burning for three months, a letter from home is worth ten thousand pieces of gold. The ending turns to his own body: his white hair is so thin from worried scratching that it can hardly hold a hairpin. One man’s aging head carries the suffering of a whole country; love of country and longing for family become one feeling.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) was captured by An Lushan’s rebels in 756 while trying to reach the new emperor, and was held in occupied Chang’an. He wrote this in March 757, before escaping that summer.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

浑欲不胜簪：胜 读 shēng（禁得起、能承受），统编教材注 shēng；但 2016 年《普通话异读词审音表》征求意见稿起，“胜”统读 shèng，部分新版教辅已改注 shèng。网站暂按 shēng。另：搔 sāo；簪 zān；溅 jiàn；抵 dǐ。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

国破山河在，

guó pò shān hé zài

The nation is shattered; hills and rivers remain.

城春草木深。

chéng chūn cǎo mù shēn

Spring in the city: grass and trees grow wild.

感时花溅泪，

gǎn shí huā jiàn lèi

Grieving for these times, I weep at flowers;

恨别鸟惊心。

hèn bié niǎo jīng xīn

torn from my family, birdsong makes me flinch.

烽火连三月，

fēng huǒ lián sān yuè

War fires have burned for months on end;

家书抵万金。

jiā shū dǐ wàn jīn

a letter from home would be worth a fortune.

白头搔更短，

bái tóu sāo gèng duǎn

I scratch my white head; the hair grows thin,

浑欲不胜簪。

hún yù bù shēng zān

too thin, almost, to hold a hairpin.
