导读About this poem

A man in the warm south meets a courier, snaps off a sprig of plum blossom and sends it to a friend far away in the cold northwest: “I have nothing else here — so I send you a branch of spring.”

中文解析

这首小诗讲了一件很简单的事：诗人在江南碰到一个送信的驿使，就折下一枝花（一般理解为梅花），请他带给远在陇头（今天甘肃、陕西一带的边地）的朋友。“江南无所有”是谦虚的话：我这里没什么好东西送你。最后一句最妙——“聊赠一枝春”，不说“一枝梅”，而说“一枝春”。用一个具体的东西代表一个大的概念，这叫“借代”。北方边地还很冷，朋友收到这枝花，就好像收到了整个江南的春天。礼物很小，情意很重，这正是中国人说的“礼轻情意重”。后来“一枝春”成了梅花的雅称，“折梅寄远”“驿寄梅花”也成了诗词里表达思念朋友的常用典故。

In English

The story is simple. In the south, the poet runs into a government courier and breaks off a flowering branch — traditionally understood as plum blossom — for him to carry to a friend at Longtou, the cold frontier hills of the northwest. “The south has nothing much to offer” is polite modesty: I have no fine gift for you. Then comes the line everyone remembers: “I simply send you a branch of spring.” He doesn’t say “a branch of plum”; he says “a branch of spring”. Using one small, concrete thing to stand for something much larger is a figure of speech called jièdài. Up in the frozen north, the friend opens the package and gets the whole southern spring in his hands. The gift is tiny, the feeling is huge — exactly what the Chinese saying 礼轻情意重 (“a light gift, a heavy friendship”) means. Ever since, “a branch of spring” has been a poetic name for plum blossom, and “sending plum blossom by courier” a stock way of saying “I miss you, my friend.”

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu Kai (dates uncertain) is traditionally said to have sent this to his friend Fan Ye (398–445), the historian who wrote the History of the Later Han. The poem survives only as a quotation in an old regional record, and scholars have long questioned who wrote it and to whom, since the geography doesn’t quite fit the two men’s lives.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

晔 读 yè。驿使 读 yì shǐ（送信的人），“使”不读 shì。陇 读 lǒng。“一枝春”的“一”按本调标 yī，朗读时自然变调。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行本。原诗见于《荆州记》（《太平御览》引用），题目一作《赠范晔诗》。首句“折花”一作“折梅”（《太平御览》卷十九等）；《太平御览》卷九百七十引作“折花”，与通行本同。
