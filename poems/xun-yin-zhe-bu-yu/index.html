导读About this poem

The poet climbs a mountain to visit a hermit, but only finds his young servant. Twenty characters, a whole conversation — and a master who vanishes into the clouds.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗讲了一个小故事：诗人到山里拜访一位隐居的人，没见到他，只在松树下见到他的小徒弟。第一句是诗人问，后三句都是童子的回答：“师父采药去了”“就在这座山里”“可是云太深，不知道在哪儿”。诗里只写了答话，问题却藏在答话里，读者自己能猜出诗人问了什么——三问三答，只用二十个字，这叫“寓问于答”。诗人的心情也一层层变化：听说出去了，有点失望；听说就在山里，又有了希望；听到云深不知处，只好失望地回去。而那位隐者，在松树、白云、草药之间，像神仙一样，让人更加向往。“云深不知处”今天也常用来形容找不到、看不清。

In English

The poem tells a tiny story. The poet climbs into the mountains to visit a hermit, doesn’t find him, and meets only his young pupil under a pine tree. The first line is the poet asking; the other three are all the boy’s answers: “Master has gone to gather herbs.” “He’s somewhere on this mountain.” “But the clouds are so thick, I don’t know where.” Only the answers are written down — we can work out the questions ourselves. Three questions and three answers in twenty characters: Chinese critics call this “hiding the questions inside the answers”. The poet’s mood shifts with each reply: disappointment (he’s out), hope (he’s nearby), then disappointment again (lost in the clouds). Meanwhile the hermit, somewhere among pines, white clouds and medicinal herbs, seems almost like an immortal, which makes him even more admirable. The last line, 云深不知处, is still used today for something hidden, hard to find or see clearly.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Jia Dao (779–843) was a Buddhist monk before returning to lay life to try the civil-service exams. He is famous for agonising over single words — the story of him debating “push” (推) or “knock” (敲) gave Chinese the word 推敲, “to weigh one’s words”. The poem is also attributed to Sun Ge, but is traditionally credited to Jia Dao.

松下问童子，

sōng xià wèn tóng zǐ

Under the pines I asked the boy.

言师采药去。

yán shī cǎi yào qù

“Master’s gone gathering herbs,” he said,

只在此山中，

zhǐ zài cǐ shān zhōng

“somewhere up on this mountain —

云深不知处。

yún shēn bù zhī chù

but the clouds are thick, and I can’t say where.”
