Sòng Yuán Èr Shǐ Ānxī
Seeing Yuan the Second Off on a Mission to Anxi

导读About this poem

A morning farewell at an inn outside Chang’an: fresh rain, green willows, one last cup of wine — because beyond Yang Pass there will be no old friends. For centuries this was the farewell song in China.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

王维的朋友元二要出使安西（今天新疆一带），王维在渭城（长安西北）为他送行。前两句写送别的早晨：一场小雨刚好打湿了路上的尘土，旅店旁边的柳树被雨洗过，绿得发亮。景色清新明朗，并不悲伤；可是“柳”谐音“留”，古人送别常折柳相赠，所以青青柳色里已经藏着“不想让你走”的心意。后两句是送别酒桌上的一句话：“再喝完这一杯吧，向西出了阳关，就再也没有老朋友了。”前面一定已经喝了很多杯，这“更尽一杯”写出了舍不得分开、一拖再拖的心情。阳关之外是荒凉的沙漠，路远又危险，这句劝酒的话又亲切又沉重。这首诗后来被谱成歌曲《阳关三叠》，成为古人送别时最常唱的歌。

In English

Wang Wei’s friend Yuan, “the Second” (the second son of his family), is leaving on an official mission to Anxi, the Tang military command in the far west, in today’s Xinjiang. They say goodbye at Weicheng, the first stop northwest of Chang’an. The first two lines set a fresh morning scene: a light rain has just settled the dust on the road, and the willows by the inn, washed clean, are a brilliant green. Nothing here sounds sad. But willow (柳 liǔ) sounds like “stay” (留 liú), and friends traditionally gave each other a willow twig at parting, so the green willows quietly say “I wish you didn’t have to go.” The second half is simply one sentence spoken at the table: “Have one more cup — once you go west through Yang Pass, there will be no old friends.” “One more” tells us they have already been drinking for a while and neither wants to stand up. Yang Pass was the gateway to the desert; beyond it lay a long, dangerous road among strangers. That is why this plain, warm toast hits so hard. The poem was set to music as “Three Refrains at Yang Pass” (阳关三叠) and became the standard song of farewell; it is also known as “Song of Weicheng”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) served as an official in the Tang capital. Nothing is known of Yuan the Second beyond this poem; Anxi was the Tang Protectorate governing the Western Regions, a journey of months from Chang’an.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“朝雨”的“朝”读 zhāo（早晨）。“浥”读 yì（湿润）。“客舍”的“舍”读 shè。“更尽”的“更”读 gèng（再）。题目“使安西”的“使”读 shǐ（出使）。
