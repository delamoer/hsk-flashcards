导读About this poem

A married woman is given two pearls by a man who admires her. She is moved — and gives them back, in tears. On the surface it is a love story; underneath, it is a poet politely turning down a powerful warlord.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗用一位已婚女子的口气说话：你明知我有丈夫，还送我一对明珠；我感激你的情意，把明珠系在红绸短衣上。可是我家高楼连着皇家园林，丈夫在宫里当侍卫；我知道你用心光明磊落，但我发誓要和丈夫同生共死。最后我流着泪把明珠还给你，只恨没在出嫁以前遇见你。诗写得委婉动人，既感激又坚决。其实这是一首“比喻诗”：当时手握兵权的藩镇李师道想拉拢张籍，张籍不愿依附，又不好得罪他，就借女子拒绝追求者来回绝。“丈夫”比喻朝廷，“明珠”比喻对方的好意。“还君明珠双泪垂，恨不相逢未嫁时”成了名句。

In English

The poem is spoken by a married woman. “You knew I had a husband, yet you gave me a pair of bright pearls. Touched by your feeling, I tied them to my red silk jacket. But my family’s tall house stands beside the palace gardens, and my husband carries a halberd as a guard in the palace. I know your heart is as open as the sun and moon, yet I have sworn to live and die with my husband. So I give your pearls back with tears running down — if only we had met before I married.” It is gentle and grateful, but completely firm. And it is really a coded message. A powerful regional warlord, Li Shidao, was trying to win Zhang Ji over to his side. Zhang Ji did not want to serve him but could not afford to offend him, so he answered with this poem: the husband stands for the imperial court, the pearls for the warlord’s generous offer. Writing about loyalty to a ruler through a woman’s loyalty to her husband is an old tradition in Chinese poetry. The closing couplet — returning the pearls in tears, wishing they had met earlier — became a famous line about love that came too late.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhang Ji (c. 766–c. 830) was a Tang poet and official, a friend of Han Yu and Bai Juyi. The poem’s full title adds “sent to Li Shidao, Minister of Works at Dongping” — a warlord who was courting talented men to his side.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

多音字：系在 jì（系上、打结，不读 xì）；还君 huán（归还）。生僻字：襦 rú（短上衣）、戟 jǐ（古代兵器）。机器朗读“系在”易读成 xì，已在“朗读用字”列替换为同音字“记”，音频需人工听验。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行版本，题目全称《节妇吟·寄东平李司空师道》。末句通行作“恨不相逢未嫁时”，一作“何不相逢未嫁时”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

君知妾有夫，

jūn zhī qiè yǒu fū

You knew that I was married,

赠妾双明珠。

zèng qiè shuāng míng zhū

and still you gave me two bright pearls.

感君缠绵意，

gǎn jūn chán mián yì

Touched by the tenderness you showed,

系在红罗襦。

jì zài hóng luó rú

I tied them to my red silk jacket.

妾家高楼连苑起，

qiè jiā gāo lóu lián yuàn qǐ

But my house stands tall beside the royal park,

良人执戟明光里。

liáng rén zhí jǐ míng guāng lǐ

and my husband stands guard in the palace with his halberd.

知君用心如日月，

zhī jūn yòng xīn rú rì yuè

I know your heart is open as the sun and moon,

事夫誓拟同生死。

shì fū shì nǐ tóng shēng sǐ

but I have sworn to live and die beside my husband.

还君明珠双泪垂，

huán jūn míng zhū shuāng lèi chuí

I hand you back your pearls, and my tears fall —

恨不相逢未嫁时。

hèn bù xiāng féng wèi jià shí

if only we had met before I was wed.
