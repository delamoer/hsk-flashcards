采桑子（轻舟短棹西湖好）

Picking Mulberries (West Lake in a Little Boat)

导读About this poem

An old statesman, newly retired, drifts across a lake in a small boat. The water is so still the boat seems not to move — until a bird takes off from the shore.

中文解析

欧阳修晚年退休，住在颍州（今安徽阜阳），写了十首《采桑子》，每首都以“西湖好”开头。这里的西湖是颍州西湖，不是杭州西湖。这一首写坐小船游湖：绿水弯弯曲曲，长堤上长满芳草，远远传来的音乐好像一路跟着船走。下阕写得特别细：没有风，水面像琉璃（玻璃一样的宝石）一样光滑，人坐在船上感觉不到船在动；只有水面轻轻荡开的波纹，和被惊起、擦着岸边飞走的水鸟，才告诉你船其实在走。这是“以动写静”——用一点点动静衬出湖面的安静。全词没有一个字写心情，但能读出一个老人放下官场后的轻松自在。

In English

In retirement Ouyang Xiu settled in Yingzhou (today’s Fuyang in Anhui) and wrote a set of ten lyrics to the tune “Picking Mulberries”, each beginning “West Lake is lovely”. This is Yingzhou’s West Lake, not the more famous one in Hangzhou. Here he goes out in a light boat with a short oar. Green water winds ahead, fragrant grass covers the long dyke, and faint music from other pleasure boats seems to follow him everywhere. The second half is beautifully observed. There is no wind, and the surface is as smooth as glass — so smooth he can’t feel the boat moving at all. Only two things give the movement away: the tiny ripples spreading from the hull, and a shore bird that startles and skims off along the bank. Using a small movement to show how still everything is (“using motion to depict stillness”) is a favourite technique of Chinese poets. The poem never mentions his feelings, but you can sense the ease of a man who has finally put down the burdens of office.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Ouyang Xiu (1007–1072) was a leading statesman, historian and essayist of the Northern Song. He loved Yingzhou’s West Lake from his time as its prefect and retired there in 1071, the year before he died; this set of lyrics comes from those last years.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

轻舟短棹西湖好，

qīng zhōu duǎn zhào xī hú hǎo

In a light boat with a short oar, West Lake is lovely:

绿水逶迤，芳草长堤，

lǜ shuǐ wēi yí, fāng cǎo cháng dī

green water winding away, sweet grass along the long dyke,

隐隐笙歌处处随。

yǐn yǐn shēng gē chù chù suí

and faint music drifting after me wherever I go.

无风水面琉璃滑，

wú fēng shuǐ miàn liú lí huá

Not a breath of wind; the lake is smooth as glass,

不觉船移，微动涟漪，

bù jué chuán yí, wēi dòng lián yī

I can’t feel the boat moving — only small ripples spreading,

惊起沙禽掠岸飞。

jīng qǐ shā qín lüè àn fēi

and a startled shore bird lifting off, skimming low along the bank.
