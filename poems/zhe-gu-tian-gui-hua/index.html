导读About this poem

Pale yellow, tiny, easy to overlook — yet Li Qingzhao crowns the osmanthus “first among flowers”, because what it offers is not colour but fragrance.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首“咏物词”——借写一样东西来表达自己的看法。李清照写的是桂花：颜色淡淡的、浅浅的黄，花朵小而柔；它不靠近人，长在幽静的地方，只把香气留给人。她说：何必要浅绿、深红那样鲜艳的颜色呢？桂花本来就是花中第一流！这里赞美的是一种不靠外表、靠内在品质取胜的美。下阕更大胆：梅花一定会嫉妒它，菊花也应该感到害羞；在栏杆旁边开放的时候，它是中秋时节的花中之冠。最后还“批评”起古代的大诗人屈原（骚人）：你真是太没有情思了，当年为什么不把桂花写进你的诗里？其实《楚辞》中也提到过桂，这里是词人故意替桂花“抱不平”，语气俏皮。全词也可以看作李清照自己的人格写照：不追求华丽，看重内在的高洁。

In English

This is a yǒngwù lyric — a poem that describes one thing in order to say something bigger. Li Qingzhao’s subject is the osmanthus: its colour is a dim, pale yellow, its tiny flowers soft; it keeps its distance from people and leaves behind only its scent. Why would it need light green or deep red? It is simply first among flowers. What she is praising is beauty that comes from within rather than from showy looks. The second stanza grows bolder: the plum blossom must be jealous, the chrysanthemum ought to be ashamed — blooming by the painted railing, the osmanthus is the queen of mid-autumn. Finally she teases the great ancient poet Qu Yuan, author of the Li Sao, who filled his verses with fragrant plants: how could he be so lacking in feeling as to leave it out? (The osmanthus does in fact appear in the Songs of Chu; she is defending the flower with playful exaggeration.) Many readers see the poem as a self-portrait: someone who cares nothing for display and everything for inner quality.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Qingzhao (1084–c. 1155) is China’s most celebrated woman poet, famous for her lyrics on love, loss and exile. This poem is usually dated to her early, happier years, before the fall of the Northern Song.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

冠中秋 guàn（居第一，不读 guān）；菊应羞 yīng；可煞 kě shà（真是、太）；情思 qíng sī（诗词里也有读 sì 的）。机器朗读“冠、应、煞”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列换成同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

暗淡轻黄体性柔，

àn dàn qīng huáng tǐ xìng róu

Dim and pale, a soft light yellow, gentle by nature,

情疏迹远只香留。

qíng shū jì yuǎn zhǐ xiāng liú

it keeps to itself, far away, and leaves only its fragrance.

何须浅碧深红色，

hé xū qiǎn bì shēn hóng sè

What need has it of pale greens or deep reds?

自是花中第一流。

zì shì huā zhōng dì yī liú

It is, quite simply, the first of all flowers.

梅定妒，菊应羞，

méi dìng dù, jú yīng xiū

The plum must envy it, the chrysanthemum blush,

画阑开处冠中秋。

huà lán kāi chù guàn zhōng qiū

blooming by the painted railing, it reigns over mid-autumn.

骚人可煞无情思，

sāo rén kě shà wú qíng sī

That poet of the Li Sao had no heart at all:

何事当年不见收。

hé shì dāng nián bù jiàn shōu

how could he leave it out of his poems?
