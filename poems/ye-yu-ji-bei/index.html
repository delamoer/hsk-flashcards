夜雨寄北

Yè Yǔ Jì Běi
Night Rain: A Letter North

导读About this poem

“You ask when I’m coming home — I can’t say.” Rain fills the autumn ponds in the mountains of Sichuan, and the poet imagines a future night when he will sit by the candle with the one he loves and tell her about this rain.

中文解析

这是诗人在四川（古代叫“巴”）写给远在北方的亲人的诗。一般认为是写给妻子，也有人认为是写给朋友。第一句像在回信：你问我什么时候回家，我也说不准。一句话里两个“期”，写出了无奈。第二句写眼前：巴山的夜雨下个不停，秋天的池塘水都涨满了，也像诗人心中涨满的愁。后两句很巧妙：什么时候我们才能一起在西窗下剪烛芯，再回头聊起今晚巴山夜雨的情景？诗人从“现在”想到“将来”，又从将来回想“现在”，今晚的孤独，将来会变成两人一起聊天的话题。“巴山夜雨”在短短四句里出现两次，一次是眼前的苦，一次是想象中的甜。“剪烛西窗”今天还用来形容亲友深夜谈心。

In English

Li Shangyin wrote this in Sichuan (the old region of Ba) to someone far away in the north — traditionally his wife, though some scholars think it was a friend. The first line answers a letter: “You ask when I’ll come home; there is no date yet.” The word 期 (“date”) appears twice, and the helplessness is in that repetition. The second line is what he sees: night rain over the Ba mountains, filling the autumn ponds — as sorrow fills him. The last two lines are what make the poem special. “When will we trim the candle together by the west window, and talk about this night of rain in the Ba mountains?” He leaps from the present to an imagined future, then looks back from that future to tonight. Tonight’s loneliness will one day be a story told side by side. So “night rain in the Ba mountains” appears twice in four lines: once as present pain, once as future comfort. 剪烛西窗 (“trimming the candle by the west window”) is still used for friends or family talking late into the night.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Shangyin (c. 813–858) was a late-Tang poet known for beautiful, often mysterious love poems. He wrote this while serving on an official’s staff in Sichuan; because his wife is believed to have died around 851, before his arrival, some scholars argue the poem was written to a friend.
