导读About this poem

Written by one of China’s most powerful emperors at the height of a joyful boat party: the autumn wind rises, geese fly south — and in the middle of the music he suddenly feels old. How long can youth last?

直译LiteralThe autumn wind rises, white clouds fly; grass and trees turn yellow and fall, geese return south.

意译MeaningThe autumn wind is up, white clouds race; leaves yellow and drop, and the geese fly south.

直译LiteralOrchids have blossoms, chrysanthemums have fragrance; I think of the fair one and cannot forget.

意译MeaningOrchids bloom, chrysanthemums scent the air; I think of someone fair and cannot let go.

03

直译LiteralFloating the towered ship, we cross the Fen River; cutting across midstream, we raise white waves.

意译MeaningOur tall ship sails across the Fen, cutting through the current in a spray of white.

04
箫鼓鸣兮发棹歌，欢乐极兮哀情多。

直译LiteralFlutes and drums sound, rowing songs begin; when joy is at its height, sorrow grows.

意译MeaningFlutes and drums play, the oarsmen sing — and at the height of the joy, sadness floods in.

05
少壮几时兮奈老何！

直译LiteralYouth and strength — how long do they last? What can be done about old age!

意译MeaningHow long can youth last? And what can anyone do against age?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

汉武帝是中国历史上很有作为的皇帝。有一年秋天，他到汾河一带祭祀，在楼船上大宴群臣，写下这首诗。前两句写秋景：秋风吹起，白云飘飞，草木变黄落叶，大雁向南飞回。第三、四句由景到人：兰花开了，菊花香了，我想念着美好的人，怎么也忘不了（“佳人”可以理解为贤才，也可以理解为心中思念的人）。接下来写宴会的热闹：楼船在汾河上航行，横渡河心，激起白色的波浪；箫鼓齐鸣，船夫唱起划船的歌。最后笔锋一转：快乐到了极点，悲伤反而多了起来——年轻力壮能有多久？衰老又能怎么办！这叫“乐极生悲”。一位拥有天下的皇帝，在最快乐的时候也感到时间的无情，这让这首诗特别打动人。

In English

Emperor Wu of Han was one of the most ambitious rulers in Chinese history. One autumn he travelled to the Fen River to offer sacrifices, threw a great banquet on a towered ship, and wrote this poem. The first lines are pure autumn: the wind rises, white clouds fly, grass and trees turn yellow and drop, and wild geese head south. Then from scenery to feeling: the orchids are in bloom, the chrysanthemums are fragrant, and he thinks of a “fair one” he cannot forget — perhaps talented men he wishes to find, perhaps someone he loves; the poem doesn’t say. Next comes the party itself: the great ship sails out, cuts across the current and throws up white waves; flutes and drums play, the boatmen sing their rowing songs. And then the turn: when joy reaches its peak, sorrow grows. How long can the strength of youth last? What can anyone do against old age? Chinese has a phrase for this, 乐极生悲, “extreme joy gives way to sorrow”. That a man who ruled all of China felt time slipping away at his happiest moment is what makes this short poem so moving.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Che (156–87 BC), Emperor Wu of Han, reigned for 54 years and greatly expanded the empire. The poem is traditionally dated to about 113 BC, when he visited Hedong to worship the Earth goddess; it is preserved in the anthology Wen Xuan (6th century), though some scholars have questioned whether he wrote it himself.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

秋风起兮白云飞，草木黄落兮雁南归。

qiū fēng qǐ xī bái yún fēi, cǎo mù huáng luò xī yàn nán guī

The autumn wind is up, white clouds race; leaves yellow and drop, and the geese fly south.

兰有秀兮菊有芳，怀佳人兮不能忘。

lán yǒu xiù xī jú yǒu fāng, huái jiā rén xī bù néng wàng

Orchids bloom, chrysanthemums scent the air; I think of someone fair and cannot let go.

泛楼船兮济汾河，横中流兮扬素波。

fàn lóu chuán xī jì fén hé, héng zhōng liú xī yáng sù bō

Our tall ship sails across the Fen, cutting through the current in a spray of white.

箫鼓鸣兮发棹歌，欢乐极兮哀情多。

xiāo gǔ míng xī fā zhào gē, huān lè jí xī āi qíng duō

Flutes and drums play, the oarsmen sing — and at the height of the joy, sadness floods in.

少壮几时兮奈老何！

shào zhuàng jǐ shí xī nài lǎo hé

How long can youth last? And what can anyone do against age?
