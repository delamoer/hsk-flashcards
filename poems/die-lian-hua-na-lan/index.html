导读About this poem

A grieving husband watches the moon: full for one night, a broken ring for all the rest. If only his wife could be like the moon and come back whole, he would melt the ice and snow for her. A tender poem of mourning, ending with the hope of meeting again as a pair of butterflies.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是纳兰性德怀念亡妻的悼亡词。上阕从月亮写起：最可怜天上的月亮那么辛苦，一个月里只有一夜是圆的，其余的夜晚都是缺的——就像人间相聚的日子少，分离的日子多。他说，如果你能像月亮一样，缺了还能再圆、永远皎洁，我宁愿不怕冰雪的寒冷，用自己的身体去温暖你。这里用了一个典故（古代的故事）：三国时荀粲的妻子发高烧，他冬天跑到院子里把身体冻冷，再回来给妻子降温。下阕回到现实：无奈人间的缘分这么容易断，只有燕子还像从前一样，轻轻地踩着帘钩说话，好像什么都没变。最后说：就算在她坟前唱完哀歌，愁也停不下来；但愿来年春天，能在花丛里认出一对双飞的蝴蝶——那就是我们。全词用月亮的圆缺比喻聚散，感情深而温柔。

In English

This is one of Nalan Xingde’s poems mourning his wife. It begins with the moon, which he pities for working so hard: it is a full ring for only one night a month, and every other night it is a broken ring. Human life is the same — the nights together are few, the nights apart are many. If you could be like the moon, he says, and always come back round and bright, I would not mind the ice and snow; I would make myself warm for you. Behind this is an old story: a man named Xun Can, whose wife had a burning fever, went out into the winter cold to chill his own body and came back to lie beside her and cool her down. The second half returns to reality. Sadly, the bonds of this world break so easily. Only the swallows are the same as before, stepping lightly on the curtain hook and chattering as if nothing had happened. Even after he has sung his grief at her autumn grave, the sorrow does not stop. His last hope: next spring, among the flowers, he will recognise a pair of butterflies resting together — the two of them, together again. The image of the waxing and waning moon for meeting and parting runs through the whole poem.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Nalan Xingde (1655–1685) was a Manchu noble and the Qing dynasty’s finest ci poet. His wife, Lady Lu, died in 1677, and this poem is traditionally read as one of the many he wrote in her memory.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

一昔 yī xī（昔同“夕”，夜晚）；玦 jué；为卿热 wèi；无那 wú nuó（同“无奈”）；栖 qī。“那”“玦”机器朗读容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列替换，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

辛苦最怜天上月，

xīn kǔ zuì lián tiān shàng yuè

How I pity the moon, working so hard up there:

一昔如环，昔昔都成玦。

yī xī rú huán, xī xī dōu chéng jué

one night a full ring, and every night after, a ring with a gap.

若似月轮终皎洁，

ruò sì yuè lún zhōng jiǎo jié

If only you could be like the moon and come back whole and bright,

不辞冰雪为卿热。

bù cí bīng xuě wèi qīng rè

I would brave the ice and snow to keep you warm.

无那尘缘容易绝，

wú nuó chén yuán róng yì jué

But the ties of this world snap so easily —

燕子依然，软踏帘钩说。

yàn zǐ yī rán, ruǎn tà lián gōu shuō

only the swallows are unchanged, stepping lightly on the curtain hook, chattering away.

唱罢秋坟愁未歇，

chàng bà qiū fén chóu wèi xiē

I have sung my lament at your autumn grave, and still the grief goes on;

春丛认取双栖蝶。

chūn cóng rèn qǔ shuāng qī dié

next spring, among the flowers, I will look for two butterflies resting side by side.
