导读About this poem

Three lines of victory and splendour — the king returns in triumph, his soldiers in silk, his palace full of beautiful women — and one last line that wipes it all away: now only partridges fly here.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

越王勾践被吴国打败后，“卧薪尝胆”，多年忍辱发愤，终于灭掉吴国。这首诗写的就是那段历史。前三句全写当年的风光：勾践打败吴国凯旋而归，有功的将士回家都穿上了锦绣衣裳；宫女像花一样美，挤满了春天的宫殿。一句比一句热闹。可第四句突然一转：如今这里只有几只鹧鸪在飞。一般的七言绝句常在第三句转折，这首却把转折留到最后一句，前面写得越繁华，最后一句的冷落就越让人吃惊——这是强烈的“对比”。鹧鸪的叫声在古人听来很悲凉，更添了荒凉的感觉。诗人只用一句话，就写出了荣华富贵终究会消失的道理。

In English

After the King of Yue, Goujian, was defeated by the state of Wu, he spent years in hardship — legend says he slept on brushwood and tasted bitter gall to keep his anger alive — and finally destroyed Wu. This poem looks back at that moment of triumph. The first three lines are all glory: Goujian comes home victorious, his loyal soldiers return dressed in embroidered silk, and palace women lovely as flowers fill the spring halls. Each line is busier and brighter than the last. Then the fourth line drops everything: “Now there are only partridges flying.” A quatrain usually turns at its third line; Li Bai saves the turn for the very end, so the emptiness hits harder after so much splendour. That sudden contrast is the whole poem. The partridge (zhègū) also matters: its call sounded mournful to Chinese ears, so it adds a note of desolation to the ruins. In a single line, all that power and beauty is gone.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) is thought to have written this during his youthful travels in the south-east around 726, at Kuaiji (today’s Shaoxing), the old capital of Yue. It is a companion piece to “Visiting the Ruins of the Gusu Terrace”.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

越王勾践破吴归，

yuè wáng gōu jiàn pò wú guī

Goujian, King of Yue, came home from crushing Wu;

义士还家尽锦衣。

yì shì huán jiā jìn jǐn yī

his loyal men returned, every one of them dressed in silk.

宫女如花满春殿，

gōng nǚ rú huā mǎn chūn diàn

Palace women, lovely as flowers, filled the halls with spring —

只今惟有鹧鸪飞。

zhǐ jīn wéi yǒu zhè gū fēi

now only partridges fly here.
