导读About this poem

Perhaps the most famous puzzle in Chinese poetry: a zither, a butterfly dream, a weeping cuckoo, pearls and jade — beautiful images whose meaning readers have argued about for a thousand years. Read it for the feeling: looking back on a life and finding it already lost.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《锦瑟》是李商隐最有名也最难懂的诗。开头说：华美的瑟为什么偏偏有五十根弦？每一根弦都让我想起过去的年华。中间四句连用四个典故：庄子梦见自己变成蝴蝶，醒来分不清是自己梦见蝴蝶，还是蝴蝶梦见了自己；古蜀国的望帝死后化作杜鹃，日夜悲啼；传说海里的鲛人哭泣，眼泪变成珍珠；蓝田出产美玉，阳光下仿佛升起轻烟。这些画面美丽又朦胧，有梦，有悲伤，有看得见却抓不住的东西。最后两句是全诗的中心：这份感情哪里要等到今天回忆才觉得怅惘，在当时就已经迷惘了。这首诗到底写什么，有人说是悼念亡妻，有人说是爱情，有人说是回顾自己的一生，至今没有定论。读的时候不必猜谜，感受那种对逝去时光的怅惘就好。

In English

“The Ornate Zither” is Li Shangyin’s most famous poem, and his most mysterious. It begins with a question: why does this beautiful zither have fifty strings? Every string, every bridge, brings back a year of his youth. The middle four lines are built from four old stories. The philosopher Zhuangzi dreamed he was a butterfly and, on waking, could not tell whether he had dreamed the butterfly or the butterfly was dreaming him. An ancient king of Shu died and became a cuckoo that cried with endless grief. In the southern sea, mermaids wept tears that turned into pearls. And the fine jade of Lantian seemed to give off a haze in the warm sun. Each image is lovely and blurred — a dream, a sorrow, something you can see but never hold. The last couplet is the heart of the poem: this feeling did not have to wait for memory to become heartbreaking; even at the time, I was already lost in it. What is the poem really about? Readers have said it mourns his wife, remembers a secret love, or looks back on his whole life — there is still no agreement. You don’t need to solve the riddle. Let the images work, and feel the ache of looking back at a time that is gone.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Shangyin (c. 813–858) was one of the great poets of the late Tang, famous for dense, beautiful and often mysterious verse. Caught between two rival court factions, he spent most of his life in minor posts. This poem stands first in his collected works, which is one reason it has been read as a look back over his whole life; it is usually dated to his last years, after the death of his wife.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

锦瑟无端五十弦，

jǐn sè wú duān wǔ shí xián

Why should this painted zither have fifty strings?

一弦一柱思华年。

yī xián yī zhù sī huá nián

Each string, each bridge, calls back a year of my youth.

庄生晓梦迷蝴蝶，

zhuāng shēng xiǎo mèng mí hú dié

Zhuangzi, dreaming at dawn, lost himself in a butterfly;

望帝春心托杜鹃。

wàng dì chūn xīn tuō dù juān

King Wang gave his longing heart to a cuckoo’s cry.

沧海月明珠有泪，

cāng hǎi yuè míng zhū yǒu lèi

Moon bright on the wide sea — pearls that were once tears;

蓝田日暖玉生烟。

lán tián rì nuǎn yù shēng yān

sun warm on Lantian — jade breathing out a haze.

此情可待成追忆，

cǐ qíng kě dài chéng zhuī yì

Did this feeling have to wait to become a memory?

只是当时已惘然。

zhǐ shì dāng shí yǐ wǎng rán

Even then, at that very moment, I was already lost.
