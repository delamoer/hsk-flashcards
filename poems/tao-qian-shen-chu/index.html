导读About this poem

A young Ming officer sits among his military books with a sword on the wall and says: I don’t want to be made a lord — I only want peace on the sea. The last line became the motto of China’s most famous fighter against pirates.

中文解析

“韬钤”指兵书（《六韬》《玉钤》都是古代兵法书），“韬钤深处”就是沉浸在兵书里。这首诗是戚继光年轻时写的。第一联说：我在小屋里暂时可以安心休息，但心里一直担忧国家，早就立下了报国的誓言。第二联写日常：叫人拿酒来招待客人，手里挥着麈尾（古人清谈时拿的拂尘），坐着谈论兵法。第三联写书房：云气围着满架的书（“牙签”是古代书上的象牙标签，代指藏书），星光映着横放的宝剑——一文一武，写出他既读书又习武。最后一联是全诗的灵魂：封侯（得到高官厚禄）不是我的心愿，我只希望海上平安无事。当时东南沿海常受倭寇（来自日本的海盗）侵扰，戚继光后来果然带领“戚家军”平定了倭患。“封侯非我意，但愿海波平”至今常被人引用，表达不求名利、只求国家安宁的胸怀。

In English

韬钤 means books on warfare (the 六韬 and 玉钤 were classic military texts), so the title means “deep in the art of war”. Qi Jiguang wrote this as a young officer. The first couplet: in my small study I can rest my head for now, but I worry about the times, and I made my vow to serve my country long ago. The second couplet shows his daily life: he calls for wine to welcome a guest, waves his whisk (麈, a fly-whisk scholars held while talking), and sits discussing strategy. The third couplet describes the room: clouds seem to guard shelves full of books (牙签, ivory tags once hung on scrolls, stand for a library), and starlight falls on a precious sword lying across the rack — books and blade together, the scholar and the soldier in one man. The final couplet is the soul of the poem: becoming a lord is not what I want; I only hope the sea will be calm. At that time Japanese pirates (倭寇) were raiding China’s southeast coast, and Qi Jiguang later led his own trained army, the “Qi Family Army”, to defeat them. The line 封侯非我意，但愿海波平 is still quoted today to describe someone who cares nothing for fame and everything for the safety of the country.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Qi Jiguang (1528–1588) was a Ming general famous for defeating the pirates on China’s southeast coast and later strengthening the Great Wall defences in the north. He is said to have written this poem in his twenties, when he first took charge of coastal defence in Shandong; it is collected in his “Zhizhitang Collection”.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

小筑暂高枕，

xiǎo zhù zàn gāo zhěn

In my little study I may rest a while,

忧时旧有盟。

yōu shí jiù yǒu méng

but I fear for the times, and my vow is old.

呼樽来揖客，

hū zūn lái yī kè

I call for wine and bow to my guests,

挥麈坐谈兵。

huī zhǔ zuò tán bīng

then sit with my whisk and talk of war.

云护牙签满，

yún hù yá qiān mǎn

Clouds keep watch over shelves of books;

星含宝剑横。

xīng hán bǎo jiàn héng

starlight rests on the sword across the rack.

封侯非我意，

fēng hóu fēi wǒ yì

To be made a lord is not what I want —

但愿海波平。

dàn yuàn hǎi bō píng

I only want the sea to be at peace.
