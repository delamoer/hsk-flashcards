导读About this poem

A misty bridge, a fisherman’s boat, peach petals floating downstream — and a question that echoes the famous tale of the Peach Blossom Spring: where is the cave that leads to paradise?

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗的背景是东晋陶渊明的《桃花源记》：一个渔人顺着开满桃花的溪水，找到一个山洞，洞里是与世隔绝、人人安乐的桃花源。诗的第一句写远处，一座高桥在野外的烟雾中若隐若现，好像飞在半空；第二句写诗人站在溪边的石矶西侧，向渔船上的人打听。问什么呢？后两句就是他的问话：桃花整天随着流水漂下来，那个山洞到底在清溪的哪一边？诗人看到落花流水，想起了传说中的桃花源，于是真心实意地问路。这一问，写出了他对美好、宁静世界的向往；而渔人怎么回答，诗里没有说，留下了想象的空间。全诗像一幅山水画，朦胧、安静，又带着一点童心般的天真。

In English

This poem builds on a famous story, “The Peach Blossom Spring” by Tao Yuanming (4th–5th century): a fisherman follows a creek lined with peach trees, finds a cave, and through it reaches a hidden village where people live in peace, cut off from the world. In line one, a high bridge seems to float through the wild mist in the distance, half there, half not. In line two the poet stands on the west side of a rocky ledge by the water and calls out to a fisherman’s boat. What does he ask? The second half is his question: peach petals float down on the stream all day long — so which side of the clear creek is the cave on? Seeing blossoms on the water, he thinks of the legendary paradise and asks the way in all seriousness. The question shows how much he longs for a quiet, perfect world; the fisherman’s answer is never given, which leaves the rest to our imagination. The whole poem is like a misty landscape painting, calm and a little dreamy, with a touch of childlike innocence.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhang Xu (c. 675–c. 750) was most famous as a calligrapher, the “Sage of Cursive Script”, known for writing while drunk. This poem is traditionally attributed to him (it is in the “Three Hundred Tang Poems”), though some scholars think it was written by the Song poet Cai Xiang.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

版本说明 About the text

采用通行本（《唐诗三百首》），与《全唐诗》一致（“溪”《全唐诗》写作异体“谿”）。作者有争议：通行本题张旭，也有学者认为是北宋蔡襄所作，网站从通行说法。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

隐隐飞桥隔野烟，

yǐn yǐn fēi qiáo gé yě yān

A high bridge floats faint beyond the wild mist;

石矶西畔问渔船。

shí jī xī pàn wèn yú chuán

from the west side of the rocks I call to a fishing boat:

桃花尽日随流水，

táo huā jìn rì suí liú shuǐ

“Peach petals ride the stream all day —

洞在清溪何处边？

dòng zài qīng xī hé chù biān

where along this clear creek is the cave?”
