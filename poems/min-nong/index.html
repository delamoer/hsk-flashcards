导读About this poem

A farmer hoes in the noon sun, sweat dripping into the soil. Who thinks of that when eating? Chinese parents still quote this poem when children leave rice in the bowl.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

前两句是一个镜头：中午太阳最毒的时候，农民还在地里锄草，汗水一滴一滴落到禾苗下的土里。后两句是一个问题：有谁知道，盘子里的饭，每一粒都是辛辛苦苦得来的？前面写画面，后面讲道理，画面越真实，道理越有力量。诗里没有大话，只有“汗滴”这样一个小小的细节，却让人一下子感受到劳动的辛苦。这首诗简单到每个孩子都能懂，所以在中国家家户户都会背。直到今天，大人看到孩子剩饭，还常说“谁知盘中餐，粒粒皆辛苦”，提醒他们珍惜粮食、尊重劳动。

In English

The first two lines are a single close-up shot: at noon, when the sun is fiercest, a farmer is still out hoeing the field, and his sweat drips drop by drop into the soil under the crops. The last two lines ask a question: who realises that every single grain in the bowl on your table came from hard work? First a picture, then a point — and the more real the picture, the stronger the point. There are no big words here, just one small detail, the dripping sweat, and it makes you feel the labour instantly. Because it is so simple that any child can understand it, the poem is known in every Chinese home. Even today, when a child leaves rice uneaten, parents will often say 谁知盘中餐，粒粒皆辛苦 — a reminder not to waste food and to respect the people who grow it.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Shen (772–846) later rose to be a chief minister of the Tang court. He is said to have written the two “Pity the Farmers” poems as a young man, moved by the hard lives of farmers; this is the second.

版本说明 About the text

采用通行的教材版本。《悯农》共两首，这是第二首（“锄禾日当午”）；第一首是“春种一粒粟，秋收万颗子……”。题目一作《古风》。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

锄禾日当午，

chú hé rì dāng wǔ

Hoeing the fields in the noonday sun,

汗滴禾下土。

hàn dī hé xià tǔ

sweat dripping into the soil.

谁知盘中餐，

shuí zhī pán zhōng cān

Who stops to think, of the food on the plate,

粒粒皆辛苦。

lì lì jiē xīn kǔ

that every grain was hard labour?
