导读About this poem

Often called China’s first poem written in praise of a single object. The orange tree grows only in the south and cannot be moved — and through it Qu Yuan describes the kind of person he wants to be: rooted, loyal, honest and unbending.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗被称为中国第一首咏物诗——“咏物”就是写一样东西，借它说自己的志向。前半写橘树本身：它是天地间的好树，只适合生长在南方，移到北方就活不了（古人说“橘生淮北则为枳”）；根深蒂固、很难迁移；绿叶白花，枝条重叠，刺很尖，果实圆圆的；青色黄色交错，颜色鲜亮，外皮鲜艳，里面洁白。后半转向赞美人：年轻时就有和别人不同的志向，独立不迁、不随波逐流，谨慎自守、品德无私，可以和天地相比。最后说愿意和橘树做永远的朋友，它虽然年轻，却可以做我的老师，可以和伯夷（古代宁死不改志向的贤人）相比。屈原一生忠于楚国，被流放也不肯离开，所以人们常说这棵橘树就是屈原自己。这种“托物言志”的写法对后世影响很大。

In English

This is often called the first Chinese poem written in praise of a single thing (咏物, “singing of an object”) — a form where a poet describes something in nature to say something about himself. The first half describes the tree. It is a fine tree of heaven and earth that grows only in the south; carry it north and it will not survive (an old saying holds that an orange planted north of the Huai River turns into a bitter trifoliate orange). Its roots go deep and it is hard to move. It has green leaves and white blossoms, layered branches with sharp thorns, and round fruit; green and yellow mingle, bright outside and pure white inside. Halfway through, the poem turns from tree to person: you had a different kind of ambition even when young; you stand alone and will not be moved; you will not drift with the current; you are careful, selfless, a match for heaven and earth. The poet ends by wishing to be the tree’s friend forever — young as it is, it could be his teacher, a model like Bo Yi, the ancient sage who starved rather than abandon his principles. Qu Yuan stayed loyal to Chu even in exile, so readers have always seen the tree as Qu Yuan himself. Speaking of your ideals through an object (托物言志) became a central habit of Chinese poetry.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC) was a nobleman and minister of the state of Chu, exiled after his advice was rejected; he is traditionally said to have drowned himself in the Miluo River. “Ode to the Orange Tree” is one of his Nine Pieces (九章); many scholars think it is an early work, written when he was young.

后皇嘉树，橘徕服兮。

Of all the fine trees of heaven and earth, the orange was made for this land.

受命不迁，生南国兮。

shòu mìng bù qiān, shēng nán guó xī

深固难徙，更壹志兮。

shēn gù nán xǐ, gèng yī zhì xī

绿叶素荣，纷其可喜兮。

曾枝剡棘，圆果抟兮。

青黄杂糅，文章烂兮。

纷缊宜修，姱而不丑兮。

嗟尔幼志，有以异兮。
