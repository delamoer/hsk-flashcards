导读About this poem

An empty mountain, voices with no one in sight, a last ray of evening sun on green moss. Wang Wei, poet and painter, makes silence you can almost hear.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

鹿柴是王维在辋川别墅附近的一个地方。第一句说山里空空的，看不见人；第二句却说听到了人说话的声音。有声音反而更显得安静——就像夜里一声虫叫让你觉得四周更静，这种手法叫“以动衬静”。后两句写光：傍晚的太阳光斜斜地照进深深的树林，又一次落在青苔上。树林深处平时照不到太阳，青苔就是证明；这一点点光一闪而过，让幽暗的林子更显得幽暗。王维也是画家，诗里先写声音，再写光和颜色，像一幅会动的画。宋代苏轼说王维“诗中有画”，说的就是这样的诗。读的时候要慢，想象自己站在那片安静的山林里。

In English

Deer Enclosure was a spot near Wang Wei’s country estate at Wangchuan, outside Chang’an. The first line says the mountain is empty and no one can be seen; the second says you can still hear people talking. The sound makes the quiet deeper — like a single cricket at night making everything else feel more silent. This is called “using movement to set off stillness”. The second half is about light. The evening sun slants into the deep forest and falls again on the green moss. Moss grows where sunlight seldom reaches, so this brief patch of light only makes the dark wood feel darker once it passes. Wang Wei was also a painter: he gives us sound first, then light and colour, like a painting that moves. The Song poet Su Shi said of him, “in his poems there are paintings”, and this is exactly the kind of poem he meant. Read it slowly and imagine standing in that hushed forest.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) was a poet, painter and devout Buddhist. In later life he bought an estate in the Wangchuan valley south of Chang’an and wrote a set of twenty short poems about its sights with his friend Pei Di; this is one of them.

空山不见人，

On the empty mountain, no one in sight,
