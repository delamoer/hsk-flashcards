导读About this poem

After eight years of civil war, the news finally comes: the rebels are beaten. Du Fu weeps, laughs, throws his books together and plans the whole journey home in one breath — often called the happiest poem he ever wrote.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗被称为杜甫“生平第一首快诗”。安史之乱打了七八年，杜甫流落在四川，忽然听说官军收复了叛军的老巢河南、河北。第一、二句写刚听到消息：一下子泪流满面，衣服都湿了——这是喜极而泣（高兴到哭）。第三、四句写家人：回头看妻子和孩子，脸上的愁容早没了；自己胡乱卷起书本，高兴得快要发狂。第五、六句写打算：大白天也要放声唱歌、尽情喝酒，趁着春光正好，和家人一起回故乡。最后两句写回家的路线：从巴峡穿过巫峡，再到襄阳，再奔洛阳，四个地名一口气连下来，好像人已经在船上飞奔。全诗节奏越来越快，把狂喜写得淋漓尽致（非常充分）。

In English

Critics have called this Du Fu's happiest poem. The An Lushan Rebellion had dragged on for nearly eight years, and Du Fu was stranded in Sichuan, far from home, when word came that government troops had retaken the rebels' heartland north of the Yellow River. The first couplet is the moment the news arrives: he bursts into tears and his clothes are soaked — tears of joy, not grief. In the second he turns to his family. His wife and children no longer look worried at all, and he starts rolling up his books any old way, almost mad with happiness. The third couplet is about celebration and plans: sing out loud in broad daylight, drink as much as you like, and travel home with the green spring for company. The last couplet is the route itself — through the Ba Gorge, through the Wu Gorge, down to Xiangyang, on to Luoyang. Four place names in one breath, as if the boat were already racing downstream. The rhythm speeds up line by line, and the reader is swept along with his excitement. (In fact, the journey home would never happen.)

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this in spring 763 in Zizhou (today's Santai, Sichuan), when the last rebel leaders were defeated and the An Lushan Rebellion finally ended. He longed to return to Luoyang, where his family came from, but he died seven years later without getting there.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

衣裳 cháng（统编教材注 cháng，古音；现代汉语“衣裳”读 yī shang）；却看 kàn；妻子 qī zǐ（妻子和儿女，不是现代的“妻子 qī zi”）；还乡 huán；蓟 jì；涕 tì；漫卷 màn juǎn。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

剑外忽传收蓟北，

jiàn wài hū chuán shōu jì běi

Here beyond the Sword Pass, sudden news: the north is ours again!

初闻涕泪满衣裳。

chū wén tì lèi mǎn yī cháng

At the first word of it, tears soak my clothes.

却看妻子愁何在，

què kàn qī zǐ chóu hé zài

I turn to my wife and children: where has all the worry gone?

漫卷诗书喜欲狂。

màn juǎn shī shū xǐ yù kuáng

I sweep my books together, wild with joy.

白日放歌须纵酒，

bái rì fàng gē xū zòng jiǔ

In broad daylight I'll sing at the top of my voice and drink my fill,

青春作伴好还乡。

qīng chūn zuò bàn hǎo huán xiāng

and with the green spring beside us, we'll head for home.

即从巴峡穿巫峡，

jí cóng bā xiá chuān wū xiá

Straight from the Ba Gorge, on through the Wu Gorge,

便下襄阳向洛阳。

biàn xià xiāng yáng xiàng luò yáng

then down to Xiangyang — and on to Luoyang!
