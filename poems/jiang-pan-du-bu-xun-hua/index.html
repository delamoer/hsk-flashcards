导读About this poem

A neighbour's garden path so full of blossom that the branches sag, butterflies that won't leave, orioles singing on cue. A cheerful snapshot of spring in Chengdu — and the only famous Tang poem that names a village woman, Huang Siniang.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

杜甫在成都草堂住下后，有一天一个人沿着江边散步赏花，写了七首绝句，这是第六首。第一句点出地点：黄四娘家门前的小路上开满了花。黄四娘是杜甫的邻居，一位普通的农家妇女，能被写进名诗里，很少见。第二句写花多：千朵万朵，把树枝都压低了，“压”字写出花开得又密又重。后两句写花间的小动物：蝴蝶舍不得离开，在花丛中不停地飞舞；黄莺自由自在，正好在这时候“恰恰”地叫起来。“时时”“恰恰”这两组叠词（同一个字重复）读起来很有节奏，好像能听到鸟叫声。整首诗色彩明亮，有花、有蝶、有鸟，写出了诗人难得的轻松和喜悦。

In English

After settling into his thatched cottage in Chengdu, Du Fu went for a walk alone along the river one spring day to look at the flowers, and wrote a set of seven quatrains about it. This is the sixth. The first line names the place: the path by Huang Siniang's house, covered in flowers. She was a neighbour, an ordinary country woman — it is unusual for someone like her to be named in a famous poem, and she has been remembered ever since. The second line is about sheer abundance: thousands upon thousands of blossoms, so many that they “press the branches down”. The second half turns to the small creatures in the garden. Butterflies linger and keep dancing, unwilling to leave; orioles, quite at ease, sing just at that moment. The paired repeated words 时时 (“again and again”) and 恰恰 (“just then”, also heard as the birds' chirping) give the lines a bouncing rhythm. With its flowers, butterflies and birdsong, the poem is bright and light — a rare happy moment in Du Fu's life.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote the series of seven poems around 761, in the spring after he built his thatched cottage on the outskirts of Chengdu with the help of friends. Huang Siniang (“Fourth Daughter Huang”) is known only from this poem.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

黄四娘家花满蹊，

huáng sì niáng jiā huā mǎn xī

The path by Huang Siniang's door is lost in flowers,

千朵万朵压枝低。

qiān duǒ wàn duǒ yā zhī dī

thousands upon thousands, weighing the branches down.

留连戏蝶时时舞，

liú lián xì dié shí shí wǔ

Butterflies linger, dancing on and on,

自在娇莺恰恰啼。

zì zài jiāo yīng qià qià tí

and carefree orioles sing out, right on cue.
