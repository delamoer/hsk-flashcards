导读About this poem

On an autumn night by the river, an exiled official hears a stranger play the pipa — and her music and her life story turn out to mirror his own. One of the most famous long poems in Chinese, and perhaps the best description of music ever written in the language.

第一段Part 1
01
浔阳江头夜送客，枫叶荻花秋瑟瑟。

直译LiteralAt the head of the Xunyang River, at night, seeing off a guest; maple leaves, reed flowers, autumn rustling.

意译MeaningBy the Xunyang River one night I saw a friend off; maple leaves and reed plumes shivered in the autumn wind.

02
主人下马客在船，举酒欲饮无管弦。

直译LiteralThe host gets off his horse, the guest is in the boat; raising wine, about to drink — no pipes or strings.

意译MeaningI left my horse, my friend went aboard; we raised our cups, but there was no music.

03
醉不成欢惨将别，别时茫茫江浸月。

直译LiteralDrunk but not happy, sadly about to part; at parting, vast and blurred, the river soaks the moon.

意译MeaningThe wine brought no joy, only the sadness of parting; the wide dim river held the drowned moon.

04
忽闻水上琵琶声，主人忘归客不发。

直译LiteralSuddenly hearing a pipa’s sound on the water; the host forgets to go home, the guest does not set off.

意译MeaningThen, across the water, the sound of a pipa — I forgot to go home, and my friend forgot to sail.

05
寻声暗问弹者谁，琵琶声停欲语迟。

直译LiteralFollowing the sound, quietly asking who the player is; the pipa stops, about to speak, but slow.

意译MeaningWe followed the sound and asked softly who was playing; the music stopped, and the answer was slow to come.

06
移船相近邀相见，添酒回灯重开宴。

直译LiteralMoving the boat close, inviting her to meet; adding wine, bringing back the lamps, starting the feast again.

意译MeaningWe drew our boat alongside and asked to meet her, poured more wine, relit the lamps, and began the feast again.

07
千呼万唤始出来，犹抱琵琶半遮面。

直译LiteralA thousand calls, ten thousand summons, only then she comes out; still holding the pipa, half hiding her face.

意译MeaningOnly after we called and called did she come out, still holding the pipa up to hide half her face.

第二段Part 2
08
转轴拨弦三两声，未成曲调先有情。

直译LiteralTurning the pegs, plucking the strings, two or three sounds; before it becomes a tune, there is already feeling.

意译MeaningShe turned the pegs and tried the strings — two or three notes, not yet a tune, but already full of feeling.

09
弦弦掩抑声声思，似诉平生不得志。

直译LiteralEvery string muffled and held down, every sound full of longing; as if telling of a whole life of not achieving one’s hopes.

意译MeaningEach string held back, each note heavy with sorrow, as if telling of a life that never went her way.

10
低眉信手续续弹，说尽心中无限事。

直译LiteralLowering her brows, letting her hands go, playing on and on; telling all the endless things in her heart.

意译MeaningEyes lowered, hands moving freely, she played on, saying everything her heart had held.

11
轻拢慢捻抹复挑，初为《霓裳》后《六幺》。

直译LiteralLightly pressing, slowly twisting, stroking down and then flicking up; first playing “Rainbow Skirt”, then “Six Measures”.

意译MeaningPressing lightly, rolling slowly, sweeping down and flicking up, she played “Rainbow Skirt”, then “Six Measures”.

12
大弦嘈嘈如急雨，小弦切切如私语。

直译LiteralThe big strings, deep and loud, like sudden rain; the small strings, soft and fine, like whispering.

意译MeaningThe thick strings roared like a sudden downpour; the thin strings murmured like lovers whispering.

13
嘈嘈切切错杂弹，大珠小珠落玉盘。

直译LiteralLoud and soft, mixed together, played; big pearls, small pearls fall on a jade plate.

意译MeaningRoar and murmur tumbled together, like pearls large and small dropping onto a jade plate.

14
间关莺语花底滑，幽咽泉流冰下难。

直译LiteralLiquid and lilting, an oriole’s song gliding under the flowers; choked and sobbing, a spring flowing with difficulty under ice.

意译MeaningNow it slipped along like an oriole singing under blossoms, now it sobbed like a spring struggling beneath the ice.

15
冰泉冷涩弦凝绝，凝绝不通声暂歇。

直译LiteralThe icy spring cold and rough, the strings freeze and break off; frozen, not flowing, the sound stops for a while.

意译MeaningThe icy spring grew cold and stiff, the strings seemed to freeze; frozen, they could not flow, and the sound fell still.

16
别有幽愁暗恨生，此时无声胜有声。

直译LiteralAnother kind of hidden sorrow and secret regret arises; at this moment, no sound is better than sound.

意译MeaningA deeper, hidden grief rose up — and in that moment, silence said more than any note.

17
银瓶乍破水浆迸，铁骑突出刀枪鸣。

直译LiteralA silver jar suddenly breaks, water bursts out; armoured horsemen charge out, swords and spears ring.

意译MeaningThen, like a silver jar bursting and water splashing out, like armoured riders charging with clashing blades —

18
曲终收拨当心画，四弦一声如裂帛。

直译LiteralThe tune ends; drawing back the plectrum, she strokes across the middle; four strings, one sound, like tearing silk.

意译Meaningthe tune ended. She swept the plectrum across the centre: all four strings cried out at once, like silk torn apart.

19
东船西舫悄无言，唯见江心秋月白。

直译LiteralEast boats, west boats, quiet, no words; only seeing in the river’s heart the autumn moon, white.

意译MeaningOn every boat around, no one said a word; there was only the white autumn moon in the middle of the river.

第三段Part 3
20
沉吟放拨插弦中，整顿衣裳起敛容。

直译LiteralHesitating, she puts the plectrum down, slipping it among the strings; straightening her clothes, she rises, composing her face.

意译MeaningShe paused, tucked the plectrum between the strings, smoothed her dress, stood, and composed herself.

21
自言本是京城女，家在虾蟆陵下住。

直译LiteralShe says of herself she was originally a girl of the capital; her home was below Toad Mound.

意译Meaning“I was a girl of the capital,” she said, “and lived down by Toad Hill.

22
十三学得琵琶成，名属教坊第一部。

直译LiteralAt thirteen she had learned the pipa fully; her name belonged to the first section of the Music Academy.

意译MeaningBy thirteen I had mastered the pipa, and was listed in the first rank of the court music school.

23
曲罢曾教善才服，妆成每被秋娘妒。

直译LiteralWhen a tune was done, she once made the masters admire her; when her make-up was done, she was always envied by the famous beauties.

意译MeaningWhen I finished playing, even the old masters bowed to me; when I was dressed, the leading beauties were jealous.

24
五陵年少争缠头，一曲红绡不知数。

直译LiteralYoung men of the Five Mounds competed to give head-wrapping gifts; for one tune, red silk beyond counting.

意译MeaningRich young men fought to shower me with gifts — for a single song, more red silk than anyone could count.

25
钿头银篦击节碎，血色罗裙翻酒污。

直译LiteralInlaid silver combs, beating time, broke; blood-red silk skirts were stained by spilled wine.

意译MeaningI broke jewelled combs beating time to the music, and stained blood-red skirts with spilled wine.

26
今年欢笑复明年，秋月春风等闲度。

直译LiteralThis year laughing, and again next year; autumn moons and spring breezes passed carelessly.

意译MeaningOne year of laughter followed another; autumn moons and spring winds slipped by without a thought.

27
弟走从军阿姨死，暮去朝来颜色故。

直译LiteralMy brother left to join the army, my auntie died; evenings go, mornings come, my looks grow old.

意译MeaningThen my brother went off to the army, the woman who raised me died, and day by day my beauty faded.

28
门前冷落鞍马稀，老大嫁作商人妇。

直译LiteralBefore the gate, deserted, saddled horses few; old, I married and became a merchant’s wife.

意译MeaningMy gate grew quiet, few horses stopped there, and no longer young, I married a merchant.

29
商人重利轻别离，前月浮梁买茶去。

直译LiteralA merchant values profit and takes parting lightly; last month he went to Fuliang to buy tea.

意译MeaningA merchant cares for profit more than for parting — last month he left for Fuliang to buy tea.

30
去来江口守空船，绕船月明江水寒。

直译LiteralSince he left, at the river mouth I guard an empty boat; around the boat, bright moon, cold river water.

意译MeaningSince then I keep an empty boat at the river mouth, with only bright moonlight and cold water all around.

31
夜深忽梦少年事，梦啼妆泪红阑干。

直译LiteralDeep in the night I suddenly dream of my youth; crying in the dream, tears with make-up run red in streaks.

意译MeaningLate at night I dream of when I was young, and weep in my sleep till my rouge runs down in red streaks.”

第四段Part 4
32
我闻琵琶已叹息，又闻此语重唧唧。

直译LiteralI heard the pipa and already sighed; again hearing these words, I sigh once more.

意译MeaningHer music had already made me sigh; hearing her story, I sighed again.

33
同是天涯沦落人，相逢何必曾相识！

直译LiteralBoth are people fallen and adrift at the edge of the sky; meeting, why must we have known each other before!

意译MeaningWe are both cast adrift at the far end of the world — to meet like this, who needs to have met before?

34
我从去年辞帝京，谪居卧病浔阳城。

直译LiteralSince last year I left the imperial capital; living in demotion, lying ill in Xunyang city.

意译MeaningLast year I was sent away from the capital, and now I live in exile, often ill, in Xunyang.

35
浔阳地僻无音乐，终岁不闻丝竹声。

直译LiteralXunyang is remote, no music; all year I don’t hear the sound of strings and flutes.

意译MeaningXunyang is far from everything: there is no music here, and all year long I hear no strings or flutes.

36
住近湓江地低湿，黄芦苦竹绕宅生。

直译LiteralI live near the Pen River, the land low and damp; yellow reeds and bitter bamboo grow around the house.

意译MeaningMy house stands near the Pen River on low, damp ground, ringed by yellow reeds and bitter bamboo.

37
其间旦暮闻何物？杜鹃啼血猿哀鸣。

直译LiteralThere, morning and evening, what do I hear? The cuckoo crying blood, the monkeys’ sad cries.

意译MeaningAnd what do I hear there, morning and night? Cuckoos crying till they bleed, and the mournful call of gibbons.

38
春江花朝秋月夜，往往取酒还独倾。

直译LiteralSpring river, flower mornings, autumn moon nights; often I take wine and again drink it alone.

意译MeaningOn spring mornings full of flowers, on autumn nights under the moon, I often take out wine and drink it alone.

39
岂无山歌与村笛？呕哑嘲哳难为听。

直译LiteralIs it that there are no mountain songs or village flutes? Harsh and jarring, hard to listen to.

意译MeaningOf course there are hill songs and village pipes — but they screech and rasp, and are hard to bear.

40
今夜闻君琵琶语，如听仙乐耳暂明。

直译LiteralTonight hearing your pipa speak, as if hearing immortal music, my ears for a moment become clear.

意译MeaningTonight, hearing your pipa speak, it was like music from heaven — for a moment my ears came alive.

41
莫辞更坐弹一曲，为君翻作琵琶行。

直译LiteralDon’t refuse, sit again and play one more tune; for you I will turn it into a “Song of the Pipa”.

意译MeaningPlease don’t refuse — sit down and play once more, and I will write it into a song of the pipa for you.

42
感我此言良久立，却坐促弦弦转急。

直译LiteralMoved by my words, she stands a long while; then sits back, tightens the strings, and the strings turn urgent.

意译MeaningMoved by what I said, she stood for a long time; then she sat down, tightened the strings, and played faster.

43
凄凄不似向前声，满座重闻皆掩泣。

直译LiteralSad and chill, not like the earlier sound; the whole company, hearing again, all cover their faces and weep.

意译MeaningIt was sadder than anything she had played before, and everyone listening covered their faces and wept.

44
座中泣下谁最多？江州司马青衫湿。

直译LiteralAmong those seated, whose tears fell most? The Jiangzhou deputy’s blue robe is wet.

意译MeaningAnd who wept most of all? The exiled officer of Jiangzhou — his blue robe soaked with tears.

中文解析

这首长诗讲了一个故事。诗人被贬到江州，一个秋夜在江边送客，忽然听到船上有人弹琵琶。弹琵琶的是一位从长安来的女子：年轻时名满京城，老了以后嫁给商人，一个人守着空船。全诗分四段：相遇、弹奏、女子自述身世、诗人自叹。最有名的是第二段写音乐：用急雨、私语、珠子落进玉盘、黄莺叫、泉水在冰下流、银瓶破裂、铁骑冲出来等一连串比喻，把听不见的声音变成看得见、摸得着的画面。“此时无声胜有声”一句，写出音乐停顿时比有声更动人。女子和诗人一个失去青春，一个失去官位，所以诗人说“同是天涯沦落人，相逢何必曾相识”——这两句今天还常被引用。结尾诗人听得泪湿青衫，把同情和自伤合在一起。

In English

The poem tells a story. Bai Juyi has been demoted to a small post in Jiangzhou. One autumn night, seeing a friend off at the river, he hears someone playing the pipa (a plucked lute) on a nearby boat. The player is a woman from the capital, Chang’an: once a celebrated musician, now past her youth and married to a tea merchant who leaves her alone on the boat for months. The poem moves in four parts: the meeting, the music, her life story, and the poet’s own story. The second part is the famous one. Bai turns sound into pictures with a chain of comparisons — sudden rain, whispering, pearls big and small falling on a jade plate, an oriole’s song, a spring choking under ice, a silver jar bursting, armoured horsemen charging out. When the music pauses, he writes 此时无声胜有声: “at this moment, silence says more than sound.” The woman has lost her youth; the poet has lost his career. That is why he says 同是天涯沦落人，相逢何必曾相识 — “we are both cast out to the ends of the earth; why must we have known each other before to understand each other?” Chinese people still quote these lines about strangers who share a hard fate. In the end, the person crying hardest in the room is the poet himself, his blue official’s robe wet with tears.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) was one of the most popular Tang poets, known for writing in plain language that ordinary people could follow. In 815 he was demoted to assistant prefect (司马) of Jiangzhou, today’s Jiujiang in Jiangxi, after speaking out too boldly at court; he wrote this poem there in the autumn of 816.

版本说明 About the text

采用统编高中语文教材（选择性必修中册）的正文，诗前的序不收。原诗又题《琵琶引》，全诗 88 句、616 字，这里一句一联共 44 行，分段照教材的四段。重要异文：“枫叶荻花秋瑟瑟”一作“秋索索”；“似诉平生不得志”一作“不得意”；“初为霓裳后六幺”一作“后绿腰”（同一支曲子的两种写法）；“小弦切切”一作“窃窃”；“幽咽泉流冰下难”一作“水下滩”，清代段玉裁考证当作“冰下难”，教材从之；“冰泉冷涩”一作“水泉”；“钿头银篦”一作“云篦”；“相逢何必曾相识”一作“相悲”；“曲罢曾教善才服”一作“常教”“伏”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

浔阳江头夜送客，枫叶荻花秋瑟瑟。

xún yáng jiāng tóu yè sòng kè, fēng yè dí huā qiū sè sè

By the Xunyang River one night I saw a friend off; maple leaves and reed plumes shivered in the autumn wind.

主人下马客在船，举酒欲饮无管弦。

zhǔ rén xià mǎ kè zài chuán, jǔ jiǔ yù yǐn wú guǎn xián

I left my horse, my friend went aboard; we raised our cups, but there was no music.

醉不成欢惨将别，别时茫茫江浸月。

zuì bù chéng huān cǎn jiāng bié, bié shí máng máng jiāng jìn yuè

The wine brought no joy, only the sadness of parting; the wide dim river held the drowned moon.

忽闻水上琵琶声，主人忘归客不发。

hū wén shuǐ shàng pí pá shēng, zhǔ rén wàng guī kè bù fā

Then, across the water, the sound of a pipa — I forgot to go home, and my friend forgot to sail.

寻声暗问弹者谁，琵琶声停欲语迟。

xún shēng àn wèn tán zhě shuí, pí pá shēng tíng yù yǔ chí

We followed the sound and asked softly who was playing; the music stopped, and the answer was slow to come.

移船相近邀相见，添酒回灯重开宴。

yí chuán xiāng jìn yāo xiāng jiàn, tiān jiǔ huí dēng chóng kāi yàn

We drew our boat alongside and asked to meet her, poured more wine, relit the lamps, and began the feast again.

千呼万唤始出来，犹抱琵琶半遮面。

qiān hū wàn huàn shǐ chū lái, yóu bào pí pá bàn zhē miàn

Only after we called and called did she come out, still holding the pipa up to hide half her face.

转轴拨弦三两声，未成曲调先有情。

zhuàn zhóu bō xián sān liǎng shēng, wèi chéng qǔ diào xiān yǒu qíng

She turned the pegs and tried the strings — two or three notes, not yet a tune, but already full of feeling.

弦弦掩抑声声思，似诉平生不得志。

xián xián yǎn yì shēng shēng sī, sì sù píng shēng bù dé zhì

Each string held back, each note heavy with sorrow, as if telling of a life that never went her way.

低眉信手续续弹，说尽心中无限事。

dī méi xìn shǒu xù xù tán, shuō jìn xīn zhōng wú xiàn shì

Eyes lowered, hands moving freely, she played on, saying everything her heart had held.

轻拢慢捻抹复挑，初为《霓裳》后《六幺》。

qīng lǒng màn niǎn mò fù tiāo, chū wéi ní cháng hòu liù yāo

Pressing lightly, rolling slowly, sweeping down and flicking up, she played “Rainbow Skirt”, then “Six Measures”.

大弦嘈嘈如急雨，小弦切切如私语。

dà xián cáo cáo rú jí yǔ, xiǎo xián qiè qiè rú sī yǔ

The thick strings roared like a sudden downpour; the thin strings murmured like lovers whispering.

嘈嘈切切错杂弹，大珠小珠落玉盘。

cáo cáo qiè qiè cuò zá tán, dà zhū xiǎo zhū luò yù pán

Roar and murmur tumbled together, like pearls large and small dropping onto a jade plate.

间关莺语花底滑，幽咽泉流冰下难。

jiān guān yīng yǔ huā dǐ huá, yōu yè quán liú bīng xià nán

Now it slipped along like an oriole singing under blossoms, now it sobbed like a spring struggling beneath the ice.

冰泉冷涩弦凝绝，凝绝不通声暂歇。

bīng quán lěng sè xián níng jué, níng jué bù tōng shēng zàn xiē

The icy spring grew cold and stiff, the strings seemed to freeze; frozen, they could not flow, and the sound fell still.

别有幽愁暗恨生，此时无声胜有声。

bié yǒu yōu chóu àn hèn shēng, cǐ shí wú shēng shèng yǒu shēng

A deeper, hidden grief rose up — and in that moment, silence said more than any note.

银瓶乍破水浆迸，铁骑突出刀枪鸣。

yín píng zhà pò shuǐ jiāng bèng, tiě jì tū chū dāo qiāng míng

Then, like a silver jar bursting and water splashing out, like armoured riders charging with clashing blades —

曲终收拨当心画，四弦一声如裂帛。

qǔ zhōng shōu bō dāng xīn huà, sì xián yī shēng rú liè bó

the tune ended. She swept the plectrum across the centre: all four strings cried out at once, like silk torn apart.

东船西舫悄无言，唯见江心秋月白。

dōng chuán xī fǎng qiǎo wú yán, wéi jiàn jiāng xīn qiū yuè bái

On every boat around, no one said a word; there was only the white autumn moon in the middle of the river.

沉吟放拨插弦中，整顿衣裳起敛容。

chén yín fàng bō chā xián zhōng, zhěng dùn yī cháng qǐ liǎn róng

She paused, tucked the plectrum between the strings, smoothed her dress, stood, and composed herself.

自言本是京城女，家在虾蟆陵下住。

zì yán běn shì jīng chéng nǚ, jiā zài há má líng xià zhù

“I was a girl of the capital,” she said, “and lived down by Toad Hill.

十三学得琵琶成，名属教坊第一部。

shí sān xué dé pí pá chéng, míng shǔ jiào fāng dì yī bù

By thirteen I had mastered the pipa, and was listed in the first rank of the court music school.

曲罢曾教善才服，妆成每被秋娘妒。

qǔ bà céng jiāo shàn cái fú, zhuāng chéng měi bèi qiū niáng dù

When I finished playing, even the old masters bowed to me; when I was dressed, the leading beauties were jealous.

五陵年少争缠头，一曲红绡不知数。

wǔ líng nián shào zhēng chán tóu, yī qǔ hóng xiāo bù zhī shù

Rich young men fought to shower me with gifts — for a single song, more red silk than anyone could count.

钿头银篦击节碎，血色罗裙翻酒污。

diàn tóu yín bì jī jié suì, xuè sè luó qún fān jiǔ wū

I broke jewelled combs beating time to the music, and stained blood-red skirts with spilled wine.

今年欢笑复明年，秋月春风等闲度。

jīn nián huān xiào fù míng nián, qiū yuè chūn fēng děng xián dù

One year of laughter followed another; autumn moons and spring winds slipped by without a thought.

弟走从军阿姨死，暮去朝来颜色故。

dì zǒu cóng jūn ā yí sǐ, mù qù zhāo lái yán sè gù

Then my brother went off to the army, the woman who raised me died, and day by day my beauty faded.

门前冷落鞍马稀，老大嫁作商人妇。

mén qián lěng luò ān mǎ xī, lǎo dà jià zuò shāng rén fù

My gate grew quiet, few horses stopped there, and no longer young, I married a merchant.

商人重利轻别离，前月浮梁买茶去。

shāng rén zhòng lì qīng bié lí, qián yuè fú liáng mǎi chá qù

A merchant cares for profit more than for parting — last month he left for Fuliang to buy tea.

去来江口守空船，绕船月明江水寒。

qù lái jiāng kǒu shǒu kōng chuán, rào chuán yuè míng jiāng shuǐ hán

Since then I keep an empty boat at the river mouth, with only bright moonlight and cold water all around.

夜深忽梦少年事，梦啼妆泪红阑干。

yè shēn hū mèng shào nián shì, mèng tí zhuāng lèi hóng lán gān

Late at night I dream of when I was young, and weep in my sleep till my rouge runs down in red streaks.”

我闻琵琶已叹息，又闻此语重唧唧。

wǒ wén pí pá yǐ tàn xī, yòu wén cǐ yǔ chóng jī jī

Her music had already made me sigh; hearing her story, I sighed again.

同是天涯沦落人，相逢何必曾相识！

tóng shì tiān yá lún luò rén, xiāng féng hé bì céng xiāng shí

We are both cast adrift at the far end of the world — to meet like this, who needs to have met before?

我从去年辞帝京，谪居卧病浔阳城。

wǒ cóng qù nián cí dì jīng, zhé jū wò bìng xún yáng chéng

Last year I was sent away from the capital, and now I live in exile, often ill, in Xunyang.

浔阳地僻无音乐，终岁不闻丝竹声。

xún yáng dì pì wú yīn yuè, zhōng suì bù wén sī zhú shēng

Xunyang is far from everything: there is no music here, and all year long I hear no strings or flutes.

住近湓江地低湿，黄芦苦竹绕宅生。

zhù jìn pén jiāng dì dī shī, huáng lú kǔ zhú rào zhái shēng

My house stands near the Pen River on low, damp ground, ringed by yellow reeds and bitter bamboo.

其间旦暮闻何物？杜鹃啼血猿哀鸣。

qí jiān dàn mù wén hé wù, dù juān tí xuè yuán āi míng

And what do I hear there, morning and night? Cuckoos crying till they bleed, and the mournful call of gibbons.

春江花朝秋月夜，往往取酒还独倾。

chūn jiāng huā zhāo qiū yuè yè, wǎng wǎng qǔ jiǔ huán dú qīng

On spring mornings full of flowers, on autumn nights under the moon, I often take out wine and drink it alone.

岂无山歌与村笛？呕哑嘲哳难为听。

qǐ wú shān gē yǔ cūn dí, ōu yā zhāo zhā nán wéi tīng

Of course there are hill songs and village pipes — but they screech and rasp, and are hard to bear.

今夜闻君琵琶语，如听仙乐耳暂明。

jīn yè wén jūn pí pá yǔ, rú tīng xiān yuè ěr zàn míng

Tonight, hearing your pipa speak, it was like music from heaven — for a moment my ears came alive.

莫辞更坐弹一曲，为君翻作琵琶行。

mò cí gèng zuò tán yī qǔ, wèi jūn fān zuò pí pá xíng

Please don’t refuse — sit down and play once more, and I will write it into a song of the pipa for you.

感我此言良久立，却坐促弦弦转急。

gǎn wǒ cǐ yán liáng jiǔ lì, què zuò cù xián xián zhuǎn jí

Moved by what I said, she stood for a long time; then she sat down, tightened the strings, and played faster.

凄凄不似向前声，满座重闻皆掩泣。

qī qī bù sì xiàng qián shēng, mǎn zuò chóng wén jiē yǎn qì

It was sadder than anything she had played before, and everyone listening covered their faces and wept.

座中泣下谁最多？江州司马青衫湿。

zuò zhōng qì xià shuí zuì duō, jiāng zhōu sī mǎ qīng shān shī

And who wept most of all? The exiled officer of Jiangzhou — his blue robe soaked with tears.
