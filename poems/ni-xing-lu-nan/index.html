拟行路难·其四

Nǐ Xíng Lù Nán · Qí Sì
The Hard Road, in the Old Style (No. 4)
鲍照Bào Zhào · Bao Zhao魏晋南北朝 Wei, Jin & Southern and Northern Dynasties

导读About this poem

Pour water on flat ground and it runs off in every direction — so why should people all have the same luck? A talented man from a poor family tries to shrug off his fate with wine, and can’t. Bitter, bold, and surprisingly modern.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

鲍照出身寒门（没有地位的普通家庭），在那个看出身做官的时代，再有才华也很难出头。这首诗就是他的不平之气。开头用了一个比喻：把水倒在平地上，水会向东西南北各处流去——人生来的命运也像这样，各不相同。这是先说一个生活中的现象，再引出道理，有点像《诗经》的“起兴”。接着他劝自己：既然命运天定，何必走着叹气、坐着发愁？于是倒酒宽慰自己，可是举起杯来，连《行路难》这首歌也唱不下去了。最后两句情绪爆发：“心非木石岂无感？”人心又不是木头石头，怎么会没有感觉！可是只能“吞声踯躅”——把话咽回去，徘徊着不敢说出口。全诗表面上说“认命”，其实一句比一句不甘心，五言七言交错，节奏像压不住的情绪。它对后来李白的《行路难》影响很大。

In English

Bao Zhao came from a humble family at a time when office went to the well-born; no amount of talent could make up for the wrong surname. This poem is his frustration in eight short lines. It opens with an image from everyday life: pour water on level ground and it runs east, west, south, north — just as people’s lots in life go their separate ways. Starting from something you can see and then drawing the lesson is a very old Chinese habit. Next he talks himself down: if fate is fixed, why sigh as I walk and brood as I sit? So he pours a drink to cheer himself — but with the cup raised, he can’t even finish singing the song “The Hard Road”. Then the dam breaks: “My heart is not wood or stone — how could it feel nothing?” And yet all he can do is swallow his words and pace, not daring to speak. On the surface the poem says “accept your fate”; underneath, every line refuses to. The lines jump between five and seven characters, like feelings that won’t stay in order. Li Bai’s famous “The Hard Road” owes a great deal to Bao Zhao.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bao Zhao (c. 414–466) served as a minor official under the Liu Song dynasty and was killed in an uprising in which his patron was caught up. He wrote eighteen poems “in the style of” the old folk song “The Hard Road” (行路难); this is the fourth.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

泻 读 xiè。酌 读 zhuó。踯躅 读 zhí zhú（徘徊不前）。“安能行叹复坐愁”的“行”读 xíng（走着），“复”读 fù。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材本（据《乐府诗集》）。首句“泻水”一作“写水”（“写”同“泻”）。组诗共十八首，这是第四首，全诗八句。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

泻水置平地，各自东西南北流。

xiè shuǐ zhì píng dì, gè zì dōng xī nán běi liú

Spill water on level ground and it runs off every way — east, west, south and north.

人生亦有命，安能行叹复坐愁？

rén shēng yì yǒu mìng, ān néng xíng tàn fù zuò chóu

Our lives, too, are ruled by fate — so why sigh as I walk, and brood as I sit?

酌酒以自宽，举杯断绝歌路难。

zhuó jiǔ yǐ zì kuān, jǔ bēi duàn jué gē lù nán

I pour some wine to comfort myself, but lifting the cup, I can’t go on singing “The Hard Road”.

心非木石岂无感？吞声踯躅不敢言。

xīn fēi mù shí qǐ wú gǎn, tūn shēng zhí zhú bù gǎn yán

My heart is not made of wood or stone — how could it feel nothing? I swallow my words and pace, and dare not say a thing.
