涉江采芙蓉

Shè Jiāng Cǎi Fúróng
Crossing the River to Pick Lotus
佚名（《古诗十九首》）Anonymous (Nineteen Old Poems)汉 Han

导读About this poem

Just eight lines: someone picks lotus flowers for the person they love — and then realises there is no way to give them. A tiny, perfect poem about distance.

01
涉江采芙蓉，兰泽多芳草。

直译LiteralWading the river, I pick lotus; the orchid marsh has many fragrant herbs.

意译MeaningI wade the river to pick lotus; the orchid banks are thick with scented grass.

02
采之欲遗谁？所思在远道。

直译LiteralPicking them, to whom do I want to give them? The one I think of is on a distant road.

意译MeaningBut who are these flowers for? The one I love is far down the road.

03
还顾望旧乡，长路漫浩浩。

直译LiteralTurning back, I gaze toward the old home; the long road stretches vast and endless.

意译MeaningI turn and look back toward home: the road runs on and on without end.

04
同心而离居，忧伤以终老。

直译LiteralOf one heart, yet living apart; in sorrow until we end old.

意译MeaningOne heart, two homes — and sorrow until we grow old.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗只有八句，写一个人思念远方的爱人。前两句很美：渡过江水去采荷花，长着兰草的水边到处是香草。可是第三句一转：“采了花想送给谁呢？我想的人在很远的地方。”美丽的花一下子变成了无处可送的花，心里的空落就出来了。接着写回头望家乡，路漫长无边。最后两句是全诗的中心：两个人心连在一起，却分开住，只能忧伤到老。古人有采花送给心上人的习惯，花越美，送不出去就越难过，这叫“以乐景写哀”——用美好的景物写悲伤。这首诗写的是谁在思念谁，有两种理解：一说是在外的游子想念妻子，一说是家中的妻子想念丈夫。不管哪一种，“同心而离居”这五个字都说出了所有异地相爱的人的心情。

In English

In just eight lines, someone misses a loved one far away. It opens with a lovely scene: wading across the river to pick lotus flowers, on banks thick with orchids and fragrant herbs. Then the third line turns everything around: “I picked them — but who are they for? The one I think of is far down the road.” In ancient China you gave flowers to the person you loved, so a beautiful bunch with no one to receive it suddenly feels empty. The speaker looks back toward the old home, and the road just stretches on and on. The last two lines are the heart of the poem: two people of one heart, living apart, growing old in sorrow. Using a happy scene to show sadness is a classic technique — the prettier the flowers, the sharper the loss. Readers still debate who is speaking: a traveller missing his wife, or a wife at home missing her husband. Either way, “one heart, two homes” sums up every long-distance love.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is the sixth of the Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首), written by unknown scholars of the late Eastern Han (around the 2nd century AD). Many of these poets were men who had left home to seek office in the capital.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

涉江采芙蓉，兰泽多芳草。

shè jiāng cǎi fú róng, lán zé duō fāng cǎo

I wade the river to pick lotus; the orchid banks are thick with scented grass.

采之欲遗谁？所思在远道。

cǎi zhī yù wèi shuí, suǒ sī zài yuǎn dào

But who are these flowers for? The one I love is far down the road.

还顾望旧乡，长路漫浩浩。

huán gù wàng jiù xiāng, cháng lù màn hào hào

I turn and look back toward home: the road runs on and on without end.

同心而离居，忧伤以终老。

tóng xīn ér lí jū, yōu shāng yǐ zhōng lǎo

One heart, two homes — and sorrow until we grow old.
