导读About this poem

Afraid the crab-apple blossoms will “fall asleep” in the dark, Su Shi lights tall candles to keep them company. A tender, playful poem about loving something beautiful while it lasts.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人深夜赏海棠。第一句写春风轻柔地吹，海棠花在月光下泛着美好的光彩；第二句写花香和夜雾混在一起，朦朦胧胧，月亮慢慢转过了回廊，说明夜已经深了。后两句是全诗的精彩之处：诗人担心夜太深，花儿会像人一样“睡去”，所以特意点起高高的蜡烛，照着这些美丽的花。这里用了拟人的手法——把花当作人来写，“红妆”本来指女子的盛装打扮，这里比喻红艳的海棠。这两句还暗用了一个典故：唐玄宗曾说醉后的杨贵妃像“海棠睡未足”。诗人爱花爱到不忍心让它在黑暗中独自开放，写出他对美好事物的珍惜。当时他被贬在黄州，这种痴情里也有一点孤独。

In English

Su Shi is up late, admiring crab-apple flowers. In the first line a soft east wind blows and the blossoms glow with a lovely sheen. In the second, their scent mixes with the night mist into a soft haze, and the moon has moved round past the corridor — it is getting late. The last two lines are what everyone remembers. Worried that, deep in the night, the flowers might “fall asleep” like people, he lights tall candles to shine on their “red make-up”. This is personification, writing about a thing as if it were a person: 红妆 normally means a woman dressed in her finest, here the bright red blossoms. There is also a hidden allusion — a Tang emperor once said his tipsy, drowsy consort Yang Guifei looked like “a crab-apple that hasn’t slept enough”. Su Shi loves the flowers too much to let them bloom alone in the dark, and the poem shows how he treasured beautiful things while they last. He was in exile in Huangzhou at the time, so his devotion also carries a touch of loneliness.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Su Shi (1037–1101), also called Su Dongpo, is said to have written this during his exile in Huangzhou (1080–1084). He was fond of a crab-apple tree there and wrote about it more than once.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

东风袅袅泛崇光，

dōng fēng niǎo niǎo fàn chóng guāng

A soft east wind, the blossoms shimmering,

香雾空蒙月转廊。

xiāng wù kōng méng yuè zhuǎn láng

a haze of scent, the moon gone round the veranda.

只恐夜深花睡去，

zhǐ kǒng yè shēn huā shuì qù

Afraid the flowers will fall asleep so late at night,

故烧高烛照红妆。

gù shāo gāo zhú zhào hóng zhuāng

I light tall candles to shine on their red finery.
