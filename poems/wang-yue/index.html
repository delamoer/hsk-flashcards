导读About this poem

A young Du Fu, about 24, stands below Mount Tai, China’s most famous mountain, and promises himself he will one day reach the top — and see every other mountain looking small.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是杜甫年轻时的作品，写他远望泰山。泰山又叫“岱宗”，是五岳之首。开头用一个问句：泰山到底是什么样子？回答是：在齐、鲁两个地方都能看到它青青的山色，没有尽头——不说多高，只说多大，一下子就写出了泰山的气势。三、四句说大自然把神奇和秀美都集中在泰山上，山的北面和南面一暗一亮，好像昏和晓被山“割”开了，一个“割”字非常有力。五、六句写诗人看得入神：层层白云让人心胸激荡，睁大眼睛一直看着归巢的鸟飞进山里。最后两句是全诗的灵魂：总有一天我要登上最高峰，看群山都变得很小。这两句今天常用来表达不怕困难、要站得更高的决心。

In English

Du Fu wrote this in his twenties, after failing the imperial exam, while travelling in the east. Mount Tai, also called Daizong, was the most honoured of China’s five sacred mountains. The poem opens with a question — what is Mount Tai really like? — and answers it by size rather than height: its green stretches on and on across the old states of Qi and Lu. The next couplet says nature gathered all its magic and beauty here, and the mountain is so huge that it “cuts” dusk from dawn: one side is already dark while the other is still bright. Then the poet is lost in looking: the layered clouds stir his chest, and he strains his eyes to follow birds flying home into the mountain. The last couplet is why the poem is famous: “One day I will stand on the very top and see all the other mountains small below me.” It is the voice of a young man full of ambition, and Chinese people still quote it to express the will to rise above difficulties.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this around 736, when he was about 24 and travelling in Shandong after failing the imperial examination. It is one of his earliest surviving poems, written long before the war years that darkened his later work.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

岱宗夫如何？

dài zōng fú rú hé

Mount Tai — what is it like?

齐鲁青未了。

qí lǔ qīng wèi liǎo

Across Qi and Lu its green goes on forever.

造化钟神秀，

zào huà zhōng shén xiù

Nature poured all its wonder and beauty here;

阴阳割昏晓。

yīn yáng gē hūn xiǎo

its shaded and sunlit slopes split dusk from dawn.

荡胸生曾云，

dàng xiōng shēng céng yún

Layers of cloud rise and stir my heart;

决眦入归鸟。

jué zì rù guī niǎo

I strain my eyes after birds flying home.

会当凌绝顶，

huì dāng líng jué dǐng

One day I will stand on the very top

一览众山小。

yī lǎn zhòng shān xiǎo

and see every other mountain small below.
