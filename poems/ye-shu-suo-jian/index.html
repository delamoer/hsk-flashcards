夜书所见

Yè Shū Suǒ Jiàn
Written at Night About What I Saw
叶绍翁Yè Shàowēng · Ye Shaoweng宋 Song

导读About this poem

Autumn wind in the parasol trees makes a traveller think of home — and then he sees a lamp in the dark where children are hunting crickets, just as he once did. A tender poem about memory and distance.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一个在外地的人，秋夜里想家。第一句写声音：秋风吹过梧桐树，叶子萧萧作响，好像把寒意一阵阵送过来。第二句点明心情：江上的秋风，勾起了游子（离家在外的人）的思乡之情。后两句画面一转：远处篱笆边，夜深了还亮着一盏灯，作者猜想，一定是孩子们在那里捉蟋蟀吧。“挑促织”就是用草拨弄、逗引蟋蟀。为什么写孩子？因为这让作者想起自己小时候在家乡也这样玩。孩子们的快乐和作者的孤单形成对比，那盏小小的灯，既是孩子们的灯，也照亮了作者对童年和家乡的回忆。前两句写冷清，后两句写温暖，冷暖对照，思乡的情绪写得含蓄又真切。

In English

A man far from home spends an autumn night thinking of it. The first line is all sound: the wind rustles through the parasol trees, and the leaves seem to send the cold toward him in waves. The second line names the feeling: the autumn wind over the river stirs a traveller’s longing for home. Then the picture changes. Far off, by a bamboo fence, one lamp is still burning late at night, and he guesses that children must be out there hunting crickets. 挑促织 means teasing a cricket out with a blade of grass, a favourite game of Chinese children for centuries. Why write about children? Because they remind him of himself as a boy, playing the same game at home. Their fun and his loneliness sit side by side, and that small lamp lights both their game and his memories. The first half is cold and lonely, the second half warm; the contrast lets his homesickness show without his ever saying “I miss home”.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Ye Shaoweng (dates uncertain, active early 13th century) was a Southern Song poet who spent much of his life in retirement near West Lake in Hangzhou. He is best known for short, fresh quatrains like this one and “Visiting a Garden but Not Finding Anyone In”.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

萧萧梧叶送寒声，

xiāo xiāo wú yè sòng hán shēng

Parasol leaves rustle, sending sounds of cold;

江上秋风动客情。

jiāng shàng qiū fēng dòng kè qíng

the autumn wind on the river stirs a traveller’s heart.

知有儿童挑促织，

zhī yǒu ér tóng tiǎo cù zhī

Children must be out there, coaxing crickets —

夜深篱落一灯明。

yè shēn lí luò yī dēng míng

deep in the night, one lamp glows by the fence.
