Wén Wáng Chānglíng Zuǒ Qiān Lóng Biāo Yáo Yǒu Cǐ Jì
Hearing That Wang Changling Has Been Demoted to Longbiao, I Send This from Afar
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang

导读About this poem

Li Bai hears that his friend, the poet Wang Changling, has been sent away to a remote post. He cannot go with him, so he gives his worried heart to the moon and asks it to follow his friend all the way there.

中文解析

王昌龄是李白的好朋友，也是著名诗人。他被降职到偏远的龙标（今湖南怀化一带），李白听说后写了这首诗寄给他。第一句写景：杨花已经落完，杜鹃鸟在不停地叫。暮春时节，花落了，春天要走了；杜鹃的叫声又凄凉，传说像在说“不如归去”。景色里已经带着离别的哀伤。第二句交代事情：听说你去龙标，要经过五条溪水——路途遥远又艰难。后两句是全诗最有名的地方：我把我的愁心交给明月，让它随着风，一直陪你到夜郎以西。人不能去，就让月亮替我去。月亮无论多远都能照到，所以诗人把它当成信使。这种把感情交给景物的写法叫“托物寄情”，想象新奇，感情真挚。

In English

Wang Changling was a famous poet and a good friend of Li Bai. When Li Bai heard that Wang had been demoted to Longbiao, a remote place in today’s western Hunan, he wrote this poem and sent it to him. The first line sets the scene: the willow catkins have all fallen, and the cuckoo keeps calling. It is late spring — the blossoms are gone, the season is leaving — and the cuckoo’s cry was said to sound like “better to go home”. Sadness is already in the landscape. The second line gives the news: I hear you must cross five rivers to reach Longbiao — a long, hard journey. The last two lines are the famous part: I give my worried heart to the bright moon and let it go with the wind, all the way west of Yelang. Li Bai cannot travel with his friend, so the moon will go instead; it shines everywhere, however far, so it can be his messenger. Handing your feelings to something in nature like this is a favourite device in Chinese poetry, and here it is both imaginative and sincere.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) and Wang Changling (c. 698–c. 757) were close friends. Wang was demoted to Longbiao, traditionally said to be for not taking care over minor matters of conduct; the poem is usually dated to around 748–749.
