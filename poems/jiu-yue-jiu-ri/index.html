九月九日忆山东兄弟

Jiǔ Yuè Jiǔ Rì Yì Shāndōng Xiōngdì
Thinking of My Brothers East of the Mountains on the Double Ninth
王维Wáng Wéi · Wang Wei唐 Tang

导读About this poem

A seventeen-year-old far from home on the Double Ninth Festival imagines his brothers climbing a hill without him. It gave Chinese a line still quoted at every holiday: “On festive days I miss my family twice as much.”

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是王维十七岁时在长安写的。农历九月九日是重阳节，古人这一天要和家人一起登高，身上插着茱萸，据说可以避开灾祸。第一句连用两个“异”字：在“异乡”做“异客”，把一个人在外的陌生和孤单写得很重。第二句“每逢佳节倍思亲”说出了每个离家的人都有的感受：平时还好，一到节日就格外想家。这句话今天仍是中国人过节时最常说的诗句。后两句很特别：诗人不写自己怎么想家人，反过来想象家里的兄弟们登上高处、插上茱萸时，发现少了一个人——是在想念他。从对方的角度写自己的思念，叫“对写”，感情更深一层。“山东”不是今天的山东省，而是华山以东，王维的家乡在今天的山西。

In English

Wang Wei wrote this at seventeen, alone in the capital Chang’an, on the ninth day of the ninth lunar month — the Double Ninth Festival (重阳节). On that day families climbed a hill together and wore sprigs of zhuyu (Japanese cornel), a fragrant plant believed to keep bad luck away. The first line uses 异 (“strange, foreign”) twice: a stranger in a strange place. That doubled word makes the loneliness heavy. The second line says what everyone away from home has felt: ordinary days are bearable, but on holidays you miss your family twice as much. It has become a proverb — Chinese people still send it to each other at Mid-Autumn and Spring Festival. Then the poem turns around. Instead of describing his own homesickness, Wang Wei pictures his brothers at home: they climb the hill, put on their zhuyu, and notice that one person is missing — him. Writing your own longing from the other side, imagining the people who miss you, is a classic technique; it makes the feeling deeper and gentler at the same time. Note that 山东 here is not today’s Shandong Province: it means “east of the mountains” (east of Mount Hua), where Wang Wei’s family lived, in today’s Shanxi.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) was a poet, painter and musician, later famous for quiet landscape poems shaped by Buddhism. A note in his collection says he wrote this poem at seventeen, while away from home seeking a career in the capital.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“为异客”的“为”读 wéi（做、成为）。“登高处”的“处”读 chù（地方）。“少一人”的“少”读 shǎo。生僻字：茱萸 zhū yú。

独在异乡为异客，

dú zài yì xiāng wéi yì kè

Alone in a strange place, a stranger among strangers,

měi féng jiā jié bèi sī qīn

on every holiday I miss my family twice as much.

yáo zhī xiōng dì dēng gāo chù

Far off, I know my brothers are climbing the hill today,

biàn chā zhū yú shǎo yī rén

each with a sprig of zhuyu — and one of us is missing.
