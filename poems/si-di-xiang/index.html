思帝乡（春日游）

Sī Dì Xiāng
Longing for the Imperial City: A Spring Outing

导读About this poem

A girl out on a spring walk, apricot petals in her hair, sees a dashing young man and decides on the spot: she will marry him, and even if he leaves her one day, she won’t regret it. Startlingly bold for a love song written over a thousand years ago.

直译LiteralA spring-day outing; apricot blossoms blow all over my head.

意译MeaningOut walking on a spring day, the wind fills my hair with apricot petals.

02

直译LiteralOn the path, whose family’s young man is so full of style?

意译MeaningWho is that young man on the path, so handsome, so carefree?

03

直译LiteralI intend to give myself in marriage to him — and that settles my whole life.

意译MeaningI’ll marry him — I’ve decided — and that will be my life.

04

直译LiteralEven if heartlessly abandoned, I could not feel ashamed.

意译MeaningAnd if one day he leaves me cold, I won’t be sorry.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首“词”，词是配合音乐唱的歌词，“思帝乡”是曲调的名字（词牌），和内容关系不大。词用一个少女的口吻来说话。春天她出门游玩，风一吹，杏花落满了头。路上看见一个不知是谁家的少年，风度翩翩，非常潇洒。她马上下了决心：我打算把自己嫁给他，这一辈子就算定了；就算将来被他无情地抛弃，我也不后悔、不觉得羞耻。古代女子的婚姻多由父母决定，诗里却是一个姑娘自己大胆地选择爱情，而且连最坏的结果都想好了，所以显得特别直率、热烈。句子长短不齐，像人说话时脱口而出，正好写出她一见钟情时的激动心情。

In English

This is a ci — a lyric written to fit a tune — and “Longing for the Imperial City” is just the name of the melody, which has little to do with the content. The speaker is a young woman. She is out on a spring walk, and the wind showers apricot blossoms all over her hair. On the path she sees a young man — whose son is he? — and he is so handsome and dashing that she makes up her mind at once. “I mean to give myself to him in marriage, and that will settle my whole life. Even if one day he coldly casts me off, I will not be ashamed.” In old China a girl’s marriage was usually arranged by her parents, so a woman choosing a husband for herself, out loud, and accepting the worst that might happen, sounds remarkably bold. The lines are uneven — three characters, then five, then six — which makes them sound like words blurted out in the moment, perfect for the rush of falling in love at first sight. Under the bright spring scene there is a hint of risk, which makes her courage all the more striking.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wei Zhuang (c. 836–910) was a poet of the late Tang who later served the Former Shu kingdom in Sichuan. He is one of the leading poets of the “Among the Flowers” (Huajian) collection of 940, the earliest anthology of ci lyrics; like many of its songs, this one is written in a woman’s voice.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

春日游，杏花吹满头。

chūn rì yóu, xìng huā chuī mǎn tóu

Out walking on a spring day, the wind fills my hair with apricot petals.

陌上谁家年少，足风流？

mò shàng shuí jiā nián shào, zú fēng liú

Who is that young man on the path, so handsome, so carefree?

妾拟将身嫁与，一生休。

qiè nǐ jiāng shēn jià yǔ, yī shēng xiū

I’ll marry him — I’ve decided — and that will be my life.

纵被无情弃，不能羞。

zòng bèi wú qíng qì, bù néng xiū

And if one day he leaves me cold, I won’t be sorry.
