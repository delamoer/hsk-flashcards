导读About this poem

Rain has just passed over the mountains; moonlight falls through the pines and a spring runs over the stones. Wang Wei, the poet-painter, shows why people say his poems are paintings — and why he would rather stay here than go back to court.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写山中雨后秋天的傍晚。“空山”不是没有东西，而是人少、安静、干净。中间两联最有名：明月从松树之间照下来，清泉在石头上流过——一句写光，一句写声，静中有动；竹林里传来笑声，原来是洗衣服的姑娘们回家了；荷叶摇动，原来是渔船顺流而下——先听见声音、看见动静，再说出原因，写得很有生活味。最后两句用了《楚辞》里“王孙兮归来”的典故：春天的花草谢了也没关系，秋天的山里一样美，我愿意留下来。宋代苏轼说王维“诗中有画”，这首诗就是最好的例子，也表达了诗人对官场之外清净生活的喜爱。

In English

An autumn evening in the hills, just after rain. “Empty mountains” doesn’t mean nothing is there; it means quiet, few people, everything washed clean. The two middle couplets are the famous part. The bright moon shines down between the pines; a clear spring flows over the rocks — one line of light, one of sound, stillness with a little movement inside it. Then life appears: the bamboo rustles with laughter as the washerwomen head home; the lotus leaves sway as a fishing boat slips downstream. Notice the order — we hear the noise and see the movement first, and only then learn the cause, just as you would if you were standing there. The last couplet turns an old line from the Songs of Chu, which calls a wandering nobleman to come home from the wild hills. Wang Wei reverses it: let the spring flowers fade — autumn here is lovely enough, and the “nobleman” can happily stay. Su Shi later said Wang Wei’s poems contain paintings, and this poem is the classic example. It also shows his love of a calm life away from the court.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761) was a high official, a painter, a musician and a devout Buddhist, often called the “Buddha of Poetry”. In mid-life he kept a country estate at Wangchuan in the Zhongnan Mountains south of Chang’an; this poem is usually linked to his retreats there.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

空山新雨后，

kōng shān xīn yǔ hòu

The quiet hills, just washed by rain —

天气晚来秋。

tiān qì wǎn lái qiū

with evening, the air turns to autumn.

明月松间照，

míng yuè sōng jiān zhào

Moonlight slants down between the pines,

清泉石上流。

qīng quán shí shàng liú

a clear spring slides over the stones.

竹喧归浣女，

zhú xuān guī huàn nǚ

Laughter in the bamboo — the washing girls going home;

莲动下渔舟。

lián dòng xià yú zhōu

the lotus leaves stir — a fishing boat drifting down.

随意春芳歇，

suí yì chūn fāng xiē

Let the flowers of spring wither if they must;

王孙自可留。

wáng sūn zì kě liú

a wanderer like me can stay on here.
