导读About this poem

Music drifts all day over Chengdu, half on the river wind, half into the clouds. “Music like this belongs in heaven” — still the standard Chinese compliment for a beautiful performance.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写音乐之美。花卿是成都的一位将军，叫花敬定。第一句写成都城里每天都有乐声，“丝管”指弦乐器和管乐器，“纷纷”写出乐声又多又热闹。第二句很有想象力：乐声一半随着江风飘走，一半飘进了云里。声音本来看不见，诗人却写得好像能看到它飞到哪里去。第三、四句是千古名句：这样的曲子只应该天上才有，人间能听到几回呢？今天中国人夸一首曲子、一段演奏特别好听，还常说“此曲只应天上有”。另外，历来有人认为这首诗是含蓄的讽刺：古代礼制规定，有些乐曲只有皇帝才能用，花敬定却随便使用，“天上”暗指皇宫。这样看，表面是夸奖，实际是提醒。两种读法都说得通。

In English

On the surface this is a poem about beautiful music. Hua Qing was General Hua Jingding, stationed in Chengdu. The first line says that every day the city is full of the sound of strings and pipes. The second is wonderfully imaginative: half of the music floats off on the wind over the river, half rises into the clouds. Sound is invisible, but Du Fu makes us see where it goes. The last two lines are the famous ones: such tunes should exist only in heaven — how many times in this world can one hear them? Chinese people still use 此曲只应天上有 to praise a lovely song or performance. But there has long been another reading. In Tang times certain music was reserved for the emperor, and General Hua was notorious for overstepping his place. “Heaven” may quietly mean the imperial court — so the compliment could be a polite warning that he was using music he had no right to. Both readings work, and that double edge is part of the poem's charm.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this around 761 in Chengdu. Hua Jingding was a general who had helped put down a local rebellion but was known for his arrogance; the idea that the poem is a veiled criticism goes back to Ming-dynasty commentators.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

锦城丝管日纷纷，

jǐn chéng sī guǎn rì fēn fēn

All day the strings and pipes fill the City of Brocade;

半入江风半入云。

bàn rù jiāng fēng bàn rù yún

half the music drifts on the river wind, half floats into the clouds.

此曲只应天上有，

cǐ qǔ zhǐ yīng tiān shàng yǒu

A tune like this belongs in heaven —

人间能得几回闻。

rén jiān néng dé jǐ huí wén

how often does the world below get to hear it?
