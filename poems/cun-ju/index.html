导读About this poem

Grass growing, birds flying, willows swaying — and children racing home from school to fly kites in the spring wind. A bright, simple poem that every Chinese primary-school pupil learns.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写早春二月乡村的景色和孩子们放风筝的快乐。前两句写景：小草长出来了，黄莺在天上飞，杨柳的长枝轻轻拂过河堤，在春天淡淡的雾气里好像喝醉了一样。“醉”字用得很妙，这是拟人（把东西当作人来写），让杨柳有了人的样子，也写出春天让人陶醉的感觉。后两句写人：孩子们放学回来得早，赶紧趁着东风把风筝放上天。“忙趁”两个字写出孩子们急急忙忙、兴高采烈的样子。全诗前半写景、后半写人，景美人乐，充满生机，语言浅显，是小学生最早学的古诗之一。

In English

This poem shows a village in early spring, the second month of the lunar calendar, and the fun children have flying kites. The first two lines are the scenery: new grass is growing, orioles are flying, and the long branches of the willows brush along the riverbank, swaying as if drunk in the soft spring haze. The word 醉 (“drunk”) is a lovely touch — it is personification, describing a thing as if it were a person, and it suggests how intoxicating spring feels. The last two lines are about people: the children have come home from school early and hurry to catch the east wind and send their paper kites up into the sky. The words 忙趁 (“rush to take advantage of”) capture their excitement perfectly. The first half paints the scene, the second half fills it with people; the landscape is beautiful and the people are happy, and the whole poem is full of life. Because the language is so plain, it is one of the first classical poems Chinese children learn at primary school.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Gao Ding (1828–1880) was a late-Qing poet from Zhejiang about whom little is known. He is said to have written this poem while living in the countryside; today it is his only widely known work.

草长莺飞二月天，

cǎo zhǎng yīng fēi èr yuè tiān

Grass springing up and orioles in flight — the second month is here;

拂堤杨柳醉春烟。

fú dī yáng liǔ zuì chūn yān

willows brush the riverbank, swaying drunk in the spring haze.

儿童散学归来早，

ér tóng sàn xué guī lái zǎo

The children are home from school early today,

忙趁东风放纸鸢。

máng chèn dōng fēng fàng zhǐ yuān

racing to catch the east wind and fly their paper kites.
