导读About this poem

Four snapshots from a house high in the hills: mist on the slopes, sunset through bamboo, birds above the eaves, and clouds drifting out of the window. Simple words, and you can almost see it.

中文解析

这首小诗写住在深山里看到的景色，四句就是四个画面。第一句：山与山之间，远远看见飘来的烟雾。第二句：透过竹林的缝隙，偷偷看到落下的太阳——“窥”是从小缝里看，说明竹子很密。第三句：鸟儿向屋檐上飞去。第四句最有意思：云从窗户里飘出来！不是从窗外飘过，而是从窗里出来，说明房子建得非常高，云就在屋子里外来去。这是一种夸张，也写出了住在山里那种和云鸟为邻的感觉。全诗没有一个字直接说“我喜欢这里”，也没有写人，但读者能感到诗人的悠闲和对山居生活的喜爱，这种写法叫“寓情于景”（把感情藏在景物里）。语言浅显，很适合初学者背诵。

In English

This little poem describes life in a house deep in the mountains, and each of its four lines is a separate picture. First: between the peaks, a haze drifts closer. Second: through the bamboo, the poet peeks at the setting sun — 窥 kuī means to peep through a narrow gap, so we know the bamboo grows thick. Third: birds fly up towards the eaves. The fourth line is the surprise: clouds come drifting out of the window! Not past the window — out of it, as if the clouds had been visiting indoors. The house must stand so high that clouds float in and out of the rooms. It’s a playful exaggeration that captures what it feels like to live with clouds and birds as neighbours. The poem never says “I love it here” and never shows a person, yet we feel the poet’s calm delight. Hiding your feeling inside the scenery is a basic technique of Chinese poetry. The words are simple, so this is a good poem for beginners to learn by heart.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wu Jun (469–520) was a writer and historian of the Liang dynasty from a humble family, admired for his fresh, clear landscape writing — his letter to his friend Zhu Yuansi is still read in Chinese schools. This is the first of his three “Mountain Lines”.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

窥 读 kuī（从小缝里看），不读 guī。檐 读 yán。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

山际见来烟，

shān jì jiàn lái yān

Between the peaks, a haze comes drifting in;

竹中窥落日。

zhú zhōng kuī luò rì

through the bamboo I glimpse the setting sun.

鸟向檐上飞，

niǎo xiàng yán shàng fēi

Birds wing their way up to the eaves,

云从窗里出。

yún cóng chuāng lǐ chū

and clouds float out through my window.
