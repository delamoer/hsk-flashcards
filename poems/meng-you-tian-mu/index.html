梦游天姥吟留别

Mèng Yóu Tiānmǔ Yín Liú Bié
A Dream Journey to Mount Tianmu: A Farewell Song
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang

导读About this poem

Li Bai dreams he flies by moonlight to a sacred mountain, climbs into a world of thunder, gods and golden palaces — then wakes on his pillow. The dream ends in a defiant line: how could I bow and scrape to the powerful?

第一段Part 1
01
海客谈瀛洲，烟涛微茫信难求；

直译LiteralSeafarers talk of Yingzhou — in misty waves, faint and vague, truly hard to find;

意译MeaningSailors speak of Yingzhou, the immortals’ isle, lost in misty waves and impossible to find;

02
越人语天姥，云霞明灭或可睹。

直译LiteralPeople of Yue talk of Tianmu — through clouds and glowing mist, flickering, it may be seen.

意译Meaningbut the people of Yue speak of Mount Tianmu, which shows itself now and then through shifting clouds.

03
天姥连天向天横，势拔五岳掩赤城。

直译LiteralTianmu joins the sky and stretches across the sky; its might rises above the Five Peaks and overshadows Chicheng.

意译MeaningTianmu meets the sky and lies across it, towering over the Five Great Mountains and dwarfing Red City Hill.

04
天台四万八千丈，对此欲倒东南倾。

直译LiteralTiantai, forty-eight thousand zhang high, facing this seems about to fall, leaning southeast.

意译MeaningEven Tiantai, forty-eight thousand feet tall, seems to bow southeast before it, ready to topple.

第二段Part 2
05
我欲因之梦吴越，一夜飞度镜湖月。

直译LiteralBecause of this I want to dream of Wu and Yue; in one night I fly across the moon on Mirror Lake.

意译MeaningSo I dream of the lands of Wu and Yue, and in one night fly over the moonlit Mirror Lake.

06
湖月照我影，送我至剡溪。

直译LiteralThe lake moon lights my shadow and sends me to Shan Creek.

意译MeaningThe moon on the lake throws my shadow ahead and sees me as far as Shan Creek.

07
谢公宿处今尚在，渌水荡漾清猿啼。

直译LiteralLord Xie’s lodging place is still there today; clear water ripples, gibbons cry shrilly.

意译MeaningThe place where Xie Lingyun once stayed is still there; clear water ripples, and gibbons cry.

08
脚著谢公屐，身登青云梯。

直译LiteralOn my feet I wear Lord Xie’s clogs; my body climbs the ladder of blue clouds.

意译MeaningI put on Xie’s mountain clogs and climb a stairway into the blue clouds.

09
半壁见海日，空中闻天鸡。

直译LiteralHalfway up the cliff I see the sun on the sea; in the air I hear the heavenly rooster.

意译MeaningHalfway up the cliff I see the sun rise from the sea, and hear the heavenly rooster crow in the sky.

10
千岩万转路不定，迷花倚石忽已暝。

直译LiteralA thousand cliffs, ten thousand turns, the path uncertain; lost in flowers, leaning on rocks, suddenly it is dark.

意译MeaningRocks without number, turns without end, the path keeps changing; charmed by flowers, resting on a stone, I find night has fallen.

11
熊咆龙吟殷岩泉，栗深林兮惊层巅。

直译LiteralBears roar, dragons howl, rumbling among rocks and springs; the deep forest trembles, ah, the layered peaks are startled.

意译MeaningBears roar and dragons howl till the rocks and springs rumble; the deep woods shudder and the high peaks quake.

12
云青青兮欲雨，水澹澹兮生烟。

直译LiteralThe clouds are dark, ah, about to rain; the water ripples, ah, giving off mist.

意译MeaningDark clouds gather, heavy with rain; the water stirs and breathes out mist.

13
列缺霹雳，丘峦崩摧。

直译LiteralLightning and thunderclaps; hills and peaks collapse and crumble.

意译MeaningLightning flashes, thunder cracks, and the hills split and crumble.

14
洞天石扉，訇然中开。

直译LiteralThe stone door of the cave-heaven, with a crash, opens in the middle.

意译MeaningThe stone gate of a cave-heaven bursts open with a roar.

15
青冥浩荡不见底，日月照耀金银台。

直译LiteralThe blue void is vast, its bottom unseen; sun and moon shine on terraces of gold and silver.

意译MeaningA blue sky opens, endless and bottomless; sun and moon blaze on terraces of gold and silver.

16
霓为衣兮风为马，云之君兮纷纷而来下。

直译LiteralRainbows for clothes, ah, wind for horses; the lords of the clouds, ah, come down in throngs.

意译MeaningDressed in rainbows and riding the wind, the lords of the clouds come streaming down.

17
虎鼓瑟兮鸾回车，仙之人兮列如麻。

直译LiteralTigers play the zither, ah, phoenixes turn the carriages; the immortals, ah, are ranked as thick as hemp.

意译MeaningTigers strum the zither, phoenixes draw the chariots round, and immortals crowd together, row on row.

18
忽魂悸以魄动，恍惊起而长嗟。

直译LiteralSuddenly my soul shudders and my spirit stirs; dazed, I start up with a long sigh.

意译MeaningAll at once my heart leaps in fright; I start awake with a long sigh.

19
惟觉时之枕席，失向来之烟霞。

直译LiteralOn waking there is only pillow and mat; lost are the mists and glowing clouds of a moment ago.

意译MeaningAwake, I find only my pillow and mat; the shining mists of a moment ago are gone.

第三段Part 3
20
世间行乐亦如此，古来万事东流水。

直译LiteralPleasures in the world are also like this; since ancient times all things are east-flowing water.

意译MeaningThe pleasures of this world are just the same: since ancient times, everything has flowed away east like the rivers.

21
别君去兮何时还？

直译LiteralLeaving you, ah, when will I return?

意译MeaningI leave you now — who knows when I’ll be back?

22
且放白鹿青崖间，须行即骑访名山。

直译LiteralFor now I let my white deer loose among green cliffs; when I must go, I’ll ride it to visit famous mountains.

意译MeaningFor now I’ll let my white deer graze among green cliffs, and when it’s time, ride it off to the great mountains.

23
安能摧眉折腰事权贵，使我不得开心颜！

直译LiteralHow can I lower my brows and bend my waist to serve the powerful, so that I can never have a happy face!

意译MeaningHow could I lower my eyes and bend my back to serve the mighty, and never once smile from the heart!

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是李白离开长安后，要去南方游历时写给东鲁朋友的告别诗，用一场梦来表达心志。第一段从传说写起：海外仙山瀛洲找不到，越人说的天姥山却能看到，它高耸入天，连天台山都好像要向它倒下。第二段写梦：诗人趁着月光飞过镜湖，到了剡溪，穿上谢灵运的木屐登山，半山看见海上日出，听到天鸡啼叫；接着天色暗下来，熊吼龙吟，雷电劈开山峦，仙洞石门轰然打开，神仙们穿着彩虹做的衣服、骑着风纷纷下来。正热闹时，他突然惊醒，只有枕头和席子，刚才的烟霞全都不见了。第三段议论：人间的快乐也像这场梦，古来万事都像东流的水。最后两句是全诗的灵魂：怎么能低头弯腰去侍奉权贵，让自己不开心呢！这首诗想象奇特，句式长短自由，是李白浪漫主义的代表作。

In English

Li Bai wrote this as a farewell to friends in Shandong when he set off south after leaving the court, and he uses a dream to say what he believes. Part one starts with legend: the island of the immortals across the sea can’t be found, but Mount Tianmu, which people of Yue talk about, can sometimes be glimpsed through cloud — so tall that even the great Tiantai range seems to lean away from it. Part two is the dream. He flies over Mirror Lake by moonlight, reaches Shan Creek, puts on the hiking clogs of the old poet Xie Lingyun and climbs a ladder into the clouds. Halfway up he sees the sun rise from the sea and hears the heavenly rooster crow. Then it grows dark: bears roar, dragons howl, lightning splits the peaks, a stone door to a cave-heaven crashes open, and immortals dressed in rainbows, riding the wind, come down in crowds. At the height of it he jerks awake — only pillow and mat remain; the misty glories are gone. Part three draws the lesson: worldly pleasure is like this dream, and everything flows east like water. The last line is the poem’s heart: how can I lower my brows and bend my back to serve the powerful, and never get to smile? Wild imagination and freely changing line lengths make this a model of Li Bai’s romantic style.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) left his court post in Chang’an around 744, disillusioned with palace politics. About two years later, leaving Eastern Lu (Shandong) for the Yue region (Zhejiang), he wrote this poem for the friends he was leaving behind. Mount Tianmu is in today’s Xinchang, Zhejiang.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

按统编教材注音：天姥 tiān mǔ；天台 tiān tāi；剡溪 shàn xī；脚著 zhuó；屐 jī；暝 míng；咆 páo；殷 yǐn（震响）；栗 lì；巅 diān；澹澹 dàn dàn；列缺 liè quē；訇然 hōng rán；悸 jì；嗟 jiē；觉 jué；向来 xiàng lái；骑 qí；宿处 sù chù；渌水 lù。“纷纷而来下”的“下”今读 xià（古音押韵读 hù）。机器朗读以上多处易读错，已在朗读用字中替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

海客谈瀛洲，烟涛微茫信难求；

hǎi kè tán yíng zhōu, yān tāo wēi máng xìn nán qiú

Sailors speak of Yingzhou, the immortals’ isle, lost in misty waves and impossible to find;

越人语天姥，云霞明灭或可睹。

yuè rén yǔ tiān mǔ, yún xiá míng miè huò kě dǔ

but the people of Yue speak of Mount Tianmu, which shows itself now and then through shifting clouds.

天姥连天向天横，势拔五岳掩赤城。

tiān mǔ lián tiān xiàng tiān héng, shì bá wǔ yuè yǎn chì chéng

Tianmu meets the sky and lies across it, towering over the Five Great Mountains and dwarfing Red City Hill.

天台四万八千丈，对此欲倒东南倾。

tiān tāi sì wàn bā qiān zhàng, duì cǐ yù dǎo dōng nán qīng

Even Tiantai, forty-eight thousand feet tall, seems to bow southeast before it, ready to topple.

我欲因之梦吴越，一夜飞度镜湖月。

wǒ yù yīn zhī mèng wú yuè, yī yè fēi dù jìng hú yuè

So I dream of the lands of Wu and Yue, and in one night fly over the moonlit Mirror Lake.

湖月照我影，送我至剡溪。

hú yuè zhào wǒ yǐng, sòng wǒ zhì shàn xī

The moon on the lake throws my shadow ahead and sees me as far as Shan Creek.

谢公宿处今尚在，渌水荡漾清猿啼。

xiè gōng sù chù jīn shàng zài, lù shuǐ dàng yàng qīng yuán tí

The place where Xie Lingyun once stayed is still there; clear water ripples, and gibbons cry.

脚著谢公屐，身登青云梯。

jiǎo zhuó xiè gōng jī, shēn dēng qīng yún tī

I put on Xie’s mountain clogs and climb a stairway into the blue clouds.

半壁见海日，空中闻天鸡。

bàn bì jiàn hǎi rì, kōng zhōng wén tiān jī

Halfway up the cliff I see the sun rise from the sea, and hear the heavenly rooster crow in the sky.

千岩万转路不定，迷花倚石忽已暝。

qiān yán wàn zhuǎn lù bù dìng, mí huā yǐ shí hū yǐ míng

Rocks without number, turns without end, the path keeps changing; charmed by flowers, resting on a stone, I find night has fallen.

熊咆龙吟殷岩泉，栗深林兮惊层巅。

xióng páo lóng yín yǐn yán quán, lì shēn lín xī jīng céng diān

Bears roar and dragons howl till the rocks and springs rumble; the deep woods shudder and the high peaks quake.

云青青兮欲雨，水澹澹兮生烟。

yún qīng qīng xī yù yǔ, shuǐ dàn dàn xī shēng yān

Dark clouds gather, heavy with rain; the water stirs and breathes out mist.

列缺霹雳，丘峦崩摧。

liè quē pī lì, qiū luán bēng cuī

Lightning flashes, thunder cracks, and the hills split and crumble.

洞天石扉，訇然中开。

dòng tiān shí fēi, hōng rán zhōng kāi

The stone gate of a cave-heaven bursts open with a roar.

青冥浩荡不见底，日月照耀金银台。

qīng míng hào dàng bù jiàn dǐ, rì yuè zhào yào jīn yín tái

A blue sky opens, endless and bottomless; sun and moon blaze on terraces of gold and silver.

霓为衣兮风为马，云之君兮纷纷而来下。

ní wéi yī xī fēng wéi mǎ, yún zhī jūn xī fēn fēn ér lái xià

Dressed in rainbows and riding the wind, the lords of the clouds come streaming down.

虎鼓瑟兮鸾回车，仙之人兮列如麻。

hǔ gǔ sè xī luán huí chē, xiān zhī rén xī liè rú má

Tigers strum the zither, phoenixes draw the chariots round, and immortals crowd together, row on row.

忽魂悸以魄动，恍惊起而长嗟。

hū hún jì yǐ pò dòng, huǎng jīng qǐ ér cháng jiē

All at once my heart leaps in fright; I start awake with a long sigh.

惟觉时之枕席，失向来之烟霞。

wéi jué shí zhī zhěn xí, shī xiàng lái zhī yān xiá

Awake, I find only my pillow and mat; the shining mists of a moment ago are gone.

世间行乐亦如此，古来万事东流水。

shì jiān xíng lè yì rú cǐ, gǔ lái wàn shì dōng liú shuǐ

The pleasures of this world are just the same: since ancient times, everything has flowed away east like the rivers.

别君去兮何时还？

bié jūn qù xī hé shí huán

I leave you now — who knows when I’ll be back?

且放白鹿青崖间，须行即骑访名山。

qiě fàng bái lù qīng yá jiān, xū xíng jí qí fǎng míng shān

For now I’ll let my white deer graze among green cliffs, and when it’s time, ride it off to the great mountains.

安能摧眉折腰事权贵，使我不得开心颜！

ān néng cuī méi zhé yāo shì quán guì, shǐ wǒ bù dé kāi xīn yán

How could I lower my eyes and bend my back to serve the mighty, and never once smile from the heart!
