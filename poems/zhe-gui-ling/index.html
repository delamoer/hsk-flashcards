导读About this poem

A young woman falls in love for the first time and is instantly lovesick — body light as a cloud, heart like drifting willow down, breath thin as gossamer. Playful repetition makes it sound like a song.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首小令用一位少女的口吻写初恋的相思。开头三句很有趣：我这一辈子从来不懂什么是相思，刚刚懂得相思，就害上了相思病。三个“相思”连用，读起来像绕口令，也写出感情来得又快又猛。接着三个比喻：身体像浮云一样轻飘无力，心像飘飞的柳絮一样乱，气息像游丝一样细弱——把相思病写得像真的病一样。她为什么这样？因为那个“千金游子”（她爱的人，一位尊贵的公子）走了，只留下一缕余香，不知他去了哪里。最后自问自答：这病什么时候最厉害？就在灯光半暗、月亮半明的时候——也就是深夜一个人醒着的时候。曲中“思”“丝”“此”“之”“时”押同一个韵，结尾四个“时”字连用，音乐感很强。

In English

This short song is spoken by a young woman in love for the first time. The opening is playful: “All my life I never knew lovesickness; now that I know it at last, I’m sick with it.” The word 相思 (“longing”) comes three times in a row, almost like a tongue-twister, showing how fast and hard the feeling hits her. Then three comparisons: her body is light and weak as a drifting cloud, her heart scattered like flying willow down, her breath thin as a floating thread of gossamer — lovesickness described as if it were a real illness. Why? The man she loves, a well-born young traveller, has left; only a wisp of his fragrance lingers, and she doesn’t know where he has gone. She ends with a question and answer: when are the symptoms worst? When the lamp is half dim and the moon half bright — late at night, alone and awake. Almost every line ends on the same rhyme (思, 丝, 此, 之, 时), and the last lines repeat 时 (“time”) four times, giving it a strong musical feel.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xu Zaisi (dates unknown, active in the early 14th century) was a Yuan sanqu writer nicknamed “Sweet Studio” (甜斋). He was paired with Guan Yunshi, “Sour Studio”, and their collected songs were known as the “Sour and Sweet Songs”.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

平生不会相思，

píng shēng bù huì xiāng sī

All my life I never knew what longing was;

才会相思，便害相思。

cái huì xiāng sī, biàn hài xiāng sī

the moment I learned it, it made me ill.

身似浮云，心如飞絮，气若游丝。

shēn sì fú yún, xīn rú fēi xù, qì ruò yóu sī

My body drifts like a cloud, my heart flies loose like willow down, my breath is thin as a spider’s thread.

空一缕余香在此，

kōng yī lǚ yú xiāng zài cǐ

Only a wisp of his scent is left here —

盼千金游子何之。

pàn qiān jīn yóu zǐ hé zhī

I wait for my fine young traveller: where has he gone?

证候来时，正是何时？

zhèng hòu lái shí, zhèng shì hé shí

And when does the sickness come on worst?

灯半昏时，月半明时。

dēng bàn hūn shí, yuè bàn míng shí

When the lamp is half dim and the moon is half bright.
