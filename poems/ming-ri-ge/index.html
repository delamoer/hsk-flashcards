导读About this poem

“Tomorrow, and then tomorrow again — how many tomorrows there are!” A light, catchy song against putting things off, so easy to remember that Chinese parents and teachers still quote it to children who say “I’ll do it tomorrow.”

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗用最简单的话讲一个大道理：不要把事情推到明天。开头一连说了好几个“明日”：明天之后又是明天，明天多得数不清。可是如果一辈子都在等明天，什么事情都会被耽误，时间白白浪费，这就是“蹉跎”。接着诗人说：世上的人如果被“明天再说”拖累，春天过去秋天又来，不知不觉就老了。“朝看水东流，暮看日西坠”是两个画面：早上看河水向东流走，晚上看太阳往西落下——水不会倒流，太阳落了这一天也就没了，时间就是这样一去不回。最后一句像是对读者直接说话：人一生能有多少个明天呢？请听听我这首《明日歌》吧。全诗反复用“明日”这个词，读起来像顺口溜，很好记，所以一直被用来劝人珍惜时间、今天的事今天做。

In English

The poem uses the simplest words to make one big point: don’t put things off until tomorrow. It opens by repeating “tomorrow” again and again. After tomorrow comes another tomorrow, and there seem to be more tomorrows than you could ever count. But if you spend your whole life waiting for tomorrow, nothing gets done and the time is simply wasted — the poet’s word for this is 蹉跎 (cuōtuó), “to let time slip by”. Then comes the warning. If people let “tomorrow” hold them back, spring goes, autumn comes, and before they notice, they are old. The next line gives two pictures: in the morning you watch the river flowing east, in the evening you watch the sun sinking in the west. Water never flows back, and once the sun has set, that day is gone for good. Time works the same way. The last line speaks straight to the reader: in a whole lifetime, how many tomorrows can you really have? So please listen to my Song of Tomorrow. Because “tomorrow” comes back in almost every line, the poem sounds like a playful rhyme and sticks in the memory. That is why it is still used to remind people to value their time and do today what can be done today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Qian Fu (1461–1504), who called himself Hetan (鹤滩, “Crane Beach”), came from Huating, in today’s Shanghai. He came first in the national palace examination of 1490 but soon left office and went home. The poem is usually given under his name, though this has been questioned; a similar but different “Song of Tomorrow” is attributed to the painter Wen Jia.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

明日累：累 lěi（受牵累，通行注音；也有人读 lèi）。朝 zhāo（早晨，不读 cháo）；蹉跎 cuō tuó；几何 jǐ hé；何其 hé qí；老将至 jiāng。

版本说明 About the text

采用最通行的文本，一般题为明代钱福（号鹤滩）所作。另有一首文字不同、题为文嘉所作的《明日歌》（“日日待明日，万世成蹉跎……晨昏滚滚水东流，今古悠悠日西坠”），网站不用。异文：“世人若被明日累”一作“世人苦被明日累”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

明日复明日，明日何其多。

míng rì fù míng rì, míng rì hé qí duō

Tomorrow, then tomorrow again — tomorrows without end.

我生待明日，万事成蹉跎。

wǒ shēng dài míng rì, wàn shì chéng cuō tuó

Spend your life waiting for tomorrow, and everything slips away undone.

世人若被明日累，春去秋来老将至。

shì rén ruò bèi míng rì lěi, chūn qù qiū lái lǎo jiāng zhì

Let “tomorrow” hold you back, and springs and autumns pass until old age is at the door.

朝看水东流，暮看日西坠。

zhāo kàn shuǐ dōng liú, mù kàn rì xī zhuì

At dawn you watch the river run east; at dusk, the sun go down in the west.

百年明日能几何？请君听我明日歌。

bǎi nián míng rì néng jǐ hé, qǐng jūn tīng wǒ míng rì gē

A lifetime holds only so many tomorrows — so listen, friend, to my Song of Tomorrow.
