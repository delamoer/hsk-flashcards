小重山（昨夜寒蛩不住鸣）

Xiǎo Chóng Shān
Layered Hills (Tune)

导读About this poem

The quiet side of a war hero. Woken at midnight by crickets, a general paces the steps under a hazy moon, his hair gone white and his homeland still lost — and wonders who would listen if he played his heart out on the zither.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

如果说《满江红》是大声呐喊，这首《小重山》就是深夜里的低声叹息。上阕：秋夜，蟋蟀叫个不停，把作者从千里之外的梦中惊醒——梦里也许正在北方作战，也许回到了故乡。已经三更了，他一个人起来绕着台阶走；四周很安静，帘外的月亮朦朦胧胧。下阕说出心事：为了功名事业，头发都白了；故乡山上的松竹都已老去，却被战事阻住，回不去。想把满腹心事弹给瑶琴听，可是懂我的人太少，就算琴弦弹断了，又有谁听呢？这里用了伯牙和钟子期“知音”的典故：钟子期死后，伯牙摔琴断弦，不再弹琴。一般认为岳飞在抗金和朝廷主和之间受到压制，这首词写的就是这种孤独。

In English

If “The River All Red” is a battle cry, this lyric is a sigh in the middle of the night. In the first half, autumn crickets chirp without stopping and wake the speaker from a dream of somewhere a thousand miles away — perhaps the northern front, perhaps home. It is already the third watch, around midnight. He gets up and walks alone around the steps. Everyone else is asleep, and beyond the curtain the moon is hazy. The second half tells us what is on his mind. He has gone white-haired chasing a great cause; the pines and bamboos on the hills of home have grown old, and the war blocks his way back. He would like to pour his feelings into the zither, but there are few who truly understand, and if the strings snapped, who would even hear? This alludes to a famous story: the musician Bo Ya had one friend, Zhong Ziqi, who understood his music perfectly. When Zhong died, Bo Ya broke his zither and never played again. Zhī yīn (“one who knows the tone”) still means a true friend in Chinese. Readers usually connect the poem to Yue Fei’s frustration with a court that preferred peace talks to his campaigns.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yue Fei (1103–1142) was a Southern Song general who fought the Jin for most of his life. The court, led by the chief minister Qin Hui, sought peace and recalled him; he was imprisoned and executed. This lyric is traditionally dated to his last years, when his campaigns were being held back.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

小重山 chóng；寒蛩 qióng（蟋蟀）；三更 gēng；绕阶行 xíng；为功名 wèi；将 jiāng；知音少 shǎo；谁 shuí（诗词中通常读 shuí）。“人悄悄”按现代词典读 qiāo qiāo（安静无声）；部分注本读 qiǎo qiǎo。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

昨夜寒蛩不住鸣。

zuó yè hán qióng bù zhù míng

All last night the autumn crickets would not stop.

惊回千里梦，已三更。

jīng huí qiān lǐ mèng, yǐ sān gēng

They broke a dream of a place a thousand miles off. Midnight already.

起来独自绕阶行。

qǐ lái dú zì rào jiē xíng

I rise and pace the steps alone.

人悄悄，帘外月胧明。

rén qiāo qiāo, lián wài yuè lóng míng

No one stirs. Beyond the curtain, a blurred moon.

白首为功名。

bái shǒu wèi gōng míng

My hair has gone white in the service of my cause.

旧山松竹老，阻归程。

jiù shān sōng zhú lǎo, zǔ guī chéng

Pines and bamboo on the hills of home grow old, and still the road back is closed.

欲将心事付瑶琴。

yù jiāng xīn shì fù yáo qín

I would tell my heart to the zither —

知音少，弦断有谁听？

zhī yīn shǎo, xián duàn yǒu shuí tīng

but few understand the music. If the strings broke, who would hear?
