茅屋为秋风所破歌

Máo Wū Wéi Qiū Fēng Suǒ Pò Gē
Song of My Thatched Hut Torn Apart by the Autumn Wind
杜甫Dù Fǔ · Du Fu唐 Tang
体裁Form

导读About this poem

An autumn gale rips the roof off Du Fu’s hut, the village boys run off with the straw, and he spends a cold, wet, sleepless night — then ends by wishing for a great house that could shelter every poor scholar in the world, even if he alone froze to death.

第一段Part 1
01
八月秋高风怒号，卷我屋上三重茅。

直译LiteralIn the eighth month the autumn sky is high and the wind howls in anger; it rolls up the three layers of thatch on my roof.

意译MeaningEighth month, the autumn sky stands high, the wind roars in fury — it peels three layers of straw from my roof.

02
茅飞渡江洒江郊，

直译LiteralThe thatch flies across the river and scatters on the riverside fields;

意译MeaningThe straw sails over the river and rains down on the far bank;

03
高者挂罥长林梢，下者飘转沉塘坳。

直译LiteralThe high pieces hang tangled on the tops of tall trees; the low pieces whirl and sink into the hollow of the pond.

意译Meaningsome snags high in the tops of the tall trees, some spins low and sinks into the muddy pond.

第二段Part 2
04
南村群童欺我老无力，忍能对面为盗贼。

直译LiteralThe boys of the south village bully me for being old and weak; they can bear to be thieves right in front of my face.

意译MeaningThe boys from the south village know I’m old and feeble — they have the nerve to rob me to my face.

05
公然抱茅入竹去，

直译LiteralOpenly they carry the thatch in their arms and go into the bamboo;

意译MeaningBold as anything, they hug the straw and vanish into the bamboo.

06
唇焦口燥呼不得，归来倚杖自叹息。

直译LiteralMy lips parched and mouth dry, shouting is no use; I come back, lean on my stick and sigh to myself.

意译MeaningI shout till my lips crack and my mouth is dry, but it’s no good; I come home, lean on my stick, and sigh.

第三段Part 3
07
俄顷风定云墨色，秋天漠漠向昏黑。

直译LiteralIn a moment the wind stops and the clouds turn ink-black; the autumn sky, vast and dim, moves toward darkness.

意译MeaningSoon the wind drops; the clouds go black as ink, and the wide autumn sky sinks slowly into night.

08
布衾多年冷似铁，娇儿恶卧踏里裂。

直译LiteralThe cloth quilt, many years old, is cold as iron; my dear child sleeps badly and kicks the lining into tears.

意译MeaningOur old cotton quilt is cold and stiff as iron, and my little one, a restless sleeper, has kicked the lining through.

09
床头屋漏无干处，雨脚如麻未断绝。

直译LiteralBy the bed the roof leaks, and there is no dry spot; the rain’s feet, thick as hemp threads, never break off.

意译MeaningThe roof leaks over the bed — not one dry patch — and the rain keeps coming down in thick, unbroken threads.

10
自经丧乱少睡眠，长夜沾湿何由彻！

直译LiteralSince going through the turmoil of war I have slept little; this long night, soaked through — how can I last until it ends?

意译MeaningSince the war and its ruin I have hardly slept; wet through, how will I ever get to the end of this long night?

第四段Part 4
11
安得广厦千万间，

直译LiteralHow could I get spacious mansions, thousands upon thousands of rooms,

意译MeaningIf only I could raise great halls with ten thousand rooms

12
大庇天下寒士俱欢颜，风雨不动安如山。

直译Literalto greatly shelter the poor scholars of the world so that all wear happy faces — unmoved by wind and rain, steady as a mountain!

意译Meaningto shelter every poor scholar under heaven and see them all smile — standing through wind and rain, firm as a mountain!

13
呜呼！何时眼前突兀见此屋，

直译LiteralAlas! When will such a house suddenly rise up before my eyes?

意译MeaningAh — when will I see that house rise tall in front of me?

14
吾庐独破受冻死亦足！

直译LiteralThen if my hut alone were broken and I froze to death, it would still be enough!

意译MeaningThen let my own hut lie in ruins and let me freeze to death — I would be content.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写的是杜甫在成都草堂的一场遭遇。第一段写八月狂风卷走屋顶的茅草，茅草飞过江，挂在树梢，沉进水塘。第二段写村里的孩子欺负他年老无力，当面抱走茅草，他喊得嘴唇干裂也没用，只能拄着拐杖叹气。第三段写夜里下雨：被子又旧又冷，硬得像铁，屋子到处漏雨，他想起战乱以来一直睡不好，这一夜更不知怎么熬到天亮。前三段都是很具体、很穷苦的生活细节，读来像一个老人在讲自己的倒霉事。最后一段突然一转：他希望有千万间又大又结实的房子，让天下所有穷苦的读书人都住得安稳、开心；只要能看到这样的房子，自己的破屋被冻死也心甘情愿。从“我”的苦想到“天下人”的苦，这就是杜甫被称为“诗圣”的原因。诗的句子有长有短，最后几句越写越长，感情也越来越激动。

In English

Du Fu wrote this at his thatched cottage in Chengdu, and it reads like an old man telling you about a very bad day. In the first part an autumn gale strips the straw from his roof: some flies across the river, some catches in the treetops, some sinks into a pond. In the second, the village boys take advantage of his age and carry the straw off right in front of him; he shouts until his lips crack, then leans on his stick and sighs. The third part is the night: the quilt is old and hard as iron, his child has kicked holes in it, rain leaks everywhere, and he wonders how he will ever last till dawn — he has hardly slept since the war began. All of this is small, concrete and poor. Then the poem turns. He wishes for thousands of huge, solid rooms to shelter every poor scholar under heaven, steady as a mountain in wind and rain — and says that if he could only see such a house, he would be content to freeze to death in his own broken hut. Moving from his own suffering to everyone’s is why Du Fu is called the “Sage of Poetry”. Notice the lines: they are uneven, and the last ones grow longer and longer as his feeling rises.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) lived through the An Lushan Rebellion, which tore the Tang dynasty apart. After years as a refugee he built a thatched cottage outside Chengdu with friends’ help; this poem is traditionally dated to the autumn of 761, when a storm damaged it.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

多音字：为 wéi（“为……所……”表示被动）；号 háo（呼啸）；三重 chóng（三层）；长林、长夜 cháng；少睡眠 shǎo；丧乱 sāng；恶卧 è（睡相不好）。生僻字：罥 juàn、坳 ào、衾 qīn、庇 bì、俄顷 qǐng、突兀 wù。“突兀见此屋”的“见”按统编教材注 xiàn（同“现”，出现）；也有人读 jiàn。机器朗读容易读错的几处已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

版本说明 About the text

采用统编教材（八年级下册）文本。异文：“茅飞渡江”维基文库本作“度江”（古代“度”“渡”通用），网站从教材作“渡”；“沉塘坳”文库本作“沈”（“沉”的古写）。网站按句读分行，长句单独成行，共四段。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

八月秋高风怒号，卷我屋上三重茅。

bā yuè qiū gāo fēng nù háo, juǎn wǒ wū shàng sān chóng máo

Eighth month, the autumn sky stands high, the wind roars in fury — it peels three layers of straw from my roof.

茅飞渡江洒江郊，

máo fēi dù jiāng sǎ jiāng jiāo

The straw sails over the river and rains down on the far bank;

高者挂罥长林梢，下者飘转沉塘坳。

gāo zhě guà juàn cháng lín shāo, xià zhě piāo zhuǎn chén táng ào

some snags high in the tops of the tall trees, some spins low and sinks into the muddy pond.

南村群童欺我老无力，忍能对面为盗贼。

nán cūn qún tóng qī wǒ lǎo wú lì, rěn néng duì miàn wéi dào zéi

The boys from the south village know I’m old and feeble — they have the nerve to rob me to my face.

公然抱茅入竹去，

gōng rán bào máo rù zhú qù

Bold as anything, they hug the straw and vanish into the bamboo.

唇焦口燥呼不得，归来倚杖自叹息。

chún jiāo kǒu zào hū bù dé, guī lái yǐ zhàng zì tàn xī

I shout till my lips crack and my mouth is dry, but it’s no good; I come home, lean on my stick, and sigh.

俄顷风定云墨色，秋天漠漠向昏黑。

é qǐng fēng dìng yún mò sè, qiū tiān mò mò xiàng hūn hēi

Soon the wind drops; the clouds go black as ink, and the wide autumn sky sinks slowly into night.

布衾多年冷似铁，娇儿恶卧踏里裂。

bù qīn duō nián lěng sì tiě, jiāo ér è wò tà lǐ liè

Our old cotton quilt is cold and stiff as iron, and my little one, a restless sleeper, has kicked the lining through.

床头屋漏无干处，雨脚如麻未断绝。

chuáng tóu wū lòu wú gān chù, yǔ jiǎo rú má wèi duàn jué

The roof leaks over the bed — not one dry patch — and the rain keeps coming down in thick, unbroken threads.

自经丧乱少睡眠，长夜沾湿何由彻！

zì jīng sāng luàn shǎo shuì mián, cháng yè zhān shī hé yóu chè

Since the war and its ruin I have hardly slept; wet through, how will I ever get to the end of this long night?

安得广厦千万间，

ān dé guǎng shà qiān wàn jiān

If only I could raise great halls with ten thousand rooms

大庇天下寒士俱欢颜，风雨不动安如山。

dà bì tiān xià hán shì jù huān yán, fēng yǔ bù dòng ān rú shān

to shelter every poor scholar under heaven and see them all smile — standing through wind and rain, firm as a mountain!

呜呼！何时眼前突兀见此屋，

wū hū, hé shí yǎn qián tū wù xiàn cǐ wū

Ah — when will I see that house rise tall in front of me?

吾庐独破受冻死亦足！

wú lú dú pò shòu dòng sǐ yì zú

Then let my own hut lie in ruins and let me freeze to death — I would be content.
