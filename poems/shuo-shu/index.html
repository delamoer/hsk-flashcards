导读About this poem

Farmers shout at a fat rat that keeps eating their grain — and everyone knew the “rat” was the lord who taxed them. One of the oldest protest songs in the world, ending with the dream of a land where life is fair.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗表面上在骂一只大老鼠：别再吃我的黍、我的麦、我的苗了！我养了你三年，你却一点也不照顾我。其实“硕鼠”是在比喻那些只拿不干、用重税剥削农民的统治者。直接说出来太危险，就借一个人人讨厌的动物来骂，这种手法叫“比”，也就是比喻。全诗三章，每章只换几个字（黍—麦—苗，顾—德—劳），越唱越愤怒。最后农民发誓要离开，去寻找一个“乐土”——没有剥削、能过上公平日子的地方。这个“乐土”成了中国人心中理想社会的说法，今天“乐土”这个词还在用。

In English

On the surface this song is yelling at a big rat: stop eating my millet, my wheat, my young crops! I fed you for three years and you never cared about me. But the listeners all understood that the “big rat” stood for the rulers who took the farmers’ harvest in heavy taxes and gave nothing back. Saying that openly was dangerous, so the song hides its target behind an animal everyone hates. This technique is called bǐ (“comparison”) — one of the three classic methods of the Book of Songs. The three verses are almost identical, changing only a few words each time (millet, wheat, young crops), and the anger builds with every repetition. At the end the farmers swear to leave and look for a lètǔ, a “happy land” where people are treated fairly. That word stuck: Chinese still calls a paradise or an ideal place 乐土 today. It is a rare voice from ordinary people nearly 3,000 years ago, and it still sounds fresh.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC; most authors are unknown. This one comes from the state of Wei (魏, in today’s southern Shanxi), a small, poor state whose folk songs often complain about greedy rulers.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

女：通“汝”，读 rǔ（“三岁贯女”“逝将去女”），意思是“你”。乐土、乐国、乐郊的“乐”读 lè。爰 yuán。永号：号 háo，大声哭喊，不读 hào。谁：古诗文习惯读 shuí。逝：通“誓”，读 shì。“女”“号”机器朗读易错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

硕鼠硕鼠，无食我黍！

shuò shǔ shuò shǔ, wú shí wǒ shǔ

Big rat, big rat — keep your teeth off my millet!

三岁贯女，莫我肯顾。

sān suì guàn rǔ, mò wǒ kěn gù

Three years I have fed you, and you never once cared for me.

逝将去女，适彼乐土。

shì jiāng qù rǔ, shì bǐ lè tǔ

I swear I’ll leave you now for a happier land.

乐土乐土，爰得我所。

lè tǔ lè tǔ, yuán dé wǒ suǒ

A happy land, a happy land — there I’ll find a place to live.

硕鼠硕鼠，无食我麦！

shuò shǔ shuò shǔ, wú shí wǒ mài

Big rat, big rat — keep your teeth off my wheat!

三岁贯女，莫我肯德。

sān suì guàn rǔ, mò wǒ kěn dé

Three years I have fed you, and you never showed me any kindness.

逝将去女，适彼乐国。

shì jiāng qù rǔ, shì bǐ lè guó

I swear I’ll leave you now for a happier country.

乐国乐国，爰得我直。

lè guó lè guó, yuán dé wǒ zhí

A happy country, a happy country — there I’ll be treated fairly.

硕鼠硕鼠，无食我苗！

shuò shǔ shuò shǔ, wú shí wǒ miáo

Big rat, big rat — keep your teeth off my young crops!

三岁贯女，莫我肯劳。

sān suì guàn rǔ, mò wǒ kěn láo

Three years I have fed you, and you never thanked me for my work.

逝将去女，适彼乐郊。

shì jiāng qù rǔ, shì bǐ lè jiāo

I swear I’ll leave you now for happier fields.

乐郊乐郊，谁之永号？

lè jiāo lè jiāo, shuí zhī yǒng háo

Happy fields, happy fields — who would still be wailing there?
