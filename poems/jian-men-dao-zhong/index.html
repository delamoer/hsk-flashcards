导读About this poem

Dust and wine stains on his coat, a light drizzle, a donkey plodding through a mountain pass: Lu You wanted to be a soldier, and asks with a wry smile whether he is fated to be “just a poet”.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是陆游四十八岁时写的。他本来在南郑（今陕西汉中）前线军中工作，离抗金战场很近，后来被调到后方的成都，心里很失落。前两句写路上的样子：衣服上沾满旅途的灰尘，还夹杂着喝酒留下的痕迹；一路远行，没有一处不让人黯然神伤（“消魂”）。后两句是名句：我这个人，难道就只该做一个诗人吗？在细雨中骑着毛驴进了剑门关。古代很多诗人（如李白、杜甫、贾岛）都有骑驴吟诗的故事，“骑驴”几乎成了诗人的标志。陆游一生最想做的是上战场收复失地，而不是只写诗；所以这一问里有自嘲，也有无奈。细雨、毛驴、雄关，画面很美，心情却很复杂。

In English

Lu You wrote this at forty-eight. He had been serving at the frontier in Nanzheng (today’s Hanzhong, Shaanxi), close to the front against the Jin, and was then transferred to Chengdu, far behind the lines — a bitter disappointment. The first two lines describe him on the road: his coat covered in travel dust mixed with wine stains, every stage of the long journey leaving him downcast. The last two lines are famous: “Am I really meant to be a poet, or not?” — and he rides a donkey through Sword Gate Pass in the fine rain. Many great poets of the past, such as Li Bai, Du Fu and Jia Dao, are pictured riding donkeys and composing verses, so the donkey had become almost a badge of the poet. But what Lu You wanted most was to fight and win back the north, not just to write. His question is half a joke at his own expense and half a sigh. The picture — drizzle, a donkey, a mighty mountain pass — is beautiful; the feeling behind it is complicated.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) wrote this in the winter of 1172, travelling from the northwestern front at Nanzheng to a desk post in Chengdu. Sword Gate (Jianmen) is a famous mountain pass on the old road into Sichuan.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

衣上征尘杂酒痕，

yī shàng zhēng chén zá jiǔ hén

Road dust on my coat, mixed with stains of wine;

远游无处不消魂。

yuǎn yóu wú chù bù xiāo hún

on this long journey every mile weighs on my heart.

此身合是诗人未？

cǐ shēn hé shì shī rén wèi

Am I fated to be nothing more than a poet?

细雨骑驴入剑门。

xì yǔ qí lǘ rù jiàn mén

In a fine drizzle, on a donkey, I ride into Sword Gate Pass.
