导读About this poem

Two lovers who can rarely meet, and parting is harder still. Li Shangyin’s “Untitled” gives Chinese its most famous image of devotion: the silkworm spins until it dies, the candle weeps until it burns to ash.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

李商隐写了很多题目叫“无题”的诗，因为内容是不方便明说的爱情。这首写一对恋人难得见面又不得不分开。第一句有两个“难”：见面难，分别更难。第二句写暮春景色：东风没有力气了，百花凋零，正像离别时无力的心情。三、四句是全诗最有名的比喻：春蚕到死才不再吐丝，蜡烛烧成灰才不再流“泪”（烛油）。“丝”和“思”同音，所以春蚕吐丝就是不停地思念。这两句写出至死不变的爱，今天也常用来赞美老师无私奉献。五、六句想象对方：早上照镜子，担心乌黑的头发变白；晚上吟诗，应该会觉得月光很冷。最后两句：对方住的地方像仙山蓬莱一样遥远，只能请青鸟（传说中的信使）殷勤地替我去看看她。全诗缠绵深情，是李商隐的代表作。

In English

Li Shangyin wrote many poems called simply “Untitled”, because they deal with love that could not be spoken of openly. Here two lovers can rarely meet, and parting is even harder — two 难 (“hard”) in the very first line. The spring scene echoes the feeling: the east wind has lost its strength and the flowers are dying. Then the famous couplet: “The spring silkworm spins its silk until it dies; the candle’s tears dry only when it turns to ash.” In Chinese, 丝 (silk) sounds like 思 (longing), so the silkworm spinning to its death is love that never stops; the candle’s dripping wax is tears. Today these lines are also used to honour teachers who give everything for their students. The next couplet imagines the beloved: in the morning she worries in the mirror that her dark hair is changing; reciting poems at night, she must feel how cold the moonlight is. The last couplet says she is as far away as Penglai, the island of immortals, so the poet can only ask the bluebird — the messenger of legend — to go and look in on her for him.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Shangyin (c. 813–858) was caught for most of his career between two rival court factions, and never rose high in office. His “Untitled” poems have been read as love poems, as political allegories, or both; who this one was written for is unknown.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

相见时难别亦难，

xiāng jiàn shí nán bié yì nán

Hard it is to meet, and harder still to part;

东风无力百花残。

dōng fēng wú lì bǎi huā cán

the east wind has gone weak, and the flowers are dying.

春蚕到死丝方尽，

chūn cán dào sǐ sī fāng jìn

The silkworm spins its silk until it dies;

蜡炬成灰泪始干。

là jù chéng huī lèi shǐ gān

the candle weeps until it burns to ash.

晓镜但愁云鬓改，

xiǎo jìng dàn chóu yún bìn gǎi

At her morning mirror she fears her dark hair turning;

夜吟应觉月光寒。

yè yín yīng jué yuè guāng hán

chanting poems at night, she must find the moonlight cold.

蓬山此去无多路，

péng shān cǐ qù wú duō lù

Her fairy mountain is not so far from here —

青鸟殷勤为探看。

qīng niǎo yīn qín wèi tàn kān

bluebird, be kind, and go look in on her for me.
