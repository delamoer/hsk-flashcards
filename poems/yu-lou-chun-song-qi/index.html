导读About this poem

A spring outing on the water: rippling waves, misty willows, apricot blossoms so thick they seem noisy — and a toast to the setting sun, begging it to stay a little longer.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写春天出游。上阕写景：城东的风光一天比一天好，水面上泛起细细的波纹，像在欢迎游船；远处绿柳像笼着一层烟，清晨只有一点点凉意；近处红杏开满枝头，春意“闹”得正欢。这个“闹”字最有名：花本来不会发出声音，词人却用一个表示声音的字，把杏花开得热热闹闹、蜂飞蝶舞的样子全写出来了——把看到的写成听到的，这种手法叫“通感”。宋祁也因此被人称为“红杏尚书”。下阕抒情：人生在世，总是苦恼欢乐太少，谁肯为了千金而轻视一次开心的笑呢？所以要为朋友举杯劝夕阳，请它在花丛间多留一会儿。表面是赏春，里面是珍惜时光、及时行乐的人生感慨。

In English

This lyric is about a spring outing. The first stanza paints the scene: the east side of the city grows lovelier by the day, fine ripples on the water seem to welcome the boats, the willows in the distance look wrapped in mist, the morning chill is only slight — and on the branches of the red apricot trees “spring is noisy”. That word 闹 (“noisy, bustling”) is what made the poem famous. Flowers make no sound, yet one noisy word lets you feel the crowd of blossoms and the buzzing of bees. Describing something seen with a word for something heard is called tōnggǎn (“synaesthesia”). It earned Song Qi the nickname “the Red Apricot Minister”. The second stanza turns to feeling: life is short and happy moments are few, so who would value a fortune more than a good laugh? He raises his cup to the setting sun on his friends’ behalf and asks it to linger among the flowers. A spring scene becomes a reminder to treasure the moment.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Song Qi (998–1061) was a Northern Song scholar-official who helped compile the New History of the Tang. This lyric made him famous; people called him the “Red Apricot Minister” after its best-known line.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

縠 hú（有皱纹的纱）；棹 zhào（船桨，代指船）；长恨 cháng hèn；为君 wèi jūn；斜阳 xié yáng（旧读 xiá，今按 xié）。机器朗读“縠、棹、长”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列换成同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

东城渐觉风光好，

dōng chéng jiàn jué fēng guāng hǎo

Day by day the east of the city grows lovelier;

縠皱波纹迎客棹。

hú zhòu bō wén yíng kè zhào

ripples fine as crinkled silk come to meet our boat.

绿杨烟外晓寒轻，

lǜ yáng yān wài xiǎo hán qīng

Past the green haze of the willows, the morning chill is faint;

红杏枝头春意闹。

hóng xìng zhī tóu chūn yì nào

on the red apricot boughs, spring is in uproar.

浮生长恨欢娱少，

fú shēng cháng hèn huān yú shǎo

All through this drifting life we grieve that joy is rare —

肯爱千金轻一笑。

kěn ài qiān jīn qīng yī xiào

so why prize a fortune over one good laugh?

为君持酒劝斜阳，

wèi jūn chí jiǔ quàn xié yáng

For your sake I raise my cup to the setting sun:

且向花间留晚照。

qiě xiàng huā jiān liú wǎn zhào

stay, and leave your evening glow among the flowers a little longer.
