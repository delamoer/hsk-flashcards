导读About this poem

A village woman who raises silkworms goes into the city and comes home in tears: everyone there is dressed in silk, and not one of them has ever raised a silkworm.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一个养蚕的农村妇女。第一句：昨天她进城去了（可能是去卖蚕丝）。第二句：回来以后，眼泪湿透了手巾。她为什么哭？后两句给出答案：城里那些浑身穿着绫罗绸缎的人，没有一个是养蚕的。养蚕非常辛苦，要日夜喂桑叶、照顾蚕宝宝，可是辛辛苦苦养蚕的人自己穿不上丝绸，穿丝绸的人却从来不劳动。诗人没有发议论，只是让我们看见她的眼泪，再把“遍身罗绮者”和“养蚕人”放在一起对比，不公平就一目了然。这首诗和罗隐的《蜂》一样，都是替劳动者说话的诗，语言浅白，力量却很大。

In English

The poem is about a country woman who raises silkworms. Line one: yesterday she went into the city — perhaps to sell her silk. Line two: when she came back, her handkerchief was soaked with tears. Why is she crying? The last two lines answer: the people there, covered from head to toe in fine silk, are not the people who raise silkworms. Raising silkworms is exhausting work — feeding them mulberry leaves day and night, keeping them warm and clean — yet the woman who does it can’t afford to wear silk, while those who wear it have never done a day of that work. The poet makes no speech about injustice. He simply shows us her tears, then puts “those dressed all in silk” next to “those who raise silkworms”, and the unfairness is plain to see. Like Luo Yin’s “Bees”, it is a poem that speaks up for working people, in very simple words that hit hard.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zhang Yu was an 11th-century Northern Song scholar from Sichuan. He is said to have failed the exams several times and turned down official posts, living as a recluse on Mount Qingcheng. Little else is known about his life; this short poem is the reason he is remembered.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

昨日入城市，

zuó rì rù chéng shì

Yesterday I went into town;

归来泪满巾。

guī lái lèi mǎn jīn

I came home with my kerchief wet with tears.

遍身罗绮者，

biàn shēn luó qǐ zhě

All those people dressed in silk —

不是养蚕人。

bù shì yǎng cán rén

not one of them has ever raised a silkworm.
