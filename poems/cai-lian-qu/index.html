导读About this poem

Girls picking lotus blend so well into the pond — green skirts like leaves, faces like flowers — that you can only tell they are there when you hear them sing.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写江南水乡的姑娘们划船采莲。第一句说姑娘的绿色罗裙和荷叶好像是用同一块布裁出来的，颜色一样；第二句说荷花（芙蓉）向着她们的脸开放，花和脸一样红润好看。人和荷花融成了一体，分不清哪是花、哪是人。第三句写她们的小船划进密密的荷塘，一下子就看不见了；第四句却说：听到歌声，才知道有人来了。诗人没有正面描写姑娘长什么样，而是用“人花难分”来写她们的美，这是一种很巧的侧面描写；最后用声音点出人物，画面里一下子有了动感和欢乐。全诗明快活泼，像一幅会唱歌的画。

In English

This poem shows girls in the water country south of the Yangtze rowing out to pick lotus. Line one says their green silk skirts and the lotus leaves look as if they were cut from the same cloth; line two says the lotus flowers open toward their faces, flowers and faces equally rosy. Girls and flowers merge, and you can’t tell which is which. Then their little boats slip into the thick of the pond and disappear completely — and only when singing drifts out do you realise someone is there. The poet never describes the girls directly. Instead he shows their beauty by making them impossible to tell apart from the lotus, a clever indirect approach; and by bringing them back through sound at the end, he fills the still picture with movement and fun. Lotus picking was a real summer job in the south, but in the poem it feels like a game of hide-and-seek. Bright and playful, it is like a painting that suddenly starts to sing.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Changling (c. 698–757) was a leading High Tang poet. “Lotus-Picking Song” is an old yuefu song title going back to the Southern Dynasties; Wang wrote two poems under it, of which this is the second and better known.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

荷叶罗裙一色裁，

hé yè luó qún yī sè cái

Lotus leaves and silk skirts, cut from the same green,

芙蓉向脸两边开。

fú róng xiàng liǎn liǎng biān kāi

lotus blooms open beside faces just as pink.

乱入池中看不见，

luàn rù chí zhōng kàn bù jiàn

They melt into the pond and are lost from sight —

闻歌始觉有人来。

wén gē shǐ jué yǒu rén lái

only a song tells you someone is there.
