Moonlit Night

导读About this poem

Held captive in war-torn Chang'an, Du Fu doesn't write about himself missing his wife — he imagines her, far away, looking at the same moon and missing him. One of the tenderest love poems in Chinese.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是杜甫写给妻子的诗。安史之乱时，他把家人安顿在鄜州，自己去投奔新皇帝，路上被叛军抓到长安。一个月夜，他想念妻子，却不直接写“我想你”，而是想象妻子在想他。第一、二句：今晚鄜州的月亮，妻子只能一个人在房里看了。第三、四句：可怜那些年幼的儿女，还不懂得思念远在长安的爸爸——孩子不懂，更显得妻子一个人的孤单。第五、六句想象妻子望月的样子：夜雾打湿了她的头发，月光照着她的手臂，让她觉得冷。她站得那么久，可见想得多深。最后两句是盼望：什么时候我们才能一起靠在窗帘边，让月光照干两人脸上的泪痕？前面是“独看”，结尾是“双照”，从一个人到两个人，盼团聚的心情就在这对比里。这种“从对方写起”的写法，中国诗歌里常用，读起来格外深情。

In English

Du Fu wrote this for his wife. During the An Lushan Rebellion he left his family in Fuzhou (in today's Shaanxi) and set out to join the new emperor, but was captured by rebels and taken to Chang'an. On a moonlit night there he misses her — yet instead of saying “I miss you”, he imagines her missing him. Tonight, the moon over Fuzhou: she will be watching it alone in her room. The little children, poor things, are too young to understand that they should be missing their father in Chang'an — which leaves her even more alone. Then he pictures her at the window: the scented night mist dampening her hair, the cold clear moonlight on her arms. She has been standing there a long time. The last couplet is a wish: when will we lean together by the thin curtain, and let the moon shine on us both until our tears are dry? The poem begins with her watching “alone” and ends with the moon shining on the “two” of them. Writing from the loved one's side, rather than one's own, is a classic device in Chinese poetry, and here it is deeply moving.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this in autumn 756, while held in rebel-occupied Chang'an. His wife, née Yang, and their children were sheltering in Fuzhou (鄜州, today's Fu County, Shaanxi), some 150 miles to the north.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

今夜鄜州月，

jīn yè fū zhōu yuè

Tonight the moon over Fuzhou

闺中只独看。

guī zhōng zhǐ dú kān

she will watch alone from her room.

遥怜小儿女，

yáo lián xiǎo ér nǚ

From far away I pity our little ones,

未解忆长安。

wèi jiě yì cháng ān

too young to know they should miss their father in Chang'an.

香雾云鬟湿，

xiāng wù yún huán shī

The scented mist must dampen her coiled hair;

清辉玉臂寒。

qīng huī yù bì hán

the clear moonlight chills her pale arms.

何时倚虚幌，

hé shí yǐ xū huǎng

When will we lean together by the sheer curtain,

双照泪痕干。

shuāng zhào lèi hén gān

and let the moon on both our faces dry the tears?
