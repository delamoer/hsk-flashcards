导读About this poem

Beacon fires light up the capital, and a scholar decides he would rather lead a hundred soldiers than sit with his books. Eight fast, loud lines full of drums, snow and banners.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《从军行》是乐府旧题，专写军队和战争。杨炯是个读书人，这首诗写一位书生听说边境告急、决心从军的心情。第一联：报警的烽火照到了长安（“西京”），书生心里再也平静不下来。第二联：将军拿着调兵的牙璋离开皇宫，精锐的骑兵已经包围了敌人的龙城。第三联写战场：大雪纷飞，天色昏暗，军旗上的图画都褪了色；狂风呼啸，夹杂着阵阵鼓声。这一联不写人，只写雪、旗、风、鼓，战斗的激烈却全出来了。最后一联是全诗的中心：宁可当一个统领百人的小军官，也比做一个只会读书的书生强。诗的节奏很快，从后方写到出征，再写到战场，一句一个镜头。最后两句说出了唐代很多读书人想到边疆建功立业的豪情。

In English

“Joining the Army” (从军行) is an old ballad title used for poems about soldiers and war. Yang Jiong was himself a scholar, and here he imagines a scholar who hears the border is in danger and decides to enlist. Couplet one: the beacon fires — the ancient alarm system — shine all the way to the capital, Chang’an (西京, the “Western Capital”), and the young man’s heart can no longer stay calm. Couplet two: the general leaves the palace carrying the 牙璋, a jade token that authorised moving troops, and the armoured cavalry is already surrounding the enemy’s stronghold, Dragon City. Couplet three is the battlefield, painted without a single person in it: snow darkens the sky until the pictures on the banners fade; the wind howls, mixed with the beat of war drums. Snow, flags, wind and drums are enough to make you feel the fighting. The last couplet is the heart of the poem: “I’d rather be a captain of a hundred men than remain a scholar.” The poem moves fast — capital, departure, battle, decision — like a series of film shots. Its final line sums up the dream of many Tang scholars: to win glory on the frontier instead of growing old over books.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yang Jiong (650–c. 693) was a child prodigy and one of the “Four Paragons of the Early Tang”. He spent most of his life in minor court posts, never at the front; the poem is thought to reflect the frontier wars against the Tibetans and Turks in the 670s–680s.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

铁骑 的“骑”传统读 jì（一人一马），2016 年《审音表》统读 qí，网站暂采用 jì。宁为 的“宁”读 nìng（宁可），“为”读 wéi（做）；百夫长 的“长”读 zhǎng；杂 zá。机器朗读这几处容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

烽火照西京，

fēng huǒ zhào xī jīng

Beacon fires blaze as far as the capital,

心中自不平。

xīn zhōng zì bù píng

and my heart can find no rest.

牙璋辞凤阙，

yá zhāng cí fèng què

The general, jade token in hand, leaves the palace gates;

铁骑绕龙城。

tiě jì rào lóng chéng

armoured horsemen ring the enemy’s Dragon City.

雪暗凋旗画，

xuě àn diāo qí huà

Snow darkens the sky till the banners lose their colours;

风多杂鼓声。

fēng duō zá gǔ shēng

the wind howls, tangled with the beat of drums.

宁为百夫长，

nìng wéi bǎi fū zhǎng

Better to lead a hundred soldiers

胜作一书生。

shèng zuò yī shū shēng

than live my life as a bookish scholar.
