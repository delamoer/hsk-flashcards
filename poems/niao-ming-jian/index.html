导读About this poem

A night so still you can hear osmanthus petals fall, until the rising moon startles a bird into song. Wang Wei uses a few small sounds to make silence audible.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写春天夜里山谷的寂静。第一句“人闲桂花落”：人心闲下来，才能注意到细小的桂花一朵朵落下。第二句“夜静春山空”，整座山好像空了。后两句很妙：月亮升起来，月光竟然惊醒了山里的鸟，它们不时在山涧里叫几声。鸟叫本来是声音，为什么反而让人觉得更静？因为只有在非常安静的环境里，月光这样轻的变化才会惊动鸟儿，几声鸟鸣也才听得这么清楚。这种手法叫“以动衬静”——用声音和动作来衬托安静。王维信佛，这首诗里的“闲”和“空”，也带着他内心的平和。

In English

A spring night in a mountain ravine. The first line links person and scene: only when your mind is at rest (闲) can you notice osmanthus flowers — tiny ones — dropping one by one. The second line widens the view: the night is still, and the whole spring mountain feels empty. The last two lines are the surprise. The moon rises, and its light is enough to startle the sleeping birds, which call out now and then in the ravine. Why does birdsong make the night feel quieter, not noisier? Because only in deep silence could something as soft as moonlight wake a bird, and only in silence would a few calls carry so clearly. Chinese critics call this “using movement to set off stillness” (以动衬静): a small sound makes you notice the quiet around it, like one ripple on a still pond. Wang Wei was a devout Buddhist, and the words 闲 (at ease) and 空 (empty) also suggest the calm of his own mind.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761), poet, painter and Buddhist. This is the first of five short poems he wrote on scenes at Yunxi (“Cloud Stream”), the country home of his friend Huangfu Yue.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

人闲桂花落，

rén xián guì huā luò

My mind at rest, I hear the osmanthus fall;

夜静春山空。

yè jìng chūn shān kōng

the night is still, the spring hills empty.

月出惊山鸟，

yuè chū jīng shān niǎo

The rising moon startles the mountain birds,

时鸣春涧中。

shí míng chūn jiàn zhōng

and now and then they call across the spring ravine.
