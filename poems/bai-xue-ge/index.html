Bái Xuě Gē Sòng Wǔ Pànguān Guī Jīng
Song of White Snow: Seeing Off Secretary Wu on His Return to the Capital

导读About this poem

Snow arrives in the eighth month on the far western frontier — so suddenly it looks like pear trees blooming overnight. A farewell feast in the freezing cold ends with hoofprints in the snow.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写边塞八月的大雪和一场雪中送别。开头四句写雪来得又早又猛：北风卷地，白草都被吹断，八月就飞起大雪。接着诗人用了一个非常新奇的比喻：一夜之间，千万棵树上挂满白雪，好像春风吹来，梨花全开了——把冰冷的雪写得像春天一样美，这是全诗最有名的两句。中间几句写寒冷：雪花飘进帘子打湿了帐幕，狐皮袍子不暖、锦缎被子嫌薄，将军的弓冻得拉不开，铁甲冷得穿不上；大沙漠里结着百丈厚冰，愁云凝在万里天空。然后写饯别酒宴上胡琴、琵琶、羌笛一齐演奏，傍晚大雪中，红旗冻得被风吹也飘不动。最后四句写送别：在轮台东门送你走，大雪盖满天山的路；山路一转就看不见你了，只有雪地上留下马蹄印。结尾不写眼泪，只写一行马蹄印，依依不舍的心情却留在读者心里。全诗想象奇特、色彩鲜明，是边塞诗中的名篇。

In English

This poem describes a heavy snowfall on the frontier in the eighth lunar month and a farewell held in the snow. The first four lines show how early and fierce the snow is: the north wind sweeps the ground and snaps the pale grass, and snow is already flying in the eighth month. Then comes a startling comparison — overnight, thousands of trees are covered in white, as if a spring wind had blown and every pear tree had burst into blossom. Turning icy snow into a spring scene is what made these lines so famous. The middle section piles up the cold: snowflakes drift through bead curtains and soak the tent hangings, fox-fur coats aren’t warm, brocade quilts feel thin, the general’s bow is too stiff to draw, iron armour is too cold to put on; the desert is locked in ice a hundred feet thick and gloomy clouds hang frozen across the sky. Next comes the farewell feast, with Central Asian fiddles, pipas and Qiang flutes playing, and at dusk, in the snow, a red flag frozen so stiff the wind can’t move it. In the last four lines the poet sees his friend off at the east gate of Luntai; snow covers the road to the Tian Shan, the path bends round a mountain and his friend is gone — only hoofprints are left in the snow. The poem ends not with tears but with those hoofprints, and the feeling of loss lingers.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Cen Shen (c. 715–770) was the great poet of the Tang’s far western frontier, where he served twice as a military secretary. He wrote this around 754 at Luntai (in today’s Xinjiang), seeing off a colleague named Wu who was going back to the capital, Chang’an.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

北风卷地白草折，

běi fēng juǎn dì bái cǎo zhé

The north wind sweeps the earth and snaps the pale grass;

胡天八月即飞雪。

hú tiān bā yuè jí fēi xuě

out here, by the eighth month, snow is already flying.

忽如一夜春风来，

hū rú yī yè chūn fēng lái

As if overnight a spring wind had come,

千树万树梨花开。

qiān shù wàn shù lí huā kāi

and on thousands of trees the pear blossoms opened.

散入珠帘湿罗幕，

sàn rù zhū lián shī luó mù

Flakes drift through bead curtains and dampen the silk drapes;

狐裘不暖锦衾薄。

hú qiú bù nuǎn jǐn qīn bó

fox furs give no warmth, brocade quilts feel thin.

将军角弓不得控，

jiāng jūn jiǎo gōng bù dé kòng

The general’s bow is too stiff to draw,

都护铁衣冷难着。

dū hù tiě yī lěng nán zhuó

the commander’s armour too cold to wear.

瀚海阑干百丈冰，

hàn hǎi lán gān bǎi zhàng bīng

Across the great desert, ice lies a hundred feet deep;

愁云惨淡万里凝。

chóu yún cǎn dàn wàn lǐ níng

dark, sorrowful clouds hang frozen for ten thousand miles.

中军置酒饮归客，

zhōng jūn zhì jiǔ yǐn guī kè

In the commander’s tent we pour wine for the one going home,

胡琴琵琶与羌笛。

hú qín pí pá yǔ qiāng dí

with fiddles, pipas and Qiang flutes playing.

纷纷暮雪下辕门，

fēn fēn mù xuě xià yuán mén

Evening snow falls thick on the camp gate;

风掣红旗冻不翻。

fēng chè hóng qí dòng bù fān

the wind tugs at the red flag, frozen too stiff to fly.

轮台东门送君去，

lún tái dōng mén sòng jūn qù

At the east gate of Luntai I see you off;

去时雪满天山路。

qù shí xuě mǎn tiān shān lù

as you leave, snow fills the road to the Tian Shan.

山回路转不见君，

shān huí lù zhuǎn bù jiàn jūn

The road bends round the mountain, and you are gone;

雪上空留马行处。

xuě shàng kōng liú mǎ xíng chù

only your horse’s tracks are left in the snow.
