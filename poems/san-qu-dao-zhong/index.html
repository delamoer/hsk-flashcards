导读About this poem

A cheerful travel snapshot from early summer: sunny days, a boat trip up a stream, then a walk through green mountain shade — where the only new thing is a few orioles singing.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首轻松愉快的纪行诗，写诗人在三衢山（今浙江衢州）路上的见闻。江南的“梅子黄时”本来应该是阴雨连绵的梅雨季，可是这几天“日日晴”，心情自然很好。他先坐小船沿着小溪走到尽头，再改走山路。山路两边绿树成荫，和来的时候一样浓密，没有减少；不同的是，多了几声黄鹂的叫声。“不减”和“添得”对比，写出初夏比春天更浓的绿意和生机。全诗像一篇小游记：时间、天气、路线、景色、声音都有，语言简单，读起来让人觉得清新明快。

In English

This is a light, happy travel poem about a journey near Mount Sanqu (today’s Quzhou in Zhejiang). In the south, “when the plums turn yellow” is normally the rainy season, with gloomy drizzle for weeks — yet this year it is sunny day after day, and the poet’s spirits are high. He first takes a small boat up a stream until the water runs out, then continues on foot along a mountain path. The trees along the path give as much green shade as they did when he came this way before — none of it has thinned. The only difference is that now there are a few orioles singing. The contrast between “not less” (不减) and “added” (添得) captures the early summer: just as green as spring, but even more alive. The poem reads like a tiny travel diary — time, weather, route, scenery and sound all in four lines — in simple language that leaves you feeling fresh and bright.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zeng Ji (1084–1166) was a Southern Song official and poet, remembered also as a teacher of the great poet Lu You. Sanqu refers to the area around Mount Sanqu in Quzhou, Zhejiang.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

梅子黄时日日晴，

méi zǐ huáng shí rì rì qíng

Plums turning yellow, and sunshine every day;

小溪泛尽却山行。

xiǎo xī fàn jìn què shān xíng

I row up the stream till it ends, then take the mountain path.

绿阴不减来时路，

lǜ yīn bù jiǎn lái shí lù

The green shade is as deep as when I came this way,

添得黄鹂四五声。

tiān dé huáng lí sì wǔ shēng

only now there are four or five orioles singing.
