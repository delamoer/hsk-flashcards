导读About this poem

Late spring in the countryside: a child chases a yellow butterfly as fast as he can — and it vanishes into a field of yellow rape flowers. A tiny, funny moment caught forever.

中文解析

这首诗写诗人住在新市一家姓徐的小客店时看到的晚春景色。前两句写景：稀稀落落的篱笆旁，一条小路伸向远处；树上的花已经落了，新长出的叶子还不多，没有形成树荫。这是春末夏初的样子。后两句写人：一个小孩飞快地奔跑，追一只黄色的蝴蝶；蝴蝶一下子飞进了金黄的油菜花地里，黄蝴蝶和黄菜花分不清，孩子再也找不到它了。诗人没有写孩子的表情，但我们能想象他站在花地边东张西望的样子，又着急又好玩。这种从生活里抓住一个小镜头的写法，正是杨万里诗的特点：新鲜、活泼，像随手拍下的照片。最后一句的“黄”和“黄”混在一起，是全诗最妙的地方。

In English

Yang Wanli wrote this while staying at a small inn kept by a Mr Xu in the town of Xinshi. The first two lines set the scene in late spring: a thin, gappy bamboo fence, a narrow path running deep into the distance, and trees whose blossoms have fallen but whose new leaves are still too few to give shade. It is that in-between moment when spring is ending and summer hasn’t quite begun. The last two lines bring in a child. He runs as fast as he can after a yellow butterfly — and the butterfly flies straight into a field of yellow rape flowers. Yellow on yellow: it is simply gone, and the child cannot find it anywhere. The poet doesn’t describe the boy’s face, but we can picture him standing at the edge of the field, looking this way and that, puzzled and a little cross. Catching one small, real moment like this, as if with a quick snapshot, is what Yang Wanli is famous for. The cleverest touch is the colour: the butterfly disappears because it matches the flowers exactly, so the poem ends with the whole field glowing gold.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yang Wanli (1127–1206), one of the great poets of the Southern Song, loved writing about small scenes of nature and daily life in plain, lively language. He wrote two poems at Mr Xu’s inn in Xinshi on a journey in spring; this second one is the famous one.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

篱落疏疏一径深，

lí luò shū shū yī jìng shēn

Past a gappy fence, a narrow path runs far away;

树头新绿未成阴。

shù tóu xīn lǜ wèi chéng yīn

the new leaves on the trees are too few for shade.

儿童急走追黄蝶，

ér tóng jí zǒu zhuī huáng dié

A child dashes after a yellow butterfly —

飞入菜花无处寻。

fēi rù cài huā wú chù xún

it flies into the yellow rape flowers and is nowhere to be found.
