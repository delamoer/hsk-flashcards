导读About this poem

War has reached even the quiet lakes and rivers of the south, and ordinary people can no longer gather firewood in peace. Its last line — “one general’s glory, ten thousand dried bones” — is still the sharpest thing ever said in Chinese about the cost of war.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

己亥年是唐僖宗乾符六年（879年）。当时黄巢起义军转战南方，唐朝军队和起义军在长江、淮河一带反复交战，水乡也被卷进了战争。第一句说：江南水乡的山山水水都已经画进了作战地图，意思是到处都在打仗。第二句说：老百姓连上山砍柴、割草这种最普通的生活都过不下去了，还有什么办法快乐呢？后两句是全诗的重点：请你别再跟我谈什么立功封侯的事了，一个将军的功业，是用成千上万士兵的白骨换来的。“一”和“万”、“成”和“枯”形成强烈对比，这种写法叫“对比”，让读者一下子看清战争的代价。这一句后来成为人们批评战争、批评追求个人功名时最常引用的名句。

In English

Jihai is the name of a year in the traditional sixty-year cycle; here it is 879. Huang Chao’s great rebellion was sweeping through southern China, and government troops and rebels fought back and forth across the rivers and lakes between the Yangtze and the Huai. The first line says the whole “land of marshes” — the quiet water country of the south — has been drawn onto the war maps: fighting is everywhere. The second asks how ordinary people can enjoy even the simplest life now, such as going out to cut firewood and grass. The second half is the heart of the poem. “Please don’t talk to me about winning glory and titles,” the poet says, because one general’s success is built on ten thousand withered bones. The contrasts — one against ten thousand, “success” against “withered” — are stark and simple, and they make the price of war impossible to ignore. That last line has been quoted ever since whenever people criticise war, or anyone who climbs to fame over other people’s lives.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Cao Song (c. 830–c. 903) was a poor scholar who failed the examinations for decades and is said to have finally passed the jinshi in 901, in his seventies. He wrote two poems titled “The Year Jihai”; this first one is by far the better known.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

泽国江山入战图，

zé guó jiāng shān rù zhàn tú

Even the lakes and hills of the south are on the war maps now;

生民何计乐樵苏。

shēng mín hé jì lè qiáo sū

how can common folk still go out for firewood in peace?

凭君莫话封侯事，

píng jūn mò huà fēng hóu shì

Please don’t talk to me of earning titles and glory —

一将功成万骨枯。

yī jiàng gōng chéng wàn gǔ kū

for one general’s triumph, ten thousand men lie bleached to bone.
