导读About this poem

Twenty-two characters of pure loneliness: standing on an ancient terrace, the poet can see no great men of the past behind him and none to come ahead — only the endless sky and earth, and he weeps.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

幽州台在今天的北京附近，相传就是战国时燕昭王为招揽人才修建的黄金台。陈子昂随军来到这里，自己的建议不被采纳，还被降了职，心中苦闷，登台写下这首诗。前两句说：往前看不到古代那些重用人才的贤明君主，往后也看不到将来的人。诗人站在时间的中间，前后都是空的。第三句视线一下子打开：只有天和地那么辽阔、那么长久。和无边的天地相比，一个人显得非常渺小、孤独，于是他独自伤心地流下眼泪。全诗没有写一处具体的景物，句子长短不一（五字、五字、六字、六字），像是心里的话直接喊出来，所以很有力量。诗里的孤独不只是个人的，也是一切怀才不遇、找不到知音的人共同的感受。

In English

The Youzhou Terrace stood near today’s Beijing; tradition identifies it with the “Golden Terrace” that King Zhao of Yan built some 1,000 years earlier to attract talented men. Chen Zi’ang came here with an army in 697. His advice had been ignored and he had been demoted, so he climbed the terrace with a heavy heart. “Looking back, I see none of the ancients; looking ahead, none of those to come.” The wise rulers who valued talent are long gone, and no one in the future can see him either: he stands alone in the middle of time. Then the view opens out: only heaven and earth, vast and endless. Against that immensity one person is tiny, and he weeps alone. There is no concrete scenery at all, and the lines are uneven — five, five, six, six characters — as if the feeling bursts straight out of him. That is where the poem’s force comes from. Its loneliness belongs not just to one man but to anyone whose gifts go unrecognised.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Chen Zi’ang (661–702) argued for a return to the strong, plain style of earlier poetry and is seen as a forerunner of the High Tang. He wrote this in 697 during a campaign against the Khitan, after his commander rejected his advice.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

前不见古人，

qián bù jiàn gǔ rén

Behind me, none of the great ones of old;

后不见来者。

hòu bù jiàn lái zhě

ahead, none of those still to come.

念天地之悠悠，

niàn tiān dì zhī yōu yōu

I think of heaven and earth, endless and vast,

独怆然而涕下。

dú chuàng rán ér tì xià

and alone, in sorrow, my tears fall.
