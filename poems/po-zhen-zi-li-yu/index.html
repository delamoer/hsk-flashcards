导读About this poem

A king looks back on the day he lost everything: forty years of a dynasty, palaces touching the sky — and then surrender, a body wasting away, and court musicians playing a farewell song as he leaves his ancestral temple in tears.

四十年来家国，三千里地山河。

直译LiteralForty years of family and state; three thousand li of land, mountains and rivers.

意译MeaningForty years my family ruled this land — three thousand li of mountains and rivers.

02
凤阁龙楼连霄汉，玉树琼枝作烟萝。

直译LiteralPhoenix pavilions and dragon towers joined the Milky Way; jade trees and gem branches made a misty tangle.

意译MeaningPhoenix halls and dragon towers rose into the stars; jewel-like trees grew thick as mist and vines.

03
几曾识干戈？

直译LiteralWhen had I ever known shields and spears?

意译MeaningWhat did I ever know of war?

下阕Second stanza
04
一旦归为臣虏，沈腰潘鬓消磨。

直译LiteralOne day I became a captive subject; Shen’s waist and Pan’s temples wore away.

意译MeaningThen all at once I was a captive; my waist grew thin, my temples grey.

05

直译LiteralWorst was the day I hurriedly took leave of the temple; the court musicians still played parting songs.

意译MeaningWorst of all was the day I fled the ancestral temple, while the court band still played songs of farewell.

06

直译LiteralShedding tears, I faced the palace maids.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词是李煜回忆亡国那一天的。上阕写过去：南唐立国将近四十年，国土三千里；宫殿高得连着天上的银河，宫里的树木花草茂盛得像烟雾缭绕。在这样的环境里长大的国君，“几曾识干戈”——哪里懂得打仗呢？这一句既是回忆，也是自责。下阕突然一转：“一旦归为臣虏”，一下子就成了俘虏。“沈腰潘鬓”用了两个典故：南朝沈约因病消瘦，腰带越来越松；晋代潘岳三十多岁就有了白发。这里说自己在囚禁中一天天消瘦、变老。最痛的是离开都城那一天：慌慌张张地拜别祖庙，宫里的乐队还在演奏离别的乐曲，他只能对着宫女流泪。上阕的富丽和下阕的凄凉形成强烈对比。宋代有人批评他亡国时不对百姓而对宫女哭，这也正说明他是一个真诚却不懂治国的诗人。

In English

Here Li Yu relives the day his kingdom fell. The first half is the past: a dynasty of nearly forty years, a realm three thousand li across, palace halls so tall they seemed to reach the Milky Way, gardens so lush that the trees looked wrapped in mist. A ruler raised in such a world — “when had I ever known spears and shields?” That line is both memory and self-reproach: he never understood war. The second half turns suddenly. “One day I became a captive subject.” 沈腰潘鬓 packs in two allusions: Shen Yue, a writer who grew so thin from illness that his belt kept loosening, and Pan Yue, whose hair turned grey in his early thirties. Li Yu is saying that captivity is making him thin and old. The sharpest pain is the day he left his capital: hurrying to bid farewell to the temple of his ancestors while the court musicians were still playing a parting song, and all he could do was weep in front of the palace maids. The rich first half and the bleak second half make a striking contrast. A Song critic later scolded him for weeping to palace girls instead of to his people — which shows exactly who he was: a sincere artist, not a statesman.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Yu (937–978), last ruler of the Southern Tang, surrendered when Song armies took his capital Jinling (Nanjing) in 975. The Southern Tang had lasted from 937 — close to forty years. This lyric is traditionally said to recall the moment he was taken north.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

沈腰 shěn yāo（姓氏读 shěn，不读 chén）；潘鬓 pān bìn；教坊 jiào fāng（不读 jiāo）；干戈 gān gē；霄汉 xiāo hàn；臣虏 chén lǔ；宫娥 gōng é。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本。“几曾识干戈”一作“几曾惯干戈”；“作烟萝”一作“似烟萝”。也有学者怀疑此词非李煜所作，但通行选本均归于他。另：“消磨”一作“销磨”，“别离歌”一作“离别歌”，网站从通行本。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

四十年来家国，三千里地山河。

sì shí nián lái jiā guó, sān qiān lǐ dì shān hé

Forty years my family ruled this land — three thousand li of mountains and rivers.

凤阁龙楼连霄汉，玉树琼枝作烟萝。

fèng gé lóng lóu lián xiāo hàn, yù shù qióng zhī zuò yān luó

Phoenix halls and dragon towers rose into the stars; jewel-like trees grew thick as mist and vines.

几曾识干戈？

jǐ céng shí gān gē

What did I ever know of war?

一旦归为臣虏，沈腰潘鬓消磨。

yī dàn guī wéi chén lǔ, shěn yāo pān bìn xiāo mó

Then all at once I was a captive; my waist grew thin, my temples grey.

最是仓皇辞庙日，教坊犹奏别离歌。

zuì shì cāng huáng cí miào rì, jiào fāng yóu zòu bié lí gē

Worst of all was the day I fled the ancestral temple, while the court band still played songs of farewell.

垂泪对宫娥。

chuí lèi duì gōng é

and I wept before the palace maids.
