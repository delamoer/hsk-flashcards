月出

Yuè Chū
The Moon Comes Out
佚名（《诗经·陈风》）Anonymous (Book of Songs)先秦 Pre-Qin

导读About this poem

The oldest poem in Chinese that links the moon with a beloved: a man stands in the moonlight, thinks of a lovely woman walking slowly under it, and his heart aches. Three short verses, each ending with a sigh of “xī”.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是《诗经·陈风》里的一首月下怀人诗。三章结构完全一样：第一句写月亮升起、月光明亮，第二句写那位美人的美丽，第三句写她慢慢走路的优美姿态，第四句写自己心里的忧愁。每章只换几个字（皎、皓、照；僚、懰、燎），这叫“重章叠句”，像歌一样一遍遍唱下去。每句末尾都用“兮”字，读起来像轻轻的叹息。月光、美人、忧愁这三样东西从此连在了一起：后来的诗人一看到月亮就想念远方的人，源头常被追到这首诗。诗里很多字今天已经不用了，意思大多是“美好”“优雅”“忧愁”，不必一个个记住，读出它的音乐感就好。

In English

This short song from the state of Chen is the first poem in Chinese where moonlight, a beautiful woman and longing appear together. All three verses follow one pattern: the moon rises and shines; the woman is lovely; she walks slowly and gracefully; and the speaker’s heart is troubled. Only a few words change from verse to verse — 皎, 皓, 照 for the moonlight, 僚, 懰, 燎 for her beauty — a technique called “repeated stanzas” (重章叠句), typical of folk songs that were meant to be sung again and again. Every line ends in 兮 (xī), a sound like a soft sigh, which gives the poem its dreamy rhythm. We never learn whether the woman is real, remembered or imagined; we only see her through the moonlight and the speaker’s ache. Later poets who looked at the moon and missed someone far away were walking a path that began here. Many of the words are rare today and mostly mean “beautiful”, “graceful” or “worried” — don’t try to memorise them all. Read it aloud and enjoy the music.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one belongs to the “Airs of Chen” (陈风), songs from the small state of Chen in today’s eastern Henan; its author is unknown.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

生僻字较多：佼 jiǎo、僚 liǎo、窈纠 yǎo jiǎo（“纠”在这里读 jiǎo）、悄 qiǎo、懰 liǔ、懮受 yǒu shòu、慅 cǎo、燎 liǎo、夭绍 yāo shào。第三章“劳心惨兮”的“惨”，朱熹注“当作懆”，传统读 cǎo，与前两章押韵；网站按 cǎo 注音，也有注本直接读 cǎn。机器朗读容易读错的几处已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

She moves so slowly, so gracefully, and my heart is heavy.

yuè chū hào xī, jiǎo rén liǔ xī

The moon comes up, shining white — and she, how charming she is.

舒懮受兮，劳心慅兮。

shū yǒu shòu xī, láo xīn cǎo xī

Her steps are easy and elegant, and my heart cannot rest.

月出照兮，佼人燎兮。

yuè chū zhào xī, jiǎo rén liǎo xī

舒夭绍兮，劳心惨兮。

shū yāo shào xī, láo xīn cǎo xī

She sways as she walks, and my heart aches.
