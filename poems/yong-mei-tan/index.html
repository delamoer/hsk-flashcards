导读About this poem

Dug out of the dark earth, coal burns to bring spring warmth to the world — and asks for nothing. The Ming minister Yu Qian saw in it the official he wanted to be.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首咏物诗（借写一样东西来表达自己的志向）。第一联写煤的来历：凿开混沌的大地，得到了黑色的金子——“乌金”就是煤；煤里藏着温暖的阳气，用意最深。第二联写煤燃烧的作用：一点小火燃起来，就像把浩浩荡荡的春天带回人间；大炉子的火光照破了沉沉的黑夜。第三联写煤的贡献和品格：铸造鼎彝（古代贵重的青铜器）全靠它的力量；它被烧成灰烬，铁石一样坚定的心还在。最后一联直接说出心愿：只希望天下百姓都能吃饱穿暖，自己不怕辛苦，从深山里走出来。表面上写煤炭，其实是作者在写自己：愿意像煤一样燃烧自己，为百姓带来温暖。于谦另一首《石灰吟》也是这种写法，两首可以对照着读。

In English

This is a poem “in praise of an object” (咏物), where describing a thing becomes a way to state one’s own ideals. The first couplet tells where coal comes from: chisel open the dark, shapeless earth and you find “black gold”, holding within it the warmth of the sun’s energy. The second couplet shows what coal does when it burns: a small flame brings back the boundless warmth of spring, and the great furnace’s light breaks through the heavy night. The third couplet praises its work and its character: casting bronze vessels (鼎彝, precious ritual bronzes) depends on its power, and even when it has burned away, its heart, firm as iron and stone, remains. The last couplet speaks plainly: may all the common people be fed and warm — for that, coal will gladly leave the mountains and take on the hard work. On the surface the poem is about coal; really Yu Qian is describing himself, a man ready to burn himself up to warm others. His better-known poem “Song of Lime” uses the same method, and the two are worth reading side by side.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yu Qian (1398–1457) was a Ming minister who saved Beijing in 1449 when the Mongols captured the emperor; he was later executed on false charges and is honoured as a national hero. He is said to have written this poem while serving as governor in Henan and Shanxi, where he was known for caring for the poor.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

爝火 jué huǒ（小火）；鼎彝 dǐng yí；混沌 hùn dùn；藏蓄 cáng xù。“爝”“彝”机器朗读可能读错，已在“朗读用字”替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本。“元赖”的“元”通“原”，有的版本写作“原赖”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

凿开混沌得乌金，

záo kāi hùn dùn dé wū jīn

Chisel open the dark earth and you find black gold,

藏蓄阳和意最深。

cáng xù yáng hé yì zuì shēn

storing the sun’s warmth deep inside.

爝火燃回春浩浩，

jué huǒ rán huí chūn hào hào

A small flame burns back a whole wide spring;

洪炉照破夜沉沉。

hóng lú zhào pò yè chén chén

the great furnace lights up the heavy night.

鼎彝元赖生成力，

dǐng yí yuán lài shēng chéng lì

Every bronze cauldron owes its making to coal,

铁石犹存死后心。

tiě shí yóu cún sǐ hòu xīn

and burned to ash, its iron heart remains.

但愿苍生俱饱暖，

dàn yuàn cāng shēng jù bǎo nuǎn

If only all the people could be fed and warm,

不辞辛苦出山林。

bù cí xīn kǔ chū shān lín

I would gladly leave the hills and do the hard work.
