导读About this poem

Moored on the Yangtze, a statesman looks back toward his home just a few hills away and asks when the moon will light his way back. Famous for one word — 绿, “green”, used as a verb.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

王安石的家在江宁（今南京），这次他坐船经过长江北岸的瓜洲，停船过夜。前两句写距离：京口和瓜洲只隔着一条长江，从这里到钟山（他家附近的山）也只隔着几座山。家看起来这么近，却回不去。第三句“春风又绿江南岸”最有名，“绿”本来是形容词，这里用作动词，意思是“吹绿了”：春风一吹，江南岸边又变成了一片绿色。传说王安石先后试过“到”“过”“入”“满”等十几个字，最后才定下“绿”字，因为只有“绿”能让人看到颜色、看到春天的变化。这就是有名的“炼字”故事——古代诗人会反复推敲一个字。最后一句问：明月什么时候能照着我回家呢？把思乡之情留在问句里，余味很长。

In English

Wang Anshi’s home was in Jiangning (today’s Nanjing), south of the Yangtze. Here he has moored his boat for the night at Guazhou, on the north bank. The first two lines are about distance: Jingkou and Guazhou are separated only by the river, and Zhongshan, the mountain near his home, is just a few hills further on. Home looks so close — yet he can’t go back. The third line is the famous one. 绿 (lǜ) normally means “green”, but here it is a verb: the spring wind has “greened” the south bank again. Legend says Wang first wrote 到 (“reached”), then tried 过 (“passed”), 入 (“entered”), 满 (“filled”) and about ten other words before settling on 绿, because only “green” lets you actually see spring arriving — the colour spreading along the shore. Chinese teachers tell this story to show what 炼字, “refining a word”, means: poets would weigh a single character again and again. The poem ends with a question: when will the bright moon shine on me as I return home? Leaving the homesickness inside a question makes it linger.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Anshi (1021–1086) was a poet and the chief minister behind the Song dynasty’s great reforms. The poem is traditionally dated to 1075, when he was called back to the capital to serve as chief minister again, though some scholars place it at other points in his career.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

一水间：间 本网站按通行读法读 jiān（中间、之间）；也有人认为是“隔开”义，读 jiàn。数重山：数 shù，重 chóng（层），不读 zhòng。照我还：还 huán（回来），不读 hái。泊船：泊 bó。

京口瓜洲一水间，

jīng kǒu guā zhōu yī shuǐ jiān

明月何时照我还？

míng yuè hé shí zhào wǒ huán

when will the bright moon light my way home?
