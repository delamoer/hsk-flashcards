导读About this poem

On a spring night, a flute plays somewhere unseen, and the tune drifts over the whole city of Luoyang. It is “Breaking the Willow”, a song of parting — and everyone who hears it starts thinking of home.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写在洛阳的一个春夜，诗人听到笛声，想起了家乡。第一句“谁家玉笛暗飞声”：不知道是谁家在吹笛子，“暗”字说明看不见吹笛的人，只听见声音悄悄飞出来。第二句笛声随着春风散开，飘满了整个洛阳城——当然一支笛子不可能真的传遍全城，这是夸张，写出笛声打动人心的力量。第三句点出吹的曲子是《折杨柳》。古人送别时折柳枝送给远行的人，因为“柳”和“留”读音相近，所以《折杨柳》是一首离别的曲子。最后一句用反问：今晚听到这首曲子，有谁不会想起故乡呢？诗人不直接说“我想家”，而是说“人人都想家”，自己的思乡之情反而更深了。

In English

One spring night in Luoyang, Li Bai hears a flute and thinks of home. The first line asks whose house the music is coming from — the word 暗 (“unseen, in the dark”) tells us he cannot see the player; the notes simply “fly out” of the night. In the second line the music scatters on the spring wind and fills the whole city. One flute could not really be heard across Luoyang; the exaggeration shows how deeply the sound reaches people. The third line names the tune: “Breaking the Willow”. In ancient China, people snapped off a willow branch for a friend who was leaving, partly because 柳 (willow) sounds like 留 (“stay”), so this was a song of parting. The last line is a question that expects no answer: who, hearing it tonight, would not feel homesick? Li Bai never says “I miss home”; by saying everyone does, he makes his own longing feel even larger.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) left his home in Sichuan in his mid-twenties and never went back. This poem is usually dated to around 734–735, when he was staying in Luoyang, the eastern capital.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

谁家玉笛暗飞声，

shuí jiā yù dí àn fēi shēng

From some unseen house, a jade flute’s notes take flight,

散入春风满洛城。

sàn rù chūn fēng mǎn luò chéng

scatter on the spring wind and fill all Luoyang.

此夜曲中闻折柳，

cǐ yè qǔ zhōng wén zhé liǔ

Tonight the tune is “Breaking the Willow” —

何人不起故园情。

hé rén bù qǐ gù yuán qíng

who could hear it and not long for home?
