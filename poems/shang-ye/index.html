导读About this poem

A woman swears to Heaven that she will love her man until mountains are worn flat, rivers run dry, thunder roars in winter and snow falls in summer. Only 35 characters long — and one of the boldest love vows in Chinese poetry.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一个女子对爱人发下的誓言。开头一声“上邪！”——“天啊！”——就像对着老天大喊，请老天作证。她说：我要和你相爱，让这份爱永远不断、不减。接着她一口气列出五件不可能发生的事：高山变平，江水干了，冬天打雷，夏天下雪，天和地合在一起。只有这些事都发生了，她才敢和爱人分开。用“不可能的事”来表示“永远不会”，比直接说“我永远爱你”有力得多。句子长短不齐，有三个字的，也有七个字的，读起来像一口气说出来的话，感情又急又真。这首诗来自民间，语言大胆直白，和文人诗的含蓄很不一样，今天还常被用来表达忠贞不渝的爱情。

In English

This is a vow of love, spoken by a woman to the man she loves. It opens with a cry — 上邪！ “O Heaven above!” — as if she is calling Heaven to be her witness. She says: I want to love you, and let this love last forever, never fading, never broken. Then, in one breath, she lists five things that can never happen: the mountains worn flat, the rivers run dry, thunder in winter, snow in summer, heaven and earth crashing together. Only when all of these happen would she dare to part from him. Saying “never” through a list of impossible things is far stronger than simply saying “I will love you forever.” The lines are uneven — some three characters long, some seven — so the poem sounds like real speech pouring out, urgent and sincere. As a folk song, it is bold and direct, very different from the restrained style of most poetry by scholars. It is still quoted today as a symbol of unshakeable, faithful love.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This anonymous song is one of the eighteen “Songs for Cymbals and Drums” (铙歌十八曲), a group of Han dynasty yuefu songs, preserved in Guo Maoqian’s Collection of Yuefu Poems (12th century). It is usually read as the vow of a young woman.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

上邪的“邪”读 yé（语气词，同“耶”，不读 xié）；夏雨雪的“雨”作动词“下”，读 yù；江水为竭的“为”读 wéi；相知的“相”读 xiāng。长命无绝衰的“衰”通行读本注 shuāi，旧读 cuī；此处采用 shuāi。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

上邪！

shàng yé

O Heaven above, hear me!

我欲与君相知，长命无绝衰。

wǒ yù yǔ jūn xiāng zhī, cháng mìng wú jué shuāi

I want to love you, and let this love live forever — never fading, never broken.

山无陵，江水为竭，

shān wú líng, jiāng shuǐ wéi jié

Not until the mountains are worn flat, not until the rivers run dry,

冬雷震震，夏雨雪，

dōng léi zhèn zhèn, xià yù xuě

not until thunder roars in winter and snow falls in summer,

天地合，乃敢与君绝！

tiān dì hé, nǎi gǎn yǔ jūn jué

not until heaven and earth crash together — only then would I dare to leave you!
