导读About this poem

Four lines paint the whole Yangtze delta in spring: birdsong, green and red, wine flags in the wind — and then hundreds of old temples fading into the misty rain. A postcard that quietly turns into a reflection on history.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写江南的春天，像一幅很大的风景画。第一句从“千里”写起，黄莺在叫，绿树映着红花，有声音也有颜色。第二句写水边的村子、靠山的城墙和迎风飘动的酒旗，充满生活气息。后两句从眼前转向历史：南朝（公元420—589年）的皇帝信佛，在这一带建了几百座寺庙，“四百八十”是说很多，不是准确数字。如今这些楼台都隐在蒙蒙的烟雨之中。前两句明亮热闹，后两句朦胧安静，一明一暗形成对比。诗人没有直接评论，却让人想到：当年花大力气建寺求佛的王朝，最后都消失了。所以这首诗既是写景名作，也有怀古的味道。

In English

This poem is a wide landscape painting of spring in Jiangnan, the land south of the lower Yangtze. The first line covers “a thousand li” at once: orioles singing, green leaves setting off red flowers — sound and colour together. The second line zooms in on villages by the water, town walls against the hills, and wine-shop flags flapping in the breeze, full of everyday life. Then the poem turns from the present to history. The emperors of the Southern Dynasties (420–589) were devout Buddhists and built hundreds of temples in this region; “four hundred and eighty” simply means “a great many”. Now all those towers and terraces stand half-hidden in the misty rain. The first half is bright and lively, the second soft and hazy, and the contrast does the talking. Du Mu makes no comment, but readers are left to think: the dynasties that spent so much on temples are long gone. That is why the poem is loved both as scenery and as a quiet meditation on the past.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–c. 852), grandson of a famous chancellor, was a Tang official and one of the finest writers of the short quatrain. He spent several years in the Jiangnan region as a young official, and this poem is traditionally read as a reflection on the late Tang court’s lavish support of Buddhism.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

多少 duō shǎo（不读 duō shao）。“四百八十寺”的“十”按今音读 shí。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行教材版本。明代杨慎认为“千里”应作“十里”（千里之内的莺啼谁也听不到），清人何文焕等反驳说这是诗的夸张写法，通行本都作“千里”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

千里莺啼绿映红，

qiān lǐ yīng tí lǜ yìng hóng

For a thousand miles the orioles sing, green leaves against red blossoms;

水村山郭酒旗风。

shuǐ cūn shān guō jiǔ qí fēng

villages by the water, walls against the hills, wine flags stirring in the breeze.

南朝四百八十寺，

nán cháo sì bǎi bā shí sì

Of all the temples the Southern Dynasties built,

多少楼台烟雨中。

duō shǎo lóu tái yān yǔ zhōng

how many towers still stand, dim in the misty rain.
