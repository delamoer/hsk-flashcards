导读About this poem

Possibly the oldest song in Chinese: just eight characters, describing how ancient hunters made a bamboo bow and went after game. Cut bamboo, join bamboo, let the clay fly, chase the meat.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首歌只有八个字、四个动作，被认为是中国最古老的诗歌之一，可能来自原始社会的打猎生活。“断竹”：砍下竹子；“续竹”：把竹子连起来做成弹弓；“飞土”：把泥做的弹丸射出去；“逐宍”：去追打猎物（“宍”就是古代的“肉”字，这里指野兽）。每句两个字，一个动词加一个名词，节奏又短又快，像打猎时的口号，从做工具一直写到打猎，干脆利落。据说古代人死后不忍心让尸体被野兽吃掉，就用弹弓守护，这首歌就是那时唱的。虽然非常简单，却能看出诗歌最初的样子：从劳动里来，有节奏、能唱。

In English

Just eight characters and four actions — this is often called one of the oldest songs in Chinese, probably going back to the hunting life of prehistoric times. 断竹: cut the bamboo. 续竹: join the pieces into a bow. 飞土: shoot pellets of baked clay. 逐宍: chase the meat — 宍 is an ancient way of writing 肉 (meat), meaning the game animals. Each line is two characters, a verb and a noun, short and quick like a hunters’ chant, and the whole process from making the tool to the hunt is over in four beats. An old story says people sang it while guarding the bodies of their dead from wild animals, because they could not bear to see them eaten. Simple as it is, it shows what poetry may have looked like at the very beginning: born from work, built on rhythm, made to be sung. Notice, too, how little Chinese needs here: no subject, no tense, no linking words — just verb, object, verb, object.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The song is recorded in the Spring and Autumn Annals of Wu and Yue (吴越春秋, 1st century AD), where an archer quotes it as an ancient song. Its real age is unknown, but it is traditionally seen as one of the very oldest Chinese songs.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

宍 ròu（古“肉”字）；弹 dàn（弹弓，不读 tán）；续 xù。机器朗读不认识“宍”，已在“朗读用字”列换成“肉”，音频需人工听验。

版本说明 About the text

采用《吴越春秋·勾践阴谋外传》所载文本，“逐宍”的“宍”是“肉”的古字，有的本子直接写作“逐肉”。网站每两句一行（共两行）。
