导读About this poem

A riddle-poem: the word “wind” never appears, yet every line shows what it does — tearing down autumn leaves, opening spring flowers, raising waves and bending bamboo.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写风，可是全诗没有一个“风”字，像一个谜语。风是看不见的，诗人就写它做了什么：第一句，秋天它能把树叶吹落；第二句，早春二月它又能吹开花朵；第三句，吹过江面，掀起千尺高的大浪；第四句，吹进竹林，让成千上万根竹子都斜倒下来。前两句写风在不同季节的作用，一个“落”、一个“开”，一个让万物凋零，一个让万物生长；后两句写风的力量，“千尺”“万竿”是夸张的写法，让人感到风势很大。用看得见的东西来写看不见的风，这种从侧面去写的方法叫“侧面描写”。诗里还用了数字：三秋、二月、千尺、万竿，读起来很有节奏。这首诗简单好记，中国很多小学生都会背。

In English

This poem is about the wind, yet the word 风 never appears in it — it reads like a riddle. You cannot see the wind, so the poet shows what it does. Line one: in autumn it strips the leaves from the trees. Line two: in early spring it makes the flowers open. Line three: crossing a river, it raises waves a thousand feet high. Line four: blowing into a bamboo grove, it bends ten thousand stems. The first two lines show the wind in different seasons, and they are opposites: one makes things fall (落), the other makes things open (开) — the wind can end life and begin it. The last two lines show its strength, with deliberate exaggeration in “a thousand feet” and “ten thousand stems”. Describing something invisible through the visible things it affects is called cèmiàn miáoxiě, “indirect description”. The numbers — three autumns, the second month, a thousand, ten thousand — also give the short poem a strong, easy rhythm. It is a perfect first poem: every word is simple, the picture is vivid, and it is fun to guess the answer before reading the title. Many Chinese children learn it in primary school.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Qiao (c. 645–714) was a high official and one of the most celebrated court poets around the time of Empress Wu. He wrote a whole series of short poems on single objects — sun, moon, wind, clouds — and this is the best known.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

解落三秋叶，

jiě luò sān qiū yè

It shakes the autumn leaves from the trees,

能开二月花。

néng kāi èr yuè huā

and coaxes open the flowers of early spring.

过江千尺浪，

guò jiāng qiān chǐ làng

Over the river it raises towering waves;

入竹万竿斜。

rù zhú wàn gān xié

into the bamboo, and ten thousand stems bow down.
