赴戍登程口占示家人·其二

Fù Shù Dēng Chéng Kǒu Zhàn Shì Jiārén · Qí Èr
Setting Out for Frontier Exile: Lines Spoken to My Family (No. 2)
林则徐Lín Zéxú · Lin Zexu明清 Ming & Qing

导读About this poem

The official who fought the opium trade is punished and sent into exile in the far west. Before leaving, he tells his family he regrets nothing — “if it serves the country, I will give my life” — and then cracks a joke to cheer his wife up.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

林则徐因为禁烟、抗击英国侵略，反而被朝廷革职，发配到遥远的新疆伊犁。这首诗是他出发前写给家人的。开头说：我能力小、责任重，早就身心疲惫，再这样下去一定撑不住。第三、四句是全诗最有名的两句：只要对国家有利，就算献出生命也愿意，怎么能因为是祸还是福就躲开或者追求呢？这表达了一心为国、不计个人得失的精神。五、六句说反话（故意说和真实想法相反的话）：被贬到边疆，正说明皇上对我恩情深厚；去当个戍卒，正适合我这种笨拙的人。最后用了一个幽默的故事：宋代有个隐士被皇帝召见，他妻子写诗说“这回断送老头皮”（这回要把老命搭进去了）。林则徐和妻子开玩笑念这句诗，在悲壮中带着从容和幽默，让人更加敬佩。

In English

Lin Zexu led China’s fight against the British opium trade — and was blamed and punished for it. Stripped of office, he was sent into exile in Ili, in far-off Xinjiang. He wrote this for his family as he set out. He begins by admitting his weakness: his strength is small, his duties were heavy, he has long been worn out, and pushing himself further would break him. Then come the two lines every Chinese schoolchild knows: if it benefits the country, I will stake my life on it — how could I run from danger or chase good fortune for my own sake? Lines five and six are ironic, saying the opposite of what he really feels: being banished shows how kind the emperor is to me; a frontier soldier’s post suits a clumsy man like me perfectly. The ending is a joke. In a Song-dynasty story, a hermit summoned by the emperor said his wife had sent him off with a poem ending “this time you’ll lose your old head”. Lin playfully recites that line to his own wife. The mix of deep commitment and calm humour is what makes the poem so admired.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lin Zexu (1785–1850) was the Qing official who destroyed vast stores of British opium at Humen in 1839, an event that led to the First Opium War. After China’s defeat he was made a scapegoat and exiled to Ili. He wrote two poems for his family on setting out from Xi’an in 1842; this is the second.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

力微任重久神疲，

lì wēi rèn zhòng jiǔ shén pí

My strength is small, my task was heavy, and I have long been tired;

再竭衰庸定不支。

zài jié shuāi yōng dìng bù zhī

to drain this tired, ordinary body any further would break it.

苟利国家生死以，

gǒu lì guó jiā shēng sǐ yǐ

If it serves my country, I will give it my life —

岂因祸福避趋之？

qǐ yīn huò fú bì qū zhī

how could I dodge danger or chase reward?

谪居正是君恩厚，

zhé jū zhèng shì jūn ēn hòu

Exile only shows how kind the emperor is,

养拙刚于戍卒宜。

yǎng zhuō gāng yú shù zú yí

and a frontier soldier’s post suits a clumsy man like me.

戏与山妻谈故事，

xì yǔ shān qī tán gù shì

Teasing my wife, I tell her an old tale,

试吟断送老头皮。

shì yín duàn sòng lǎo tóu pí

and recite the line: “This time it’ll cost the old man his head.”
