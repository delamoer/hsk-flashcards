导读About this poem

A little pine, prickly and hidden among tall weeds, slowly grows above them. People don’t notice it will become a giant until it already touches the clouds — a poem about overlooked talent.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一棵小松树。第一句：它从小就长在很深的草丛里，针叶像刺一样竖着（“刺头”）。第二句：到了现在，渐渐觉得它比周围的蓬草、蒿草高出来了。第三、四句是诗的重点：当时的人看不出这棵小松将来会长成高耸入云的大树，一直要等到它真的“凌云”了，才说它高。诗人借小松说人：有才华的人年轻时往往不被人注意，甚至被看不起，等他成功了，大家才来称赞。这里面有诗人自己的感受，也有对世人只看眼前、不识人才的批评。对学习者来说，这首诗用字简单，意思也很实在：不要小看还没长大的东西，也不要因为别人看不见你就放弃。

In English

The poem describes a young pine tree. Line one: from the time it was tiny, it grew deep in tall grass, its needles sticking up like prickles. Line two: now, little by little, it seems to rise above the weeds around it. The last two lines carry the point. People at the time can’t see that this little pine will one day become a tree that “soars to the clouds”; only when it actually reaches the clouds do they say, “How tall it is!” The pine is really a person. Talented people are often ignored or looked down on when they are young, and praised only after they succeed. There is a bit of the poet’s own frustration here, and a criticism of a world that only sees what is already obvious. For learners, the words are simple and the lesson is practical: don’t underestimate something that hasn’t grown yet — and don’t give up just because no one has noticed you.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Xunhe (846–904) was a late Tang poet from Anhui who failed the exams for years before finally passing in 891. His poems are plain-spoken and often sympathetic to ordinary people. A popular legend claims he was the son of the poet Du Mu, but this is unproven.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

自小刺头深草里，

zì xiǎo cì tóu shēn cǎo lǐ

Since it was tiny, a prickly head deep in the grass,

而今渐觉出蓬蒿。

ér jīn jiàn jué chū péng hāo

now, slowly, it’s rising above the weeds.

时人不识凌云木，

shí rén bù shí líng yún mù

No one sees the tree that will touch the clouds —

直待凌云始道高。

zhí dài líng yún shǐ dào gāo

not until it reaches them do they call it tall.
