归园田居·其三

Guī Yuán Tián Jū · Qí Sān
Returning to Live in the Country (No. 3)
陶渊明Táo Yuānmíng · Tao Yuanming魏晋南北朝 Wei, Jin & Southern and Northern Dynasties

导读About this poem

A former official tries farming and is not very good at it — his beans are smothered by weeds. Yet walking home under the moon with wet clothes, he is perfectly content.

直译LiteralThe path is narrow, grass and trees grow tall; the evening dew wets my clothes.

意译MeaningThe path is narrow, the grass grows high, and the evening dew soaks my clothes.

直译LiteralWet clothes are not worth regretting, so long as my wishes are not betrayed.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人辞官后亲自种地的一天，像一篇小小的劳动日记。第一联说：在南山下种豆，结果草长得很茂盛，豆苗却稀稀拉拉——说明他种地并不在行，读起来有点幽默。第二联写他早上起来就去除草，一直干到月亮出来，才扛着锄头回家。“带月荷锄归”画面很美：月光下一个扛着锄头的身影，劳累中带着诗意。第三联写回家的小路很窄，路边草木长得很高，傍晚的露水打湿了衣服。最后一联是全诗的中心：衣服湿了没什么可惜的，只要不违背自己的心愿就好。这个“愿”，就是不想在官场里违心做事，而要过自由、真实的生活。全诗语言朴素，像说家常话，却写出了诗人坚定的选择。

In English

This poem reads like a small diary entry about one day of farm work after the poet gave up his official career. The first couplet is gently funny: he planted beans below the Southern Mountain, but the weeds are thriving and the bean sprouts are thin. He is clearly not an expert farmer. In the second couplet he gets up at dawn to clear the weeds and works until the moon comes out, then walks home with his hoe on his shoulder. “Carrying the moon, I shoulder my hoe and go home” is a lovely picture: tiredness, but also poetry. Next, the path is narrow, the grass and trees grow tall on both sides, and the evening dew soaks his clothes. The last couplet is the heart of the poem: wet clothes are nothing to regret, as long as I don’t go against what I truly want. That wish is to stop bending himself to fit official life and to live freely and honestly instead. The language is as plain as everyday talk, but the choice it describes is firm.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Tao Yuanming (365?–427) resigned from his last post, as a county magistrate, in 405 and never returned to office. He wrote the five (some editions six) “Returning to Live in the Country” poems soon after, about his new life as a farmer.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“带月荷锄归”的“荷”读 hè（扛着），不读 hé；“晨兴理荒秽”的“兴”读 xīng（起床），“秽”读 huì。“道狭草木长”的“长”：有的注本读 cháng（又高又长），有的读 zhǎng（生长、丛生），本页暂按 zhǎng。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

种豆南山下，草盛豆苗稀。

zhòng dòu nán shān xià, cǎo shèng dòu miáo xī

I planted beans below the Southern Mountain — the weeds grew thick, the sprouts grew thin.

晨兴理荒秽，带月荷锄归。

chén xīng lǐ huāng huì, dài yuè hè chú guī

Up at dawn to clear the weeds, I come home with my hoe on my shoulder and the moon beside me.

道狭草木长，夕露沾我衣。

dào xiá cǎo mù zhǎng, xī lù zhān wǒ yī

The path is narrow, the grass grows high, and the evening dew soaks my clothes.

衣沾不足惜，但使愿无违。

yī zhān bù zú xī, dàn shǐ yuàn wú wéi

Wet clothes are nothing to regret, as long as I stay true to what I want.
