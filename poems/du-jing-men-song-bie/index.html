导读About this poem

Sailing out of the Sichuan mountains, young Li Bai watches the hills fall away and the Yangtze spread across an endless plain. Then comes a twist: the one seeing him off is the river of his home town.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

年轻的李白坐船出了四川，经过荆门山，进入古代楚国的地方。前两句交代行程。中间两联写沿途的景色，是全诗最精彩的部分：“山随平野尽，江入大荒流”——船走出三峡，两岸的高山渐渐消失，眼前是一望无际的平原，长江在广阔的原野上奔流。“随”和“入”两个动词写出了视野由窄变宽的变化，气势非常大。接着写夜景和白天的景：月亮倒映在江中，像从天上飞下来的一面镜子；云彩变幻，结成海市蜃楼一样的楼阁。最后两句出人意料：题目说“送别”，送他的不是朋友，而是故乡的江水——江水一路流来，陪他走了万里。诗人不直接说想家，却说故乡的水舍不得他，这种把物当作人来写的拟人手法，让思乡之情显得更深、更温暖。

In English

A young Li Bai sails out of Sichuan, passes Jingmen Mountain and enters the old land of Chu. The first couplet simply states the journey. The middle couplets paint what he sees, and they are the heart of the poem. “The mountains end with the level plain; the river flows into the boundless wild”: leaving the gorges behind, the high cliffs fall away and suddenly there is open plain to the horizon, with the Yangtze rolling across it. The two verbs 随 (“follow”) and 入 (“enter”) capture the view opening from narrow to vast. Then night and day: the moon’s reflection in the river looks like a mirror flying down from the sky, and the clouds pile up into towers like a mirage at sea. The ending is a surprise. The title says “farewell,” but the one seeing him off is not a friend — it is the water of his home region, which has followed him ten thousand li. Instead of saying “I miss home,” he says home misses him. Treating the river as a person makes his homesickness feel both deeper and warmer.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) wrote this around 725, aged about 24, on his first journey out of Sichuan. Jingmen Mountain stands on the south bank of the Yangtze in today’s Yidu, Hubei, where the river leaves the gorges for the plain.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

渡远荆门外，

dù yuǎn jīng mén wài

I’ve sailed far, out past Jingmen,

来从楚国游。

lái cóng chǔ guó yóu

to roam the old country of Chu.

山随平野尽，

shān suí píng yě jìn

The mountains fade into the open plain;

江入大荒流。

jiāng rù dà huāng liú

the river rolls on into the boundless wild.

月下飞天镜，

yuè xià fēi tiān jìng

The moon drops in the water, a mirror flown from the sky;

云生结海楼。

yún shēng jié hǎi lóu

clouds gather into towers, like a mirage at sea.

仍怜故乡水，

réng lián gù xiāng shuǐ

Yet it’s the water of home that loves me still —

万里送行舟。

wàn lǐ sòng xíng zhōu

it has followed my boat ten thousand miles to see me off.
