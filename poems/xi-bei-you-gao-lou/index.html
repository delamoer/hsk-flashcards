西北有高楼

Xī Běi Yǒu Gāo Lóu
In the Northwest Stands a Tall Tower
佚名（《古诗十九首》）Anonymous (Nineteen Old Poems)汉 Han

导读About this poem

A passer-by hears a sad song drifting down from a tower as high as the clouds. What hurts him is not the singer’s pain but that no one understands her — the poem that made 知音, “one who truly hears your music”, a lifelong Chinese ideal.

西北有高楼，上与浮云齐。

直译LiteralIn the northwest there is a tall tower; its top is level with the floating clouds.

意译MeaningFar to the northwest a tower rises, tall enough to touch the drifting clouds.

02
交疏结绮窗，阿阁三重阶。

直译LiteralCriss-cross lattices on patterned windows; a four-eaved hall above triple steps.

意译MeaningIts windows are carved in delicate lattice; its hall sits high above three flights of steps.

03
上有弦歌声，音响一何悲！

直译LiteralFrom above comes the sound of strings and singing; how sad the sound is!

意译MeaningFrom up there comes a song with strings — and oh, how sad it sounds.

04
谁能为此曲？无乃杞梁妻。

直译LiteralWho could make this tune? Could it be the wife of Qi Liang?

意译MeaningWho could make music like this? Someone, perhaps, like Qi Liang’s grieving widow.

05
清商随风发，中曲正徘徊。

直译LiteralThe clear shang notes rise on the wind; in the middle of the tune, it lingers back and forth.

意译MeaningThe clear, cold notes drift out on the wind; halfway through, the melody wavers and circles.

06
一弹再三叹，慷慨有余哀。

直译LiteralOne strum, then sighs again and again; deeply stirred, with grief left over.

意译MeaningEvery phrase she plays is followed by sighs; her feeling runs high, and sorrow lingers after.

07
不惜歌者苦，但伤知音稀。

直译LiteralI do not pity the singer’s pain; I only grieve that those who understand the music are few.

意译MeaningIt isn’t her pain that hurts me most — it’s that so few can truly hear her.

08
愿为双鸿鹄，奋翅起高飞。

直译LiteralI wish we were a pair of great swans, beating our wings and rising high.

意译MeaningIf only we were two wild swans, beating our wings and rising together into the sky.

中文解析

诗人走在路上，看见西北方有一座高楼，高得和浮云一样齐，窗子雕着精美的花格，台阶有三层。楼上传来弦乐和歌声，声音非常悲伤。他猜想：能唱出这种曲子的，难道是杞梁的妻子吗？（传说杞梁战死，他的妻子痛哭十天，城墙都为她倒塌。）曲调随风飘来，弹到一半反复回旋，弹一遍就叹息好几次，激动的情绪里有说不尽的哀伤。最打动人的是“不惜歌者苦，但伤知音稀”：我不心疼唱歌的人有多苦，我难过的是能听懂她的人太少了。其实诗人自己也是一个没有知音的人，他在别人的歌声里听到了自己的孤独。最后他希望和歌者变成一对鸿鹄，一起高飞远去。“知音”的典故来自伯牙和钟子期，这首诗让“知音难得”成了中国人反复吟咏的主题。

In English

Walking along, the poet sees a tower in the northwest, so tall it seems level with the drifting clouds, with finely carved lattice windows and three flights of steps. From high up comes the sound of strings and a voice singing — terribly sad. Who could make such music, he wonders — someone like the wife of Qi Liang? (Legend says that when Qi Liang died in battle, his wife wept for ten days until a city wall collapsed.) The clear notes float down on the wind; halfway through, the melody circles back again and again; after each phrase the singer sighs, and beneath the strong feeling there is grief that will not end. Then the key couplet: “I don’t grieve for the singer’s suffering; I grieve that so few truly understand her.” The poet, we realise, is lonely in just the same way: in a stranger’s song he hears his own isolation. He ends with a wish that the two of them could become a pair of great swans and fly away together. The idea of the 知音, the friend who truly “knows your music”, goes back to the legend of Bo Ya and Zhong Ziqi; this poem made “how rare a true listener is” a theme Chinese poets returned to again and again.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首) are anonymous five-character poems from the late Eastern Han (2nd century AD), collected in the Wen Xuan anthology. Many are thought to be by educated men frustrated in their careers, far from home; one early anthology, the Yutai Xinyong, credits this poem to Mei Cheng, but most scholars treat it as anonymous.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

阿阁 ē gé（不读 ā）；三重 chóng；谁能为此曲 wéi；无乃 wú nǎi；杞梁 qǐ liáng；中曲 zhōng qǔ；一弹 tán；不惜 xī；鸿鹄 hóng hú（鹄不读 gào、hào）；愿为 wéi。机器朗读的“阿”“重”已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用《文选》所收《古诗十九首》第五首的通行文本，全诗十六句。末句《文选》作“双鸣鹤”，《玉台新咏》作“双鸿鹄”，今通行本多用“鸿鹄”；“窗”“弦”古本或作“牕”“絃”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

西北有高楼，上与浮云齐。

xī běi yǒu gāo lóu, shàng yǔ fú yún qí

Far to the northwest a tower rises, tall enough to touch the drifting clouds.

交疏结绮窗，阿阁三重阶。

jiāo shū jié qǐ chuāng, ē gé sān chóng jiē

Its windows are carved in delicate lattice; its hall sits high above three flights of steps.

上有弦歌声，音响一何悲！

shàng yǒu xián gē shēng, yīn xiǎng yī hé bēi

From up there comes a song with strings — and oh, how sad it sounds.

谁能为此曲？无乃杞梁妻。

shuí néng wéi cǐ qǔ, wú nǎi qǐ liáng qī

Who could make music like this? Someone, perhaps, like Qi Liang’s grieving widow.

清商随风发，中曲正徘徊。

qīng shāng suí fēng fā, zhōng qǔ zhèng pái huái

The clear, cold notes drift out on the wind; halfway through, the melody wavers and circles.

一弹再三叹，慷慨有余哀。

yī tán zài sān tàn, kāng kǎi yǒu yú āi

Every phrase she plays is followed by sighs; her feeling runs high, and sorrow lingers after.

不惜歌者苦，但伤知音稀。

bù xī gē zhě kǔ, dàn shāng zhī yīn xī

It isn’t her pain that hurts me most — it’s that so few can truly hear her.

愿为双鸿鹄，奋翅起高飞。

yuàn wéi shuāng hóng hú, fèn chì qǐ gāo fēi

If only we were two wild swans, beating our wings and rising together into the sky.
