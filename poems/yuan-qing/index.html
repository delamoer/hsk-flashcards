导读About this poem

A beautiful woman rolls up a bead curtain, sits frowning, and cries — but whom is she angry with? Twenty characters that show a feeling without ever explaining it.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗像一幅人物画：一位美丽的女子卷起珠帘，向外张望，好像在等什么人；等不到，就静静地坐了很久，皱起了眉头。接着我们看见她脸上湿湿的泪痕——可是她到底在恨谁呢？诗人说“不知”。题目叫“怨情”，全诗却没有一个字直接写她的心事，只写了卷帘、深坐、皱眉、流泪几个动作，留给读者自己去猜：也许是丈夫远行不归，也许是被心上人冷落。这种“只写外表、不说内心”的写法叫含蓄，话说得少，意思反而更多。最后一句用问句结束，让人读完还在回味。

In English

The poem is like a portrait. A lovely woman rolls up a curtain of pearls and looks out, as if waiting for someone. No one comes, so she sits a long time, and her fine eyebrows draw together in a frown. Then we notice the wet streaks of tears on her face. Whom does she resent? “I don’t know,” says the poet. The title promises “resentment,” yet not one word tells us what she is thinking. We only see a few actions — rolling up the curtain, sitting, frowning, crying — and must guess the rest ourselves: perhaps a husband who has been away too long, perhaps a lover who has turned cold. Chinese critics call this hánxù (“holding back”): saying less so that the reader feels more. Ending on an unanswered question keeps the poem alive after the last word. It also shows a quieter side of Li Bai, better known for wine, mountains and the moon.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762), the “Immortal of Poetry”, wrote many poems in the voice of lonely women, a popular genre in Tang poetry. The date and occasion of this one are unknown.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

美人卷珠帘，

měi rén juǎn zhū lián

A lovely woman rolls up the pearl-bead curtain,

深坐颦蛾眉。

shēn zuò pín é méi

sits on and on, her fine brows drawn in a frown.

但见泪痕湿，

dàn jiàn lèi hén shī

All you can see are the damp tracks of tears —

不知心恨谁。

bù zhī xīn hèn shuí

but whom does her heart blame? No one can tell.
