导读About this poem

“Who says you have no clothes? Share my robe!” Soldiers of the state of Qin sing as they sharpen their weapons for war. A rousing marching song about comradeship, still quoted whenever people stand together.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是秦国士兵出征前唱的战歌。开头就是一句反问：“岂曰无衣？”——谁说你没有衣服？我的战袍和你一起穿！接着说：国君要出兵了，修好我们的戈和矛，你的敌人就是我的敌人。三章层层推进：同袍、同泽（内衣）、同裳（下衣），衣服越分越细，感情越来越亲；从“同仇”到“偕作”（一起行动）再到“偕行”（一起上战场），行动越来越近。句子短，节奏快，像喊口号一样有力，读起来能感到士兵们互相鼓励、团结一心的豪气。今天“同袍”“同仇敌忾”这些说法都和这首诗有关，“同袍”至今还用来称呼一起奋斗的战友。

In English

This is a war song sung by soldiers of the state of Qin before battle. It opens with a challenge: 岂曰无衣 — “Who says you have no clothes?” If you have none, we’ll share my robe. Then: the king is raising an army, so let’s mend our spears and halberds; your enemy is my enemy. Each verse goes one step further. The clothing gets more personal — the outer robe, then the undershirt, then the lower garment — as the bond between the men grows closer; the action moves from sharing a foe to getting up together to marching off together. The lines are short and punchy, like a chant, and you can almost hear men slapping each other’s shoulders. That spirit is why the poem lasted: 同袍 (“sharing a robe”) is still used in Chinese for comrades-in-arms, and 同仇敌忾, “sharing the same hatred of the enemy”, echoes its words. Qin was the tough frontier state that would eventually unify China, and this song shows the fighting spirit people associated with it.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from about the 11th to the 6th century BC. This one is from the state of Qin (秦), on the western frontier in today’s Shaanxi and Gansu, which fought constantly with nomadic peoples and later unified China in 221 BC. The author is unknown.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

泽 zé（通“襗”，贴身的内衣）；裳 cháng（下衣，古代读 cháng，不读 shang）；戟 jǐ；偕 xié；行 xíng。“裳”机器朗读易错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

岂曰无衣？与子同袍。

qǐ yuē wú yī, yǔ zǐ tóng páo

Who says you have nothing to wear? My robe is yours too.

王于兴师，修我戈矛。

wáng yú xīng shī, xiū wǒ gē máo

The king calls up his army — let’s get our spears ready.

与子同仇！

yǔ zǐ tóng chóu

Your enemy is mine!

岂曰无衣？与子同泽。

qǐ yuē wú yī, yǔ zǐ tóng zé

Who says you have nothing to wear? My shirt is yours too.

王于兴师，修我矛戟。

wáng yú xīng shī, xiū wǒ máo jǐ

The king calls up his army — let’s get our halberds ready.

与子偕作！

yǔ zǐ xié zuò

We rise up together!

岂曰无衣？与子同裳。

qǐ yuē wú yī, yǔ zǐ tóng cháng

Who says you have nothing to wear? My clothes are yours too.

王于兴师，修我甲兵。

wáng yú xīng shī, xiū wǒ jiǎ bīng

The king calls up his army — let’s get our armour ready.

与子偕行！

yǔ zǐ xié xíng

We march out together!
