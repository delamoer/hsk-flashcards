明月何皎皎

Míng Yuè Hé Jiǎo Jiǎo
How Bright the Moon

导读About this poem

Moonlight on the bed curtains, a sleepless night, pacing in and out of the room until the tears come — a poem made almost entirely of small movements.

点 虚线字 看读音和意思 · Tap a dotted word for its sound and meaning

01
明月何皎皎，照我罗床帏。

直译LiteralThe bright moon, how white and shining! It lights my silk bed curtains.

意译MeaningHow bright the moon is, shining on the silk curtains of my bed!

02
忧愁不能寐，揽衣起徘徊。

直译LiteralWorried and sad, I cannot sleep; gathering my robe, I rise and pace.

意译MeaningToo troubled to sleep, I pull on a robe and rise to pace the floor.

03
客行虽云乐，不如早旋归。

直译LiteralTravelling, though it is said to be pleasant, is not as good as turning home early.

意译MeaningThey say life on the road is pleasant — better to come home soon.

04
出户独彷徨，愁思当告谁！

直译LiteralGoing out the door, I wander alone; my sad thoughts — whom should I tell?

意译MeaningI step outside and wander alone; who is there to hear my sorrow?

05
引领还入房，泪下沾裳衣。

直译LiteralStretching my neck to look, I return into the room; tears fall and wet my clothes.

意译MeaningI gaze far off, then turn back in, and tears fall on my clothes.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写一个人在月夜里因为思念睡不着。明亮的月光照在丝绸床帐上，她（或他）心里忧愁，无法入睡，于是披上衣服起来走来走去。接着她想：在外面漂泊虽然说是快乐的，但还不如早点回家。走出门外，一个人徘徊，这满心的愁苦能对谁说呢？只好伸长脖子望一望，又回到房里，眼泪落下来，打湿了衣裳。全诗几乎全是动作：不能睡、披衣、起身、徘徊、出门、彷徨、远望、回房、流泪，一个动作接一个动作，把坐立不安的心情写得非常细致。月亮是中国诗歌里最常见的思念象征，这首诗是很早的一个例子，后来李白的《静夜思》也有相似的情景。至于是谁在思念，一说是在外的游子想家，一说是家中女子盼丈夫，网站采用后一种读法朗读。

In English

Someone lies awake on a moonlit night, unable to sleep for longing. Bright moonlight falls on the silk curtains of the bed; troubled, the speaker throws on a robe and gets up to pace the room. A thought: life on the road may be called pleasant, but it is better to come home early. Out the door, alone, wandering back and forth — who is there to tell this sorrow to? Craning the neck to look into the distance, then back inside, tears falling until the clothes are wet. Nearly every line is an action: can’t sleep, robe on, up, pacing, out the door, wandering, looking, back in, crying. The restless movements say more than any description of feelings could. The moon is the most common symbol of longing in Chinese poetry, and this is one of its earliest appearances; Li Bai’s “Thoughts on a Quiet Night” sets a very similar scene. Readers disagree about the speaker — a traveller homesick on the road, or a woman at home waiting for her husband. Our recording follows the second reading.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is the nineteenth and last of the Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首), written by unknown poets of the late Eastern Han (around the 2nd century AD).

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

帏 wéi；揽 lǎn；乐 lè；愁思 sī；还入房 huán；裳 cháng（古代“裳”指下衣，旧读 cháng，今“衣裳”口语读 shang）；彷徨 páng huáng（不读 fǎng）。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

明月何皎皎，照我罗床帏。

míng yuè hé jiǎo jiǎo, zhào wǒ luó chuáng wéi

How bright the moon is, shining on the silk curtains of my bed!

忧愁不能寐，揽衣起徘徊。

yōu chóu bù néng mèi, lǎn yī qǐ pái huái

Too troubled to sleep, I pull on a robe and rise to pace the floor.

客行虽云乐，不如早旋归。

kè xíng suī yún lè, bù rú zǎo xuán guī

They say life on the road is pleasant — better to come home soon.

出户独彷徨，愁思当告谁！

chū hù dú páng huáng, chóu sī dāng gào shuí

I step outside and wander alone; who is there to hear my sorrow?

引领还入房，泪下沾裳衣。

yǐn lǐng huán rù fáng, lèi xià zhān cháng yī

I gaze far off, then turn back in, and tears fall on my clothes.
