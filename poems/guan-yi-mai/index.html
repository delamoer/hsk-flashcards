导读About this poem

A government official watches farmers reaping wheat under a burning sun, then notices a poor woman gleaning leftover grain because taxes took her whole crop — and turns the question on himself. A rare Tang poem in which the poet feels ashamed of his own comfortable salary.

第一段Part 1
01
田家少闲月，五月人倍忙。

直译LiteralFarm families have few idle months; in the fifth month people are twice as busy.

意译MeaningFarmers rarely have a quiet month, and in the fifth month they work twice as hard.

02
夜来南风起，小麦覆陇黄。

直译LiteralIn the night the south wind rose; the wheat covers the ridges, yellow.

意译MeaningA south wind blew all night, and by morning the ridges are gold with wheat.

03
妇姑荷箪食，童稚携壶浆。

直译LiteralWives and mothers-in-law carry baskets of food; children bring jugs of drink.

意译MeaningWomen shoulder baskets of rice, children carry jugs of broth,

04
相随饷田去，丁壮在南冈。

直译LiteralTogether they go to bring food to the fields; the strong men are on the southern slope.

意译Meaningwalking together to feed the reapers, the strong men out on the southern slope.

05
足蒸暑土气，背灼炎天光。

直译LiteralFeet steamed by the hot earth’s vapour, backs burned by the blazing sky’s light.

意译MeaningThe hot ground steams their feet, the fierce sun burns their backs.

06
力尽不知热，但惜夏日长。

直译LiteralStrength used up, they do not feel the heat; they only value the long summer day.

意译MeaningWorn out, they no longer feel the heat — they only wish the long day longer.

第二段Part 2
07
复有贫妇人，抱子在其旁。

直译LiteralThere is also a poor woman, holding a child, at their side.

意译MeaningAnd there beside them, a poor woman with a baby in her arms:

08
右手秉遗穗，左臂悬敝筐。

直译LiteralHer right hand holds left-behind ears of grain; from her left arm hangs a broken basket.

意译Meaningher right hand clutches fallen ears of wheat, a broken basket hangs from her left arm.

09
听其相顾言，闻者为悲伤。

直译LiteralListening to what she says to them, those who hear grieve because of it.

意译MeaningWhen she speaks to the others, everyone who hears her grieves.

10
家田输税尽，拾此充饥肠。

直译Literal“Our family’s fields all went to pay taxes; I pick up these to fill a hungry belly.”

意译Meaning“Our whole crop went in taxes — I glean these to keep from starving.”

第三段Part 3
11
今我何功德，曾不事农桑。

直译LiteralNow what merit do I have? I have never worked at farming or silk.

意译MeaningAnd I — what have I done to deserve it? I have never ploughed a field or tended a silkworm,

12
吏禄三百石，岁晏有余粮。

直译LiteralAn official’s pay of three hundred dan; at year’s end there is grain to spare.

意译Meaningyet my pay is three hundred measures of grain, and at year’s end I still have some left.

13
念此私自愧，尽日不能忘。

直译LiteralThinking of this, I am secretly ashamed; all day I cannot forget it.

意译MeaningThe thought shames me, and all day long I cannot let it go.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写唐代农民五月收麦的情景。前半部分是一幅劳动图：南风一吹，麦子一夜变黄，全家出动——妇女和孩子送饭送水，青壮年在南边山冈上割麦，脚下是蒸人的热气，背上是火辣的太阳，累得已经感觉不到热，只盼白天再长一点，好多干些活。接着画面转到一个贫苦的妇人：她抱着孩子，在旁边捡别人落下的麦穗，因为自家的田全都拿去交了税，只能靠拾麦穗填饱肚子。最后诗人把目光转回自己：我从没种过地，却拿着三百石的俸禄，年底还有余粮——想到这里，心里惭愧，一整天都忘不了。先写别人、再反省自己，这种写法在古诗里很少见。白居易主张诗歌要反映现实、为百姓说话，这首诗就是代表。语言平实，像在讲眼前的事，所以并不难懂。

In English

The poem opens with a busy harvest scene. One night of warm south wind turns the wheat gold, and the whole village goes out: women and children carry food and drink to the fields, while the strong men cut wheat on the southern slope. Their feet are steamed by the hot earth, their backs scorched by the sun; they are too tired to feel the heat and only wish the summer day were longer, so they could get more done. Then the camera moves to one figure at the edge: a poor woman holding a baby, picking up stray ears of wheat with one hand, a broken basket on her arm. Her family’s own harvest has all gone to pay taxes, so gleaning is how they eat. In the last lines Bai Juyi turns to himself. He has never farmed a day in his life, yet he draws a salary of three hundred dan of grain and still has some left at the end of the year. The thought shames him, and he cannot put it out of his mind. Few classical poets judged themselves this openly. Bai believed poetry should record real life and speak for ordinary people, and this plain, direct poem is one of the clearest examples of that belief.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) was one of the most popular poets of the Tang dynasty and believed poems should be easy enough for ordinary people to understand. He wrote this around 806–807, when he was a junior county official in Zhouzhi, near the capital Chang’an — the salary in the last lines is his own.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“曾不事农桑”的“曾”按通行注音读 céng，也有注本读 zēng（表示“竟然”），本站采用 céng。其他：刈 yì；荷 hè（挑、背）；箪食 dān sì（“食”指饭食，读 sì）；饷 xiǎng；陇 lǒng（同“垄”）；冈 gāng；秉 bǐng；敝 bì；“闻者为悲伤”的“为”读 wèi（因此）；“吏禄三百石”的“石”读 dàn（古代容量单位）；岁晏 yàn；尽日 jìn。机器朗读容易读错的几处已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

田家少闲月，五月人倍忙。

tián jiā shǎo xián yuè, wǔ yuè rén bèi máng

Farmers rarely have a quiet month, and in the fifth month they work twice as hard.

夜来南风起，小麦覆陇黄。

yè lái nán fēng qǐ, xiǎo mài fù lǒng huáng

A south wind blew all night, and by morning the ridges are gold with wheat.

妇姑荷箪食，童稚携壶浆。

fù gū hè dān sì, tóng zhì xié hú jiāng

Women shoulder baskets of rice, children carry jugs of broth,

相随饷田去，丁壮在南冈。

xiāng suí xiǎng tián qù, dīng zhuàng zài nán gāng

walking together to feed the reapers, the strong men out on the southern slope.

足蒸暑土气，背灼炎天光。

zú zhēng shǔ tǔ qì, bèi zhuó yán tiān guāng

The hot ground steams their feet, the fierce sun burns their backs.

力尽不知热，但惜夏日长。

lì jìn bù zhī rè, dàn xī xià rì cháng

Worn out, they no longer feel the heat — they only wish the long day longer.

复有贫妇人，抱子在其旁。

fù yǒu pín fù rén, bào zǐ zài qí páng

And there beside them, a poor woman with a baby in her arms:

右手秉遗穗，左臂悬敝筐。

yòu shǒu bǐng yí suì, zuǒ bì xuán bì kuāng

her right hand clutches fallen ears of wheat, a broken basket hangs from her left arm.

听其相顾言，闻者为悲伤。

tīng qí xiāng gù yán, wén zhě wèi bēi shāng

When she speaks to the others, everyone who hears her grieves.

家田输税尽，拾此充饥肠。

jiā tián shū shuì jìn, shí cǐ chōng jī cháng

“Our whole crop went in taxes — I glean these to keep from starving.”

今我何功德，曾不事农桑。

jīn wǒ hé gōng dé, céng bù shì nóng sāng

And I — what have I done to deserve it? I have never ploughed a field or tended a silkworm,

吏禄三百石，岁晏有余粮。

lì lù sān bǎi dàn, suì yàn yǒu yú liáng

yet my pay is three hundred measures of grain, and at year’s end I still have some left.

念此私自愧，尽日不能忘。

niàn cǐ sī zì kuì, jìn rì bù néng wàng

The thought shames me, and all day long I cannot let it go.
