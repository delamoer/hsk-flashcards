导读About this poem

Twenty characters about opening a letter. A man stranded far in the north finally receives a letter from home — and before he reads a word, a thousand tears fall.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

庾信本来是南方梁朝的官员，出使北方时被留了下来，再也回不了家乡。这时南方的将军王琳托人给他带来一封信。前两句写距离：自己在遥远的北方（“玉关”是玉门关，这里泛指北方边远的地方），南方的金陵（今南京）来的信使很少很少。后两句只写一个动作：打开你从万里之外寄来的信，眼泪就流下来了。“千行泪”和“万里书”数字一大一大，正好相对，一边是泪多，一边是路远——这种字数相同、意思相配的写法叫“对仗”。诗人没说信里写什么，也没说自己有多想家，只写开信时的眼泪，读者自然就懂了。全诗简单、短小，却非常动人。

In English

Yu Xin was an official of the southern Liang dynasty. Sent north as an envoy, he was detained there and never allowed to go home. Then a letter reached him from Wang Lin, a southern general still loyal to the old court. The first two lines are about distance: he is out at the “Jade Pass” — the far north-western frontier, used here to mean the remote north in general — and messengers from Jinling (today’s Nanjing) almost never come. The last two lines are just one action: he opens the letter that has travelled ten thousand li, and a thousand lines of tears pour down. Notice how the two numbers face each other: 千行泪 (“a thousand lines of tears”) and 万里书 (“a letter from ten thousand li”). Lines that match word for word and meaning for meaning like this are called duìzhàng (parallelism). The poet never tells us what the letter says, or how much he misses home. He shows only the tears that come as he opens it, and that is enough. A tiny poem, easy words, a very big feeling.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yu Xin (513–581) was a celebrated court poet of the Liang dynasty in the south. Sent as an envoy to the northern court in 554, he was detained when the north conquered the Liang capital, and spent the rest of his life in the north, longing for home. Wang Lin (526–573) was a Liang general who kept fighting in the south.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

玉关道路远，

yù guān dào lù yuǎn

The road out to the Jade Pass is long,

金陵信使疏。

jīn líng xìn shǐ shū

and messengers from Jinling seldom come.

独下千行泪，

dú xià qiān háng lèi

Alone, I let a thousand tears fall

开君万里书。

kāi jūn wàn lǐ shū

as I open your letter from ten thousand miles away.
