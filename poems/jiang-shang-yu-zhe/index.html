导读About this poem

People on the riverbank love the taste of perch — but look at the tiny boat out there, appearing and vanishing in the waves. A four-line reminder of the danger behind something delicious.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗只有二十个字，写的是江边两种人。前两句写岸上的人：江边来来往往的人，只喜欢鲈鱼味道鲜美。鲈鱼是江南有名的美食。后两句诗人请我们看江上：你看那一条小小的船（“一叶舟”，小得像一片树叶），在大风大浪里一会儿出现，一会儿又看不见了。打鱼的人冒着生命危险，岸上的人却只想到鱼好吃。前后一对比，诗人对渔民辛苦和危险的同情就表达出来了，也提醒人们：享受的时候不要忘了别人付出的代价。“出没风波里”五个字很有画面感，一个“没”字（mò，沉下去、看不见）让人替小船捏一把汗。

In English

In just twenty characters this poem shows two kinds of people by a river. The first two lines are about the people on shore: crowds coming and going along the bank who care only about how tasty the perch is — perch from the lower Yangtze was a famous delicacy. Then the poet asks us to look out at the water: see that boat, small as a single leaf, appearing and disappearing among the wind and waves. The fisherman is risking his life, while the people on the bank think only of their dinner. By setting the two halves side by side, the poet shows his sympathy for the hard, dangerous work of fishermen, and reminds us not to forget what others pay for our enjoyment. The last line is very visual. The word 没 (mò, “to sink out of sight”) makes us hold our breath as the little boat vanishes behind a wave — and then reappears.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Fan Zhongyan (989–1052) was a Northern Song statesman, general and reformer, best remembered for his saying that one should “worry before the world worries, and rejoice after the world rejoices”. Grown up in poverty, he often wrote with concern for ordinary people.

江上往来人，

jiāng shàng wǎng lái rén

The crowds that come and go along the river

但爱鲈鱼美。

dàn ài lú yú měi

only love the taste of perch.

君看一叶舟，

jūn kàn yī yè zhōu

出没风波里。

chū mò fēng bō lǐ

lost and found again among the wind and waves.
