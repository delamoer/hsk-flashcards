导读About this poem

Willow catkins drift everywhere in late spring, and nobody cares. Su Shi turns them into a lonely woman dreaming of her far-off lover — and ends with one of the most beautiful lines in Song poetry: they are not catkins, but a parting lover’s tears.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

杨花就是柳絮。朋友章质夫写了一首杨花词，苏轼用同样的韵脚和了这一首（“次韵”）。上阕把杨花写成一个思念远人的女子：杨花像花又不像花，也没有人怜惜，任凭它飘落。它离开树枝，落在路边，看起来无情，其实有它的情思。接着写她柔肠寸断、困倦得娇眼要睁又闭；梦里随风飘到万里之外，去寻找心上人，却又被黄莺的叫声惊醒。下阕从花写到人：我不遗憾杨花飞尽，只遗憾西园里落花再也接不回枝头。早上一场雨过后，杨花的踪迹在哪里？变成了一池细碎的浮萍（古人认为杨花落水化为浮萍）。春色如果分成三份，两份化作了尘土，一份随流水而去。仔细看来，那不是杨花，点点都是离别之人的眼泪。这首词写物又写人，物和人融为一体，被认为比章质夫的原作更好，是咏物词的名篇。

In English

Yánghuā, “poplar flowers”, are really willow catkins — the white fluff that floats everywhere in late spring. Su Shi’s friend Zhang Zhifu wrote a poem about them, and Su replied using the same rhyme words, a practice called cì yùn. The first half turns the catkins into a lonely woman. They are like flowers, yet not flowers; no one pities them as they fall. They leave home and drift by the roadside — they seem to have no feeling, but in fact they are full of it. Her tender heart aches, her sleepy eyes try to open and close again; in a dream she flies ten thousand miles on the wind to find her love, only to be woken by an oriole’s call. The second half moves from the catkins to the poet. He doesn’t mind the catkins flying away; what he minds is that the fallen petals in the west garden can never be put back. After the morning rain, where have the catkins gone? They have become a pond of broken duckweed (people believed catkins turned into duckweed in water). Spring is split into three parts: two have become dust, one has gone with the stream. Look closely — these are not catkins at all, but drop after drop of a parting lover’s tears. Object and feeling melt into each other so completely that readers have long rated it above the poem it answers.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Su Shi (1037–1101) wrote this around 1081 in Huangzhou, in reply to a willow-catkin poem by his friend Zhang Jie (style name Zhifu). Su himself noted the old belief that catkins falling into water turn into duckweed.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

似花还似非花、欲开还闭、又还被 中的“还”读 hái；从教 cóng jiāo（教：使、让）；傍 bàng；萦 yíng；缀 zhuì。“无情有思”的“思”作名词“情思”，读 sì（也有读 sī 的）。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行版本。原作者章质夫（章楶）的《水龙吟·杨花》在先，此为和作。“抛家傍路”，《花庵词选》作“抛街傍路”。末句古本按词律断作“细看来不是，杨花点点，是离人泪”，网站按今天通行的语意标点：“细看来，不是杨花，点点是离人泪。”

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

似花还似非花，

sì huā hái sì fēi huā

A flower, and yet not quite a flower,

也无人惜从教坠。

yě wú rén xī cóng jiāo zhuì

no one grieves, and down it drifts.

抛家傍路，思量却是，无情有思。

pāo jiā bàng lù, sī liáng què shì, wú qíng yǒu sì

It leaves its home to lie along the road; it looks unfeeling, but think again — it longs.

萦损柔肠，困酣娇眼，欲开还闭。

yíng sǔn róu cháng, kùn hān jiāo yǎn, yù kāi hái bì

Her soft heart twisted with ache, her sleepy eyes flutter open and fall shut.

梦随风万里，寻郎去处，又还被、莺呼起。

mèng suí fēng wàn lǐ, xún láng qù chù, yòu hái bèi, yīng hū qǐ

In dreams she rides the wind ten thousand miles to find him — and an oriole’s cry wakes her again.

不恨此花飞尽，恨西园、落红难缀。

bù hèn cǐ huā fēi jìn, hèn xī yuán, luò hóng nán zhuì

I don’t mind the catkins flying off; what hurts is the west garden’s petals, which no one can put back.

晓来雨过，遗踪何在？一池萍碎。

xiǎo lái yǔ guò, yí zōng hé zài, yī chí píng suì

Rain at dawn — and where have they gone? A pond full of scattered duckweed.

春色三分，二分尘土，一分流水。

chūn sè sān fēn, èr fēn chén tǔ, yī fēn liú shuǐ

Split spring into three: two parts are dust, and one part flows away with the stream.

细看来，不是杨花，点点是离人泪。

xì kàn lái, bù shì yáng huā, diǎn diǎn shì lí rén lèi

Look closer: these are not catkins at all, but tear after tear of someone left behind.
