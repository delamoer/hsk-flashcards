导读About this poem

Half landscape, half job application. Meng Haoran paints Dongting Lake in all its power, then slips in a polite request: he would like to “cross the water” too — that is, to serve the state — but has no boat.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首“干谒诗”，也就是古人写给有权势的人、希望得到推荐做官的诗。前四句写洞庭湖：八月秋水上涨，湖面和岸一样平，水天相连，分不清哪里是湖、哪里是天；湖上水汽蒸腾，笼罩了整个云梦泽，波涛好像要撼动岳阳城。“蒸”“撼”两个动词写出了湖的气势，是千古名句。后四句转到自己：想渡湖却没有船，比喻想做官却没有人引荐；在太平盛世闲居在家，自己觉得惭愧。最后借古话“临渊羡鱼，不如退而结网”，说自己只能坐看别人钓鱼，白白羡慕。整首诗求人推荐，却写得含蓄得体，不卑不亢。

In English

This is a ganye poem (干谒诗) — a poem sent to a powerful official in the hope of being recommended for office. The first half is pure landscape. In the eighth month the autumn floods fill Dongting Lake until the water is level with its banks; lake and sky merge into one. Mist rises off the water and spreads over the whole Yunmeng marshland, and the waves seem strong enough to shake the city walls of Yueyang. The two verbs 蒸 (“to steam”) and 撼 (“to shake”) give the lake enormous energy, and that couplet is one of the most quoted in Tang poetry. Then the poem turns to the poet himself. He wants to cross the lake but has no boat or oars — a metaphor: he wants to serve but has nobody to recommend him. Living idle in such a peaceful, well-governed age makes him feel ashamed. The ending borrows an old saying, “Better to go home and weave a net than stand by the pool wishing for fish”: he can only sit and watch the anglers, envying their catch. It is a request for help, but made with dignity — neither begging nor boasting.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Meng Haoran (689–740) never held office. The poem is traditionally said to have been presented around 733 to the chancellor Zhang Jiuling (some scholars suggest Zhang Shuo instead), hoping for a recommendation; it did not lead to a post.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

八月湖水平，

bā yuè hú shuǐ píng

In the eighth month the lake swells level with its shores,

涵虚混太清。

hán xū hùn tài qīng

holding the open sky until water and heaven are one.

气蒸云梦泽，

qì zhēng yún mèng zé

Its mist rolls out over the Yunmeng marshes;

波撼岳阳城。

bō hàn yuè yáng chéng

its waves pound until Yueyang’s walls seem to tremble.

欲济无舟楫，

yù jì wú zhōu jí

I long to cross, but I have no boat and no oars;

端居耻圣明。

duān jū chǐ shèng míng

sitting idle in so fine an age, I feel ashamed.

坐观垂钓者，

zuò guān chuí diào zhě

I sit and watch the anglers at their lines —

徒有羡鱼情。

tú yǒu xiàn yú qíng

all I have is the wish for a fish of my own.
