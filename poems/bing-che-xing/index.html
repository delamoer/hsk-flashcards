兵车行

Bīng Chē Xíng
Ballad of the War Carts
杜甫Dù Fǔ · Du Fu唐 Tang
体裁Form

主题Themes
边塞 Frontier & war爱国 Love of country

导读About this poem

Carts rumble, horses neigh, and families weep in the dust as young men march off to the frontier. A soldier tells a passer-by what endless wars have done to the villages — one of the strongest anti-war poems in Chinese.

第一段Part 1
01
车辚辚，马萧萧，行人弓箭各在腰。

直译LiteralCarts rumble, horses neigh; the marching men each have bow and arrows at the waist.

意译MeaningCarts rumble and clatter, horses cry out; every marching man has a bow and arrows at his hip.

02
耶娘妻子走相送，尘埃不见咸阳桥。

直译LiteralFathers, mothers, wives and children run to see them off; in the dust the Xianyang Bridge cannot be seen.

意译MeaningFathers and mothers, wives and children run beside them, and the dust hides the Xianyang Bridge.

03
牵衣顿足拦道哭，哭声直上干云霄。

直译LiteralTugging at clothes, stamping their feet, blocking the road, they weep; the sound of weeping rises straight up to the clouds.

意译MeaningThey clutch at sleeves, stamp their feet, stand in the road and wail; the crying climbs straight into the clouds.

第二段Part 2
04
道旁过者问行人，行人但云点行频。

直译LiteralA passer-by at the roadside asks a marching man; the marching man only says, “The call-ups come often.

意译MeaningA traveller by the road asks one of the soldiers. He only says, “They call us up again and again.

05
或从十五北防河，便至四十西营田。

直译LiteralSome, from fifteen, guarded the river in the north; and then, at forty, farmed army land in the west.

意译MeaningSome went north at fifteen to guard the Yellow River, and at forty were still out west, farming for the army.

06
去时里正与裹头，归来头白还戍边。

直译LiteralWhen they left, the village head wrapped their heads for them; coming back white-haired, they still guard the border.

意译MeaningWhen they left, the village head had to tie their headcloths for them; they came home white-haired — and were sent back to the border.

07
边庭流血成海水，武皇开边意未已。

直译LiteralOn the frontier the blood that flows has become a sea; the Martial Emperor’s wish to expand the borders has not ended.

意译MeaningOut on the frontier the blood runs into a sea, and still the Martial Emperor wants more land.

08
君不闻汉家山东二百州，千村万落生荆杞。

直译LiteralHave you not heard: in the two hundred prefectures east of the mountains of the Han house, thousands of villages grow thorns and brambles?

意译MeaningHaven’t you heard? In the two hundred districts east of the mountains, village after village is choked with thorns.

09
纵有健妇把锄犁，禾生陇亩无东西。

直译LiteralEven if there are strong women holding hoe and plough, the grain grows in the fields with no east or west.

意译MeaningStrong women may take up the hoe and plough, but the grain comes up in the fields in no rows at all.

10
况复秦兵耐苦战，被驱不异犬与鸡。

直译LiteralMoreover, the soldiers of Qin can endure hard fighting, so they are driven no differently from dogs and chickens.

意译MeaningAnd because we men of Qin are tough fighters, they drive us on like dogs and chickens.

第三段Part 3
11
长者虽有问，役夫敢申恨？

直译LiteralThough you, sir, ask, how would a conscript dare to voice his grievance?

意译MeaningYou are kind to ask, sir, but how could a man like me dare to complain?

12
且如今年冬，未休关西卒。

直译LiteralTake this winter, for example: the soldiers west of the pass have not been released.

意译MeaningTake this winter: the troops from west of the pass still haven’t been sent home.

13
县官急索租，租税从何出？

直译LiteralThe officials press hard for the rent; where are rent and taxes to come from?

意译MeaningThe officials are hounding us for taxes — but where is the money to come from?

14
信知生男恶，反是生女好。

直译LiteralTruly I know that bearing a son is bad, and bearing a daughter, on the contrary, is good.

意译MeaningNow we know it: a son is bad luck, and a daughter is the better thing to have.

15
生女犹得嫁比邻，生男埋没随百草。

直译LiteralA daughter can still be married to the neighbours; a son is buried and lost among the hundred grasses.”

意译MeaningA daughter can still marry the family next door; a son ends up buried in the wild grass.”

第四段Part 4
16
君不见青海头，古来白骨无人收。

直译LiteralHave you not seen, by the shores of Qinghai, white bones since ancient times that no one gathers?

意译MeaningHaven’t you seen the shores of Lake Qinghai, where white bones lie from ages past and no one comes to bury them?

17
新鬼烦冤旧鬼哭，天阴雨湿声啾啾！

直译LiteralNew ghosts are tormented by injustice, old ghosts weep; when the sky is dark and the rain is wet, their voices wail and wail!

意译MeaningThe new ghosts rage at their wrongs, the old ghosts weep, and under dark skies in the wet rain you can hear them whimpering.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗开头就像电影镜头：战车隆隆，战马嘶叫，出征的人腰挎弓箭，父母妻儿跑着送行，尘土大得连咸阳桥都看不见，哭声直冲云霄。接着诗人借路人和士兵的一问一答，让士兵自己讲：有人十五岁就到北方守黄河，四十岁又被派到西边屯田；走的时候还要村长帮着裹头巾，回来头发白了，又被派去守边。皇帝不断扩张边疆，边地的血流得像海水；家乡千村万落长满野草，田地荒芜。征兵、收税一样不少，老百姓甚至觉得生男孩不如生女孩——女儿还能嫁给邻居，儿子只会死在荒野。结尾写青海边的白骨没人收，阴雨天新鬼旧鬼一起哭，阴森悲惨。这首诗用的是乐府民歌的写法，句子长短不齐，有“君不见”这样的呼告；表面写“汉家”“武皇”，实际是批评唐玄宗的穷兵黩武（不停发动战争）。

In English

The poem opens like a film shot: carts rumbling, horses neighing, young men with bows at their waists, and parents, wives and children running alongside, the dust so thick you can’t see the bridge at Xianyang, the crying rising to the clouds. Then a passer-by asks a soldier what is going on, and the soldier does the talking. Some men were sent north to guard the Yellow River at fifteen and west to farm army land at forty; when they left, the village head had to tie their headcloth for them because they were so young, and they came back white-haired only to be sent out again. The emperor keeps pushing the borders outward, and the blood on the frontier could fill a sea. Back home, villages are overgrown with brambles; women try to plough but the crops grow anyhow. Taxes are still demanded. People have even started to feel that a daughter is better than a son: she can marry next door, while a son ends up buried in the weeds. The ending is a ghost scene — bones nobody collects by Lake Qinghai, and on rainy days the new ghosts and the old ones crying together. Du Fu writes in the free style of old folk ballads, with lines of different lengths and direct calls to the reader (“Have you not seen…?”). He speaks of the “Han” emperor, but readers have always understood that he meant Emperor Xuanzong of his own Tang dynasty and his endless wars.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote this in Chang’an, probably around 751, during Emperor Xuanzong’s costly frontier campaigns against the Tibetans in the west and the Nanzhao kingdom in the southwest. Setting criticism of the present in the Han dynasty was a common, safer convention.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

多音字：兵车行、行人、点行 xíng；干云霄 gān（冲上）；过者 guò；长者 zhǎng（年长的人）；恶 è（不好）；县官 xiàn；卒 zú（士兵）；索 suǒ；被驱 bèi。生僻字：辚 lín、荆杞 qǐ、陇亩 lǒng、啾啾 jiū。耶娘 yé niáng（同“爷娘”）。“还戍边”的“还”，网站读 hái（又、仍然）；也有注本读 huán（回到、再去）。机器朗读容易读错的几处已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

车辚辚，马萧萧，行人弓箭各在腰。

chē lín lín, mǎ xiāo xiāo, xíng rén gōng jiàn gè zài yāo

Carts rumble and clatter, horses cry out; every marching man has a bow and arrows at his hip.

耶娘妻子走相送，尘埃不见咸阳桥。

yé niáng qī zǐ zǒu xiāng sòng, chén āi bù jiàn xián yáng qiáo

Fathers and mothers, wives and children run beside them, and the dust hides the Xianyang Bridge.

牵衣顿足拦道哭，哭声直上干云霄。

qiān yī dùn zú lán dào kū, kū shēng zhí shàng gān yún xiāo

They clutch at sleeves, stamp their feet, stand in the road and wail; the crying climbs straight into the clouds.

道旁过者问行人，行人但云点行频。

dào páng guò zhě wèn xíng rén, xíng rén dàn yún diǎn xíng pín

A traveller by the road asks one of the soldiers. He only says, “They call us up again and again.

或从十五北防河，便至四十西营田。

huò cóng shí wǔ běi fáng hé, biàn zhì sì shí xī yíng tián

Some went north at fifteen to guard the Yellow River, and at forty were still out west, farming for the army.

去时里正与裹头，归来头白还戍边。

qù shí lǐ zhèng yǔ guǒ tóu, guī lái tóu bái hái shù biān

When they left, the village head had to tie their headcloths for them; they came home white-haired — and were sent back to the border.

边庭流血成海水，武皇开边意未已。

biān tíng liú xuè chéng hǎi shuǐ, wǔ huáng kāi biān yì wèi yǐ

Out on the frontier the blood runs into a sea, and still the Martial Emperor wants more land.

君不闻汉家山东二百州，千村万落生荆杞。

jūn bù wén hàn jiā shān dōng èr bǎi zhōu, qiān cūn wàn luò shēng jīng qǐ

Haven’t you heard? In the two hundred districts east of the mountains, village after village is choked with thorns.

纵有健妇把锄犁，禾生陇亩无东西。

zòng yǒu jiàn fù bǎ chú lí, hé shēng lǒng mǔ wú dōng xī

Strong women may take up the hoe and plough, but the grain comes up in the fields in no rows at all.

况复秦兵耐苦战，被驱不异犬与鸡。

kuàng fù qín bīng nài kǔ zhàn, bèi qū bù yì quǎn yǔ jī

And because we men of Qin are tough fighters, they drive us on like dogs and chickens.

长者虽有问，役夫敢申恨？

zhǎng zhě suī yǒu wèn, yì fū gǎn shēn hèn

You are kind to ask, sir, but how could a man like me dare to complain?

且如今年冬，未休关西卒。

qiě rú jīn nián dōng, wèi xiū guān xī zú

Take this winter: the troops from west of the pass still haven’t been sent home.

县官急索租，租税从何出？

xiàn guān jí suǒ zū, zū shuì cóng hé chū

The officials are hounding us for taxes — but where is the money to come from?

信知生男恶，反是生女好。

xìn zhī shēng nán è, fǎn shì shēng nǚ hǎo

Now we know it: a son is bad luck, and a daughter is the better thing to have.

生女犹得嫁比邻，生男埋没随百草。

shēng nǚ yóu dé jià bǐ lín, shēng nán mái mò suí bǎi cǎo

A daughter can still marry the family next door; a son ends up buried in the wild grass.”

君不见青海头，古来白骨无人收。

jūn bù jiàn qīng hǎi tóu, gǔ lái bái gǔ wú rén shōu

Haven’t you seen the shores of Lake Qinghai, where white bones lie from ages past and no one comes to bury them?

新鬼烦冤旧鬼哭，天阴雨湿声啾啾！

xīn guǐ fán yuān jiù guǐ kū, tiān yīn yǔ shī shēng jiū jiū

The new ghosts rage at their wrongs, the old ghosts weep, and under dark skies in the wet rain you can hear them whimpering.
