导读About this poem

A wife whose husband has gone far away counts the miles, watches her clothes grow loose, and ends by telling herself to eat well — the opening poem of the Nineteen Old Poems, and one of the most quietly heartbreaking.

01
行行重行行，与君生别离。

直译LiteralYou go, you go, and go on going; you and I, parted while alive.

意译MeaningOn and on you went, and on again — torn from me while we both still live.

02
相去万余里，各在天一涯。

直译LiteralApart from each other more than ten thousand li; each at one edge of the sky.

意译MeaningTen thousand li now lie between us, each at a far edge of the sky.

03
道路阻且长，会面安可知？

直译LiteralThe road is blocked and long; meeting again — how can it be known?

意译MeaningThe road is hard and endless; who knows if we will meet again?

04
胡马依北风，越鸟巢南枝。

直译LiteralNorthern horses lean on the north wind; southern birds nest on south branches.

意译MeaningA northern horse leans into the north wind; a southern bird nests on the southern bough.

05
相去日已远，衣带日已缓。

直译LiteralOur parting grows daily more distant; my belt grows daily looser.

意译MeaningEach day the distance grows, and each day my belt hangs looser.

06
浮云蔽白日，游子不顾反。

直译LiteralFloating clouds cover the white sun; the wanderer does not think of returning.

意译MeaningDrifting clouds have hidden the sun; the traveller does not turn for home.

07
思君令人老，岁月忽已晚。

直译LiteralMissing you makes one old; the years suddenly are already late.

意译MeaningMissing you has made me old, and suddenly the year is ending.

08
弃捐勿复道，努力加餐饭。

直译LiteralCast it aside, do not speak of it again; make an effort to eat more meals.

意译MeaningEnough — I’ll say no more. Just take care, and eat well.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是《古诗十九首》的第一首，写一位妻子思念远行的丈夫。开头“行行重行行”五个字里有四个“行”，像脚步一步一步走远，分别的距离一下子就出来了。接着写两人“各在天一涯”，路又远又难走，不知道什么时候才能再见。“胡马依北风，越鸟巢南枝”是比喻：北方的马留恋北风，南方的鸟把巢筑在朝南的树枝上，动物都不忘故乡，人怎么能不回来？“衣带日已缓”写她一天天消瘦，衣带越来越松，不说“瘦”，却让人看到瘦。“浮云蔽白日”暗示丈夫可能被别的事或别的人留住了。最后两句最动人：“这些话不说了，你要好好吃饭。”她把痛苦咽下去，反过来关心对方——也是在劝自己撑下去。全诗语言朴素，感情真挚，被后人称为“五言之冠冕”。

In English

This is the first of the Nineteen Old Poems, a set of anonymous Han-dynasty poems that shaped all later five-character verse. A wife thinks of her husband, who has travelled far away. The title line uses 行 (“to go”) four times in five characters — you can almost hear the footsteps getting fainter. Now they are “each at one end of the sky”, the road is long and blocked, and she has no idea when they will meet again. Then comes a comparison: horses from the north lean toward the north wind, birds from the south build nests on south-facing branches. Even animals remember home — so why doesn’t he? She never says she is getting thin; she says her belt is looser every day. “Drifting clouds hide the white sun” hints that something, or someone, is keeping him away. The ending is the most moving part: “Let’s drop it, say no more — just try to eat well.” She swallows her pain and turns to caring for him, which is also a way of telling herself to hold on. Plain words, deep feeling: later critics called these poems the crown of five-character poetry.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Nineteen Old Poems (古诗十九首) were written by unknown scholars of the late Eastern Han (around the 2nd century AD) and gathered into the anthology Wenxuan in the 6th century. Many are about separation — officials and soldiers far from home, and the families they left behind.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

行行重行行，与君生别离。

xíng xíng chóng xíng xíng, yǔ jūn shēng bié lí

On and on you went, and on again — torn from me while we both still live.

相去万余里，各在天一涯。

xiāng qù wàn yú lǐ, gè zài tiān yī yá

Ten thousand li now lie between us, each at a far edge of the sky.

道路阻且长，会面安可知？

dào lù zǔ qiě cháng, huì miàn ān kě zhī

The road is hard and endless; who knows if we will meet again?

胡马依北风，越鸟巢南枝。

hú mǎ yī běi fēng, yuè niǎo cháo nán zhī

A northern horse leans into the north wind; a southern bird nests on the southern bough.

相去日已远，衣带日已缓。

xiāng qù rì yǐ yuǎn, yī dài rì yǐ huǎn

Each day the distance grows, and each day my belt hangs looser.

浮云蔽白日，游子不顾反。

fú yún bì bái rì, yóu zǐ bù gù fǎn

Drifting clouds have hidden the sun; the traveller does not turn for home.

思君令人老，岁月忽已晚。

sī jūn lìng rén lǎo, suì yuè hū yǐ wǎn

Missing you has made me old, and suddenly the year is ending.

弃捐勿复道，努力加餐饭。

qì juān wù fù dào, nǔ lì jiā cān fàn

Enough — I’ll say no more. Just take care, and eat well.
