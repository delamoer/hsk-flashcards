出其东门

Chū Qí Dōng Mén
Out Through the East Gate

导读About this poem

Outside the city gate the girls are as many as clouds — but the young man only has eyes for one, in a plain white dress. The source of the idiom 美女如云, and one of the oldest poems about faithful love.

第一章Stanza 1
01

直译LiteralI go out of the east gate: there are girls like clouds.

意译MeaningOut through the east gate — girls everywhere, as many as clouds.

02

直译LiteralAlthough they are like clouds, none is the one my thoughts dwell on.

意译MeaningSo many, like clouds — but none of them is the one I think about.

03

直译LiteralWhite silk dress, grey-green scarf — she alone gives me joy.

意译MeaningOnly the girl in the plain white dress and grey-green scarf can make me happy.

第二章Stanza 2
04
出其闉阇，有女如荼。

直译LiteralI go out through the outer gate: there are girls like reed flowers.

意译MeaningOut past the gate tower — girls everywhere, like white reed flowers.

05
虽则如荼，匪我思且。

直译LiteralAlthough they are like reed flowers, none is the one I long for.

意译MeaningSo many, like flowers — but none of them is the one I want.

06

直译LiteralWhite silk dress, madder-red scarf — with her I could be happy.

意译MeaningOnly the girl in the plain white dress and rose-red scarf — with her I’d be glad.

中文解析

城东门外，出游的姑娘多得像天上的云、像满地的白茅花。可是男子说：她们虽然多，却都不是我心里想的那一个。我想的只是那个穿着白绢衣服、戴着青灰色（或浅红色）头巾的姑娘——她穿得朴素，大概家境也一般，可只有她能让我快乐。诗用“如云”“如荼”写姑娘之多、之美，然后一句“匪我思存”把这一切都推开，用众多来衬托唯一，这种手法叫“衬托”。两章结构相同，只换了几个字，是民歌常见的“重章叠句”。今天形容美女很多的成语“美女如云”就和这首诗有关。古往今来，人们用它来赞美专一的爱情：世界再大、选择再多，心里只有一个人。

In English

Outside the east gate of the city, crowds of young women are out enjoying the day — as many as clouds in the sky, as thick as white reed flowers. But the young man says: there are so many, yet none is the one my heart is set on. The one he wants wears a plain white silk dress and a grey-green (in the second stanza, a rose-red) headscarf — simple clothes, perhaps a girl from an ordinary family — and only she can make him happy. The poem first piles up the crowd with two vivid comparisons, 如云 and 如荼, then sweeps them all aside with one phrase, 匪我思存, “not the one I think of”. Using the many to set off the one is a technique called 衬托 (contrast that highlights). Like many folk songs, the two stanzas repeat the same frame with a few words changed. The idiom 美女如云, “beautiful women as many as clouds”, is linked to this poem. Readers have long loved it as a picture of single-hearted love: however many choices there are, there is only one person for him.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

The Book of Songs gathers 305 songs from roughly the 11th to the 6th century BC. The “Airs of Zheng” (郑风) are famous for their love songs; the east gate of the Zheng capital (in present-day Henan) is said to have been a popular place for spring outings.

出其东门，有女如云。

chū qí dōng mén, yǒu nǚ rú yún

Out through the east gate — girls everywhere, as many as clouds.

虽则如云，匪我思存。

suī zé rú yún, fěi wǒ sī cún

So many, like clouds — but none of them is the one I think about.

缟衣綦巾，聊乐我员。

gǎo yī qí jīn, liáo lè wǒ yún

Only the girl in the plain white dress and grey-green scarf can make me happy.

出其闉阇，有女如荼。

chū qí yīn dū, yǒu nǚ rú tú

Out past the gate tower — girls everywhere, like white reed flowers.

虽则如荼，匪我思且。

suī zé rú tú, fěi wǒ sī jū

So many, like flowers — but none of them is the one I want.

gǎo yī rú lǘ, liáo kě yǔ yú
