归去来兮辞

Guī Qù Lái Xī Cí
Home, I’m Going Home
陶渊明Táo Yuānmíng · Tao Yuanming魏晋南北朝 Wei, Jin & Southern and Northern Dynasties

导读About this poem

After just eighty days as a county magistrate, Tao Yuanming quits and goes home to his farm — and this is his song of the journey back, the joy of arriving, and the quiet life he chooses instead of a career.

第一段Part 1
01
归去来兮，田园将芜胡不归？

直译LiteralGo back! The fields and gardens are about to go to weeds — why not go back?

意译MeaningHome, I’m going home! My fields are running to weeds — why wait?

02
既自以心为形役，奚惆怅而独悲！

直译LiteralSince I myself let my heart be the slave of my body, why be gloomy and grieve alone?

意译MeaningI let my mind serve my belly — so why sit here alone and mourn it?

03
悟已往之不谏，知来者之可追；

直译LiteralI realise the past cannot be corrected; I know what is to come can still be pursued.

意译MeaningWhat is past can’t be put right, but what is to come can still be won.

04
实迷途其未远，觉今是而昨非。

直译LiteralTruly I have strayed on the road but not far yet; I feel today is right and yesterday was wrong.

意译MeaningI lost my way, but not far; now I see I am right today and was wrong before.

05
舟遥遥以轻飏，风飘飘而吹衣。

直译LiteralThe boat sways far off, lightly drifting; the wind blows lightly, fluttering my clothes.

意译MeaningMy boat rocks gently along; the breeze tugs softly at my robe.

06
问征夫以前路，恨晨光之熹微。

直译LiteralI ask a traveller about the road ahead, and regret that the morning light is still faint.

意译MeaningI ask a traveller how far it is, impatient at the dim light of dawn.

第二段Part 2
07
乃瞻衡宇，载欣载奔。

直译LiteralThen I see my humble house, now glad, now running.

意译MeaningAt last I see my little house — and I’m so glad I start to run.

08
僮仆欢迎，稚子候门。

直译LiteralThe servant boys welcome me, the little children wait at the gate.

意译MeaningThe servants come to greet me, the children wait at the gate.

09
三径就荒，松菊犹存。

直译LiteralThe three paths are nearly overgrown, but the pines and chrysanthemums are still there.

意译MeaningThe garden paths are overgrown, but the pines and chrysanthemums are still here.

10
携幼入室，有酒盈樽。

直译LiteralLeading the little ones, I enter the room, and there is wine filling the cup.

意译MeaningI take the little ones inside, and there is wine waiting in the jar.

11
引壶觞以自酌，眄庭柯以怡颜。

直译LiteralI take up the jug and cup to pour for myself; I glance at the courtyard trees and my face brightens.

意译MeaningI pour myself a cup, look out at the trees in the yard, and smile.

12
倚南窗以寄傲，审容膝之易安。

直译LiteralLeaning on the south window to express my pride, I see a room just big enough for my knees is easy to live in.

意译MeaningI lean at the south window, carefree, and see how easy life is in a room just big enough to sit in.

13
园日涉以成趣，门虽设而常关。

直译LiteralWalking in the garden every day becomes a pleasure; though there is a gate, it is always shut.

意译MeaningA daily stroll in the garden is my delight; there is a gate, but it stays closed.

14
策扶老以流憩，时矫首而遐观。

直译LiteralLeaning on my old-man’s stick I walk and rest; now and then I raise my head and gaze far.

意译MeaningWith my walking stick I wander and rest, and now and then I lift my head to look far off.

15
云无心以出岫，鸟倦飞而知还。

直译LiteralThe clouds, without intention, come out of the mountain caves; the birds, tired of flying, know to return.

意译MeaningClouds drift out of the hills without a purpose; birds tired of flying know it’s time to come home.

16
景翳翳以将入，抚孤松而盘桓。

直译LiteralThe sunlight dims as it is about to set; I stroke a lone pine and linger.

意译MeaningThe light grows dim as the sun goes down; I stroke a lonely pine and cannot leave.

第三段Part 3
17
归去来兮！请息交以绝游。

直译LiteralGo back! Let me stop making friends and end my outings.

意译MeaningHome, I’m going home! Let me break off with the world and stop going out.

18
世与我而相违，复驾言兮焉求？

直译LiteralThe world and I are at odds; if I drive out again, what would I seek?

意译MeaningThe world and I don’t suit each other — why ride out again, and look for what?

19
悦亲戚之情话，乐琴书以消忧。

直译LiteralI delight in the heartfelt talk of my relatives, and enjoy the zither and books to dispel my cares.

意译MeaningI enjoy my family’s warm talk, and play the zither and read to chase my worries away.

20
农人告余以春及，将有事于西畴。

直译LiteralThe farmers tell me that spring has come, and there will be work in the western fields.

意译MeaningThe farmers tell me spring is here, and there is work to do in the western fields.

21
或命巾车，或棹孤舟。

直译LiteralSometimes I order a covered cart, sometimes I row a lone boat.

意译MeaningSome days I take a little cart, some days I row a boat alone.

22
既窈窕以寻壑，亦崎岖而经丘。

直译LiteralNow winding deep to seek the valley, now rough and rugged passing over the hills.

意译MeaningI wind down deep to find a valley, or climb rough tracks over the hills.

23
木欣欣以向荣，泉涓涓而始流。

直译LiteralThe trees, joyful, are coming into bloom; the springs, trickling, begin to flow.

意译MeaningThe trees are glad and burst into leaf; the springs bubble and start to run.

24
善万物之得时，感吾生之行休。

直译LiteralI admire how all things meet their season, and feel that my own life is coming to its end.

意译MeaningI love how everything finds its season — and feel my own life drawing to its close.

第四段Part 4
25
已矣乎！寓形宇内复几时？

直译LiteralIt is over! How much longer can I lodge my body in this world?

意译MeaningEnough! How long can this body stay in the world?

26
曷不委心任去留？

直译LiteralWhy not follow my heart and let it go or stay as it will?

意译MeaningWhy not let my heart decide whether I go or stay?

27
胡为乎遑遑欲何之？

直译LiteralWhy be so restless — where do I want to go?

意译MeaningWhy rush about anxiously — where is there to go?

28
富贵非吾愿，帝乡不可期。

直译LiteralWealth and rank are not my wish; the land of the immortals cannot be hoped for.

意译MeaningI don’t want riches or rank, and heaven is too far to hope for.

29
怀良辰以孤往，或植杖而耘耔。

直译LiteralI cherish a fine day to go out alone, or plant my staff and weed and hoe.

意译MeaningOn a fine day I walk out alone, or leave my stick in the ground and weed the fields.

30
登东皋以舒啸，临清流而赋诗。

直译LiteralI climb the eastern bank to let out a long whistle, and face the clear stream to compose poems.

意译MeaningI climb the east bank and sing out long; I sit by the clear stream and write poems.

31
聊乘化以归尽，乐夫天命复奚疑！

直译LiteralFor now I ride along with change until I reach my end; happy with heaven’s will, what more is there to doubt?

意译MeaningSo I’ll go along with change until the end — content with what heaven gives, why doubt it?

中文解析

陶渊明做了八十多天彭泽县令，觉得做官是让“心”替“身体”打工，违背自己的本性，于是辞官回家，写下这篇辞。第一段是决心：田园都快荒了，为什么不回去？过去的错已经没法改，将来还来得及，“觉今是而昨非”。他坐船往家赶，嫌天亮得太慢。第二段写到家：远远看见房子就高兴得跑起来，仆人迎接，孩子在门口等；院子里的小路荒了，松树和菊花还在。他喝酒、看树、靠窗，屋子很小却住得安心；每天在园子里散步，大门常关着。“云无心以出岫，鸟倦飞而知还”是全文最有名的一句：云自然地飘出山谷，鸟飞累了知道回巢，诗人也像倦鸟一样回到了家。第三段写回家后的生活：不再和官场来往，和亲人说说心里话，弹琴读书，春天到了去田里干活，有时坐车、有时划船，看草木生长、泉水流动。最后一段想到生命短暂：富贵不是我想要的，成仙也指望不上，不如顺着自然过完一生，还有什么可怀疑的？文章句子整齐，常用“兮”“以”“而”，读起来像唱歌。网站只收正文，没收前面的序。

In English

Tao Yuanming spent about eighty days as magistrate of Pengze county and decided that office was making his mind the servant of his body — against his nature. So he resigned and wrote this rhapsody about going home. The first part is the decision: his fields are going to weeds, so why not go back? The past can’t be fixed, but the future can; he now sees he is right today and was wrong before. He hurries home by boat, wishing dawn would come sooner. The second part is the arrival: he sees his little house and starts to run, the servants greet him, the children wait at the gate, the paths are overgrown but the pines and chrysanthemums are still there. He drinks, looks at the trees, leans by the window; the room is tiny but he is at ease, and the gate stays shut. The most famous line is here: clouds drift out of the hills without any purpose, and birds tired of flying know to come home — just like the poet himself. The third part is his new life: no more official friends, warm talk with family, music and books, spring work in the fields, trips by cart or boat, trees coming into leaf and springs starting to flow. The last part turns to the shortness of life: he wants neither wealth nor immortality, so he will follow nature to the end and doubt nothing. The lines are balanced pairs full of the particles xi, yi and er, which makes the piece sound like a song. Our page has the main text only, without Tao’s prose preface.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Tao Yuanming (365–427), the great poet of the countryside, wrote this in 405 when he resigned as magistrate of Pengze. He never took office again and spent the rest of his life farming, drinking and writing.

归去来兮，田园将芜胡不归？

guī qù lái xī, tián yuán jiāng wú hú bù guī

Home, I’m going home! My fields are running to weeds — why wait?

既自以心为形役，奚惆怅而独悲！

jì zì yǐ xīn wéi xíng yì, xī chóu chàng ér dú bēi

I let my mind serve my belly — so why sit here alone and mourn it?

悟已往之不谏，知来者之可追；

wù yǐ wǎng zhī bù jiàn, zhī lái zhě zhī kě zhuī

What is past can’t be put right, but what is to come can still be won.

实迷途其未远，觉今是而昨非。

shí mí tú qí wèi yuǎn, jué jīn shì ér zuó fēi

I lost my way, but not far; now I see I am right today and was wrong before.

舟遥遥以轻飏，风飘飘而吹衣。

zhōu yáo yáo yǐ qīng yáng, fēng piāo piāo ér chuī yī

My boat rocks gently along; the breeze tugs softly at my robe.

问征夫以前路，恨晨光之熹微。

wèn zhēng fū yǐ qián lù, hèn chén guāng zhī xī wēi

I ask a traveller how far it is, impatient at the dim light of dawn.

乃瞻衡宇，载欣载奔。

nǎi zhān héng yǔ, zài xīn zài bēn

At last I see my little house — and I’m so glad I start to run.

僮仆欢迎，稚子候门。

tóng pū huān yíng, zhì zǐ hòu mén

The servants come to greet me, the children wait at the gate.

三径就荒，松菊犹存。

sān jìng jiù huāng, sōng jú yóu cún

The garden paths are overgrown, but the pines and chrysanthemums are still here.

携幼入室，有酒盈樽。

xié yòu rù shì, yǒu jiǔ yíng zūn

I take the little ones inside, and there is wine waiting in the jar.

引壶觞以自酌，眄庭柯以怡颜。

yǐn hú shāng yǐ zì zhuó, miǎn tíng kē yǐ yí yán

I pour myself a cup, look out at the trees in the yard, and smile.

倚南窗以寄傲，审容膝之易安。

yǐ nán chuāng yǐ jì ào, shěn róng xī zhī yì ān

I lean at the south window, carefree, and see how easy life is in a room just big enough to sit in.

园日涉以成趣，门虽设而常关。

yuán rì shè yǐ chéng qù, mén suī shè ér cháng guān

A daily stroll in the garden is my delight; there is a gate, but it stays closed.

策扶老以流憩，时矫首而遐观。

cè fú lǎo yǐ liú qì, shí jiǎo shǒu ér xiá guān

With my walking stick I wander and rest, and now and then I lift my head to look far off.

云无心以出岫，鸟倦飞而知还。

yún wú xīn yǐ chū xiù, niǎo juàn fēi ér zhī huán

Clouds drift out of the hills without a purpose; birds tired of flying know it’s time to come home.

景翳翳以将入，抚孤松而盘桓。

jǐng yì yì yǐ jiāng rù, fǔ gū sōng ér pán huán

The light grows dim as the sun goes down; I stroke a lonely pine and cannot leave.

归去来兮！请息交以绝游。

guī qù lái xī, qǐng xī jiāo yǐ jué yóu

Home, I’m going home! Let me break off with the world and stop going out.

世与我而相违，复驾言兮焉求？

shì yǔ wǒ ér xiāng wéi, fù jià yán xī yān qiú

The world and I don’t suit each other — why ride out again, and look for what?

悦亲戚之情话，乐琴书以消忧。

yuè qīn qī zhī qíng huà, lè qín shū yǐ xiāo yōu

I enjoy my family’s warm talk, and play the zither and read to chase my worries away.

农人告余以春及，将有事于西畴。

nóng rén gào yú yǐ chūn jí, jiāng yǒu shì yú xī chóu

The farmers tell me spring is here, and there is work to do in the western fields.

或命巾车，或棹孤舟。

huò mìng jīn chē, huò zhào gū zhōu

Some days I take a little cart, some days I row a boat alone.

既窈窕以寻壑，亦崎岖而经丘。

jì yǎo tiǎo yǐ xún hè, yì qí qū ér jīng qiū

I wind down deep to find a valley, or climb rough tracks over the hills.

木欣欣以向荣，泉涓涓而始流。

mù xīn xīn yǐ xiàng róng, quán juān juān ér shǐ liú

The trees are glad and burst into leaf; the springs bubble and start to run.

善万物之得时，感吾生之行休。

shàn wàn wù zhī dé shí, gǎn wú shēng zhī xíng xiū

I love how everything finds its season — and feel my own life drawing to its close.

已矣乎！寓形宇内复几时？

yǐ yǐ hū, yù xíng yǔ nèi fù jǐ shí

Enough! How long can this body stay in the world?

曷不委心任去留？

hé bù wěi xīn rèn qù liú

Why not let my heart decide whether I go or stay?

胡为乎遑遑欲何之？

hú wèi hū huáng huáng yù hé zhī

Why rush about anxiously — where is there to go?

富贵非吾愿，帝乡不可期。

fù guì fēi wú yuàn, dì xiāng bù kě qī

I don’t want riches or rank, and heaven is too far to hope for.

怀良辰以孤往，或植杖而耘耔。

huái liáng chén yǐ gū wǎng, huò zhí zhàng ér yún zǐ

On a fine day I walk out alone, or leave my stick in the ground and weed the fields.

登东皋以舒啸，临清流而赋诗。

dēng dōng gāo yǐ shū xiào, lín qīng liú ér fù shī

I climb the east bank and sing out long; I sit by the clear stream and write poems.

聊乘化以归尽，乐夫天命复奚疑！

liáo chéng huà yǐ guī jǐn, lè fú tiān mìng fù xī yí

So I’ll go along with change until the end — content with what heaven gives, why doubt it?
