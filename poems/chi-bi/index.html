导读About this poem

A rusty halberd turns up in the sand at the old battlefield of Red Cliff, and Du Mu asks a bold question: what if the wind had not blown east? A playful, provocative take on one of China’s most famous battles.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

公元208年的赤壁之战，是三国历史上最有名的一仗：孙权和刘备联合，用火攻打败了曹操的大军，靠的就是一阵东风。这首诗从一个小东西写起：沙里埋着一截折断的戟，铁还没有锈烂，诗人把它磨洗干净，认出是前朝的旧物。由小见大，引出怀古之情。后两句是一个大胆的假设：如果东风不帮周瑜，曹操就会赢，大乔、小乔这两位江东美人就会被关进曹操的铜雀台。诗人不写千军万马，只写两个女子的命运，用小事写大事，很巧妙。言外之意是：历史的成败有时只是运气，英雄的成功也离不开机会。这种“翻案”式的写法，是杜牧怀古诗的特色。

In English

The battle of Red Cliff in 208 AD is the most famous fight of the Three Kingdoms period: Sun Quan and Liu Bei joined forces and burned Cao Cao’s huge fleet — a plan that only worked because the wind blew from the east. Du Mu starts not with armies but with one small object. A broken halberd lies buried in the sand, its iron not yet rusted through; he cleans it and recognises it as a relic of that earlier age. From this tiny thing the whole past opens up. Then comes a daring “what if”. If the east wind had not helped Zhou Yu, the southern commander, Cao Cao would have won, and the two Qiao sisters — famous beauties, one married to Sun Quan’s brother, the other to Zhou Yu — would have been locked away in Cao Cao’s Bronze Sparrow Terrace. Instead of describing the battle, the poet shows its outcome through the fate of two women, a clever way of making a huge event personal. The hidden point is that success in history can depend on chance, and even heroes need luck. Turning an accepted story on its head like this is typical of Du Mu’s poems about the past.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Mu (803–c. 852) was a keen student of military history and wrote a commentary on Sunzi’s Art of War. The poem is traditionally linked to his time as prefect of Huangzhou, near one of the places identified as the Red Cliff battlefield.

折戟沉沙铁未销，

zhé jǐ chén shā tiě wèi xiāo

A snapped halberd lies in the sand, its iron not yet eaten away;

自将磨洗认前朝。

zì jiāng mó xǐ rèn qián cháo

I pick it up, scrub it clean, and see it comes from an older age.

东风不与周郎便，

dōng fēng bù yǔ zhōu láng biàn

If the east wind had not blown Zhou Yu’s way,

铜雀春深锁二乔。

tóng què chūn shēn suǒ èr qiáo

the two Qiao sisters would be locked in Bronze Sparrow Terrace, deep in spring.
