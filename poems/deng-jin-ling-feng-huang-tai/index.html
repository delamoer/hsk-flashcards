登金陵凤凰台

Dēng Jīnlíng Fènghuáng Tái
Climbing Phoenix Terrace in Jinling
李白Lǐ Bái · Li Bai唐 Tang

导读About this poem

Standing on an old terrace in Nanjing, Li Bai sees that the phoenixes and the kingdoms are gone while the river flows on — and, looking towards the capital, feels the sting of his own exile from court.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

金陵就是今天的南京，三国的吴和东晋都曾在这里建都。传说南朝时有凤凰飞来，人们就修了凤凰台。诗一开头连用三个“凤”字：凤凰台上曾经有凤凰游玩，现在凤凰飞走了，台也空了，只有长江还在自己流——繁华会消失，自然却永恒。第二联怀古：吴国宫殿的花草埋没了小路，东晋的达官贵人也都变成了古老的坟墓。第三联转写眼前的大景：三座山一半隐在青天外，江水被白鹭洲分成两道。最后一联最重要：总是因为浮云能遮住太阳，看不见长安，让人发愁——“浮云蔽日”比喻小人蒙蔽皇帝，李白是在说自己被排挤出朝廷。全诗从怀古写到现实，从风景写到心事，气势开阔，被认为可以和崔颢的《黄鹤楼》相比。

In English

Jinling is today’s Nanjing, once the capital of the Wu kingdom and the Eastern Jin. Legend says phoenixes gathered here in the fifth century, so a terrace was built to mark the omen. The opening line uses “phoenix” three times in quick succession: phoenixes once played on Phoenix Terrace; now they are gone, the terrace is empty, and only the river flows on by itself. Glory disappears; nature stays. The second couplet looks back: flowers and grass of the Wu palace have buried its paths, and the grand nobles of Jin are now just old burial mounds. The third couplet turns to the huge view before him — three peaks half lost beyond the blue sky, the river split in two by White Egret Isle. The final couplet carries the real weight: “it’s always because drifting clouds can hide the sun that I cannot see Chang’an, and I grieve.” Clouds blocking the sun was a standard image for bad officials blinding the emperor; Li Bai, recently pushed out of court, is talking about himself. The poem moves from history to the present and from scenery to heartache, and is often compared with Cui Hao’s “Yellow Crane Tower”, which it seems to answer.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) left the court in Chang’an around 744 after being slandered by powerful courtiers, and spent years wandering the lower Yangtze. This poem was probably written during a visit to Jinling in that period; it is traditionally said he wrote it to rival Cui Hao’s famous poem.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

总为 wèi（因为）；鹭 lù。机器朗读“总为”容易读成 wéi，已在朗读用字中替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行文本。重要异文：“二水中分白鹭洲”一作“一水中分白鹭洲”；“吴宫花草”一作“吴宫芳草”。网站用通行的“二水”“花草”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

凤凰台上凤凰游，

fèng huáng tái shàng fèng huáng yóu

Once phoenixes played on Phoenix Terrace;

凤去台空江自流。

fèng qù tái kōng jiāng zì liú

now they are gone, the terrace stands empty, and the river just flows on.

吴宫花草埋幽径，

wú gōng huā cǎo mái yōu jìng

Weeds and flowers have swallowed the paths of the Wu palace,

晋代衣冠成古丘。

jìn dài yī guān chéng gǔ qiū

and the fine lords of Jin are only old grave mounds.

三山半落青天外，

sān shān bàn luò qīng tiān wài

Three peaks fade, half hidden, at the edge of the blue sky;

二水中分白鹭洲。

èr shuǐ zhōng fēn bái lù zhōu

White Egret Isle splits the river into two.

总为浮云能蔽日，

zǒng wèi fú yún néng bì rì

Always the drifting clouds come and cover the sun —

长安不见使人愁。

cháng ān bù jiàn shǐ rén chóu

I cannot see Chang’an, and my heart is heavy.
