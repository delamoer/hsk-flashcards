导读About this poem

Swallows back, pear blossoms falling, orioles singing — and a village girl on her way to pick mulberry leaves can’t stop smiling, because she just won a game against her friends. A fresh, cheerful spring picture with no sorrow in it at all.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词写清明前后乡村少女的春日生活。上阕是一幅春景图：燕子飞回来的时候正是春社，梨花落了就到了清明；池塘边点缀着三四点青苔，树叶深处传来一两声黄鹂的叫声，白天越来越长，柳絮轻轻飘飞。“三四点”“一两声”用很小的数字写景，显得清新、安静。下阕出现了人物：在采桑的小路上，碰到了东边邻居家爱笑的女伴。她问对方为什么这么高兴：是昨晚做了个好梦吗？原来是今天早上斗草赢了！笑容从两边脸颊上漾开来。“斗草”是古代女孩子们春天常玩的游戏，比谁采的花草多、品种奇。晏殊的词多写富贵人家的闲愁，这首却轻快活泼，充满乡村的生活气息，在他的作品里很特别。

In English

This ci paints country girls in spring, around the Qingming festival. The first stanza is pure scenery. The swallows return at the time of the spring sacrifice to the earth god, and when the pear blossoms have fallen it is Qingming. By the pond there are three or four spots of green moss; deep among the leaves an oriole sings once or twice; the days are growing longer and willow fluff floats lightly in the air. The tiny numbers — “three or four spots”, “one or two notes” — keep the picture fresh, light and quiet. The second stanza brings in people. On the path through the mulberry trees, the speaker meets her smiling friend from the house to the east. Why so happy? Did you have a lovely dream last night? No — she won at “grass fighting” this morning, and the smile spreads across both her cheeks. Grass fighting was a spring game for girls: they gathered plants and flowers and competed over who had the most, or the rarest. Yan Shu usually wrote about the quiet melancholy of elegant, wealthy households, so this bright, playful picture of village life stands out among his work.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yan Shu (991–1055) was a child prodigy who was brought to court at fourteen and later rose to chief minister under Emperor Renzong of the Song. He was a leading ci poet of the early Song and a patron of younger talents such as Ouyang Xiu and Fan Zhongyan.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

燕子来时新社，梨花落后清明。

yàn zi lái shí xīn shè, lí huā luò hòu qīng míng

The swallows come back for the spring festival; the pear blossoms fall, and it is Qingming.

池上碧苔三四点，叶底黄鹂一两声，

chí shàng bì tái sān sì diǎn, yè dǐ huáng lí yī liǎng shēng

A few green dots of moss beside the pond, an oriole calling once or twice among the leaves,

日长飞絮轻。

rì cháng fēi xù qīng

the days grow long, and willow fluff floats by.

巧笑东邻女伴，采桑径里逢迎。

qiǎo xiào dōng lín nǚ bàn, cǎi sāng jìng lǐ féng yíng

On the mulberry path I meet my friend from next door, all smiles.

疑怪昨宵春梦好，元是今朝斗草赢，

yí guài zuó xiāo chūn mèng hǎo, yuán shì jīn zhāo dòu cǎo yíng

“A sweet dream last night?” I wonder — no, she won the flower game this morning,

笑从双脸生。

xiào cóng shuāng liǎn shēng

and the smile keeps rising in her cheeks.
