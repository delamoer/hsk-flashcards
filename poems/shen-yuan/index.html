导读About this poem

At seventy-five, Lu You returns to the garden where he last saw the wife he was forced to divorce. The garden has changed — but the green water under the bridge once held her reflection.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗是陆游七十五岁时重游沈园写的。相传他年轻时和表妹唐琬结婚，感情很好，却因为母亲不喜欢唐琬，被迫分开。几年后两人在绍兴的沈园偶然相遇，陆游在墙上题了一首《钗头凤》，不久唐琬就去世了。四十多年后，诗人又来到沈园。第一句写黄昏：城墙上斜阳西下，号角声显得很悲哀。第二句说沈园已经不是从前的样子了。后两句最动人：让人伤心的桥下，春水依旧是绿的——当年，她就像一只受惊的鸿雁那样轻盈的身影，曾经倒映在这水里。“惊鸿”出自曹植《洛神赋》“翩若惊鸿”，形容女子体态轻盈美好。景物变了，水还是那么绿，人却不在了，用“不变”写“变”，痛苦更深。

In English

Lu You wrote this at seventy-five, on a return visit to Shen Garden in Shaoxing. The traditional story goes like this. As a young man he married his cousin Tang Wan and they were deeply in love, but his mother disliked her and forced them apart. Years later they met by chance in this garden; he wrote a lyric on its wall, and not long afterwards she died. Now, more than forty years on, he is back. The first line sets the mood: the sun slanting over the city wall, the sound of painted horns sorrowful at dusk. The second says the garden is no longer what it was. The last two lines are the heart of it. Under the bridge that breaks his heart, the spring water is still green — the same water that once held her reflection, light and graceful as a startled swan. That phrase, 惊鸿, comes from a famous ancient description of a goddess. Everything has changed except the water, and that one unchanged thing makes the loss unbearable.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) wrote two quatrains titled “Shen Garden” in 1199. The story of his first marriage to Tang Wan and their meeting in Shen Garden is traditional; scholars debate some details, but it has become one of China’s best-known love stories.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

沈园 Shěn Yuán（姓氏、地名读 shěn，不读 chén）；画角 huà jiǎo；曾是 céng。机器朗读“沈”容易读错，已在“朗读用字”列换成同音字，音频需人工听验。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

城上斜阳画角哀，

chéng shàng xié yáng huà jiǎo āi

The sun slants low over the city wall; the horns sound grief;

沈园非复旧池台。

shěn yuán fēi fù jiù chí tái

Shen Garden’s ponds and terraces are not what they were.

伤心桥下春波绿，

shāng xīn qiáo xià chūn bō lǜ

Under the bridge that breaks my heart, the spring water is still green —

曾是惊鸿照影来。

céng shì jīng hóng zhào yǐng lái

here, light as a startled swan, her reflection once came and went.
