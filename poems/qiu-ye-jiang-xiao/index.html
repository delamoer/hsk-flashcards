导读About this poem

The Yellow River and Mount Hua, grand as ever — but they lie in enemy hands. The people left behind in the north weep, looking south year after year for an army that never comes.

中文解析

这首诗写于1192年秋天，陆游六十八岁，住在山阴。一个闷热的秋夜，天快亮时他走出篱笆门乘凉，想到被金人占领的北方。前两句写北方的山河：三万里长的黄河向东流入大海，五千仞高的华山直上云天。“三万里”“五千仞”都是夸张，写出祖国山河的雄伟。可是这么壮丽的山河却在敌人手里。后两句写北方的百姓：他们在金人的统治下眼泪都流干了，一年又一年向南方盼望，等着宋朝的军队来收复，却一次次落空。“又一年”三个字很沉重，写出了漫长的等待和失望。诗没有直接批评朝廷，但读者能感到作者的愤怒和悲哀：前面的山河越壮美，后面的遗民就越可怜。

In English

Lu You wrote this in the autumn of 1192, aged sixty-seven, at home in Shanyin. On a hot autumn night he stepped out of his garden gate before dawn to cool off, and his thoughts went north, to the land held by the Jin. The first two lines show that land at its grandest: the Yellow River, thirty thousand lǐ long, flowing east into the sea; Mount Hua, five thousand rèn high, rubbing against the sky. Both numbers are deliberate exaggerations — they make the mountains and rivers of the homeland feel immense. But this magnificent country is in enemy hands. The second couplet turns to the people. The Chinese left behind under Jin rule have cried until there are no tears left in the “barbarian dust”, and they look south, year after year, for the imperial army to come and free them — and again it does not come. The phrase 又一年 (“another year”) carries all that waiting and disappointment. Lu You never attacks the court by name, yet the anger is clear: the grander the land in the first half, the more pitiful the people in the second. It is the second of two poems with this title.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) spent his life urging the Southern Song to win back northern China, lost to the Jin in 1127. He wrote this pair of poems in the autumn of 1192, in retirement at Shanyin.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

三万里河东入海，

sān wàn lǐ hé dōng rù hǎi

The Yellow River runs thirty thousand miles east to the sea;

五千仞岳上摩天。

wǔ qiān rèn yuè shàng mó tiān

Mount Hua climbs five thousand fathoms to brush the sky.

遗民泪尽胡尘里，

yí mín lèi jìn hú chén lǐ

Under the invaders’ dust, the people left behind have no tears left;

南望王师又一年。

nán wàng wáng shī yòu yī nián

they look south for the emperor’s army — another year goes by.
