导读About this poem

Standing by a river in the south, a general who longs to win back the lost north looks toward the old capital — and sees only mountains in the way. A short lyric where rivers, hills and a bird’s cry carry a whole country’s grief.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词是辛弃疾路过江西造口时题在墙上的。上阕从眼前的江水写起：郁孤台下的清江水里，流着多少逃难行人的眼泪——这里暗指几十年前金兵南下、百姓流离的往事。作者向西北望去，想看见象征故都的“长安”，可惜被无数座山挡住了。“山”在这里是一种比喻：挡住视线的山，也像挡住收复北方之路的种种阻力。下阕笔锋一转：青山再多也遮不住江水，江水终究向东流去——这是全词最有力量的两句，表达了一种不可阻挡的信念。可是最后又回到愁绪：傍晚江边，作者正发愁，深山里传来鹧鸪的叫声。古人说鹧鸪的叫声像“行不得也哥哥”（走不了啊），这声音正好说中了他壮志难成的苦闷。全词借景抒情（借写景物来表达感情），句句写山水，句句是心事。

In English

Xin Qiji wrote this lyric on a wall at Zaokou, a river stop in Jiangxi. The first half starts with what he sees: the clear river below Yugu Terrace. But he asks how many refugees’ tears are in that water — a quiet reminder of the years when Jurchen armies swept south and ordinary people fled for their lives. He looks northwest toward “Chang’an”, the old capital that stands for the lost north, and sees only mountains, one after another. The mountains block his view, and they also stand for everything that blocks the road back north. Then the second half turns: however many green hills there are, they cannot stop the river — in the end it flows east. These two lines are the strongest in the poem, a stubborn belief that what is right will get through. Yet the lyric closes in sorrow again. At dusk by the river he is full of worry, and from deep in the hills comes the call of the partridge. People said the bird’s cry sounded like “You can’t go on, brother!” — exactly the frustration of a man whose plans are always blocked. The technique is to let scenery speak for feeling: every line is about rivers and hills, and every line is really about his heart.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xin Qiji (1140–1207) grew up in the Jin-occupied north, fought the Jurchens as a young man and fled south to the Song court, where his calls to retake the north were mostly ignored. He wrote this around 1176 while serving as a judicial official in Jiangxi; Zaokou is traditionally linked to the Jin pursuit of a Song empress dowager there in 1129.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

郁孤台下清江水，

yù gū tái xià qīng jiāng shuǐ

Beneath Yugu Terrace the clear river runs —

中间多少行人泪。

zhōng jiān duō shǎo xíng rén lèi

how many refugees’ tears are in it?

西北望长安，

xī běi wàng cháng ān

I look northwest toward the old capital;

可怜无数山。

kě lián wú shù shān

all I see, alas, are endless hills.

青山遮不住，

qīng shān zhē bù zhù

But green hills cannot hold it back:

毕竟东流去。

bì jìng dōng liú qù

the river flows east in the end.

江晚正愁余，

jiāng wǎn zhèng chóu yú

Dusk on the water fills me with gloom,

山深闻鹧鸪。

shān shēn wén zhè gū

and deep in the hills, a partridge calls.
