导读About this poem

A bronze statue is hauled away from a fallen dynasty’s palace — and weeps. Li He’s strange, sorrowful poem contains one of the most quoted lines in Chinese: “If heaven had feelings, heaven too would grow old.”

中文解析

汉武帝曾在长安建了一座铜人，手捧铜盘承接露水，想喝了长生不老。几百年后，魏明帝下令把铜人拆下运往洛阳，传说铜人被搬上车时流下了眼泪。李贺就写了这个故事。开头说：葬在茂陵的汉武帝，像秋风里匆匆来去的过客，夜里还能听见他的马叫，天亮却毫无踪影。宫殿里桂树还在飘香，三十六宫却长满了青苔。接着写魏国官员拉着铜人上路，东关的冷风刺痛眼睛；铜人只带着汉朝的月亮出了宫门，想起旧主，流下像铅水一样沉重的眼泪。路边枯萎的兰花为它送行，“天若有情天亦老”——老天如果有感情，也会因为悲伤而变老。最后铜人捧着盘子在荒凉的月下独自远去，渭城越来越远，水声越来越小。全诗写朝代兴亡、人生无常，也寄托了李贺自己的感伤。

In English

Emperor Wu of Han once set up a bronze immortal in Chang’an, holding a plate to catch dew; drinking the dew was supposed to make him live forever. Centuries later, after the Han had fallen, Emperor Ming of Wei ordered the statue carried off to Luoyang — and legend says it wept as it was loaded onto the cart. Li He imagines that journey. Emperor Wu, buried in the Maoling tomb, was only a passing guest in the autumn wind: at night his horses can still be heard neighing, at dawn there is no trace. Osmanthus still scents the painted railings, but the thirty-six palaces are green with moss. Wei officials drag the statue eastward; a bitter wind stings its eyes. It leaves the palace gate with nothing but the Han moon, and its tears, remembering its old master, are heavy as molten lead. Withered orchids by the road see it off — and here comes the famous line: if heaven had feelings, heaven too would grow old with grief. The statue goes on alone under a desolate moon, holding its plate, while the city of Weicheng falls behind and the sound of the river fades. The poem is about the rise and fall of dynasties and the fact that nothing lasts — not even an emperor who wanted to live forever. Li He, of royal descent but shut out of an official career, pours his own sadness into the bronze figure.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li He (790–816), a distant member of the Tang royal family, wrote this around 813 after resigning a minor court post because of illness. The story of the weeping bronze statue comes from older historical anecdotes about the year 237.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

茂陵刘郎秋风客，

mào líng liú láng qiū fēng kè

The Han emperor in his tomb was a passing guest of the autumn wind;

夜闻马嘶晓无迹。

yè wén mǎ sī xiǎo wú jì

at night his horses neigh, at dawn there is no trace.

画栏桂树悬秋香，

huà lán guì shù xuán qiū xiāng

Osmanthus by the painted rails still hangs its autumn scent,

三十六宫土花碧。

sān shí liù gōng tǔ huā bì

but moss grows green in all thirty-six palaces.

魏官牵车指千里，

wèi guān qiān chē zhǐ qiān lǐ

Wei officials haul the cart toward a city far away;

东关酸风射眸子。

dōng guān suān fēng shè móu zǐ

at the east gate a bitter wind stabs at its eyes.

空将汉月出宫门，

kōng jiāng hàn yuè chū gōng mén

It leaves the palace carrying nothing but the Han moon,

忆君清泪如铅水。

yì jūn qīng lèi rú qiān shuǐ

and, remembering its lord, weeps tears heavy as molten lead.

衰兰送客咸阳道，

shuāi lán sòng kè xián yáng dào

Withered orchids see it off along the Xianyang road —

天若有情天亦老。

tiān ruò yǒu qíng tiān yì lǎo

if heaven had feelings, heaven too would grow old.

携盘独出月荒凉，

xié pán dú chū yuè huāng liáng

Alone, holding its plate, it moves on under a bleak moon;

渭城已远波声小。

wèi chéng yǐ yuǎn bō shēng xiǎo

Weicheng falls behind, the river’s voice grows faint.
