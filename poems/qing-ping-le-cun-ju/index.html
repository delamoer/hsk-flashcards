导读About this poem

A little snapshot of one farming family: an old couple chatting a bit tipsily, two sons at work, and the youngest lying by the stream peeling lotus pods instead of helping.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词像一幅农家生活的小画。上阕先写环境：低矮的茅草屋，小溪边长满青草。接着听到有人用柔和的吴地口音说话，带着几分醉意，互相逗乐，原来是一对白发的老公公和老婆婆，感情很好。下阕写三个儿子：大儿子在溪东的豆地里锄草，二儿子正在编鸡笼，最可爱的是小儿子，什么活也不干，趴在溪边剥莲蓬吃。“亡赖”就是“无赖”，这里不是骂人，而是说他调皮、淘气，词人用的是喜爱的语气。全词没有一个难懂的词，像白描一样，只写人物在做什么，一家人的勤劳、和睦和快乐却都在里面了。辛弃疾晚年闲居乡下，这首词写出了他对平静生活的喜爱。

In English

This lyric is a small painting of a farming family. The first half sets the scene: a low thatched cottage, green grass along a stream. Then voices — soft southern Wu dialect, a little tipsy, teasing each other affectionately — and we see who is talking: a white-haired old man and his wife, still sweet to each other after all these years. The second half introduces their three sons. The eldest is hoeing the bean field east of the stream; the middle one is busy weaving a chicken coop. And the one the poet likes best? The youngest, a little rascal, who does no work at all — he lies on his tummy by the water peeling lotus pods to eat the seeds. The word 亡赖 (“good-for-nothing”) sounds like a scolding, but here it is said with a smile, the way a grandparent calls a child “you little monkey”. There is nothing difficult in the language: the poet simply shows each person doing one thing, and the family’s hard work, warmth and happiness come through by themselves. Xin Qiji wrote it while living in the countryside in his later years, and you can feel how much he enjoyed this quiet life.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Xin Qiji (1140–1207), a soldier-poet who longed to win back northern China, was dismissed from office in 1181 and spent years living at Daihu near Shangrao in Jiangxi. This lyric is usually dated to that period of village life.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

词牌“清平乐”的“乐”读 yuè。翁媪 wēng ǎo；亡赖 wú lài（“亡”通“无”，不读 wáng）；剥莲蓬 bāo lián peng（“蓬”在“莲蓬”里口语读轻声，本页逐字注 péng）；相媚好 xiāng mèi hǎo。机器朗读“亡”“媪”“剥”易错，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

茅檐低小，

máo yán dī xiǎo

A thatched roof, low and small,

溪上青青草。

xī shàng qīng qīng cǎo

green grass growing by the stream.

醉里吴音相媚好，

zuì lǐ wú yīn xiāng mèi hǎo

Soft local voices, a little tipsy, teasing each other sweetly —

白发谁家翁媪？

bái fà shuí jiā wēng ǎo

whose white-haired old couple is this?

大儿锄豆溪东，

dà ér chú dòu xī dōng

The eldest son hoes the beans across the stream,

中儿正织鸡笼。

zhōng ér zhèng zhī jī lóng

the middle son is weaving a chicken coop,

最喜小儿亡赖，

zuì xǐ xiǎo ér wú lài

and best of all, the little rascal,

溪头卧剥莲蓬。

xī tóu wò bāo lián péng

lies by the water peeling lotus pods.
