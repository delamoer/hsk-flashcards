导读About this poem

Red magnolia flowers open on the treetops of a lonely valley and fall again, one after another, with no one there to see. Wang Wei turns a small scene into a quiet meditation on nature going its own way.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

辛夷坞是王维辋川别墅附近的一个山谷，长满了辛夷花（一种紫红色的木兰花，花开在树枝顶端，样子像荷花，所以诗里叫它“木末芙蓉花”）。第一、二句写花开：在高高的树梢上，辛夷绽放出红色的花萼，非常鲜艳。第三、四句笔锋一转：山涧边的人家寂静无人，花纷纷地开，又纷纷地落。没有人来欣赏，它照样开；没有人惋惜，它照样落。诗里没有一个字写诗人自己的感受，却让人感到一种宁静、自在的心情——花不为谁开，也不为谁落，一切顺其自然。这和王维信奉的佛教思想很接近，所以这首诗常被认为带有“禅意”。

In English

Xinyi Hollow was a little valley near Wang Wei’s Wangchuan estate, full of xinyi trees — a kind of magnolia whose purple-red flowers open at the very tips of the branches and look like lotus blossoms. That is why the first line calls them “lotus flowers at the ends of the boughs”. The first two lines show the flowers opening: high on the treetops, the mountain puts out bright red buds. Then the poem turns. The cottages by the stream are silent; nobody lives there. The flowers keep opening and falling, in great numbers, all by themselves. No one comes to admire them, and they bloom anyway; no one is sad to see them go, and they fall anyway. The poet never says what he feels, yet the scene leaves a calm, untroubled mood. The flowers do not bloom for anyone or fall for anyone — they simply follow their nature. This idea is close to the Buddhist outlook Wang Wei followed, which is why readers often say the poem has a “Zen” quality (禅意). The repeated 纷纷 (“one after another, in great numbers”) quietly gives the last line its gentle rhythm of opening and falling.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761) and his friend Pei Di each wrote twenty short poems on scenes around Wang’s Wangchuan estate, collected as the Wangchuan Collection (辋川集). This is one of Wang Wei’s, probably from his later years.
