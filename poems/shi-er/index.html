导读About this poem

The last poem of an 85-year-old patriot, written on his deathbed: he knows death ends everything — except his wish to see his divided country whole again.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是陆游临终前写给儿子们的诗，可以说是他的遗嘱。那时北方的中原已经被金国占领八十多年，南宋只剩半个国家。第一句说：我本来就知道，人死了一切都空了；第二句马上转折：只是遗憾没能亲眼看到国家统一。一个“但”字，把个人的生死放下，把国家放在心上。后两句是对儿子的嘱咐：等到朝廷的军队向北收复中原的那一天，你们在家里祭祀祖先的时候，千万别忘了告诉我。人已经死了，还要在地下等这个好消息，感情真挚又悲壮。全诗没有一个难字，像普通的家常话，却是中国最有名的爱国诗之一。

In English

Lu You wrote this as he was dying, and it reads like a last will. By then northern China, the old heartland of the Central Plains, had been in the hands of the Jin for more than eighty years; the Song held only the south. The first line is calm: “I always knew that when you die, everything becomes empty.” The second turns on one word, 但 (“only”): “only it grieves me not to see the Nine Provinces united.” He lets go of his own life without fuss, but not of his country. The last two lines are instructions to his sons. When the imperial army marches north and wins back the Central Plains, they must not forget to tell him — at the family ceremony where offerings are made to the ancestors. He has already accepted death, yet he still wants the good news to reach him in the grave. There is no difficult word in the poem; it sounds like a father talking at home, which is exactly why it moves people. It is one of the best-known patriotic poems in China, and children learn it in primary school.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Lu You (1125–1210) was the most prolific poet of the Southern Song, with nearly ten thousand poems, and spent his life urging the court to recover the north. He wrote this poem at the end of 1209 or the start of 1210, shortly before he died at about 85.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

元知：元 读 yuán，同“原”，“本来”的意思。乃翁 nǎi wēng（你们的父亲）。无特殊争议读音。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

死去元知万事空，

sǐ qù yuán zhī wàn shì kōng

I always knew that death leaves all things empty —

但悲不见九州同。

dàn bēi bù jiàn jiǔ zhōu tóng

my only grief: I will not see our country whole.

王师北定中原日，

wáng shī běi dìng zhōng yuán rì

The day our army marches north and takes back the Central Plains,

家祭无忘告乃翁。

jiā jì wú wàng gào nǎi wēng

when you make the family offerings, do not forget to tell your father.
