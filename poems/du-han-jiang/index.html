导读About this poem

After a long exile with no news from home, a man finally nears his village — and suddenly he is too afraid to ask anyone how his family is. A perfect picture of a feeling everyone knows.

中文解析

诗人被贬到岭南（五岭以南，今天的广东、广西一带），那时交通不便，和家里断了音信。他在那里过了一个冬天，又到了春天。后来他偷偷逃回北方，渡过汉江，离家越来越近了。按常理，离家越近应该越高兴、越着急打听消息，可是诗人说“近乡情更怯”——越靠近家乡，心里反而越害怕，连路上遇到从家乡来的人也不敢问。为什么？因为太久没有消息，他怕听到家人出事的坏消息。这种又盼又怕的矛盾心情，很多人都有过，诗人用十个字就说清楚了。“近乡情怯”今天已经成了成语，用来形容久别回乡时的复杂心情。

In English

The poet had been banished to Lingnan, the far south beyond the mountains (today’s Guangdong and Guangxi). Travel was slow and letters rarely arrived, so he lost all contact with his family. A winter passed there, and then a spring. At last he slipped away and headed north; crossing the Han River, he is getting close to home. You would expect him to be excited and eager for news. Instead he says: “The nearer I get to home, the more timid I feel — I don’t dare ask the people coming from there.” Why? After so long without a word, any news could be bad news. As long as he doesn’t ask, everyone at home is still safe in his mind. This mixture of hope and dread is something almost everyone has felt — waiting for exam results, opening a letter, coming back after years away. The poem catches it in ten characters, without a single difficult word. The phrase 近乡情怯 has become an idiom for the nervous, complicated feeling of returning home after a long absence.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Song Zhiwen (c. 656–712) was a leading court poet who helped shape the rules of Tang regulated verse. Exiled to the far south in 705 for his ties to Empress Wu’s favourites, he secretly fled back north the next year; the poem is traditionally dated to that journey. Some editions attribute it to the later poet Li Pin.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

岭外音书断，

lǐng wài yīn shū duàn

Beyond the southern mountains, no letters reached me;

经冬复历春。

jīng dōng fù lì chūn

a winter passed, and then a spring.

近乡情更怯，

jìn xiāng qíng gèng qiè

Now, close to home, I grow more afraid —

不敢问来人。

bù gǎn wèn lái rén

I dare not ask the travellers coming from there.
