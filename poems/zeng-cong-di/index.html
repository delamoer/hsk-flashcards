导读About this poem

Eight short lines about a pine tree standing straight in a howling mountain wind — written to encourage a younger cousin to stay upright whatever life throws at him.

直译LiteralTall and upright, the pine on the mountain; whistling and cold, the wind in the valley.

直译LiteralHow mighty the sound of the wind! How sturdy the pine branches!

意译MeaningHow fierce the wind’s roar — and how tough the pine’s branches!

03
冰霜正惨凄，终岁常端正。

直译LiteralIce and frost are at their most bitter; all year round it stays upright.

意译MeaningWhen ice and frost are at their cruelest, it stands straight all year long.

04
岂不罹凝寒？松柏有本性。

直译LiteralDoes it not suffer the frozen cold? Pines and cypresses have their own nature.

意译MeaningDoesn’t it feel the freezing cold? It does — but a pine is made to stand firm.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是刘桢写给堂弟的诗，全诗只写一棵松树。开头两句先画出画面：山上一棵高高挺立的松树，山谷里刮着瑟瑟的冷风。“风声一何盛，松枝一何劲”，两个“一何”（多么）连用，风越猛，越显出松枝的刚劲。接着把时间拉长：冰霜最凄冷的时候，松树一年到头都是端端正正的样子。最后诗人自问自答：难道松树不受严寒之苦吗？不，是因为松柏天生就有这样的本性。这种写法叫“托物言志”——借写一样东西，表达做人的志向。诗人没有直接说“你要坚强”，而是让堂弟从松树身上看到应该怎样做人：在困难和压力面前保持正直，不改变自己。孔子说过“岁寒，然后知松柏之后凋也”，这首诗正是这句话的诗意表达。

In English

Liu Zhen wrote this for his younger cousin, and the whole poem is about a single pine tree. The first two lines set the scene: a tall pine standing straight on a mountain, a cold wind moaning through the valley below. Then comes a pair of exclamations — “how loud the wind! how strong the branches!” — and the harder the wind blows, the tougher the pine looks. Next the poem stretches across a whole year: even when ice and frost are at their bitterest, the pine stays upright from season to season. Finally the poet asks a question and answers it himself: doesn’t the pine suffer in the deep cold? Of course it does — but standing firm is simply its nature. This technique is called tuō wù yán zhì, “using an object to express an ideal”: you describe a thing, and let it speak for what you believe. The poet never says “be strong”. He lets his cousin see in the pine how a person should live — keeping straight under pressure, never bending to circumstances. Confucius once said that only in the cold of winter do we see that pines and cypresses are the last to fade; this poem turns that saying into a picture.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Zhen (?–217) was one of the “Seven Masters of the Jian’an Era”, the circle of poets around Cao Cao and his sons, and was known for his proud, upright character. He wrote three poems for his cousin, each using a different image — duckweed, pine, and phoenix; this is the second.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

亭亭山上松，瑟瑟谷中风。

tíng tíng shān shàng sōng, sè sè gǔ zhōng fēng

A pine stands tall on the mountain; a cold wind moans through the valley.

风声一何盛，松枝一何劲！

fēng shēng yī hé shèng, sōng zhī yī hé jìng

How fierce the wind’s roar — and how tough the pine’s branches!

冰霜正惨凄，终岁常端正。

bīng shuāng zhèng cǎn qī, zhōng suì cháng duān zhèng

When ice and frost are at their cruelest, it stands straight all year long.

岂不罹凝寒？松柏有本性。

qǐ bù lí níng hán, sōng bǎi yǒu běn xìng

Doesn’t it feel the freezing cold? It does — but a pine is made to stand firm.
