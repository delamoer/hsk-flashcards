导读About this poem

Twenty characters about moss: it grows where the sun never reaches, its “flowers” are as small as grains of rice — and still it blooms as proudly as the peony. A tiny poem that became famous all over China in 2018, when village children sang it on TV.

这首诗被谱成了歌Sung as a song

梁俊《苔》Liang Jun

Yuan Mei's 20-character poem: moss grows where the sun never reaches, its flowers tiny as grains of rice, yet it blooms like the peony. Village teacher Liang Jun taught it to children in the mountains of Guizhou and sang it with one of them on Classics Sung Anew (经典咏流传). The video includes the interview.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写的是最不起眼的青苔。第一句写它生长的地方：太阳照不到的阴暗角落。可是第二句一转：就算在这样的地方，春天的生机也照样自己来了——“青春”在古诗里指春天、草木青绿的生命力，不是今天说的“年轻时光”；“恰”字有一种“偏偏、照样”的意思。后两句最有名：苔藓的“花”像米粒那么小，却也学着牡丹的样子开放。牡丹又大又艳，被叫作“花中之王”，苔花和它一比，小得几乎看不见，但它不自卑，也要尽情地开一次。诗人借写苔表达一个道理：再平凡、再弱小的生命，也有自己的价值，也有开花的权利。这种借物说理的写法和《石灰吟》《竹石》一样，叫“托物言志”。2018年，央视节目《经典咏流传》里，支教老师梁俊带着贵州山区的孩子唱了这首诗，感动了很多人，这首几百年来不太出名的小诗从此家喻户晓。

In English

This poem is about one of the most overlooked things in nature: moss. The first line tells us where it lives — in shady corners the sunlight never reaches. Then the second line turns: even there, the life of spring arrives all by itself. In old poetry 青春 means spring and the fresh green energy of growing things, not “youth” as it does today. The little word 恰 adds a sense of “all the same, just as it should”. The last two lines are the famous ones. The moss’s “flowers” are as small as grains of rice, yet they also try to bloom like the peony. (Botanically, moss has no flowers — the tiny heads are spore capsules — but to the poet they are flowers.) The peony is big and showy, called the “king of flowers”; next to it the moss flower is almost invisible. But it does not feel small. It blooms anyway, with everything it has. Through the moss, the poet says something about people: even the most ordinary, humble life has its own worth and its own right to flower. Writing about a thing to express an idea like this is called 托物言志, the same method as in Song of Lime and Bamboo and Rock. For centuries this was a little-known poem. Then in 2018, on the CCTV programme Classics Sung Today (经典咏流传), the volunteer teacher Liang Jun sang it with children from a poor mountain village in Guizhou, and it moved the whole country.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yuan Mei (1716–1798) was a Qing poet and essayist who left official life in his thirties and spent decades in his Garden of Contentment (随园) in Nanjing. He believed poetry should come from real, personal feeling, and he often wrote about small, everyday things — like the cowherd boy in What I Saw, or this moss.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

白日不到处，

bái rì bù dào chù

Where sunlight never finds its way,

青春恰自来。

qīng chūn qià zì lái

spring still arrives, all on its own.

苔花如米小，

tái huā rú mǐ xiǎo

The moss puts out flowers as tiny as rice,

也学牡丹开。

yě xué mǔ dān kāi

yet they bloom like the peony all the same.
