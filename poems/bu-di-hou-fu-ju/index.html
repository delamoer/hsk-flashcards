导读About this poem

Most chrysanthemum poems are quiet and gentle. This one, by a failed exam candidate who later led a huge rebellion, is a battle cry: when my flowers bloom, all others die, and the whole capital wears golden armour.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

题目的意思是：考试落榜以后，写菊花。菊花在中国诗里一般象征隐士的清高淡泊（比如陶渊明“采菊东篱下”），可是这首诗里的菊花完全不同，充满了杀气和霸气。第一句：等到秋天九月初八（重阳节前一天，重阳节有赏菊的习俗）。第二句：我的花一开，别的花全都凋谢了——“杀”字很重，好像菊花把百花打败了。第三句：冲天的香气像军阵一样，穿透了整个长安城。第四句：满城都是金黄色的菊花，像士兵穿着黄金铠甲。诗人把菊花写成一支军队，把开花写成攻城。后来黄巢真的率领起义军攻进了长安，所以人们常说这首诗是他的“预言”。读这首诗，可以感受到一个失意的人心里压着的愤怒和野心。

In English

The title says it all: written after failing the imperial exam. In Chinese poetry the chrysanthemum usually stands for a quiet, unworldly hermit — think of Tao Yuanming “picking chrysanthemums by the eastern fence”. Here it becomes something else entirely: fierce and threatening. Line one: wait until autumn, the eighth day of the ninth month — the eve of the Double Ninth Festival, when people admire chrysanthemums. Line two: when my flowers open, a hundred other flowers are “killed”. The verb 杀 is startlingly violent; the chrysanthemum doesn’t just outlast the others, it defeats them. Line three: its fragrance shoots up to the sky in battle formations and pierces the city of Chang’an. Line four: the whole city is wearing golden armour — every street full of yellow blooms, like soldiers in gold. The poet turns flowers into an army and blooming into a siege. Years later Huang Chao really did lead a rebel army into Chang’an, so the poem is often read as a prophecy. Either way, you can feel the anger and ambition of a frustrated man pushing through every line.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Huang Chao (820–884) came from a family of salt traders and failed the civil service exam. In 875 he joined, then led, a massive peasant rebellion, and in 880 captured Chang’an and declared himself emperor; he was defeated and died in 884. The poem is traditionally said to date from after his exam failure; its exact date is uncertain.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

待到秋来九月八，

dài dào qiū lái jiǔ yuè bā

Wait till autumn, the ninth month, the eighth day —

我花开后百花杀。

wǒ huā kāi hòu bǎi huā shā

when my flowers open, all others wither and die.

冲天香阵透长安，

chōng tiān xiāng zhèn tòu cháng ān

Their scent will storm the sky in ranks and pour through Chang’an,

满城尽带黄金甲。

mǎn chéng jìn dài huáng jīn jiǎ

till the whole city is clad in golden armour.
