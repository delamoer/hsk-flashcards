导读About this poem

A spring scene painted almost entirely with nouns: warm sun, soft breeze, willows, a swing, singing orioles, dancing swallows, a little bridge over flowing water. Simple words, a bright picture.

中文解析

白朴写过春、夏、秋、冬四首《天净沙》，这是第一首。全曲只有二十八个字，几乎全是名词，一个接一个排开，就像一幅画：春天的山、温暖的太阳、柔和的风；栏杆、楼阁、窗帘；院子里有杨柳和秋千；黄莺在叫，燕子在飞；小桥下流水潺潺，水面飘着落花（“飞红”）。不用动词连接，只把景物摆在一起，让读者自己去看、去感受，这种写法叫“列锦”，马致远的《天净沙·秋思》“枯藤老树昏鸦”也是这样。不同的是，《秋思》冷清悲凉，这首却明亮温暖，充满生机。最后的“飞红”是落花，暗示春天正在过去，热闹里带着一点淡淡的惜春之情。

In English

Bai Pu wrote four songs to the tune “Sky-Clear Sand”, one for each season; this is spring. It has just 28 characters, and almost all of them are nouns, laid out one after another like a painting: spring hills, warm sun, gentle breeze; railings, pavilions, window curtains; willows and a swing in the courtyard; orioles singing, swallows darting; a small bridge over flowing water, with fallen petals (“flying red”) drifting on it. There are hardly any verbs linking these images — the poet just sets them side by side and lets you look for yourself. This technique, a string of noun images, is the same one Ma Zhiyuan used in his famous “Autumn Thoughts” (“withered vines, old trees, crows at dusk”). The difference is the mood: Ma’s autumn is bleak and lonely, while this spring is bright, warm and full of life. Only the last image, “flying red” — falling petals — hints that spring is already passing, adding a light touch of regret to all the colour.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Pu (1226–after 1306) was one of the great Yuan playwrights, author of Rain on the Parasol Trees. As a boy he lost his family in the fall of the Jin capital and was raised for a time by the poet Yuan Haowen; he never took office under the Yuan.

春山暖日和风，

chūn shān nuǎn rì hé fēng

Spring hills, warm sun, a gentle breeze;

阑干楼阁帘栊，

lán gān lóu gé lián lóng

railings, towers, curtained windows;

杨柳秋千院中。

yáng liǔ qiū qiān yuàn zhōng

willows and a swing in the yard.

啼莺舞燕，

tí yīng wǔ yàn

Orioles singing, swallows dancing,

小桥流水飞红。

xiǎo qiáo liú shuǐ fēi hóng

a little bridge, running water, petals drifting down.
