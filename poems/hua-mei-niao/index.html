导读About this poem

A songbird singing freely in the woods sounds far better than one in a golden cage. A short, clear poem about why freedom matters more than comfort.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

画眉是一种叫声好听的鸟，很多人喜欢把它关在笼子里养。前两句写山林里的画眉：它们想怎么唱就怎么唱，叫声千变万化，在红红紫紫的山花和高高低低的树之间飞来飞去。后两句是作者的感想：直到听过被锁在金笼子里的画眉，才知道它们的叫声远远比不上在林子里自由自在地鸣叫。笼子再贵，也是笼子。这首诗用了“对比”的手法：林中和笼中、自由和束缚放在一起，道理就很清楚了。很多人认为，作者借鸟写自己——当时他被贬到滁州，远离朝廷，反而觉得山林生活更自在。

In English

The hwamei (literally “painted eyebrow”, for the white ring around its eye) is a songbird that people in China have long kept in cages for its beautiful voice. The first two lines show it in the wild: a hundred trills, a thousand calls, changing as it pleases, while it moves among red and purple mountain flowers and trees of every height. The last two lines give the poet’s conclusion: only after hearing a bird locked in a golden cage did he realise how much better it sings in the woods, free and at ease. However precious the cage, it is still a cage. The poem works by contrast — forest and cage, freedom and confinement, set side by side — and the lesson needs no explaining. Many readers think Ouyang Xiu was also talking about himself: written, it is often said, after he was demoted from the court to the small town of Chuzhou, where he came to enjoy the freedom of life in the hills.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Ouyang Xiu (1007–1072) was a leading statesman, historian and essayist of the Northern Song. The poem is traditionally dated to his years as prefect of Chuzhou (1045–1048), after he was sent away from the capital for political reasons.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

百啭千声随意移，

bǎi zhuàn qiān shēng suí yì yí

A hundred trills, a thousand calls, changing however it likes,

山花红紫树高低。

shān huā hóng zǐ shù gāo dī

among red and purple flowers and trees both tall and short.

始知锁向金笼听，

shǐ zhī suǒ xiàng jīn lóng tīng

Only now I see: locked in a golden cage, its song

不及林间自在啼。

bù jí lín jiān zì zài tí

is nothing like the free song it sings in the woods.
