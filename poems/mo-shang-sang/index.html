陌上桑

Mò Shàng Sāng
Mulberries by the Road

导读About this poem

How do you describe a beautiful woman without describing her face? This Han folk song shows Luofu’s beauty entirely through her basket, her clothes — and the farmers who forget to plough when she walks by.

01
日出东南隅，照我秦氏楼。

直译LiteralThe sun rises in the southeast corner and shines on our Qin family’s tower.

意译MeaningThe sun comes up in the southeast and lights the Qin family’s house.

02
秦氏有好女，自名为罗敷。

直译LiteralThe Qin family has a lovely daughter; her own name is Luofu.

意译MeaningThe Qins have a lovely daughter — Luofu is her name.

03
罗敷喜蚕桑，采桑城南隅。

直译LiteralLuofu likes silkworms and mulberries; she picks mulberry leaves at the south corner of the town.

意译MeaningLuofu loves her silkworms, and picks mulberry leaves south of town.

04
青丝为笼系，桂枝为笼钩。

直译LiteralGreen silk makes the basket cords; cassia branch makes the basket hook.

意译MeaningHer basket hangs on cords of green silk, from a hook of cassia wood.

05
头上倭堕髻，耳中明月珠。

直译LiteralOn her head, a drooping-to-one-side bun; in her ears, bright-moon pearls.

意译MeaningHer hair is swept into a stylish bun; moon-bright pearls hang from her ears.

06
缃绮为下裙，紫绮为上襦。

直译LiteralPale-yellow silk makes her lower skirt; purple silk makes her short upper jacket.

意译MeaningA skirt of pale-yellow silk, a jacket of purple silk.

07
行者见罗敷，下担捋髭须。

直译LiteralTravellers see Luofu, put down their loads and stroke their beards.

意译MeaningPassers-by catch sight of Luofu, set down their loads and stroke their beards.

08
少年见罗敷，脱帽著帩头。

直译LiteralYoung men see Luofu, take off their hats and show their headbands.

意译MeaningYoung men catch sight of Luofu, whip off their hats and fuss with their headbands.

09
耕者忘其犁，锄者忘其锄。

直译LiteralPloughmen forget their ploughs; hoers forget their hoes.

意译MeaningThe ploughman forgets his plough, the hoer forgets his hoe.

10
来归相怨怒，但坐观罗敷。

直译LiteralComing home, they are cross with each other — only because they watched Luofu.

意译MeaningBack home they quarrel with their wives — all for having stood and stared at Luofu.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《陌上桑》讲的是一个叫罗敷的采桑女子的故事。这里选的是第一段，写罗敷的美。开头说太阳从东南方升起，照着秦家的楼，秦家有个好女儿叫罗敷。她喜欢养蚕采桑，到城南去采桑叶。接下来写她的用具和打扮：青丝做的篮子绳，桂树枝做的篮子钩；头上梳着时髦的发髻，耳朵上戴着明月珠，穿着浅黄色的裙子、紫色的上衣。最妙的是后半段：诗人没有写罗敷长什么样，而是写看到她的人——走路的人放下担子摸胡子，年轻人脱下帽子整理头巾，耕地的忘了犁地，锄地的忘了锄草，回家后互相埋怨，只是因为看罗敷看呆了。这种写法叫“侧面描写”：不直接写一个人，而是通过别人的反应来表现她，让读者自己去想象她有多美。后面两段（本页未选）写一位官员想带走罗敷，被她机智地拒绝。

In English

This Han folk song tells the story of Luofu, a young woman who picks mulberry leaves to feed silkworms. The part chosen here is the first section, all about her beauty. The sun rises in the southeast and shines on the Qin family’s house, where there is a lovely daughter named Luofu. She loves raising silkworms and goes to pick mulberry leaves south of the town. First we see her things: a basket with cords of green silk and a hook of cassia wood, a fashionable hair bun, pearls in her ears, a pale-yellow silk skirt and a purple jacket. Then comes the clever part. The poet never describes her face — he describes the people who see her. Travellers put down their loads and stroke their beards; young men take off their hats to fix their headbands; ploughmen forget their ploughs, hoers forget their hoes, and back home they bicker — all because they stood staring at Luofu. Showing someone through other people’s reactions, so readers imagine the rest, is called “indirect description”. In the rest of the song (not included here) a high official tries to take Luofu away, and she turns him down with sharp wit.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

An anonymous Han-dynasty folk song from the Yuefu tradition, recorded in the Song shu and the Yutai xinyong and later in the Yuefu shiji, where it is titled “Song of the Sun Rising in the Southeast Corner” (日出东南隅行). It is one of the best-loved narrative poems of the Han.

版本说明 About the text

节选第一段（“日出东南隅”至“但坐观罗敷”，共二十句）。全诗三段五十三句，后两段写使君调戏罗敷、罗敷夸夫拒绝。采用通行的《乐府诗集》文本；“罗敷喜蚕桑”，《玉台新咏》作“罗敷善蚕桑”，另有“自言名罗敷”“青丝为笼绳”“绿绮为下裙”“脱巾著帩头”等异文，网站从通行的《乐府诗集》文本。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

日出东南隅，照我秦氏楼。

rì chū dōng nán yú, zhào wǒ qín shì lóu

The sun comes up in the southeast and lights the Qin family’s house.

秦氏有好女，自名为罗敷。

qín shì yǒu hǎo nǚ, zì míng wéi luó fū

The Qins have a lovely daughter — Luofu is her name.

罗敷喜蚕桑，采桑城南隅。

luó fū xǐ cán sāng, cǎi sāng chéng nán yú

Luofu loves her silkworms, and picks mulberry leaves south of town.

青丝为笼系，桂枝为笼钩。

qīng sī wéi lóng xì, guì zhī wéi lóng gōu

Her basket hangs on cords of green silk, from a hook of cassia wood.

头上倭堕髻，耳中明月珠。

tóu shàng wō duò jì, ěr zhōng míng yuè zhū

Her hair is swept into a stylish bun; moon-bright pearls hang from her ears.

缃绮为下裙，紫绮为上襦。

xiāng qǐ wéi xià qún, zǐ qǐ wéi shàng rú

A skirt of pale-yellow silk, a jacket of purple silk.

行者见罗敷，下担捋髭须。

xíng zhě jiàn luó fū, xià dàn lǚ zī xū

Passers-by catch sight of Luofu, set down their loads and stroke their beards.

少年见罗敷，脱帽著帩头。

shào nián jiàn luó fū, tuō mào zhuó qiào tóu

Young men catch sight of Luofu, whip off their hats and fuss with their headbands.

耕者忘其犁，锄者忘其锄。

gēng zhě wàng qí lí, chú zhě wàng qí chú

The ploughman forgets his plough, the hoer forgets his hoe.

来归相怨怒，但坐观罗敷。

lái guī xiāng yuàn nù, dàn zuò guān luó fū

Back home they quarrel with their wives — all for having stood and stared at Luofu.
