导读About this poem

Red beans grow in the south, and the Chinese call them “longing seeds”. The poet asks a friend to pick plenty of them — because nothing holds longing better.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

红豆是南方的一种植物，种子鲜红，传说古代一位女子在树下思念远方的丈夫，哭死在树下，化成了红豆，所以红豆又叫“相思子”。诗人先说红豆长在南方，再轻轻问一句：春天来了，它又长出几枝了？问的是红豆，其实是在想远方的人。第三句劝朋友：多摘一些吧；最后一句说出原因：这东西最能代表思念。全诗没有一个字直接说“我想你”，而是把感情放在一个小小的红豆上，这叫“托物寄情”——借一个东西来表达感情。正因为说得含蓄，所以读起来更深情。后来“红豆”成了中国人表达思念和爱情的固定意象，今天的歌曲里也常唱到。

In English

Red beans (hóngdòu) grow in southern China. Their seeds are a brilliant red, and a legend says a woman who wept to death under a tree while longing for her absent husband turned into these seeds — so they are also called “longing seeds”. The poem starts with a fact: red beans grow in the south. Then it asks, lightly, how many new branches have grown this spring? The question is about the plant, but the poet is really thinking of someone far away. The third line urges the friend: pick lots of them. The last line explains why: nothing stands for longing better. The poem never says “I miss you”. Instead it puts all the feeling into one small red seed — a technique called tuō wù jì qíng, “entrusting feelings to an object”. Because the feeling is held back, it feels deeper. Ever since, the red bean has been the classic Chinese image of longing and love, and it still appears in pop songs today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) was a poet, painter and musician, famous for quiet landscape poems. This poem is traditionally said to have been sent to a friend, the musician Li Guinian; it was a popular song in the Tang dynasty.

红豆生南国，

hóng dòu shēng nán guó

Red beans grow in the south —

chūn lái fā jǐ zhī

how many new sprigs this spring?

yuàn jūn duō cǎi xié

Gather plenty for me, friend:

此物最相思。

cǐ wù zuì xiāng sī

nothing holds longing like these.
