导读About this poem

Four lines from the far north-west frontier: clouds over snow mountains, a lonely fort, armour worn through by sand — and a soldier’s vow not to go home until the war is won.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写守卫西北边疆的士兵。前两句是一幅很大的画面：青海湖上空的长云，把远处的雪山都遮暗了；士兵站在孤城上，远远望着玉门关。青海、雪山、孤城、玉门关，几个地名一连串排开，写出边疆的辽阔和荒凉。第三句“黄沙百战穿金甲”是全诗最有力的一句：在黄沙里打了上百次仗，连铁甲都磨穿了，可见战争多么长久、多么艰苦。最后一句突然一转，士兵发誓：“不打败敌人，绝不回家。”前面越写艰苦，后面的誓言就越有力量。“楼兰”本是汉代西域的一个小国，这里借指侵扰边境的敌人——用古代的名字说今天的事，是唐诗常见的写法。全诗只有二十八个字，有景、有人、有决心，是盛唐边塞诗的代表作。

In English

This poem is about soldiers guarding China’s north-western frontier. The first two lines paint a huge landscape: long clouds over Qinghai Lake darken the snow mountains, and from a lonely fort a soldier looks far off toward the Jade Gate Pass. The string of place names — Qinghai, snow mountains, lonely fort, Jade Gate — makes the frontier feel vast and empty. The third line is the strongest in the poem: after a hundred battles in the yellow sand, even their metal armour has been worn through. That one image tells you how long and hard the war has been. Then the last line turns sharply into a vow: “Until we break Loulan, we will never go home.” The harder the first three lines make life sound, the more weight the vow carries. Loulan was a small kingdom in the Western Regions back in the Han dynasty, centuries earlier; Tang poets often used old Han names to talk about their own day, so here it simply means “the enemy”. In twenty-eight characters the poem gives you a landscape, a soldier and his resolve — a classic of High Tang frontier poetry.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Changling (c. 698–757) was a leading poet of the High Tang, famous above all for his seven-character quatrains, and was called the “Master of the Seven-Character Quatrain”. “Joining the Army” is a set of seven frontier poems; this is the fourth.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

无特殊读音。注意：长云 cháng；不还 huán（回来，不读 hái）。

版本说明 About the text

采用统编教材（五年级下册）文本，与《全唐诗》一致。《全唐诗》注异文：“玉门关”一作“雁门关”，“终不还”一作“竟不还”。组诗共七首，这是第四首。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

青海长云暗雪山，

qīng hǎi cháng yún àn xuě shān

Long clouds over Qinghai dim the snowy peaks;

孤城遥望玉门关。

gū chéng yáo wàng yù mén guān

from a lone fort I look far out to the Jade Gate Pass.

黄沙百战穿金甲，

huáng shā bǎi zhàn chuān jīn jiǎ

A hundred battles in the yellow sand have worn our armour thin,

不破楼兰终不还。

bù pò lóu lán zhōng bù huán

but till Loulan falls, not one of us goes home.
