导读About this poem

One moonlit autumn night in Chang’an, every household is beating cloth to make winter clothes for husbands at the frontier. Six lines turn a whole city’s longing into a single sound.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写唐代长安城里妻子们思念远方戍边丈夫的心情。开头两句一个写看到的，一个写听到的：城里一片月光，千家万户都传来“捣衣”的声音——古代做冬衣前，要把布料放在石头上用木棒捶打，所以秋天的捣衣声总让人想起寄给远方的寒衣。第三四句说秋风吹也吹不完，阵阵风声里全是对玉门关外亲人的思念，把看不见的感情写得像风一样到处都是。最后两句直接说出愿望：什么时候才能平定敌人，丈夫不再远征？前四句含蓄，后两句直白，像一声叹息。这首诗用的是南方民歌“子夜歌”的旧题，语言简单，却写出了一个时代普通人的痛苦。

In English

This poem is about the wives of Chang’an whose husbands are away guarding the far frontier. The first two lines give one thing seen and one thing heard: a single sheet of moonlight over the city, and from ten thousand homes the thud of 捣衣 — beating cloth. Before sewing winter clothes, women pounded the fabric on a stone with a wooden stick, so in Chinese poetry that autumn sound always means clothes being made for someone far away. Lines three and four say the autumn wind can never blow it all away: every gust carries feelings for the men beyond Jade Gate Pass. Longing, which cannot be seen, is made to fill the air like the wind itself. Then the last two lines drop all imagery and simply ask: when will the enemy be defeated, so my husband can stop fighting far away? The quiet first half and the plain cry at the end feel like a held breath and then a sigh. Li Bai borrowed an old southern folk-song title, “Midnight Songs”, and kept its simple, songlike language — but used it to speak for a whole generation of ordinary families.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762) wrote a set of four “Midnight Songs of Wu”, one for each season; this is the autumn one. In his time the Tang empire kept large armies on its northwestern borders, and many families were separated for years.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

长安一片月，

cháng ān yī piàn yuè

One wide sheet of moonlight over Chang’an,

万户捣衣声。

wàn hù dǎo yī shēng

and from every house, the thud of cloth being beaten.

秋风吹不尽，

qiū fēng chuī bù jìn

The autumn wind keeps blowing and never blows it away:

总是玉关情。

zǒng shì yù guān qíng

all this longing for the men at Jade Gate Pass.

何日平胡虏，

hé rì píng hú lǔ

When will the raiders be beaten at last,

良人罢远征。

liáng rén bà yuǎn zhēng

and my husband come home from that faraway war?
