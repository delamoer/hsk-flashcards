清平乐（红笺小字）

A Letter That Cannot Be Sent (to the tune “Clear and Peaceful Music”)

导读About this poem

Someone writes a love letter in tiny characters on red paper, pouring out a whole life’s feelings — but there is no goose or fish to carry it. Alone in a tower at sunset, the writer watches the river flow east, still searching for a face that is gone.

红笺小字，说尽平生意。

直译LiteralSmall characters on red letter paper say all the feelings of a lifetime.

意译MeaningIn tiny characters on red paper I have written all my heart.

直译LiteralThe wild goose is in the clouds, the fish in the water — sadly, this feeling is hard to send.

意译MeaningBut the goose keeps to the clouds, the fish to the water, and this aching love has no way to travel.

03

直译LiteralIn the slanting sun I lean alone in the west tower; distant hills face exactly the curtain hook.

直译LiteralThat face — I do not know where it is; the green waves, as before, flow east.

意译MeaningThat face — I don’t know where it is now; only the green river keeps flowing east.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

上阕写写信：在红色的信纸上用小字写满了一生的情意，可是写好了又怎样呢？鸿雁在云里飞，鱼儿在水中游，都不来替我送信，心中的惆怅没法寄出去。古人相信鸿雁和鲤鱼能传递书信，所以“雁”和“鱼”在诗词里常代表书信。诗人反过来写它们“在云”“在水”、各自逍遥，写出音信不通的无奈。下阕写望人：夕阳西下，我独自靠在西楼上，远处的青山正好对着窗帘的挂钩，好像也在一起等待。那个人现在不知在什么地方，只有绿水依旧向东流去。这里化用了唐诗“人面不知何处去，桃花依旧笑春风”，用不变的流水衬托人已不在。全词没有一个“想”字，却处处都是思念，语言含蓄而有余味。

In English

The first stanza is about writing a letter. On small sheets of red paper, in tiny characters, the writer has said everything that has been in their heart all their life. But what use is it? The wild goose is up in the clouds and the fish is down in the water, and neither will carry the letter, so all that longing has nowhere to go. In old China people believed geese and carp could carry messages, so in poetry “goose” and “fish” often simply mean a letter. Here the poet turns that around: the goose and the fish are just going about their own business, and the letter stays where it is. The second stanza is about waiting. At sunset the writer leans alone on the rail of the west tower; far-off hills sit exactly opposite the curtain hook, as though they were waiting too. That face — where is it now? Only the green water flows east as it always has. The line borrows from a famous Tang poem in which a man returns to find the girl gone but the peach blossoms still smiling. The poem never says “I miss you”, yet longing is in every image, and it lingers after the last line.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Yan Shu (991–1055) was a child prodigy who rose to chief minister under Emperor Renzong of the Song, and a leading writer of short, elegant ci lyrics. The occasion of this one is not known; in the ci tradition the speaker may be the poet himself or an imagined lover.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

词牌“清平乐”的“乐”一般读 yuè（音乐的乐），也有人读 lè，本站采用 yuè。其他：红笺 jiān；斜阳 xié yáng；惆怅 chóu chàng。
