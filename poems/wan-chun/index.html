导读About this poem

Spring is about to leave, and every plant rushes to show off its colours — even the plain willow catkins, which have no talent but fill the sky like snow.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写暮春时节，用的是拟人手法——把植物写得像人一样会想、会做。花草树木好像知道春天快要走了，纷纷拿出最美的红色、紫色来“斗”，比谁更香更艳。后两句写杨花和榆荚：它们没有鲜艳的颜色，好像“没有才思”，却不甘落后，只知道满天飞舞，像下雪一样。这两句的意思有不同理解：有人说是笑杨花榆荚没本事；也有人说是赞美它们虽然平凡，却也大胆地参与春天，有自己的美。正因为可以有多种读法，这首小诗显得很有趣。读的时候可以想一想：你更喜欢哪种理解？

In English

This poem about the last days of spring uses personification — plants that think and act like people. The grass and trees seem to know spring will soon be gone, so they bring out their best reds and purples and compete (斗, literally “fight”) to be the brightest. The last two lines turn to willow catkins and elm seeds. They have no bright colours — no “talent”, the poet jokes — but they won’t be left out either; all they know how to do is whirl across the sky like snow. Readers have understood these lines in different ways. Some think Han Yu is laughing at the catkins for having nothing to offer. Others think he is praising them: plain as they are, they join in spring boldly and have a beauty of their own. Some even read it as a comment on writers who show off without real talent. Because the poem leaves room for all these readings, it stays lively and fun to discuss. Which reading do you like best?

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Han Yu (768–824) was a leading Tang writer and thinker, famous for championing a plain “ancient style” of prose. This is the first of two poems titled “Late Spring”, probably written in his later years in Chang’an.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

草树知春不久归，

cǎo shù zhī chūn bù jiǔ guī

Grass and trees can tell that spring will soon be leaving,

百般红紫斗芳菲。

bǎi bān hóng zǐ dòu fāng fēi

so every red and purple flower competes to be the loveliest.

杨花榆荚无才思，

yáng huā yú jiá wú cái sī

The willow fluff and elm seeds have no gift for colour —

惟解漫天作雪飞。

wéi jiě màn tiān zuò xuě fēi

all they know is to fill the sky, flying like snow.
