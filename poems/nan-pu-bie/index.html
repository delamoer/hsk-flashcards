导读About this poem

An autumn farewell by the water in twenty simple characters: every look back breaks the heart, so — go well, and don’t turn around.

中文解析

“南浦”本义是南边的水边，自从古代楚辞里写“送美人兮南浦”，它就成了送别地点的代称。第一、二句写环境：在南浦凄凉地分别，秋天的西风轻轻吹着。“凄凄”“袅袅”两个叠词，读起来缓慢绵长，就像离别时舍不得走的心情。第三句很特别：“一看肠一断”——每回头看一次，心就碎一次。第四句于是劝对方：“好好走吧，别回头了！”不回头不是不在乎，而是太在乎，怕再看一眼更难分开。语言像说话一样朴素，却把离别的痛写得很真。

In English

南浦 literally means “the southern shore”. Ever since an ancient Chu poem said “I see you off at the southern shore”, the phrase has stood for any place of farewell, the way “the station platform” might in English. The first two lines set the scene: a sad parting by the water, an autumn west wind blowing softly. The doubled words 凄凄 (“mournful”) and 袅袅 (“gently drifting”) slow the rhythm down, like people who can’t bring themselves to leave. Line 3 is striking: 一看肠一断, “one look, one broken heart” — every time the traveller looks back, the heart breaks again (Chinese speaks of the “gut” breaking with grief). So line 4 urges: “Go well — don’t look back!” Not looking back doesn’t mean not caring; it means caring so much that one more glance would make parting impossible. The language is as plain as everyday speech, yet it gets the pain of saying goodbye exactly right.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) was famous for writing poems so plain that, legend says, he read them to an old woman and revised any line she couldn’t understand. When and to whom he wrote this farewell is not known.
