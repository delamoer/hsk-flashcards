导读About this poem

A little child sneaks out in a boat to pick white lotus — and gives the game away, because the boat leaves a clear path through the duckweed. A tiny comic scene told in twenty characters.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗像一个小故事。一个小孩撑着一条小船，偷偷去池塘里采白莲花，采完划回来。他以为没人知道，可是他不懂得藏起自己的踪迹：水面上漂满了浮萍，小船划过的地方，浮萍被分开，留下了一道清清楚楚的水路——这就把他“出卖”了。诗人用“偷采”和“不解”两个词，写出小孩的调皮和天真：做“坏事”很认真，却完全没想到会被发现。最后一句“浮萍一道开”是一个很有画面感的细节，读者仿佛站在岸边，看着那条绿色中间的水路笑了。整首诗语言浅白，像随手画的一幅小画，充满童趣，也能看出诗人看孩子时那种温和、喜爱的心情。

In English

This poem tells a tiny story. A small child poles a little boat out onto the pond, picks some white lotus flowers on the sly, and rows back. The child thinks no one will know — but doesn’t know how to hide the evidence. The water is covered in floating duckweed, and wherever the boat passed, the duckweed has parted, leaving a clear open lane behind it. That trail gives the secret away. Two words carry the humour: 偷采 (“picking on the sly”) and 不解 (“didn’t know how to”). The child is very serious about the mischief and never imagines being found out. The last line, “a path opens through the duckweed”, is a sharp visual detail: you feel you are standing on the bank, looking at that lane through the green and smiling. The language is plain, like a quick sketch, full of a child’s fun — and you can feel the poet’s gentle fondness as he watches.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Bai Juyi (772–846) spent his last years in Luoyang, where his house had a garden with a pond. This is the second of two short poems titled “On the Pond”, traditionally dated to his old age there.
