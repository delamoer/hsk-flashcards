导读About this poem

Alone in a bamboo grove, the poet plays his zither and whistles long into the night. No one knows he is there — except the moon, who comes to keep him company.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗也是王维写辋川别墅的一组诗中的一首。诗人一个人坐在幽深的竹林里，一会儿弹琴，一会儿撮起嘴唇发出长长的声音（“长啸”是古人抒发心情的一种方式）。第三句说：树林这么深，没有人知道我在这里；第四句说：只有明月来照着我。别人不知道，月亮却知道，月亮好像成了朋友。全诗用的都是很平常的字：坐、弹、啸、照，没有一个字写心情，可是读完能感觉到诗人的安静、自在。这里的孤独不是难过，而是一个人享受安静的快乐。和《独坐敬亭山》的“只有敬亭山”比一比：李白有山作伴，王维有月作伴，两首诗都把自然当作知己。

In English

This is another of Wang Wei’s poems about his estate at Wangchuan. The poet sits alone deep in a bamboo grove, sometimes playing the qin (a seven-string zither), sometimes giving a long whistle — xiào, a drawn-out sound made through pursed lips, which ancient scholars used to release their feelings. The third line says the forest is so deep that no one knows he is there; the fourth, that only the bright moon comes to shine on him. People don’t know, but the moon does, and it becomes his companion. The words are all ordinary — sit, play, whistle, shine — and none of them names a feeling, yet the poem leaves you with his calm and freedom. This solitude isn’t sad: it is the pleasure of being alone and quiet. Compare it with Li Bai’s “Sitting Alone Facing Jingting Mountain”: Li Bai has a mountain for company, Wang Wei has the moon. Both turn nature into a friend who understands.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701?–761) was a poet, painter, musician and Buddhist. The Bamboo Lodge was one of the twenty sights on his Wangchuan estate south of Chang’an, each celebrated in a short poem.
