葬花吟

Zàng Huā Yín
Song of Burying Flowers

导读About this poem

In Dream of the Red Chamber, the lonely, sensitive Lin Daiyu gathers fallen petals and buries them, weeping as she sings about her own fate. These are the opening and closing lines of her song, famous in China through the 1987 TV series.

第一段Part 1
01
花谢花飞花满天，红消香断有谁怜？

直译LiteralFlowers fade, flowers fly, flowers fill the sky; the red fades, the scent is cut off — who pities them?

意译MeaningPetals wither, petals fly, petals fill the sky — their colour gone, their scent gone, who feels for them?

02
游丝软系飘春榭，落絮轻沾扑绣帘。

直译LiteralFloating threads softly tie onto the spring pavilion; falling catkins lightly stick to and brush the embroidered curtain.

意译MeaningGossamer drifts and clings to the spring pavilion; catkins settle softly on the embroidered blinds.

03
闺中女儿惜春暮，愁绪满怀无释处。

直译LiteralThe girl in her chamber grieves that spring is late; her heart full of sorrow, with nowhere to let it go.

意译MeaningA girl in her room mourns the end of spring, her heart full of sorrow with nowhere to set it down.

04
手把花锄出绣帘，忍踏落花来复去。

直译LiteralHolding a flower hoe she goes out of the curtain; could she bear to tread on fallen flowers, coming and going?

意译MeaningHoe in hand, she steps out past the curtain — how can she bear to walk on fallen petals, back and forth?

第二段Part 2
05
尔今死去侬收葬，未卜侬身何日丧？

直译LiteralNow you die and I gather and bury you; not yet known — on what day will I die?

意译MeaningNow you have died, and I bury you; who can tell on what day I will die?

06
侬今葬花人笑痴，他年葬侬知是谁？

直译LiteralToday I bury flowers and people laugh at my foolishness; in another year, who will it be that buries me?

意译MeaningToday I bury flowers and people call me foolish — some year to come, who will bury me?

07
试看春残花渐落，便是红颜老死时。

直译LiteralJust look: spring wanes and flowers gradually fall — that is the time when a fair face grows old and dies.

意译MeaningLook — as spring fades and petals slowly fall, so a young face grows old and dies.

08
一朝春尽红颜老，花落人亡两不知！

直译LiteralOne morning spring is over and the fair face is old; flowers fallen, person gone — neither knows!

意译MeaningOne day spring is gone and beauty has grown old; the flower falls, the girl is gone, and neither knows the other’s end.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《葬花吟》出自小说《红楼梦》第二十七回，是作者曹雪芹替书中的林黛玉写的诗。黛玉从小失去父母，住在亲戚家，敏感又孤单。春天快过去了，她看到花瓣满天飘落，觉得没有人怜惜它们，就用花锄把落花收起来埋掉，一边埋一边哭着唱出这首诗。开头写满天的落花、飘着的游丝和柳絮，春天的美正在消失；接着写一个舍不得春天的女孩，拿着花锄走来走去，不忍心踩到落花。结尾是全诗最感人的地方：今天我埋葬花，别人笑我傻；将来我死了，又有谁来埋葬我呢？春天走完的时候，花落了，人也老了、死了，两边都不知道彼此。黛玉写花，其实是在写自己：花的命运就是她的命运。这首诗也暗示了她后来的悲剧。1987年电视剧《红楼梦》把它谱成插曲，非常有名。

In English

This song comes from chapter 27 of Dream of the Red Chamber, the great 18th-century Chinese novel. Cao Xueqin wrote it for his heroine Lin Daiyu. Daiyu has lost her parents and lives with relatives; she is gifted, sensitive and very lonely. As spring ends, she sees petals filling the sky and feels that no one pities them, so she takes a little hoe, gathers the fallen flowers and buries them, weeping and singing this song. The opening shows spring’s beauty slipping away: petals everywhere, drifting threads of gossamer, willow catkins brushing against embroidered curtains. Then we see a girl who cannot bear to let spring go, walking back and forth with her hoe, unwilling to step on the fallen flowers. The ending is the most moving part. Today I bury flowers and people laugh at me for being foolish — but one day, who will bury me? When spring is over, the flowers fall and the girl grows old and dies, and neither will know what became of the other. Daiyu sings about flowers, but she is really singing about herself: their fate is hers. The song also hints at the tragic end waiting for her in the novel. The 1987 TV series set these lines to music, and the song is still well known. Note the word 侬 (“I”), an old southern word Daiyu uses for herself.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Cao Xueqin (c. 1715–c. 1763) wrote Dream of the Red Chamber, the story of a great family’s decline, in poverty near Beijing; the song is “by” his heroine Lin Daiyu. In the 1987 CCTV series the song was set to music by Wang Liping and sung by Chen Li.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

谁 shuí（诗中读 shuí，口语读 shéi）；软系 ruǎn xì；春榭 chūn xiè；落絮 luò xù；花锄 huā chú；侬 nóng（我）；未卜 wèi bǔ；何日丧 sàng；一朝 yī zhāo（不读 cháo）；便是 biàn shì。

版本说明 About the text

节选。全诗通行本五十二句，这里选了开头八句（“花谢花飞花满天”至“忍踏落花来复去”，第1段）和结尾八句（“尔今死去侬收葬”至“花落人亡两不知”，第2段），中间省略。文本采用通行的人民文学出版社本（以庚辰本为底本），与1987年电视剧插曲歌词一致。异文：“花谢花飞花满天”一作“花谢花飞飞满天”；“手把花锄出绣帘”一作“出绣闺”。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

花谢花飞花满天，红消香断有谁怜？

huā xiè huā fēi huā mǎn tiān, hóng xiāo xiāng duàn yǒu shuí lián

Petals wither, petals fly, petals fill the sky — their colour gone, their scent gone, who feels for them?

游丝软系飘春榭，落絮轻沾扑绣帘。

yóu sī ruǎn xì piāo chūn xiè, luò xù qīng zhān pū xiù lián

Gossamer drifts and clings to the spring pavilion; catkins settle softly on the embroidered blinds.

闺中女儿惜春暮，愁绪满怀无释处。

guī zhōng nǚ ér xī chūn mù, chóu xù mǎn huái wú shì chù

A girl in her room mourns the end of spring, her heart full of sorrow with nowhere to set it down.

手把花锄出绣帘，忍踏落花来复去。

shǒu bǎ huā chú chū xiù lián, rěn tà luò huā lái fù qù

Hoe in hand, she steps out past the curtain — how can she bear to walk on fallen petals, back and forth?

尔今死去侬收葬，未卜侬身何日丧？

ěr jīn sǐ qù nóng shōu zàng, wèi bǔ nóng shēn hé rì sàng

Now you have died, and I bury you; who can tell on what day I will die?

侬今葬花人笑痴，他年葬侬知是谁？

nóng jīn zàng huā rén xiào chī, tā nián zàng nóng zhī shì shuí

Today I bury flowers and people call me foolish — some year to come, who will bury me?

试看春残花渐落，便是红颜老死时。

shì kàn chūn cán huā jiàn luò, biàn shì hóng yán lǎo sǐ shí

Look — as spring fades and petals slowly fall, so a young face grows old and dies.

一朝春尽红颜老，花落人亡两不知！

yī zhāo chūn jìn hóng yán lǎo, huā luò rén wáng liǎng bù zhī

One day spring is gone and beauty has grown old; the flower falls, the girl is gone, and neither knows the other’s end.
