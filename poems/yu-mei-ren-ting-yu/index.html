导读About this poem

A whole life in three scenes, each one simply “listening to the rain”: as a carefree youth, as a wandering adult, and as an old man in a monastery. A short poem that feels like a long life.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首词用“听雨”串起人的一生，选了三个画面。少年时，在歌楼上听雨，红烛、罗帐，热闹又浪漫，雨声只是背景。中年时，在旅途的小船上听雨，江面宽阔，乌云低垂，一只失群的孤雁在西风里叫——这是漂泊和孤独。老年时，在寺庙的僧房下听雨，鬓角已经斑白。最后说：人生的悲欢离合，本来就是无情的，就让台阶前的雨一滴一滴下到天亮吧。三个场景，三种心情，同一种雨声，听的人却变了。这种把一生压缩在几个画面里的写法，非常有力。结尾看似平静、“不在乎”，其实是经历了太多以后的无奈。作者经历了宋朝灭亡，后半生漂泊隐居，词里也有国破家亡的影子。

In English

This ci strings a whole life together with one repeated act: listening to the rain. It gives us three pictures. As a youth, he hears rain from a singing house — red candles, silk bed-curtains, everything warm and romantic; the rain is just background music. In middle age, he hears it on a boat far from home: a wide river, low clouds, and a lone wild goose, separated from its flock, crying in the west wind — a picture of drifting and loneliness. Now, old, he hears it under the eaves of a monk’s lodging, his temples already flecked with white. And the ending: joy and grief, meeting and parting — none of it cares about us. So let the rain on the steps drip on, drop by drop, until dawn. Three scenes, three moods; the rain sounds the same, but the listener has changed. Compressing a lifetime into a few snapshots gives the poem great force. The calm of the ending is not indifference but the weariness of someone who has lived through too much — Jiang Jie saw the Song dynasty fall and spent his later years wandering and in hiding.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Jiang Jie (c. 1245–c. 1305) passed the top civil examination in 1274, just before the Mongol conquest ended the Southern Song. He refused to serve the Yuan and lived out his life in retreat; this poem is usually read as looking back from old age.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

少年听雨歌楼上，

shào nián tīng yǔ gē lóu shàng

Young, I heard the rain from a singing-house upstairs,

红烛昏罗帐。

hóng zhú hūn luó zhàng

red candlelight soft on silk curtains.

壮年听雨客舟中，

zhuàng nián tīng yǔ kè zhōu zhōng

In my prime, I heard it from a boat far from home —

江阔云低，断雁叫西风。

jiāng kuò yún dī, duàn yàn jiào xī fēng

wide river, low clouds, a lost goose crying into the west wind.

而今听雨僧庐下，

ér jīn tīng yǔ sēng lú xià

Now I hear it under a monastery’s eaves,

鬓已星星也。

bìn yǐ xīng xīng yě

my hair already flecked with grey.

悲欢离合总无情，

bēi huān lí hé zǒng wú qíng

Grief and joy, partings and meetings — none of them care.

一任阶前，点滴到天明。

yī rèn jiē qián, diǎn dī dào tiān míng

Let the rain on the steps drip on, drop by drop, till dawn.
