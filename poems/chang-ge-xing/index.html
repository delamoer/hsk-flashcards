导读About this poem

Garden plants glowing in spring, rivers that never flow back west — and then the famous warning: “If you don’t work hard while you’re young, you’ll grieve in vain when you’re old.” Chinese parents still quote it to their children.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗从园子里的葵菜写起：早上叶子上挂着露水，等着太阳出来把它晒干。春天把阳光和雨水洒给大地，万物都闪着光彩，生机勃勃。可是诗人马上担心起来：秋天一到，花和叶子就会变黄、枯萎。接着又写到江河——成百条河流都向东流进大海，什么时候见过它们流回西边呢？植物有盛衰，流水不回头，都是在说时间一去不返。最后两句才说出道理：“少壮不努力，老大徒伤悲。”年轻时不努力，老了只能白白后悔。前面用景物打比方，最后点明道理，这种写法叫“托物言理”，道理就不显得生硬。这两句今天已经成了中国人最常说的劝学名言之一。

In English

The poem begins in a garden: green mallow plants hold morning dew on their leaves, waiting for the sun to dry it. Spring pours out warmth and rain, and everything glows with life. But the poet immediately worries: when autumn comes, the flowers and leaves will turn yellow and wither. Then he turns to rivers — a hundred of them all flow east into the sea, and who has ever seen one flow back west? Plants that bloom and fade, water that never returns: both are pictures of time that cannot be won back. Only in the last two lines does the poem state its point: “If you don’t work hard while young and strong, you will grieve in vain when old.” Using images first and saving the lesson for the end makes the advice feel natural rather than preachy — a technique Chinese teachers call “using things to express a truth” (托物言理). Those last two lines have become one of the most quoted sayings about study and effort in Chinese; parents and teachers still use them today.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is an anonymous Han dynasty yuefu song, preserved in Guo Maoqian’s Collection of Yuefu Poems (12th century) under “Songs in Concert” (相和歌辞). “Long song” (长歌) probably names the style of singing — long, drawn-out notes — rather than the length of the poem.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

朝露的“朝”读 zhāo（早晨）；晞 xī（晒干）；焜黄 kūn huáng；华叶的“华”读 huā（同“花”）；少壮的“少”读 shào（年轻）。华叶衰的“衰”现行教材和通行读本注 shuāi；为押韵（与晞、辉、归、悲同韵）旧读 cuī；此处采用 shuāi。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

青青园中葵，朝露待日晞。

qīng qīng yuán zhōng kuí, zhāo lù dài rì xī

Green mallows fill the garden, their morning dew waiting for the sun.

阳春布德泽，万物生光辉。

yáng chūn bù dé zé, wàn wù shēng guāng huī

Warm spring pours out its kindness, and everything shines with life.

常恐秋节至，焜黄华叶衰。

cháng kǒng qiū jié zhì, kūn huáng huā yè shuāi

Yet I always fear the coming of autumn, when flowers and leaves fade to yellow.

百川东到海，何时复西归？

bǎi chuān dōng dào hǎi, hé shí fù xī guī

A hundred rivers run east to the sea — when have they ever flowed back west?

少壮不努力，老大徒伤悲。

shào zhuàng bù nǔ lì, lǎo dà tú shāng bēi

Work hard while you are young, or grieve for nothing when you are old.
