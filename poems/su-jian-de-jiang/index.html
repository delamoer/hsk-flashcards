宿建德江

Sù Jiàndé Jiāng
Mooring for the Night on the Jiande River
孟浩然Mèng Hàorán · Meng Haoran唐 Tang

导读About this poem

A boat ties up at dusk by a misty river island, and a traveller’s loneliness comes back fresh. Only twenty characters, but the last two lines — the sky sinking below the trees, the moon coming close in the water — are among the most admired scenes in Tang poetry.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

诗人坐船漂泊在外，傍晚把船停靠在江中一个雾气笼罩的小洲旁。“日暮客愁新”是全诗的关键：天一黑，旅人的愁绪又重新涌上来。后两句只写景，却把愁写得更深。原野空旷，远远望去，天空好像比树还低；江水清澈，水中的月亮倒影好像靠近了人。“低”和“近”写的是人眼中的真实感受，空旷的天地衬出一个人的渺小，而能“亲近”他的，只有水里的月亮——这就是“借景抒情”：不直接说孤独，而让景物替人说话。全诗二十个字，前半叙事，后半写景，平静的画面里藏着很深的寂寞。

In English

The poet is travelling by boat, far from home. At dusk he moors beside a small island wrapped in river mist. The key line is the second: “the day grows dark, and a traveller’s sorrow is new again” — every evening the loneliness returns as if for the first time. The last two lines say nothing about feelings at all; they simply describe what he sees, and that is exactly how they express the feeling. The open plain is so wide that, looking far off, the sky seems to sit lower than the trees. The river is so clear that the moon’s reflection seems to come right up to him. “Low” and “near” are what the eye actually experiences, not what is literally true. A vast, empty world makes one person look very small, and the only thing that comes close to keep him company is the moon in the water. Chinese critics call this technique “expressing feeling through scenery” (借景抒情): let the landscape speak for the heart. Notice the shape of the poem: two lines of story, then two lines of pure picture, with the sadness hidden inside the calm.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Meng Haoran (689–740) came from Xiangyang in today’s Hubei and spent most of his life as a recluse after failing to win an official post. He is traditionally thought to have written this while wandering in the southeast after that disappointment; the Jiande River is a stretch of the Xin’an River in today’s Zhejiang.

移舟泊烟渚，
