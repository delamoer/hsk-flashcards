导读About this poem

A bamboo bites into a crack in the rock and holds on through every storm. Written beside a painting by Zheng Banqiao, China’s best-loved painter of bamboo, it is a short, vivid picture of toughness.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是郑板桥题在自己画的竹石图上的一首诗。第一句用了一个很生动的词“咬定”：竹子像用牙咬住一样，死死抓住青山不放——这是拟人的写法，把竹子当成人来写。第二句说明原因：它的根本来就扎在破裂的岩石缝里，环境很艰苦。后两句写它经受了无数次的折磨和打击，还是那么坚韧挺拔；不管东西南北哪边吹来的风，随你吹吧！“任尔”两个字写出一种不在乎、不服输的气概。诗人写竹子，其实也在写自己：做人要有坚定的立场，不怕困难，不随风倒。语言很口语化，节奏有力，读起来像一句口号，所以“咬定青山不放松”今天常被用来鼓励人坚持目标。

In English

Zheng Banqiao wrote this poem on one of his own paintings of bamboo growing out of rock. The first line has a wonderfully physical verb: the bamboo “bites” (咬定) the green mountain and will not let go. Treating the plant as if it were a person like this is personification. The second line gives the reason it holds so hard — its roots were set from the start in a crack in broken rock, a harsh place to grow. The second half sums up a lifetime of storms: a thousand grindings, ten thousand blows, and the bamboo is still firm and strong. Then comes the defiant last line — let the wind blow from east, west, south or north, as you please! The words 任尔 (“let you …”) carry a tone of “do your worst, I don’t care”. Of course the poem is about people too: hold on to what you believe, don’t be afraid of hardship, and don’t bend whichever way the wind blows. The language is close to everyday speech and the rhythm is strong, almost like a slogan. That is why “bite the green mountain and don’t let go” (咬定青山不放松) is still used today to encourage people to stick to their goals.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Zheng Xie (1693–1765), better known as Zheng Banqiao, was a Qing painter, calligrapher and poet, one of the “Eight Eccentrics of Yangzhou”. He served as a county magistrate in Shandong and was known for caring about ordinary people. He painted bamboo throughout his life and often wrote poems on his paintings, as here.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

咬定青山不放松，

yǎo dìng qīng shān bù fàng sōng

It bites into the green mountain and will not let go;

立根原在破岩中。

lì gēn yuán zài pò yán zhōng

its roots took hold, from the very start, in cracked rock.

千磨万击还坚劲，

qiān mó wàn jī hái jiān jìng

Ground down a thousand times, struck ten thousand, it stands firm —

任尔东西南北风。

rèn ěr dōng xī nán běi fēng

so blow, wind, from east or west, from south or north!
