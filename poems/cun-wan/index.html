导读About this poem

A pond brimming with water, the sun sinking behind the hills, and a little cowherd riding home sideways on his buffalo, playing a flute with no tune at all. A perfect snapshot of a summer evening in the countryside.

中文解析

这首诗像一幅农村傍晚的画。前两句写景：池塘边长满了青草，池塘里的水也满到了岸边；远处的山好像用嘴衔着落日，夕阳的影子倒映在清凉的水波里。“衔”字是拟人，写出太阳正落到山口、一半在山后的样子；“浸”字写出倒影在水中摇动。后两句写人：放牛的孩子回家了，他不是正着骑，而是横着坐在牛背上，拿着短笛随便吹，根本不成曲调。一个“横”字、一个“信口”，写出孩子的调皮和无忧无虑。前面是安静的山水，后面是活泼的孩子，静中有动，读完让人也觉得轻松自在。

In English

This poem is like a painting of a village at dusk. The first two lines are scenery: grass covers the pond’s banks and water fills the pond to the brim; the hills seem to hold the setting sun in their mouth, and its reflection soaks into the cool ripples. “Holding in the mouth” (衔) is personification — the sun is sinking into a gap between the hills, half hidden — and “soaking” (浸) shows its reflection trembling in the water. The last two lines bring in a person. A little cowherd is going home, sitting sideways on his water buffalo instead of astride it, blowing on a short flute whatever comes into his head, with no proper tune at all. Those two details — sitting sideways, playing any old notes — show how carefree and a bit mischievous the boy is. The quiet landscape and the lively child balance each other: stillness with a touch of movement. By the end the reader feels just as relaxed as the boy.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Almost nothing is known about Lei Zhen, a Song-dynasty poet; even his dates are unrecorded. This one short poem, now taught in Chinese primary schools, is what keeps his name alive.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

“陂”在这里读 bēi（池塘的岸），不读 pō 或 pí（统编教材注 bēi）。“衔”读 xián。漪 yī。“横牛背”的“横”读 héng。腔 qiāng。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

草满池塘水满陂，

cǎo mǎn chí táng shuǐ mǎn bēi

Grass crowds the pond, water brims to the bank,

山衔落日浸寒漪。

shān xián luò rì jìn hán yī

the hills bite into the setting sun, its glow soaking in the cool ripples.

牧童归去横牛背，

mù tóng guī qù héng niú bèi

A cowherd boy rides home, sideways on his buffalo,

短笛无腔信口吹。

duǎn dí wú qiāng xìn kǒu chuī

piping on a little flute — no tune, just whatever comes.
