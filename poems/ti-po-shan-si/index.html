导读About this poem

An early-morning walk into an old mountain temple: a winding path, flowers and trees, birds, a still pool — and a silence so deep you hear only the temple bell.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这首诗写诗人清晨游览破山寺（在今江苏常熟虞山）后院的所见所感。前两句写清早走进古寺，初升的太阳照着高高的树林。三四句是千古名句：弯弯曲曲的小路通向幽深僻静的地方，僧人住的禅房被繁茂的花木围着。五六句写山光让鸟儿也欢快起来，清澈的潭水倒映着天光云影，看着它，人心里的杂念也一下子消失了。“空”在这里是动词，意思是“使……变得空净”。最后两句写这里一切声音都静下来，只剩下敲钟、击磬的声音。用一点声音来衬托四周的安静，这种手法叫“以动衬静”：有了钟磬声，反而更显得山寺宁静。全诗写景清幽，又带着佛家“心静”的意味，读完让人心里也安静下来。“曲径通幽”今天还常用来形容园林小路，或比喻经过曲折才找到好地方。

In English

The poet visits the back courtyard of Poshan Temple (on Mount Yu in today’s Changshu, Jiangsu) early one morning. In the first couplet he walks into the old temple as the rising sun lights the tall trees. The second couplet is the famous one: a winding path leads to a secluded spot, where the monks’ meditation rooms are buried among flowers and trees. In the third, the mountain light makes even the birds happy, and the clear pool, reflecting the sky, empties a person’s mind of clutter — here 空 (“empty”) works as a verb: “to make empty”. In the last couplet every sound in the world falls silent, and only the temple bell and stone chime remain. Using a little sound to bring out a great silence is called “setting stillness against movement”: the bell makes the quiet feel deeper. The scenery is calm and fresh, with a Buddhist sense of a quiet mind, and reading it slowly is itself a little calming. 曲径通幽, “a winding path leads to a secluded place”, is still used for garden paths, or for finding something good after a roundabout journey.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Chang Jian (708–?) passed the imperial exam in 727 but held only minor posts; he later lived as a recluse and wrote mostly about mountains, temples and quiet places. Little else is known about his life.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

清晨入古寺，

qīng chén rù gǔ sì

At first light I walk into the old temple;

初日照高林。

chū rì zhào gāo lín

the new sun touches the tall trees.

曲径通幽处，

qū jìng tōng yōu chù

A winding path leads somewhere hidden and still,

禅房花木深。

chán fáng huā mù shēn

where the monks’ rooms lie deep in flowers and trees.

山光悦鸟性，

shān guāng yuè niǎo xìng

Mountain light makes the birds glad;

潭影空人心。

tán yǐng kōng rén xīn

the still pool clears the mind of every care.

万籁此都寂，

wàn lài cǐ dōu jì

Here all the world’s sounds fall silent;

但余钟磬音。

dàn yú zhōng qìng yīn

only the temple bell and chime ring on.
