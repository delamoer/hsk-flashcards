导读About this poem

What did the moon look like when you were little? Li Bai remembers: a white jade plate, a fairy’s mirror — with an immortal, a cassia tree and a rabbit making medicine up there.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

诗人回忆小时候看月亮：那时还不认识月亮，就把它叫作“白玉盘”，又怀疑它是仙人瑶台上的镜子，飞到了青云上面。这两个比喻都很像孩子的想法——圆、白、亮、会飞，天真又可爱。后四句写月亮里的传说：月中有仙人，垂着两只脚；有一棵桂树，长得圆圆的、枝叶茂盛；还有一只白兔在捣仙药。诗人好奇地问：药捣好了，要给谁吃呢？这些都是中国古代关于月亮的神话（月宫、桂树、玉兔），今天中秋节时孩子们还会听到。语言简单，想象丰富，很适合初学者朗读。

In English

Li Bai looks back to childhood: before he knew what the moon was, he called it a “white jade plate,” and wondered if it was a mirror from the fairies’ Jade Terrace, flying up above the blue clouds. Both comparisons are exactly how a child thinks — round, white, shiny, and somehow flying — and that is their charm. The next four lines bring in the old stories about the moon. An immortal sits up there with his feet dangling; a cassia tree grows full and round; a white rabbit pounds the medicine of immortality. Then the child’s question: when the medicine is ready, who is going to eat it? These are China’s traditional moon legends — the moon palace, the cassia tree and the Jade Rabbit — still told to children at the Mid-Autumn Festival today. With simple words and a lot of imagination, this is a perfect first poem for reading aloud. The full poem goes on to a darker picture of the moon being eaten away, which is not included here.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Li Bai (701–762), the “Immortal of Poetry”, loved the moon more than any other subject. “Old Song of the Bright Moon” is the title of a yuefu (ballad) tune that he rewrote; when and where he wrote it is unknown.

版本说明 About the text

节选全诗前八句（全诗十六句，后八句写蟾蜍蚀月、后羿射日等）。统编一年级教材只选前四句，网站选前八句。文本与维基文库《全唐诗》本一致；异文：“青云端”一作“白云端”，“何团团”一作“作团团”，“与谁餐”一作“谁与餐”，网站不用。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

小时不识月，

xiǎo shí bù shí yuè

When I was little and didn’t know the moon,

呼作白玉盘。

hū zuò bái yù pán

I called it a plate of white jade.

又疑瑶台镜，

yòu yí yáo tái jìng

Or maybe, I thought, a mirror from the fairy palace

飞在青云端。

fēi zài qīng yún duān

flying high above the blue clouds.

仙人垂两足，

xiān rén chuí liǎng zú

Up there an immortal dangles his feet,

桂树何团团。

guì shù hé tuán tuán

and the cassia tree grows round and thick.

白兔捣药成，

bái tù dǎo yào chéng

The white rabbit pounds the magic medicine —

问言与谁餐？

wèn yán yǔ shuí cān

but tell me, who is it for?
