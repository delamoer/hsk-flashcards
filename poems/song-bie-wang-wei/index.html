导读About this poem

The poem skips the goodbye itself and starts right after it: the friend has gone, the gate is closed at dusk, and the poet is already wondering whether he will come back when the grass turns green next spring.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

一般的送别诗会写分别时的场面：喝酒、握手、流泪。这首诗却从“送罢”写起——朋友已经走了。诗人回到山中的家，天色晚了，他轻轻关上柴门。“掩”这个动作很平常，却让人感到屋里一下子冷清了。后两句是诗人心里的问题：明年春天，青草又会变绿，你还会回来吗？草每年都会绿，人却不一定回来，用不变的自然对比难测的人事。“归不归”三个字只是问，没有回答，留下的是牵挂和不舍。全诗不说一个“愁”字，却处处是离别后的思念。

In English

Most farewell poems describe the parting itself — the wine, the last handshake, the tears. This one begins after it is over: “Having seen you off in the hills…” The friend has gone. The poet walks back to his mountain home; it is getting dark, and he pulls the brushwood gate shut. Closing a gate is an ordinary act, but here it makes us feel how empty the house has suddenly become. The second half is a question in his mind: next spring the grass will turn green again — will you come back? The contrast is gentle but sharp. Grass comes back every year without fail; a friend may not. The final words, 归不归 (“will you return or not?”), simply ask and leave the answer open, and that open question carries all his worry and affection. The poem never uses a word like “sad”. The closing of a gate and one unanswered question are enough to show how much the friend is missed. The last line also echoes an old line from the Songs of Chu about a wanderer who has not come home though spring grass is growing.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Wang Wei (701–761), poet, painter and Buddhist, spent long stretches at his retreat in the Zhongnan Mountains. Who the departing friend was is not recorded; the poem is also known as “Farewell in the Mountains” (山中送别).

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

山中相送罢，

shān zhōng xiāng sòng bà

Back in the hills after seeing you off,

日暮掩柴扉。

rì mù yǎn chái fēi

I close my brushwood gate as the sun goes down.

春草明年绿，

chūn cǎo míng nián lǜ

Next spring the grass will be green again —

王孙归不归？

wáng sūn guī bù guī

my friend, will you come back too?
