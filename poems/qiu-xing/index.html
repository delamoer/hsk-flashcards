导读About this poem

Stranded in the Yangtze Gorges, an ageing Du Fu watches autumn strip the maple woods, hears the whole town pounding winter clothes, and feels his heart tied to a lonely boat that cannot take him home.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

《秋兴八首》是杜甫晚年的代表作，这是第一首。当时他漂泊到夔州（今重庆奉节），离家乡很远，又老又病。首联写秋景：白露凋零了枫树林，巫山巫峡一片萧瑟阴森。颔联把画面推到最大：江上的波浪好像连着天在翻涌，边塞上的风云好像压到地面，天地都暗了——这既是眼前景，也暗示国家还在动乱。颈联是名句：菊花已经开过两次，也就是在这里过了两个秋天，每次看到都流下往日的泪；那条孤零零的小船一直系在岸边，系住的是一颗想回故乡的心。“系”字一语双关：既系船，也系心。尾联写声音：家家户户在赶做冬衣，傍晚白帝城高处传来急促的捣衣声。“砧”是捣衣用的石头，在古诗里常引起游子思家。全诗由景到情，沉郁厚重，被认为是七律的高峰。

In English

“Autumn Thoughts” is a set of eight poems from Du Fu’s last years, and this is the first. He was stranded in Kuizhou (today’s Fengjie, Chongqing), deep in the Yangtze Gorges, old, ill and far from home. The opening couplet sets the season: white dew has withered the maple woods, and the mountains and gorges of Wushan feel bleak and gloomy. The second couplet makes the picture enormous: waves on the river surge as if joined to the sky, and wind and cloud over the frontier press down on the earth, darkening everything — a real landscape that also hints at a country still in turmoil. The third couplet is the famous one: the chrysanthemums have bloomed twice — two autumns spent here — and each time they bring the tears of earlier days; a lonely boat stays moored at the bank, and what it ties down is a heart longing for home. The verb 系 (“to tie”) works both ways: it ties the boat and ties the heart. The last couplet turns to sound: every household is rushing to make winter clothes, and at dusk the quick beat of washing-stones drifts down from high Baidi Town. In classical poetry that pounding often makes a traveller think of home. Moving from landscape to feeling, dense and sombre, the poem is often counted among the finest seven-character regulated verses.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Du Fu (712–770) wrote the eight “Autumn Thoughts” in 766 in Kuizhou, on the Yangtze, where he lived for about two years. Unable to return north after the An Lushan Rebellion, he looks back on Chang’an and the lost glory of the Tang throughout the series.

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

玉露凋伤枫树林，

yù lù diāo shāng fēng shù lín

White dew has withered the maple woods;

巫山巫峡气萧森。

wū shān wū xiá qì xiāo sēn

over the Wu hills and gorge, the air turns grim.

江间波浪兼天涌，

jiāng jiān bō làng jiān tiān yǒng

On the river, waves heave up to meet the sky;

塞上风云接地阴。

sài shàng fēng yún jiē dì yīn

over the passes, wind and cloud sink dark to the ground.

丛菊两开他日泪，

cóng jú liǎng kāi tā rì lèi

Twice now the chrysanthemums have bloomed, and twice my old tears fell;

孤舟一系故园心。

gū zhōu yī xì gù yuán xīn

my lonely boat stays tied here, and with it my heart for home.

寒衣处处催刀尺，

hán yī chù chù cuī dāo chǐ

Everywhere, scissors and rulers race to make winter clothes;

白帝城高急暮砧。

bái dì chéng gāo jí mù zhēn

from high Baidi Town, the dusk rings with pounding stones.
