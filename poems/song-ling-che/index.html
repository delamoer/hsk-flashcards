送灵澈上人

Sòng Líng Chè Shàng Rén
Seeing Off the Monk Lingche
刘长卿Liú Zhǎngqīng · Liu Zhangqing唐 Tang

导读About this poem

A farewell with no tears and no speeches: an evening bell, a monk with his hat on his back, and the setting sun following him into the hills.

题跋 · 解析Commentary

中文解析

这是一首送别诗，送的是一位和尚朋友灵澈。前两句写远处：青翠的竹林里藏着寺庙，傍晚的钟声远远传来——钟声在召唤僧人回寺，也告诉我们天快黑了。后两句写人：灵澈背着斗笠，身上披着夕阳的余光，一个人朝青山深处越走越远。诗人没有写自己说了什么、心里多难过，只写他一直站着目送朋友，直到人影消失在山里，这份不舍都在“远”字里。全诗像一幅淡淡的山水画，安静、清冷，也带着僧人那种不留恋的淡泊。

In English

This is a farewell poem for a monk friend, Lingche, who is going back to his temple. The first two lines look into the distance: a temple hidden among deep green bamboo, and its evening bell drifting over from far away. The bell calls the monks home, and it also tells us night is coming. The last two lines turn to the person: Lingche carries his bamboo hat on his back, the last of the sunset on his shoulders, and walks alone deeper and deeper into the green hills. The poet never says what he said at the parting or how he feels. He just shows us that he kept watching until his friend disappeared — the feeling sits in the final word, 远 (“far”). The whole poem is like a pale ink landscape painting: quiet, cool and unhurried, matching the calm of a monk who doesn’t cling to anything. It is a good example of how much a Chinese quatrain can leave unsaid.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

Liu Zhangqing (c. 726–c. 790) was a mid-Tang official, twice demoted, best known for his five-character poems. Lingche (746–816) was a poet-monk; the poem is thought to have been written in Runzhou (today’s Zhenjiang) as he returned to the Bamboo Grove Temple there.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

荷笠 hè lì（“荷”是背、扛，读 hè，不读 hé）；杳杳 yǎo yǎo；斜阳 xié；作者名“长卿”读 zhǎng。机器朗读“荷”易读成 hé，已在“朗读用字”列替换同音字。

版本说明 About the text

采用通行的《唐诗三百首》文本。“荷笠带斜阳”，维基文库（据《全唐诗》）作“荷笠带夕阳”，意思相同。

全文 · 拼音 Full text with pinyin

苍苍竹林寺，

cāng cāng zhú lín sì

Deep in dark green, the Bamboo Grove Temple —

杳杳钟声晚。

yǎo yǎo zhōng shēng wǎn

its evening bell comes faint from far away.

荷笠带斜阳，

hè lì dài xié yáng

Hat on his back, the low sun on his shoulders,

青山独归远。

qīng shān dú guī yuǎn

he walks alone, far into the green hills.
