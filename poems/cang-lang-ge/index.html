Song of the Canglang River

导读About this poem

Two lines, sung by a fisherman more than 2,000 years ago: if the river is clean, wash your hat strings in it; if it is muddy, wash your feet. A tiny song about how to live in a clean world — and a dirty one.

中文解析

这首短歌只有两句：沧浪河的水清，就用来洗我帽子上的带子；沧浪河的水浑，就用来洗我的脚。帽带（缨）戴在头上，是干净、体面的东西；脚是最脏的地方。水清水浊，就像社会的好坏：世道清明，就出来做官、做事；世道混乱，就退回去，过自己的日子，不跟着变坏。在《楚辞·渔父》里，被流放的屈原说“举世皆浊我独清”，宁死也不肯同流合污；渔父笑了笑，唱着这首歌走了——他的意思是：人要懂得随着环境调整自己。在《孟子》里，孔子听到这首歌，又读出另一层意思：水清水浊是水自己决定的，人受到尊重或侮辱，也是自己招来的。一首小歌，有好几种读法，所以流传了两千多年。

In English

The whole song is two lines: when the Canglang River runs clear, I wash my hat strings in it; when it runs muddy, I wash my feet. Hat strings (缨) are worn on the head — clean and dignified; feet are the dirtiest part of you. The clear or muddy water stands for the state of the world. When times are good, go out and serve; when times are bad, step back and live quietly, without letting the dirt change you. The song appears in two famous books, each reading it differently. In “The Fisherman” (渔父) from the Songs of Chu, the exiled Qu Yuan declares, “The whole world is muddy, and I alone am clean” — he would rather die than compromise. The fisherman just smiles, sings this song and rows away: be flexible, adapt to the times. In the Mencius, Confucius hears children singing it and draws another lesson: whether water is clean or muddy decides how people use it, and in the same way, people bring respect or insult on themselves. Two lines, several ways to read them — which is why it has lasted for over 2,000 years.

作者与背景 The poet & the moment

This is an anonymous folk song from the south, probably the state of Chu, before 221 BC. It is recorded in the Mencius (4th century BC), where Confucius hears a child singing it, and in “The Fisherman”, a piece traditionally attributed to Qu Yuan, where a fisherman sings it.

读音注意 Pronunciation notes

濯 zhuó（洗）；缨 yīng。“沧浪”读 cāng láng，一般不读 làng。

版本说明 About the text

采用《楚辞·渔父》的文本（“濯吾缨”“濯吾足”）；《孟子·离娄上》作“濯我缨”“濯我足”。网站每句一行（共两行）。
